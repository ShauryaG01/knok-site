01 Overview

Overview

eClerx Services is a KPO and analytics firm with deep roots in financial services, e-commerce operations, and cable/telecom verticals. Product Managers here are expected to bridge client-side business problems with internal platform and analytics capabilities, so interviews tend to blend business acumen with data-first thinking.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 48 open Product Manager roles at eClerx across India, part of a broader market of 2,009 PM openings nationally. Candidates report a process that typically runs three to five rounds, covering a mix of case studies, behavioural questions, and stakeholder management scenarios.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these questions coming up most often across eClerx PM interviews:

  1. Walk me through a product you built or improved end-to-end. What was your north-star metric?
  2. eClerx serves large enterprise clients. How do you manage a product when the 'user' and the 'buyer' are completely different people?
  3. Describe a time you had to prioritise a client request that conflicted with your product roadmap. What did you decide and why?
  4. How would you measure the success of an internal analytics dashboard used by operations teams?
  5. A financial services client says their ops team is spending too much time on manual reconciliation. How do you turn that into a product brief?
  6. Tell me about a time you used data to change a stakeholder's mind. What data did you use, and how did you present it?
  7. How do you work with offshore delivery teams when defining requirements? What goes wrong and how do you prevent it?
  8. eClerx operates across B2B and B2B2C models in some verticals. How does that change how you think about your user?
  9. Describe how you would run a discovery sprint with a new enterprise client who is not sure what they want.
  10. A feature shipped and adoption is low. Walk me through how you would diagnose and fix it.
  11. How do you decide between building a configurable platform feature versus a one-off client customisation?
  12. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a client request. How did you frame it and what was the outcome?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you used data to change a stakeholder's mind.

*Situation:* My team was under pressure to add a bulk-export feature to our ops dashboard because one senior client manager kept requesting it in every review call.

*Task:* I needed to either build it or make a case for why we should not, without damaging the client relationship.

*Action:* I pulled usage logs and found that only a small fraction of active users had ever opened the export menu, and some of those were internal testers. I also checked support tickets and saw zero requests from end users themselves. I put together a one-page visual showing the usage funnel, support volume, and the backlog items with higher user demand. I presented it to the stakeholder as 'here is what the data tells us about where we get the most impact,' rather than 'your request is wrong.'

*Result:* The stakeholder agreed to defer the export feature. We shipped a higher-priority workflow automation instead, which the client's own ops report flagged as reducing average handle time meaningfully in the following quarter.

---

Q: How do you manage a product when the user and the buyer are different people?

*Situation:* At a previous role, our platform was purchased by enterprise procurement teams but used daily by junior analysts in client firms.

*Task:* I had to keep the buyer satisfied on cost and compliance while making the product genuinely useful for analysts who had no say in the purchase.

*Action:* I set up two separate feedback loops: quarterly business reviews with client account leads (buyers) and monthly in-app surveys plus fortnightly calls with actual users. I maintained a dual backlog, tagging each item as 'buyer-critical' or 'user-critical.' During roadmap planning I ensured we shipped at least one high-visibility user item per cycle so adoption stayed strong, which in turn gave the buyer proof of value.

*Result:* Renewal conversations became easier because we could show both sides concrete wins. The account team shared that our platform was cited in the majority of renewal calls as a reason clients stayed.

---

Q: Describe how you would run a discovery sprint with a new enterprise client who is not sure what they want.

*Situation:* A new financial services client signed a contract but their brief was vague: 'we need better visibility into our operations.'

*Task:* I needed to convert a fuzzy mandate into a prioritised problem statement within two weeks so engineering could start.

*Action:* I structured a four-session discovery sprint. Session one was a stakeholder mapping exercise to identify who experiences the pain daily versus who owns the budget. Session two was shadowing ops analysts for a half-day each. Session three was a 'pain auction' where each stakeholder received a set of votes to allocate across problems we had observed. Session four was a draft problem statement read-back where I presented our interpretation and invited corrections.

*Result:* We identified that the core issue was a multi-day lag in reconciliation reporting, not a dashboard design problem as originally assumed. The revised brief cut first-milestone scope significantly and we hit delivery on time.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences together) and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

CIRCLES (Comprehend, Identify, Report, Cut, List, Evaluate, Summarise) works well for product design questions. eClerx interviews often start with an operations problem, so framing your answer around comprehending the user's job-to-be-done before jumping to solutions signals PM maturity.

Metrics-first framing is particularly valued at eClerx given their analytics heritage. When asked 'how would you measure X,' lead with the metric you would own (not just track), explain why that metric is a leading indicator of business value, and then name one counter-metric you would watch to avoid gaming.

The client-user-business triangle is useful for any question about prioritisation or trade-offs. eClerx sits between enterprise clients and their end users, so showing that you can articulate all three perspectives, and explain whose need takes precedence in which context, consistently impresses interviewers according to candidate reports.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Business-outcome orientation. eClerx PMs are accountable to enterprise SLAs and client satisfaction scores, not just internal OKRs. Interviewers want to see that you connect every feature decision back to a business outcome, not just user delight.

Comfort with ambiguity from clients. A recurring theme in candidate reports is that eClerx values PMs who can structure unclear problems quickly. Showing that you have a repeatable discovery process (not just instinct) is more reassuring than showing you have strong opinions.

Cross-functional fluency. eClerx delivery teams are large, distributed, and often offshore. Interviewers probe for how you write requirements, handle misalignment, and keep offshore engineering teams unblocked without micromanaging.

Data literacy without data dependency. You should be comfortable reading SQL output, interpreting funnel reports, and questioning data quality, but also able to make a call when data is thin. Both extremes (ignoring data entirely and being paralysed without it) are red flags.

Client communication skills. Because eClerx is a services business, PMs often participate directly in client calls. Interviewers assess whether you can translate technical constraints into client-friendly language and manage expectations professionally under pressure.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Company and domain grounding. Read eClerx's publicly available annual reports and investor presentations to understand which verticals drive revenue. Note the language they use to describe their platform products versus managed services. Review the specific job description and map each listed requirement to a concrete story from your own experience.

Week 2: Case and metric practice. Practise at least five operations-improvement case studies, framing each answer in terms of measurable outcomes. Prepare your north-star metric answer for every product you have worked on. Rehearse the CIRCLES framework out loud so it feels natural rather than mechanical.

Week 3: Behavioural story bank. Write out six to eight STAR stories covering: prioritisation under pressure, stakeholder conflict, using data to make a decision, cross-functional coordination, a product that failed and what you learned, and a time you said no. Time yourself to keep each answer under three minutes.

Before each round. Candidates report that eClerx interviewers often open with 'tell me about eClerx and why this role.' Prepare a specific two-minute answer that references their verticals and how your background is directly relevant. Generic answers are a common early filter.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating it like a pure tech-product interview. eClerx is a services and analytics company. Answers heavy on consumer app examples without a B2B or operations angle can fall flat. Ground your stories in client value and operational impact wherever possible.
  1. Vague metrics in STAR answers. Saying 'improved efficiency' without any anchor weakens your case. Use directional language or a publicly reported figure if one exists. Even 'the client ops team reported a meaningful reduction in turnaround time' is stronger than nothing.
  1. Ignoring the client layer. Many candidates talk about users and business goals but forget that at eClerx there is always an enterprise client in the middle. Not acknowledging this layer signals you have not understood the business model.
  1. Over-engineering the discovery process. While structure is valued, candidates who describe discovery processes requiring months of research before any decision get pushback. Show that you can be structured and fast at the same time.
  1. Not asking questions at the end. eClerx typically allocates time at the end of each round for your questions. Asking nothing, or asking only about compensation, is commonly cited as a negative signal. Prepare two or three questions about team structure, client relationships, or how success is measured in the first few months on the job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the eClerx PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to five rounds, though this varies by seniority and the specific team hiring. Rounds commonly cover a mix of HR screening, a case study or take-home assignment, and one or more competency interviews. There is no official published process, so it is worth asking your recruiter at the start what to expect for your specific role.

Does eClerx give a product case study, and how should I prepare for it?

Several candidates report receiving a case study, sometimes as a take-home and sometimes in a live discussion format. Cases tend to be operations-focused rather than consumer-product focused, often involving a scenario like improving an internal dashboard or reducing manual work for a client's ops team. Practise structuring your answer around a clear problem statement, measurable success criteria, and a phased delivery plan rather than jumping straight to solutions.

What is the salary range for a Product Manager at eClerx?

Specific eClerx figures are not publicly verified at meaningful scale. Using broad market bands from industry surveys: Associate PM roles typically fall in the 12-20 LPA range, PM roles with 3-6 years of experience in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PM roles in the 40-60 LPA range. Actual offers depend on your level, negotiation, and the exact scope of the role, so treat these as reference points rather than guarantees.

Is domain experience in financial services or e-commerce required?

Domain experience is valued but candidates from adjacent industries such as SaaS, analytics, or enterprise software have reported receiving offers by demonstrating strong operations thinking and a fast ramp-up ability. The more important signal seems to be whether you understand B2B product dynamics and can structure ambiguous client problems quickly. Highlighting any experience with enterprise clients, data products, or process automation strengthens your case regardless of vertical.

How competitive is the PM hiring market right now?

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 2,009 Product Manager openings across India, with Bangalore (271 roles) and Delhi (177 roles) as the largest hubs. eClerx had 48 open PM roles at the same point, which is a meaningful volume for a single company. Competition is real, so tailoring your application and preparing thoroughly for the case and behavioural rounds matters more than applying broadly without preparation.

What should I ask eClerx interviewers at the end of a round?

Strong questions candidates have found useful include: how is PM success measured in the first six months, how much direct client exposure does the role have, and how does the product team collaborate with delivery and account management. These questions signal business awareness and genuine interest in the day-to-day reality of the role. Avoid questions easily answered by the company website, as that can signal low preparation to the interviewer.
