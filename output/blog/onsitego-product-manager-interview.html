01 Overview

Overview

Onsitego is one of India's leading extended warranty and after-sales service companies, protecting electronics, appliances, and gadgets for millions of consumers. The business sits at a unique crossroads: part insurance, part logistics, part customer experience, and increasingly, a technology platform connecting OEMs, retailers, and field service engineers across the country.

For PMs, this means building products that customers use mostly when something has already gone wrong with their device. Reliability, clarity, and speed are the defining product values. Teams typically span the consumer app, B2B integrations with brands and retail chains, service partner management workflows, and internal claim processing tools.

Onsitego currently has 14 open Product Manager roles. Salary bands in the market typically place Associate PMs at 12-20 LPA, mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience at 24-40 LPA, Senior PMs at 40-60 LPA, and Group or Principal PMs at 55-90 LPA and above.

Candidates report the interview process typically runs 3-4 rounds: an initial HR or hiring manager screen, a product case or take-home assignment, one or two rounds with cross-functional stakeholders or engineering leads, and a final discussion with senior leadership. Round names and sequence can vary by team.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from the nature of Onsitego's business and what candidates typically report from their interviews. Prepare a structured answer or story for each.

  1. Walk me through how you would redesign the claim filing experience on the Onsitego consumer app to reduce drop-offs at each step.
  2. How would you build a dashboard for Onsitego's service partner network to help technicians manage job assignments and cut turnaround time?
  3. Onsitego sells to consumers (B2C) and to retail or OEM partners (B2B). How do you prioritise the roadmap when their needs conflict?
  4. How would you increase warranty plan attach rates at the point of purchase, both on e-commerce platforms and in offline retail stores?
  5. Describe a product or feature you shipped from start to finish. How did you handle scope changes midway?
  6. How would you define and measure the success of a newly launched device protection plan?
  7. A large retail partner is seeing high claim rejection rates and threatening to promote a competitor's plan instead. How do you investigate and fix this?
  8. How would you design a self-serve claims portal that meaningfully reduces inbound support volume?
  9. The OEM warranty and Onsitego's plan overlap for the first year of device ownership. How would you position and differentiate Onsitego's offering to drive purchases?
  10. Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineering team on a feature's feasibility. How did you resolve it?
  11. Describe a situation where data changed your mind on a product direction.
  12. If Onsitego were entering a new vertical (two-wheelers or medical equipment), how would you evaluate the opportunity and decide what to build first?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a product or feature you shipped from start to finish. How did you handle scope changes midway?

*Situation:* At my previous company I was the PM for a self-serve claim filing flow on our mobile app. About three weeks before the planned launch, the operations team flagged that field service partners in smaller cities could not support real-time slot booking, which was the centrepiece of the new flow.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to delay the full launch, ship a reduced version, or find a workaround that still delivered value on schedule.

*Action:* I pulled claim volume data to see where existing requests were concentrated. The bulk came from the top metros. I negotiated with the logistics team to commit to real-time slot booking only in metro areas for launch, rewrote the in-app copy to set honest expectations for customers in smaller cities, and set up a manual scheduling fallback handled by the support team. I added the full rollout to the top of the next sprint backlog with clear acceptance criteria.

*Result:* We launched on time without breaking our customer promise. Drop-offs in the claim filing flow fell noticeably, and the smaller-city rollout followed in the next release. The retrospective flagged the scoping decision as a good example of pragmatic delivery.

---

Q: Describe a situation where data changed your mind on a product direction.

*Situation:* I was convinced that adding a live chat widget to our post-purchase confirmation screen would reduce 'where is my warranty?' support calls. I had a rough business case and informal team buy-in.

*Task:* Before committing engineering time, I ran a quick research sprint to validate the assumption.

*Action:* I reviewed several months of support ticket tags and found the majority of inbound contacts were not about warranty status. They were about claim eligibility questions that came up weeks or months after purchase. I also interviewed a handful of recent customers and found they rarely returned to the confirmation screen after day one. The data pointed to the week-of-expiry reminder notification as the higher-leverage touchpoint, not the confirmation page.

*Result:* I shelved the live chat proposal and redirected the team to build a smarter expiry reminder with a built-in eligibility check. Support contacts in that category fell in the quarter that followed. The experience reinforced the habit of separating 'where I think users struggle' from 'where the data shows they actually struggle.'

---

Q: A large retail partner is seeing high claim rejection rates and threatening to switch to a competitor's plan. How do you investigate and fix this?

*Situation:* I faced this directly. A key retail chain partner told us their floor staff were fielding too many customer complaints about rejected claims, and the partner's sales team had started recommending a rival protection plan.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause fast enough to preserve the relationship, and to figure out whether this was a product issue, a process failure, or a communication gap.

*Action:* I pulled rejection data for customers who had bought through this specific partner, segmented by rejection reason code. The top reason was 'damage type not covered under selected plan.' This told me customers were buying a lower-tier plan but filing claims for accidental damage that only the higher-tier plan covered. I cross-checked with checkout session recordings and found the plan comparison screen was ambiguous about what 'accidental damage' included. I worked with design to rewrite the coverage comparison copy, added a one-screen coverage summary to the post-purchase email, and ran a short briefing session with the partner's floor staff on explaining plan tiers.

*Result:* Rejection rates for this partner's customers dropped noticeably in the weeks after the changes. The partner reversed their decision to switch, and we applied the same copy improvements across all retail partners. The root cause was a messaging gap, not a policy flaw.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and improvement questions

Start with who uses the feature and when. For Onsitego, the key moment is almost always high-stress: a device has broken and the customer needs fast resolution. Frame your answer around: (1) who the user is, (2) what they are anxious about at that moment, (3) what friction exists today, (4) what you would change and why, and (5) how you would measure improvement. Avoid jumping to solutions before articulating the user's emotional state.

For prioritisation questions

Use a RICE-style structure: how many users does this affect (Reach), how much does it move the key metric (Impact), how confident are you in that estimate (Confidence), and how much engineering effort does it require (Effort)? For Onsitego, also note whether a feature benefits the B2C consumer side, the B2B partner side, or both, since leadership will typically want to know whose roadmap is being served.

For metrics questions

For a warranty or service product, a useful funnel to walk through is: plan purchase, plan activation (customer registers the device), first claim filed, claim resolved successfully, and plan renewal or upgrade. Pick two or three metrics that signal genuine customer value rather than vanity counts, and pair each with a guardrail metric to prevent gaming.

For root cause and partner problems

Use a structured breakdown: Is this a product issue (the feature is broken or confusing), a process issue (operations or partners are not following the right steps), or a communication issue (users or partners have wrong expectations)? Candidates who jump to product fixes without ruling out process or communication causes typically face pushback in Onsitego interviews.

For new category or market entry questions

Cover three things: the size and shape of the opportunity (who has the problem, how often, what they do today), what Onsitego uniquely brings to that category (service network, brand trust, existing customer data), and what the smallest useful first version would look like to test before scaling.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep customer empathy for stressful moments

Onsitego's product is used when a customer's laptop dies or their washing machine breaks down. Interviewers want to see that you understand this emotional context, not just the transaction mechanics. Candidates who treat warranty as a pure financial product and miss the service experience side tend to struggle.

Comfort with both B2B and B2C thinking

Onsitego serves consumers directly and also depends on retail partners and OEMs to sell and service its plans. Interviewers typically probe whether you can hold both perspectives: what the end customer needs versus what a channel partner needs, and how to balance those when they pull in different directions.

Operations awareness

A lot of Onsitego's product complexity lives in the service partner network and logistics layer, not just the app. Candidates who pitch purely digital solutions without thinking about field execution, partner capacity, or geographic coverage gaps get challenged quickly. Show that you ask 'how does this actually get delivered on the ground?' before committing to a design.

Structured, data-informed decision making

Interviewers look for candidates who break problems down before proposing solutions, who can name the metric they would move and the guardrail they would watch, and who can tell a credible story about changing direction when data said they were wrong.

Clear communication across functions

Onsitego PMs work closely with engineering, operations, sales, and support teams. Interviewers pay attention to how you describe past collaboration, particularly whether you take ownership of outcomes rather than attributing wins to yourself and misses to others.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the domain

Download the Onsitego app and go through the full purchase and claim flow as a regular user. Read their plan comparison pages carefully, noting where the language is clear and where it is ambiguous. Look up publicly available news about Onsitego's partnerships and product announcements. Spend time understanding how extended warranty plans compete with OEM warranties and standalone insurance products.

Week 2: Build your story bank

Write out 5-6 STAR stories covering: a product you shipped end-to-end, a time data changed your decision, a time you handled a difficult stakeholder or partner, a prioritisation call made under constraint, and a time you had to course-correct. Practice telling each story in under three minutes.

Week 3: Practice product cases out loud

Pick two or three questions from the list above and practice answering them by speaking, not just writing. Record yourself and replay to catch filler words and unstructured tangents. Ask a peer to push back mid-answer on your assumptions, as interviewers at Onsitego typically probe with follow-up questions.

Before each round

Review the job description for the specific team (consumer product, B2B platform, internal tools) and tailor your examples to that context. Prepare two or three focused questions about the team's current roadmap priorities and how PM success is measured at Onsitego. Candidates who ask sharp, informed questions consistently stand out in the final round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Onsitego like a pure tech startup

Onsitego's core value chain depends on a physical service network: technicians, spare parts logistics, and geographic coverage. Candidates who pitch solutions as if the product is only a mobile app, without considering operational feasibility, tend to lose credibility quickly.

Skipping the partner lens

Many candidates prepare only for B2C product questions. Onsitego interviewers often introduce B2B partner scenarios involving retail chains, OEMs, or service engineers mid-discussion. If you have not thought about what a channel partner cares about, these questions can catch you unprepared.

Jumping to solutions before defining the problem

A common pattern in interview feedback is candidates proposing features before clearly stating who has the problem, how often, and what they are doing today. Take at least a minute to frame the problem before pitching any solution.

Vague metrics

Saying 'user satisfaction improved' or 'engagement went up' without specifying which metric, over what period, and compared to what baseline is a red flag in product interviews. Have a specific, nameable metric ready for every result you cite.

Not acknowledging the insurance and regulatory layer

Onsitego's plans are regulated products with defined coverage terms. Candidates who suggest adding or removing covered scenarios without flagging compliance or policy implications come across as inexperienced in the domain. You do not need deep insurance knowledge, but saying 'this would need validation against plan terms and any applicable regulation' signals domain maturity.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Onsitego PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically runs 3-4 rounds. These usually include an initial HR or hiring manager screen, a product case or take-home assignment, one or two stakeholder or technical rounds, and a final discussion with senior leadership. The exact sequence can vary depending on the team and the seniority of the role.

Is there a take-home assignment or case study in the Onsitego PM interview?

Many candidates report receiving a product case or take-home assignment, typically after the first screen. The case often involves a product improvement, a metrics question, or a prioritisation scenario relevant to Onsitego's business. You are usually given a day or two to complete it, and the following round typically involves walking the hiring team through your thinking.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Onsitego?

Based on market data, Associate PM roles typically fall in the 12-20 LPA range, mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PMs in the 40-60 LPA range. Group or Principal PM positions can go to 55-90 LPA and above. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and negotiation.

Do I need a background in insurance or warranty to get a PM role at Onsitego?

No specialist insurance background is required. However, you should understand the basics of how extended warranty and protection plans work, including coverage tiers, claim processes, and the role of channel partners. Spending a few hours using the product and reading publicly available plan terms before your interview typically gives candidates a noticeable edge.

How important is the B2B side of the product for Onsitego PM interviews?

More important than most candidates expect. Onsitego works closely with retail chains, e-commerce platforms, and OEM brands, and PMs often own features that serve these partners directly. Interviewers commonly probe how you would handle conflicting priorities between end consumers and channel partners, so preparing a structured answer to this scenario is worth your time.
