01 Role Overview

Role Overview

UI/UX Designers decide how a product looks and how easy it is to use. UX (User Experience) focuses on research, user flows, and wireframes. UI (User Interface) handles the visual layer: colours, typography, buttons, and spacing. In most Indian companies, especially startups, one person handles both.

The demand spans product startups, IT services firms building client-facing tools, fintech apps, edtech platforms, and e-commerce. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 29 active UI/UX Designer openings across India. Delhi leads with 8 openings, Bangalore has 6, and Mumbai has 4. Hyderabad and Pune have 1 each, and Chennai showed no active listings in this snapshot.

Job titles vary widely: 'Product Designer', 'Interaction Designer', 'Visual Designer', and 'UX Researcher' all overlap significantly with UI/UX Designer skills. Search for all of them when looking for openings.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Core design tool: Figma
Figma is the industry standard in India. It covers wireframing, interactive prototyping, design systems, and developer handoff. Start here before anything else. Adobe XD is fading from job descriptions; Figma proficiency is what recruiters check first.

UX fundamentals
User research is the backbone of good UX. You need to know how to run user interviews, conduct usability tests, and turn findings into clear insights. Alongside this, learn information architecture (how content is organised) and how to map user flows before you open Figma.

Visual and UI skills
Typography, colour theory, grid systems, and spacing are non-negotiable. You also need to understand design systems and component libraries, responsive design for mobile and web, and at least basic micro-interactions.

Collaboration and communication
Designers in India frequently present work to product managers and senior stakeholders. Practise explaining your design decisions in plain business language, not design jargon. Writing clear documentation for developer handoff is equally important.

Good to have
Basic HTML and CSS knowledge makes handoff conversations smoother and prevents you from designing things that are hard to build. Familiarity with accessibility standards (WCAG) is appearing more often in job descriptions at larger product companies. Basic analytics literacy, reading usage data in Mixpanel or Google Analytics, helps you back design decisions with real evidence.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

1. Learn Figma from scratch (Weeks 1-4)
Start with free tutorials on YouTube and Figma's own learning resources. Focus on frames, components, auto-layout, and building a simple prototype. Do not jump to advanced features until the basics feel comfortable.

2. Study UX theory alongside tools (Weeks 2-6)
Read 'Don't Make Me Think' by Steve Krug. Work through Google's UX Design Certificate on Coursera, publicly reported as one of the most widely recognised entry-level credentials among Indian hiring managers. Understand the design thinking process: Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test.

3. Build a practice project (Weeks 4-8)
Pick a real problem you have experienced. Redesign a clunky app you use daily, or solve a local problem like a college canteen ordering flow. This is not portfolio work yet; it is learning by doing.

4. Complete 3-5 portfolio case studies (Months 2-4)
Each case study should show your process, not just the final screens. Document your research, wireframes, iterations, and final design. Recruiters in India consistently say the thinking behind the work matters more than the polished visuals alone.

5. Get community feedback (Months 3-5)
Share work on Behance, Dribbble, and LinkedIn. Post in Indian design communities and ask for honest critique. Revising based on real feedback sharpens your skills faster than any course.

6. Publish your portfolio (Month 4-5)
Framer, Wix, or even a well-structured Notion page works. Three to five strong case studies with clear problem statements, your process, and the outcome beat twenty rushed screens every time.

7. Apply and network actively (Month 5 onwards)
Update your LinkedIn headline, Naukri profile, and resume. Apply to companies whose products you use and understand. Reach out to designers at those companies on LinkedIn with short, specific messages. Attend design meetups in your city.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

The time to your first UI/UX role depends heavily on your starting point. A fresh graduate with no design background typically needs 8-12 months of focused effort. Someone switching from a related field (graphic design, front-end development, or product management) can often make the move in 4-6 months.

Internships are a legitimate and smart path in India. Many designers land a 3-6 month internship first, build real product work to show, and convert to full-time. Do not skip internships because they feel like a step down; they are a fast track to portfolio work that actually ships.

For mid-career growth, Glassdoor and industry surveys commonly cite that designers with solid portfolios and 3-5 years of experience move into senior or lead roles. Specialising in UX research or building deep expertise in design systems can speed up that progression.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

The Naukri and LinkedIn reality
Most Indian recruiters use Naukri as their primary sourcing tool for mid-level roles, while LinkedIn dominates for product companies and startups. Keep both profiles complete and keyword-rich. Use the exact job title you are targeting, 'UI/UX Designer' or 'Product Designer', not a creative variant, so you appear in recruiter searches.

Formal education paths
NID (National Institute of Design) and IIT design programs are the most recognised formal routes in India. However, most working designers in the industry are self-taught or completed short courses. A strong portfolio consistently outweighs a degree at the hiring stage for individual contributor roles.

Communities worth joining
India has active design communities. Facebook groups focused on Indian UX and product design, India-specific Slack groups for designers, and local chapters of IxDA (Interaction Design Association) are good starting points. Bengaluru, Delhi, and Mumbai have the most active in-person design events.

How companies actually hire
Startups in India often give a design assignment during hiring: solve a real or hypothetical problem within 2-3 days. Practise timed design sprints so the format does not catch you off guard. IT services companies also hire UI/UX designers, but the work leans toward enterprise software and client deliverables rather than consumer product design.

Freelancing as a portfolio builder
Many Indian designers take on freelance projects through Upwork or personal referrals before their first full-time role. Even small paid projects, a restaurant website or a local business app, count as real work and show real clients.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a formal design degree to become a UI/UX Designer in India?

No. Most hiring managers at Indian product companies and startups care about your portfolio and problem-solving ability far more than your degree. NID and IIT design programs carry strong brand recognition, but a well-documented portfolio of 3-5 case studies consistently wins over a certificate with no work to show. Google's UX Design Certificate on Coursera is publicly reported as widely accepted by Indian recruiters and is a practical structured option if you prefer guided learning over self-study.

Figma or Adobe XD: which should I learn first?

Learn Figma. It is the dominant tool in Indian product companies and startups, and job descriptions overwhelmingly ask for Figma proficiency. Adobe XD is still in use at some larger firms but has been losing ground steadily. Once you are strong in Figma, picking up XD later takes only a few days if a specific role requires it.

What salary can I expect as a fresher UI/UX Designer in India?

The data snapshot for this role did not include salary ranges, so treat any figure as a rough market reference. Glassdoor and LinkedIn commonly cite entry-level UI/UX Designer salaries at 3-6 LPA for freshers at IT services companies, with product-led startups sometimes going higher. Your city matters: openings in Bangalore, Delhi, and Mumbai tend to pay more than smaller markets. Building a strong portfolio is the most effective lever you have on your starting offer.

How important is a portfolio compared to a resume?

For a UI/UX Designer, the portfolio is more important than the resume. Most hiring managers look at your Behance link or portfolio site before reading your resume in detail. A portfolio that shows your design process, research, and iterations, not just polished final screens, is what gets you shortlisted. Treat your resume as the document that gets your application opened; treat your portfolio as the document that gets you the interview call.

Do I need to know how to code?

You do not need to code to work as a UI/UX Designer. Basic HTML and CSS knowledge helps: it makes conversations with developers easier, prevents you from designing things that are hard to build, and signals technical credibility in interviews. Many designers in India start with zero coding knowledge and do well. If you have time after building your core design skills, a short HTML and CSS primer is a worthwhile investment, not a requirement.

How do I get my first project if I have no clients and no work experience?

Create your own briefs: redesign an existing app you find frustrating, document the whole process with user research and iterations, and publish it as a case study. Volunteer to design for NGOs, college clubs, or local small businesses for free or a small fee. Freelance platforms like Upwork accept new profiles, and even small paid projects count as real work. The goal is to have something concrete to show and talk through in an interview, and self-initiated projects serve that purpose just as well as client work at the start.
