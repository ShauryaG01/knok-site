02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have interviewed at Exotel report questions that test B2B product thinking, API product experience, and comfort with Indian enterprise dynamics. Here are the questions you should practice most:

  1. How would you prioritize the roadmap for Exotel's cloud telephony API platform when large enterprise clients and growing SMBs have conflicting needs?
  2. Exotel competes with global players and local alternatives. How would you differentiate Exotel's offering for an Indian enterprise buyer?
  3. Walk us through how you would define success metrics for a new AI-powered call analytics feature.
  4. A major banking client reports a spike in call drop rates after a recent release. How do you investigate and respond?
  5. How would you improve the developer onboarding experience for a startup integrating Exotel's voice API for the first time?
  6. Describe your approach to building a product for markets where many users rely on low-bandwidth connections or basic devices.
  7. Exotel is exploring expansion into a new vertical, such as healthcare. How would you evaluate the opportunity and structure a go-to-market hypothesis?
  8. How do you balance shipping fast for competitive reasons against maintaining high reliability for enterprise SLAs?
  9. Tell me about a time you worked with a sales or customer success team and that collaboration changed a product decision.
  10. How would you design a self-serve pricing and billing experience for Exotel's API products?
  11. A competitor launches a feature that several of your top customers are requesting. What is your process for deciding whether to build, buy, or partner?
  12. How would you use product data to identify customers at risk of churning, and what interventions would you propose?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A major banking client reports a spike in call drop rates after a recent release. How do you investigate and respond?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we shipped a routing-layer update and within hours our largest fintech client flagged that outbound call completion rates had dropped noticeably.

*Task:* I had to quickly determine whether this was a product bug, an infrastructure issue, or a client-side configuration problem, and communicate transparently with the client throughout.

*Action:* I pulled our real-time dashboards and compared drop-rate metrics before and after the release window. I looped in the engineering lead and we identified that the update had changed a timeout parameter affecting calls routed through a specific carrier. I set up a war room, kept the client's account manager updated every hour, and we rolled back the specific change while a proper fix was being built. I then wrote a clear post-mortem and shared a root-cause summary with the client.

*Result:* The issue was resolved within a few hours. The client appreciated the transparency and the structured communication, and we used the incident to build an automated regression test for carrier-specific call quality metrics.

---

Q: How would you prioritize the roadmap when enterprise clients and SMBs have conflicting needs?

*Situation:* At a previous B2B SaaS role, our enterprise segment wanted deep customisation and dedicated support workflows, while our SMB segment needed a simpler, faster self-serve experience.

*Task:* I had to build a roadmap that served both segments without fragmenting the engineering team or diluting the core product.

*Action:* I ran a structured prioritisation exercise using a RICE-style framework, scoring each request by reach, impact, confidence, and effort. I also segmented revenue contribution and churn risk by customer tier. This revealed that a small number of enterprise features were driving the majority of contract renewals, while SMB activation was being blocked by a handful of onboarding friction points. I proposed a split-track quarterly plan: one stream focused on enterprise configurability, one on SMB self-serve improvements, with a shared infrastructure investment that benefited both.

*Result:* The approach got alignment from the CTO and sales leadership. Enterprise renewal rate improved over the following quarters and SMB activation, measured by first successful API call within the first week, also improved based on internally reported metrics.

---

Q: Exotel is exploring expansion into healthcare. How would you evaluate the opportunity?

*Situation:* In a previous role, I was asked to assess whether our communication platform should build a vertical product for the healthcare space.

*Task:* I needed to structure the evaluation from first principles rather than simply reacting to inbound interest from a few hospital clients.

*Action:* I started by mapping the specific jobs-to-be-done in healthcare communication: patient appointment reminders, teleconsultation, discharge follow-ups, and emergency escalations. I then assessed regulatory requirements (data residency, consent frameworks), existing competition, and the size of the addressable customer base. I interviewed several potential buyers to validate pain points and willingness to pay. I also estimated the engineering investment required for compliance features. The analysis showed strong demand but a meaningful compliance investment that would take several months to complete properly.

*Result:* I recommended a phased approach: start with a lighter-weight integration that existing healthcare clients could use immediately, while building toward full compliance certification. Leadership approved the phased plan and we signed pilot customers within the first quarter of launch.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and improvement questions, use a structured sequence: start with the user and their job-to-be-done, then identify pain points, then propose solutions with trade-offs, then define how you would measure success. Exotel interviewers are particularly attentive to whether you account for Indian infrastructure realities such as variable network quality and regional language needs.

For prioritisation questions, bring a clear scoring framework. RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) works well and is easy to explain out loud. Always segment your analysis by customer type, since Exotel serves both enterprise and SMB customers and the right answer often differs by segment.

For metrics and analytical questions, structure your answer in three layers: input metrics (what drives the outcome), output metrics (the outcome itself), and guardrail metrics (what you do not want to break). For a call quality feature, for example, you might track API call success rate as the output, developer integration time as an input, and enterprise SLA breach rate as the guardrail.

For root cause and incident questions, use a systematic drill-down: confirm the scope and severity, isolate whether the issue is in code, infrastructure, or a third-party dependency, and communicate with stakeholders in parallel rather than after the investigation is complete.

For 'tell me about a time' questions, use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and keep your Result concrete. Vague results like 'the team was happy' are weak. Prefer outcomes you can describe specifically, even if the exact figure was internal.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

B2B product intuition. Exotel's products are used by businesses, not end consumers. Interviewers want to see that you understand procurement cycles, SLA expectations, and the weight that enterprise relationships carry. Treating an Exotel product question the same as a consumer app question is a common error.

Technical fluency without engineering depth. You do not need to write code, but you should be comfortable discussing APIs, webhooks, latency, and uptime in a way that shows you can work credibly with an engineering team. Candidates who cannot engage with these concepts at a conversational level typically do not progress.

Data-driven reasoning. Every claim you make in the interview should be tied to a metric or a way of measuring success. Interviewers at Exotel pay close attention to how quickly you reach for data and whether you can define the right metric for a given problem.

Customer empathy with enterprise context. Exotel's customers include large banks, logistics companies, and healthcare providers. Demonstrating empathy for the pressures those buyers face (compliance requirements, uptime needs, procurement complexity) earns strong marks.

Communication clarity. PM roles at Exotel require alignment across sales, engineering, and customer success. Interviewers observe whether you structure your answers clearly, handle pushback without becoming defensive, and can simplify complex trade-offs for a mixed audience.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the space

Create a free Exotel account or explore their developer documentation to understand how their voice and messaging APIs actually work. Read their product blog and any publicly available case studies. Map their product surface: what does each feature do, who uses it, and what problem does it solve. Also read up on how competitors in the cloud communications space position themselves.

Week 2: Build your case library

Prepare multiple strong examples for each of these themes: prioritisation under competing pressures, handling a product failure or incident, influencing without authority, and using data to make a product decision. Structure each example in STAR format and practice saying it out loud so it flows naturally. Record yourself and listen back to catch filler words or unclear transitions.

Week 3: Practice live

Do mock interviews with a peer or a senior PM in your network. Focus on B2B and API product cases specifically. Candidates report that Exotel interviewers ask follow-up questions actively, so practice defending your recommendations rather than just presenting them. Aim to handle 'why not X instead?' without losing composure.

In the final few days, review your salary expectations against the bands in the overview section. Know your target level and be ready to discuss compensation directly if asked.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Exotel like a consumer product company. The most common mistake is answering questions with consumer-app instincts: obsessing over UI polish, ignoring SLAs, or defining success purely through engagement metrics. For a B2B communications platform, reliability, developer experience, and enterprise trust matter more.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would track user satisfaction' without defining what that means in context, how you would measure it, and what a good result looks like signals shallow product thinking. Always get specific about how you would actually measure the outcome.

Skipping the Indian market context. Exotel's primary market is India and Southeast Asia. Not acknowledging the constraints and opportunities of these markets (network variability, regional language support, price sensitivity, compliance requirements) is a missed opportunity to show genuine market fit.

Overcomplicating the framework. Using a framework is good; reading out a framework without adapting it to the specific question is not. Interviewers want to see judgment, not recitation. Apply frameworks lightly and show your reasoning.

Not asking clarifying questions. Starting to answer immediately without scoping the problem suggests you are pattern-matching rather than thinking. Take a moment to clarify the user segment, the metric being optimised, and any constraints before diving in.

Weak results in STAR answers. Ending a story with 'the project was a success' or 'stakeholders were aligned' leaves the interviewer with nothing concrete to evaluate. Describe the specific outcome, even if it is directional rather than a precise number.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Exotel PM interview process typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screening call, followed by one or two product case rounds, an analytical or metrics discussion, and a final conversation focused on leadership, culture, or cross-functional experience. Exotel does not publish a fixed round structure, so the exact sequence can vary by team and level. Ask your recruiter upfront what to expect for your specific role.

Does Exotel ask a take-home assignment as part of the PM interview?

Some candidates report receiving a short take-home case study, particularly for senior roles, while others go through only live rounds. The format is not consistent across all PM openings. If you are given a take-home, treat it as an opportunity to show structured thinking, clear trade-offs, and an understanding of Exotel's B2B context rather than simply a polished slide deck.

What salary can a PM expect at Exotel?

Knok jobradar data shows general India-wide PM salary bands of 12-20 LPA for Associate PM roles, 24-40 LPA for PMs with three to six years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PM roles. These are not Exotel-specific figures. Use them as a starting reference and cross-check against publicly reported data on Glassdoor for more recent signals before your compensation discussion.

Do I need a technical background to become a PM at Exotel?

Exotel builds API-first products for enterprise clients, so comfort with technical concepts is genuinely important. You are not expected to write code, but you should be able to discuss APIs, webhooks, latency, and uptime in a way that lets you collaborate credibly with engineers. Candidates from engineering, developer relations, or solutions consulting backgrounds tend to find the technical bar approachable, while those with purely business backgrounds benefit from self-study on API fundamentals before interviewing.

How should I talk about competitor products like Twilio during the interview?

It is fine and often impressive to reference competitors, as it shows market awareness. Frame your comparison in terms of customer outcomes: what does each product do well for specific segments, where does Exotel have a structural advantage (regional support, pricing, enterprise integrations), and how would you use those advantages in a product or go-to-market strategy. Avoid generic statements like 'Exotel is better' without specific reasoning behind them.
