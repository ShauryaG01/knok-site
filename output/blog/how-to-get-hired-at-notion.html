01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Notion builds the all-in-one workspace that millions of teams use to write, plan, and organise work. The company runs intentionally lean, which means every hire is expected to own outcomes from day one rather than wait for direction. As of mid-2026, Notion has 155 open roles spanning engineering, product, design, go-to-market, and operations.

Roles are split between their San Francisco headquarters and a growing set of remote-friendly positions. If you are based in India, filter for fully remote listings and read location requirements carefully before applying. Competition is real because Notion has a strong employer brand, but 155 openings means serious candidates have genuine pathways in across multiple functions. The company moves at a steady pace through hiring, so expect the full process to span a few weeks from first contact to offer.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Notion's process is structured but not rigid. Stages vary by team, and timelines differ, but the general flow for most roles looks like this.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. Recruiters look for clear, concise writing on your resume. Because Notion is a writing-first company, a cluttered or vague resume sends the wrong signal. A focused cover letter that shows you understand what Notion builds can help you stand out from the pile.

Stage 2: Recruiter call. If your profile passes the screen, a recruiter reaches out for a short introductory call. This covers your background, what draws you to Notion, and basic logistics like location and availability. Be ready to explain why you want to work at a product-led company specifically.

Stage 3: Hiring manager conversation. The next step is a deeper discussion with the person who would be your direct manager. Expect questions about how you have handled past challenges, what you built or shipped, and how you think about your craft. This is also your best chance to ask sharp questions about team direction and priorities.

Stage 4: Skills assessment. Most roles include a practical component. For engineers, this is typically a live coding session or a take-home project. For product, design, or marketing roles, expect a case study or a written exercise. Notion cares about the quality of thinking behind your work, not just whether you completed it quickly. Treat any take-home as a serious, polished submission.

Stage 5: Final loop. The last stage brings in cross-functional partners and sometimes includes a dedicated values conversation. If your assessment involved a project, you may present it to the panel. Interviewers at this stage are watching how you communicate, how you handle pushback, and whether you collaborate well with people outside your direct function.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Notion hires for a specific profile. If you match it clearly, your chances go up considerably.

Writing clarity. Notion runs on written communication internally. Candidates who can express complex ideas in plain, well-structured prose get noticed quickly. Before you apply, practice writing crisp summaries of your past projects.

Ownership and initiative. The team is small relative to the product's scale. They want people who spot a problem and fix it without waiting to be asked. In interviews, share examples where you drove something end-to-end, not just contributed to a larger effort led by someone else.

Product intuition. Even for non-product roles, Notion values people who think about the end user. If you have used Notion deeply, built templates for your team, or written thoughtfully about productivity tools, bring that up.

Systems thinking. Whether you are in engineering, ops, or marketing, Notion values people who think in frameworks and can explain the 'why' behind their decisions, not just the 'what'. Interviewers will push on your reasoning.

Collaborative confidence. Notion's culture involves a lot of async debate over written documents. They want people who can hold a reasoned position under pushback while staying genuinely open to changing their mind when the argument warrants it.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product seriously. If you do not use Notion day-to-day, start now. Build a real workspace, explore templates, and form opinions about what works well and what could be better. Interviewers will notice if your product knowledge is surface-level or borrowed from a quick YouTube video.

Prepare written project summaries. Before each interview stage, write out two or three past projects in crisp paragraphs: the problem, your approach, the result, and what you learned. Notion interviewers frequently ask you to walk through past work in detail, and having these written out beforehand sharpens your thinking.

Research the company's product direction. Read Notion's official blog and changelog to understand where the product is heading. Referencing a specific recent feature or a product decision you found interesting shows genuine interest, not just rehearsed answers.

Practice structured answers. For behavioural questions, build answers that have a clear beginning (the situation and your role), a middle (what you did and why), and an end (what happened and what you would do differently). Avoid rambling stories without a point.

Prepare sharp questions. Notion values intellectual curiosity. At each stage, have at least two specific questions ready, ones that show you have thought about the team's actual challenges rather than generic questions about 'the culture.'

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Notion has a distinct culture worth understanding before you send in your application.

Writing is the default. Most decisions at Notion get worked out in written documents before any meeting happens. If you prefer to think out loud verbally and have never written a structured one-pager to align a team, you may find the culture uncomfortable at first. Practising written communication before you join will help you ramp faster.

Small team, big ownership. Notion is intentionally lean relative to its product scale. That means less bureaucracy, but also more pressure to deliver without hand-holding. People who thrive here tend to be self-directed and comfortable making calls with incomplete information.

Product obsession. The team genuinely uses what they build, and there is a high bar for taste in both design and writing. Sloppy internal work stands out in a bad way. Bringing the same care to your interview materials that you would bring to your actual work signals cultural fit early.

Candid feedback culture. Notion encourages direct feedback, which means you will receive honest assessments of your work and are expected to give the same to colleagues. People who take feedback personally or who soften feedback until it loses meaning tend to struggle here.

Remote-friendly but async-heavy. Notion supports distributed work, but relies on written communication to keep teams aligned. If you are applying for a remote role from India, strong async communication skills matter as much as your technical or domain skills.

Frequently asked

How many open roles does Notion have right now?

As of mid-2026, Notion has 155 open roles across functions including engineering, product, design, go-to-market, and operations. The mix changes as teams grow and new priorities emerge. Check their careers page regularly because openings appear and close quickly at a company this size.

Does Notion hire remotely from India?

Notion has hired remotely from various countries, and some listings are fully remote without a strict geographic restriction. Your best approach is to filter for remote roles and read location requirements carefully for each one. Roles tied to their San Francisco office typically require US residency or relocation, so focus your energy on the listings that explicitly allow international remote candidates.

What is the pay like at Notion?

Notion does not publish salary bands publicly for most roles. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest total compensation is competitive with other well-funded software companies at a similar stage. For India-based remote roles, the compensation structure may differ from US-based packages, so ask the recruiter for the band early in the process rather than waiting until the offer stage.

Is there a coding test in the Notion interview process?

For engineering roles, yes. The format varies by team but commonly includes a live coding session or a take-home project. Notion cares about the clarity of your thinking and the quality of your code, not just whether the solution runs. For non-engineering roles, expect a written exercise or a case study tailored to your function rather than a coding component.

How important is it to have used Notion before applying?

Very important. Interviewers frequently ask about your experience with the product, and answers that show real, hands-on use stand out immediately. Before your recruiter call, spend time building an actual workspace in Notion so you can speak specifically about features you rely on and form genuine opinions about what could be improved.

What is the biggest reason candidates get rejected at Notion?

Based on what is publicly reported by former interviewers and candidates, the most common reasons are weak writing skills, surface-level product knowledge, and answers that sound rehearsed rather than reflective. Notion wants to see how you actually think, not a polished script. Candidates who treat the process as a conversation rather than a performance tend to do better.
