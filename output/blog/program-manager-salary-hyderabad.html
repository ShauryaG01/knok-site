01 Salary Overview

Salary Overview

Finding a straight answer on what a Technical Program Manager earns in Hyderabad is genuinely difficult. Most Indian job postings do not display a salary range. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 12 active TPM openings in Hyderabad, and like the vast majority of Indian tech listings, they do not mention pay upfront. Employers prefer to calibrate offers individually based on your current CTC and competing pressure, which puts job seekers at an information disadvantage unless they research the market beforehand.

Because the postings themselves reveal nothing, this guide draws on publicly reported data from platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi, plus patterns from industry surveys. Treat all figures here as directional benchmarks, not guarantees. Sample sizes for TPM roles specifically in Hyderabad are thin on Indian platforms, so there is real uncertainty in the ranges. That said, you need a starting number before you can negotiate, and these are the most honest estimates available from public sources.

02 By Experience Level

By Experience Level

TPM compensation scales sharply with seniority because the scope of the role changes fundamentally at each level. A junior TPM is largely a coordinator across one or two teams. A principal TPM is setting multi-year engineering roadmaps and managing dependencies across entire organisations. The ranges below are drawn from publicly reported data on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, and from industry surveys covering Indian tech hubs. Hyderabad generally tracks close to Pune and a step below Bangalore for comparable levels.

04 Negotiation Tips

Negotiation Tips

Walking into a TPM salary negotiation without preparation is how you leave money on the table. A few approaches that consistently make a difference.

Know the market before the first call. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for TPM roles at the specific company you are targeting. Company-level data is more useful than city averages because product companies and GCCs pay on entirely different scales from IT services firms.

Anchor on total compensation, not just base. TPM roles at product companies often come with significant RSU grants. Ask for the full package breakdown early in the process so you are comparing apples to apples.

Do not reveal your current CTC first. You are not legally required to disclose your current salary in most states. Deflect by saying you are targeting a specific range rather than sharing a number, and let the employer make the first offer.

Competing offers are your strongest lever. If you are seriously job hunting, run parallel processes so you have real numbers to reference. A competing offer, even from a company you are less excited about, gives you credible negotiating ground.

Negotiate joining bonus separately. If the company cannot move on base or RSUs, a joining bonus to cover notice-period losses is often easier for them to approve and does not set a base-salary precedent.

05 Total Comp Breakdown

Total Comp Breakdown

Base salary is what most people focus on, but for TPM roles at product companies and GCCs, the full package is what actually matters.

Base salary is the fixed monthly component. At product companies this often represents a smaller share of total comp than it appears, because of the weight of stock.

Variable pay or performance bonus is tied to individual and company performance. At IT services firms this is typically a fixed percentage of base. At product companies it can be discretionary and substantially higher.

RSUs (Restricted Stock Units) are standard at listed product companies and their Indian subsidiaries. A four-year vest with a one-year cliff is the common structure. The value changes with the stock price, so an offer that looks strong today may shift over the vesting period.

Joining bonus is often offered to cover notice-period salary loss or unvested equity you are leaving behind. It is usually a one-time payment and may carry a clawback clause if you leave within a year.

Retirals include Provident Fund contributions and gratuity. These are often included in the CTC figure but are not immediately liquid cash.

When comparing two offers, always convert both to net take-home or total cash equivalent before deciding. A higher CTC driven by large PF components or a long RSU cliff may deliver less actual cash in the near term than a slightly lower but more cash-heavy offer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Why do so few TPM job postings in Hyderabad mention salary?

Indian employers across sectors routinely omit salary from job postings. The practice gives companies flexibility to calibrate offers individually based on a candidate's current CTC and the pressure of competing bids, rather than committing to a public range. This puts job seekers at an information disadvantage. Checking Glassdoor and levels.fyi for the specific company before you apply is the simplest way to close that gap.

Is Hyderabad a good city for TPM roles compared to Bangalore?

Bangalore has significantly more active TPM volume, with 41 openings tracked in July 2026 versus 12 in Hyderabad. That said, Hyderabad has consistent demand from established product companies and large GCC offices, making it a viable market if you are already based there. If you are open to relocation, Bangalore offers more openings and industry surveys suggest it pays at the top of Indian TPM ranges. The right city depends on your priorities and willingness to move.

What qualifications do companies typically look for in a TPM in Hyderabad?

Most TPM roles require a B.Tech or equivalent engineering background plus several years of hands-on technical experience. Mid-to-senior roles commonly ask for a track record of owning cross-functional programs, familiarity with agile at scale, and the ability to communicate technical trade-offs to senior leadership. Prior software engineering experience is a strong differentiator, especially for roles at product companies. A PMP or similar certification is sometimes listed but is rarely a hard requirement.

How is a TPM different from a Project Manager or Delivery Manager?

A TPM (Technical Program Manager) is expected to have enough technical depth to evaluate engineering trade-offs, challenge timelines based on complexity, and drive decisions across multiple teams. A Project Manager typically manages timelines and resources without deep technical input. A Delivery Manager in an IT services context is often closer to client relationship management and account delivery. In Indian product companies and GCCs, the TPM title signals technical credibility and is compensated accordingly.

Can I negotiate a TPM offer if I am switching from a non-TPM background?

Yes, but expect your first TPM offer to reflect the employer's risk in hiring someone without direct TPM experience, even if your overall years in tech are high. The way to negotiate effectively from a non-TPM background is to quantify the impact of programs you have already run informally and demonstrate you understand the full scope of the role. A referral from inside the company or a competing offer from another employer carries more weight than certifications in this situation.
