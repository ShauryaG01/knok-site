03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Lateral - APAC typically runs across three to four rounds, though the exact structure can vary by role and seniority. Here is what candidates commonly report:

Round 1: Application review and initial screen
After you apply, the team reviews your resume and cover note. If shortlisted, you can expect a short call from a recruiter or HR contact to confirm your background, notice period, and basic role expectations. This call is conversational, not a grilling.

Round 2: Competency or structured interview
This is usually a video or in-person interview with a hiring manager. Expect questions about your past experience, how you have handled tricky situations with candidates or clients, and your understanding of APAC markets. Behavioural questions using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) are common here.

Round 3: Case study or practical task
For consulting or business development-facing roles, candidates are often asked to work through a short case. This might involve mapping a talent market, drafting a candidate pitch, or analysing a hiring brief. The goal is to see how you think, not just what you know.

Round 4: Senior leadership conversation
Final round candidates typically meet a director or senior partner. This is part culture-fit check and part two-way conversation: they want to know why Lateral - APAC specifically, and you should have sharp questions ready about the team, clients, and growth path.

End-to-end, the process commonly takes two to four weeks, though timelines can stretch for senior roles.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Lateral - APAC hires people who thrive in relationship-driven, fast-moving environments. A few traits stand out consistently:

Commercial mindset. The business lives on placing the right people with the right clients. If you can demonstrate that you understand revenue drivers, client expectations, and candidate motivations, you will stand out.

APAC awareness. Roles here touch markets across Southeast Asia, India, ANZ, and beyond. Familiarity with how hiring norms, salary expectations, and candidate behaviour differ across these markets is a genuine differentiator.

Communication and persuasion. Whether you are writing a candidate brief or convincing a passive candidate to consider a move, clear and compelling communication is core to the job.

Resilience and ownership. Recruitment is a high-volume, sometimes unpredictable field. The company looks for people who take ownership of outcomes rather than shifting blame when a search takes longer than expected.

Curiosity about people and industries. The best consultants in this space are genuinely interested in what makes businesses tick and what motivates professionals. This curiosity shows up in how you ask questions during interviews.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the company and its markets before round one. Lateral - APAC operates across multiple APAC verticals. Know the broad industries they serve, the kind of roles they fill, and at least two or three trends shaping talent in those spaces right now.

Build a story around your relationship-building track record. If you have ever convinced someone to change their mind, retained a difficult client, or navigated a complex negotiation, prepare a crisp STAR story around it. These examples land well here.

Practice your 'why recruitment' answer. If you are not already in the industry, interviewers will probe your motivation. Connect it to something specific: a time you matched two people or opportunities and saw the impact, your interest in markets and careers, or a mentor who worked in search.

Prepare sharp questions for each round. Questions about the team's current mandates, how success is measured in the first few months, and what the top performers have in common show genuine intent. Avoid generic questions that a quick search could answer.

Polish your written communication. Roles at talent firms often involve drafting candidate profiles, job briefs, and client notes. If asked to submit a written task, treat it as a live sample of your professional writing: clear, structured, and free of jargon.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Lateral - APAC operates in a high-accountability, client-first environment. The culture is typically described by people who have worked in similar APAC-focused search firms as energetic and target-oriented, with a strong emphasis on collaboration within teams even as individual performance is tracked closely.

Meritocracy with mentorship. Promotion in firms like this tends to follow results: if you are bringing in clients, closing searches, or building strong candidate pipelines, the path upward is clear. At the same time, the best firms in this space pair junior consultants with experienced mentors who share market knowledge and client context.

Regional diversity. Working across APAC means your colleagues and clients span multiple cultures, time zones, and communication styles. People who adapt quickly and listen carefully tend to thrive here.

Pace and ambiguity. Mandates change, timelines shift, and candidate availability is unpredictable. The culture rewards people who stay calm under pressure and find creative solutions rather than simply giving up.
