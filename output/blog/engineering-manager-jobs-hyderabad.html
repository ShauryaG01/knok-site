05 How To Stand Out

How To Stand Out

Engineering Manager is a leadership role, and hiring managers look for evidence that you can handle both people and product delivery. A resume that lists only technical skills will be filtered out early.

Lead with impact, not responsibilities. Quantify team size, delivery improvements, and business outcomes you drove. 'Led a team of engineers' is weak; 'Grew a team, improved release frequency, and delivered three major product milestones on schedule' tells a real story and gives the interviewer something concrete to ask about.

Show you can handle ambiguity. Hyderabad's active sectors (IT services, SaaS, pharma tech) want managers who can define roadmaps when requirements are still incomplete. Prepare specific examples of how you set direction under uncertainty, not just how you executed once requirements were clear.

Highlight cross-functional work. Engineering Managers here are often expected to partner with product, business, and sometimes directly with clients. Demonstrating that you can work across functions is a genuine differentiator at this level.

Tailor your resume for each company type. A Wipro (services) role will value client delivery and large-team management. A Veeva or HighRadius (product) role will want product thinking and agile execution. The same resume will not work for both without meaningful changes.

Secure an internal referral where possible. With only a handful of openings per company, your application gets far more serious attention if someone inside vouches for you before your resume lands.

06 Application Strategy

Application Strategy

With 16 openings in Hyderabad and a small set of companies hiring, a focused approach beats a scattershot one.

Start by shortlisting companies that match your domain experience. If your background is in SaaS or product companies, Veeva, HighRadius, Progress Software, and Turing are natural fits. If your strength is large-scale delivery or IT services, Wipro is worth prioritising first.

For each company, find the hiring manager or engineering director on LinkedIn before you apply. A short, specific message explaining why you are interested in that particular company (not a generic 'I am exploring new opportunities' note) puts you on their radar before your resume even lands in the system.

Apply through the company's official careers page, not only through job boards. Engineering Manager roles receive far fewer applications than individual contributor roles, so a direct application paired with a personal outreach message has a genuine chance of being read carefully.

Track your applications in a simple spreadsheet. Note when you applied, who you contacted, and when to follow up. A polite follow-up message after 10-12 days is appropriate if you have not heard back.
