03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Stage 1: Application and resume screen
Most candidates first submit a resume through the company careers page or a job aggregator. A recruiter or HR coordinator typically reviews applications within one to two weeks. A strong, role-tailored resume moves you faster through this filter.

Stage 2: Recruiter call
If your profile matches, expect a short screening call from HR, usually around half an hour. This conversation covers your current role, notice period, salary expectations, and basic fit. Be clear and honest here. Trying to game compensation discussions at this stage usually backfires.

Stage 3: Technical or functional assessment
Depending on the role, this is either a take-home assignment, a timed online test, or a short case exercise. Engineering roles typically involve coding problems; product or business roles may involve a case study or a written brief. Completing this within the window given shows you respect their process.

Stage 4: Technical interview rounds
This is usually one to three video calls with the hiring team. For engineering roles, expect data structures, system design, or domain-specific questions. For non-engineering roles, expect scenario-based questions and past-work walkthroughs. Each round may involve a different interviewer, so repeat your context clearly each time.

Stage 5: Hiring manager or leadership round
Many tech companies include a final conversation with a senior manager or department head. This round is less about hard skills and more about how you think, communicate, and whether you would fit the team. Prepare two or three strong examples from your past work.

Stage 6: Offer and background check
If rounds go well, HR sends a verbal or written offer. Salary negotiation typically happens here. Background verification of past employment and education follows once you accept. Total time from application to offer varies by team and role, so ask your recruiter upfront about their expected schedule.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Technical competence in your domain
Invisibletech, like most product-focused tech companies, looks for candidates who can demonstrate practical skill rather than just theoretical knowledge. For engineers, this means writing clean, working code under interview conditions. For business roles, it means structured thinking and clear communication.

Ownership mindset
Smaller and mid-size tech companies consistently value candidates who take initiative without waiting for instructions. In interviews, stories where you identified a problem yourself and drove it to resolution carry more weight than stories where you executed someone else's plan.

Communication clarity
Being able to explain your work simply is taken seriously. If you are in a technical role, being able to translate complex ideas for non-technical colleagues is a plus. Avoid jargon-heavy answers in interviews. Clear thinking expressed clearly is what stands out.

Learning agility
Tech roles evolve fast. Companies hiring in 2026 often ask how you stay current, how you handled a situation where you had to learn something new quickly, and how you respond to feedback. Have real answers ready for each of these.

Cultural alignment
Public information on Invisibletech's stated values is limited, so research the company's LinkedIn page, recent news, and any employee reviews before your interview. Showing you did your homework on the company specifically, and not just the role, makes a visible difference.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Tailor your resume before applying
With 11 open roles currently listed, there may be more than one position that loosely fits your background. Read each job description carefully and adjust your resume to reflect the language and priorities in that specific listing. Generic resumes get filtered out first.

Research the company thoroughly
Look up Invisibletech on LinkedIn, check recent posts or announcements, and read any employee reviews on platforms like Glassdoor. Understand what the company builds, who their customers are, and what problems they are solving. You will almost certainly be asked 'Why Invisibletech?' and a vague answer loses points fast.

Prepare your stories in STAR format
Situation, Task, Action, Result. Pick six to eight strong examples from your past work that cover different scenarios: a project you led, a conflict you resolved, a technical challenge you overcame, a time you failed and what you learned. These examples become the raw material for most interview questions.

Prepare questions to ask
Interviewers notice when a candidate has no questions. Prepare three to five genuine questions about the team, the product direction, what success looks like in the first few months, or how the team collaborates. Avoid asking about leave policies or perks in early rounds.

Do a mock interview
One real mock interview with a friend or peer in the same domain is worth more than hours of passive preparation. Record yourself if you can, and watch it back. Most people are surprised by their own habits: filler words, rushing answers, or skipping context.

Sort out logistics early
For video interviews, test your internet, camera, and audio the day before. For in-person rounds, confirm the address and plan to arrive a little early. These basics eliminate unnecessary stress on the day.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Public information on Invisibletech's internal culture is limited as of mid-2026, so this section draws on what is observable externally and what is typical for tech companies in a similar growth phase.

Fast-moving environment
Companies with active hiring pipelines (Invisibletech currently has 11 open roles) are typically in a phase of growth. That usually means roles are broad, priorities shift, and individuals are expected to operate with some ambiguity. If you prefer very structured environments with fixed job descriptions, clarify the team's working style directly in your interviews.

Collaboration across functions
Most modern tech companies have moved away from hard silos between engineering, product, and business teams. Expect to work across functions even if your role sits in one domain. Ask in your interview how cross-team projects are typically handled.

Performance and accountability
Growth-phase tech companies tend to move fast and hold people accountable for results. This is a positive environment for people who want to see the impact of their work quickly, but it can be demanding. Ask about how goals are set and reviewed.

Remote and hybrid norms
Many Indian tech companies have settled into a hybrid working model post-2024. Ask directly about the expected office days and whether that is flexible. Do not assume remote-first without confirming.

For a more grounded picture, read recent employee reviews and, if possible, speak to someone who currently works or recently worked at Invisibletech before your final round.
