01 Overview

Overview

Cendyn is a global hospitality technology company best known for its CRM, revenue, and sales solutions built for hotels and resort groups. A Platform Engineer at Cendyn typically sits at the intersection of DevOps, cloud infrastructure, and developer experience, keeping the SaaS platform reliable, scalable, and secure for hotel clients worldwide.

As of July 2026, Cendyn had 24 open roles listed across job boards. The Platform Engineer opening is one of them. Candidates report the interview process typically runs three to four rounds covering system design, hands-on technical questions, and a behavioural conversation with engineering leadership.

Cendyn's platform touches real-time hotel operations, so interviewers place strong weight on reliability thinking, incident ownership, and cloud-native tooling depth. Coming prepared on Kubernetes, CI/CD pipelines, infrastructure-as-code, and observability will serve you well.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you designed or maintained a Kubernetes-based platform.

*Situation:* My team was running microservices across multiple cloud VMs with inconsistent deployment scripts, which caused environment drift between staging and production.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the migration to a managed Kubernetes cluster and standardise the deployment process across all teams.

*Action:* I designed a Kubernetes architecture with separate namespaces per environment, created Helm charts for every service, and introduced a GitOps workflow using ArgoCD so that all deployments were version-controlled and auditable. I also configured horizontal pod autoscaling and defined resource requests and limits for each workload.

*Result:* Environment drift issues dropped sharply, and product teams could onboard a new service independently without needing platform team involvement each time.

---

Q: Describe a production incident you owned end to end.

*Situation:* A third-party API our platform depended on started timing out, which cascaded into slow responses across the product during peak hotel check-in hours.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer responsible for isolating the fault, restoring service, and keeping stakeholders informed throughout.

*Action:* I used distributed traces to pinpoint the failing external dependency quickly. I added a circuit breaker in the API gateway, rerouted traffic to a cached fallback, and sent regular status updates to stakeholders at consistent intervals until service was fully restored. I then drafted a post-mortem with a permanent fix proposal.

*Result:* Service was restored within the same incident window. The post-mortem was accepted and circuit breakers were added to all external dependency calls, preventing a repeat.

---

Q: How have you handled cloud cost optimisation?

*Situation:* Our monthly cloud bill had grown faster than leadership expected, driven by over-provisioned resources added during a rapid feature push.

*Task:* I was asked to audit the infrastructure and identify savings without affecting platform reliability or SLAs.

*Action:* I used cloud-native cost tools to find idle and oversized resources, right-sized compute for non-critical workloads, and moved predictable batch jobs to reserved or spot instances. I also set up automated shutdowns for dev and staging environments outside working hours.

*Result:* Monthly cloud spend dropped noticeably. The savings were noted in our quarterly infrastructure review and part of the budget was reallocated to additional monitoring tooling.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioural question. Situation and Task together should take no more than a third of your answer. Spend the most time on Action, since that is where interviewers learn how you think. End with a concrete Result, even if it is qualitative.

For system design questions, use a four-step structure. First, clarify requirements and constraints (multi-region? multi-tenant? what SLA?). Second, sketch the high-level components. Third, go deep on one or two critical parts such as autoscaling or secret management. Fourth, discuss trade-offs you consciously made.

For incident questions, follow the timeline format. Detection, triage, mitigation, resolution, post-mortem. Cendyn runs always-on hotel systems, so showing structured incident thinking scores well.

For 'how do you balance X and Y' questions, avoid false choices. Show you have frameworks: feature flags for gradual rollouts, separate change windows for risky infrastructure changes, service-level objectives to make reliability trade-offs explicit.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Reliability ownership. Hotel operations run around the clock. Interviewers want to see that you treat uptime as your personal responsibility, not just a ticket in a backlog.

Cloud-native depth. Candidates report that Kubernetes, Terraform, and at least one major cloud provider come up in almost every technical round. Surface-level familiarity is not enough. Be ready to go several levels deep on a topic you claim to know well.

Developer empathy. Platform Engineers at Cendyn support product engineering teams. Interviewers look for people who understand the developer experience and can build internal tooling that product teams actually want to use.

Cross-functional communication. Cendyn integrates with many hotel systems, which means incidents and changes affect multiple stakeholders. Showing that you communicate clearly during incidents and across teams matters as much as technical skill.

Security and compliance awareness. Hospitality data includes payment and guest information. Interviewers typically probe on secret management, least-privilege access, and audit logging.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical review.
Revise Kubernetes architecture (pods, namespaces, autoscaling, resource management), Helm chart design, and GitOps workflows. Practise drawing a multi-tenant SaaS platform on paper and explaining every component out loud.

Week 2: Infrastructure-as-code and CI/CD.
Refresh your Terraform or Pulumi skills. Walk through a CI/CD pipeline you have built and be ready to explain every stage, including rollback strategy and secret injection.

Week 3: Observability and incidents.
Revise how logs, metrics, and traces work together. Draft three incident stories from your past using the detection-triage-mitigation-resolution-post-mortem format.

Week 4: Cendyn context and behavioural prep.
Read Cendyn's public blog and product pages to understand their hospitality focus. Prepare answers for the questions listed above using STAR. Do at least two mock interviews out loud, not just in your head.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating platform work as pure ops. Cendyn's Platform Engineering is a software role. Candidates who cannot write code or design APIs for internal developer tooling are typically screened out early.

Vague incident stories. Saying 'I helped resolve an outage' without owning a specific action is a red flag. Interviewers want to hear what you personally did, not what the team did collectively.

Skipping trade-offs in system design. A design with no trade-offs signals that you have not thought it through. Every architecture choice has a cost: name it.

Over-indexing on a single cloud provider. If your experience is entirely on one platform, acknowledge it and show awareness of how the concepts translate to others Cendyn might use.

Not asking about the platform's current state. Candidates who ask no questions about existing architecture, pain points, or roadmap come across as disinterested. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the panel.
