01 Overview

Overview

Cendyn builds CRM, sales, and revenue management software for the global hospitality industry. Hotels, resorts, and hotel chains use Cendyn products to manage guest relationships, coordinate sales teams, and optimise pricing decisions. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 24 open roles at Cendyn, signalling active hiring across product and tech teams.

Candidates report a process that typically covers a recruiter or HR screen, a hiring manager conversation focused on background and motivation, one or two product and case rounds, and sometimes a final discussion with senior leadership. Because Cendyn is a B2B SaaS company built around the hospitality vertical, interviewers pay close attention to how well you understand hotel operations and enterprise buyer needs, not just PM theory in the abstract.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Cendyn PM interviews, based on what candidates report and what the role demands.

  1. Walk me through a product you owned end-to-end. How did you decide what to build first?
  2. Cendyn serves hotel revenue managers and sales teams every day. How would you improve a feature that helps a revenue manager make faster pricing decisions?
  3. Tell me about a time you had to choose between a large enterprise client's urgent request and a capability that would benefit your broader customer base.
  4. How do you define and measure success for a B2B SaaS product? Which metrics would you track for a hotel CRM module?
  5. Describe a situation where you pushed back on a stakeholder (sales, engineering, or a client) and how you handled the conversation.
  6. How would you approach reducing churn for a hotel that is barely using a core integration in your product?
  7. Give an example of using customer research or usage data to change your team's direction on a feature.
  8. How do you work with engineering when the timeline is tight and scope needs to be cut?
  9. Cendyn has customers in multiple countries with different hotel management workflows. How would you localise a feature without bloating the product?
  10. Tell me about a product launch that did not go as planned. What went wrong and what did you take from it?
  11. How would you prioritise a roadmap for a new CRM module aimed at small independent hotels versus large hotel chains?
  12. What does 'customer success' mean in a hospitality SaaS business, and how does a PM contribute to it directly?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you had to choose between a large enterprise client's request and a broader product capability.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a large hospitality client wanted a custom report format that only their finance team would use, requiring several weeks of engineering effort.

*Task:* My job was to decide whether to build the custom report or redirect that effort toward a self-serve reporting feature our entire customer base had been requesting for months.

*Action:* I set up a discovery call with the client to understand the real need behind the request, which turned out to be 'faster audit-ready exports.' I then scoped a configurable export template that would serve their need and give all customers the same flexibility. I got the client's sign-off through their account team within a week.

*Result:* We shipped the configurable export feature, the large client was satisfied, and adoption across other accounts was strong in the first quarter after launch. It also reduced one-off custom reporting requests noticeably, according to our support team.

---

Q: Describe a time you pushed back on a stakeholder.

*Situation:* Our sales team wanted to promise a new feature to close a deal with a hotel chain. The feature was not on the roadmap and would require months of engineering work.

*Task:* I needed to protect the team's roadmap commitments without damaging the sales relationship or losing the deal.

*Action:* I joined the pre-sales meeting and asked the team to walk me through exactly what the hotel chain needed. It became clear the client wanted better visibility into group booking status, not a full new module. I proposed a lighter version using existing data that engineering could build in a few weeks, then presented this solution directly alongside the sales team.

*Result:* The client accepted the lighter solution, the deal closed, and engineering stayed on track with the core roadmap. Sales began looping me into client conversations earlier after that experience.

---

Q: Tell me about a product launch that did not go as planned.

*Situation:* We launched a new integration between our CRM and a popular hotel PMS. We had tested it in staging but not under real booking volumes.

*Task:* I was the PM responsible for the launch timeline, client communication, and post-launch monitoring.

*Action:* Within two days of go-live, several hotels reported sync delays during peak check-in hours. I pulled engineering into a war room, set a daily update cadence with affected clients through the customer success team, and later held a retrospective to understand why load testing had not been part of our launch checklist.

*Result:* The sync issue was resolved that same week. Client trust held because of the communication cadence we maintained. The retrospective produced a new launch checklist the team has used on every integration release since.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief and spend most of your answer on Action and Result. Cendyn interviewers care about your reasoning process, not just what happened.

For product design and improvement questions, try a structured approach: start with the user (who are they, what is their job to be done), then the problem (what pain are they feeling today), then prioritised solutions, then how you would measure success. For Cendyn, the user is typically a hotel revenue manager, GM, or sales director, so anchor your answer in their actual daily workflow rather than abstract personas.

For prioritisation questions, show a clear framework such as RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) or a simple value-versus-effort grid. Also flag strategic fit and what your largest accounts are asking for, since Cendyn is enterprise-focused and client input on the roadmap carries real weight.

For metrics questions, go beyond surface metrics. For a B2B SaaS product, think about activation (are new users completing setup?), engagement (are they using the feature regularly?), retention (are accounts renewing?), and expansion (are accounts buying more seats or modules?). Cendyn products are often sticky once integrated with a hotel's PMS, so integration health and churn signals are especially relevant to call out.

For 'why Cendyn' questions, be specific. Mention their product portfolio, their focus on the hotel commercial stack, or a particular product challenge you find interesting. Generic answers about 'loving travel' will not land well with a product team.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity. Cendyn is not a consumer app company. They want PMs who are genuinely curious about how hotels operate, how revenue managers approach pricing, and what makes hospitality technology a distinct vertical. You do not need a hospitality background, but you should show you have done the research.

Customer empathy for enterprise buyers. Cendyn's customers are hotel GMs, revenue managers, and sales directors running high-stakes, high-pressure operations. Interviewers want to see that you can build genuine empathy for these users, not just map them to abstract personas.

Data-driven decision-making. Expect questions on how you use data to prioritise, validate ideas, and measure outcomes. Come prepared with specific examples of metrics you have tracked and decisions you changed based on what the data showed.

Stakeholder management. Cendyn's PMs navigate sales, customer success, engineering, and enterprise clients at the same time. Interviewers value candidates who can handle competing priorities and communicate clearly across all these groups without losing momentum.

Plain, business-relevant communication. Product decisions at Cendyn need to translate into language that hotel operators understand. Practice explaining technical trade-offs in terms that a GM or revenue manager would immediately connect with.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the product and the domain first. Spend time on Cendyn's website and product pages. Understand what their CRM, revenue cloud, and sales products actually do. Read about how hotel revenue management works, what a PMS is, and how hotel commercial teams are structured. Employee reviews on Glassdoor can give you a feel for the culture and current team priorities.

Build a story bank. Collect product stories from your career that cover prioritisation decisions, stakeholder pushback, data-driven direction changes, and launches (including ones that went wrong). For each story, map it to STAR format and make sure your Result includes a measurable or observable outcome.

Practice case questions with a hospitality lens. Try questions like 'How would you improve Cendyn's group sales module?' or 'What metrics would you track for a hotel CRM onboarding flow?' Practice out loud, not just in your head. Timing yourself on answers helps you stay tight in the actual round.

Prepare a few sharp questions for each round. Good ones include asking about the current roadmap focus, the biggest customer problem the team is solving right now, and how the PM team works with customer success. Thoughtful questions signal that you think like a PM, not just a candidate looking to clear the round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a consumer PM interview. Cendyn is a B2B enterprise company. If your answers are heavy on consumer product examples and light on enterprise dynamics such as sales cycles, multi-stakeholder buying, and integration complexity, you will lose credibility quickly.

Not knowing what Cendyn actually builds. Candidates who walk in without knowing the difference between Cendyn's CRM and revenue products signal low motivation. Spend meaningful time on their product pages before any round.

Weak metrics answers. Saying 'I would track DAU and retention' is not specific enough for an enterprise SaaS context. Be ready to discuss integration health, account activation rates, and renewal signals that map directly to hotel operations.

Generic 'why this company' answers. Saying 'I love travel and hospitality' is not a product answer. Connect your interest to a specific product problem Cendyn is solving or a customer segment they serve.

Rushing past the Result in STAR stories. Many candidates spend too long on context and run out of time before reaching impact. Practice leading with a one-sentence summary of the result, then filling in the story behind it.

Not asking questions. Cendyn interviewers expect PMs to be curious. Candidates who ask nothing at the end of a round leave a flat impression regardless of how well the rest of the conversation went.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Cendyn PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product or case round, and sometimes a final round with senior leadership or a cross-functional stakeholder. The exact structure varies by role level and team. Ask your recruiter for a clear picture of the process at the start so you can prepare the right material for each stage.

Does Cendyn have a technical round for PM roles?

Candidates report that Cendyn PM interviews are not heavily technical in the coding sense. However, expect questions about working with engineering teams, understanding API and PMS integrations, and making technical trade-off decisions. Familiarity with hotel technology systems such as PMS and channel managers is a plus but is typically not a hard requirement for PM roles.

What is the salary range for PM roles at Cendyn in India?

Based on knok jobradar data for the Indian market, PM roles (3-6 years of experience) fall in the 24-40 LPA band and Senior PM roles are in the 40-60 LPA band. Actual offers from Cendyn will vary by specific role, experience level, and city. It is worth discussing your compensation expectations with the recruiter in the first call to avoid surprises further into the process.

How important is hospitality industry knowledge for this interview?

It is genuinely important, though candidates without hospitality backgrounds do clear the process. Interviewers want to see that you understand the workflows of hotel revenue managers, GMs, and sales teams well enough to build products for them. Even a focused reading session on hotel commercial operations and how PMS systems work will make your answers noticeably sharper in the case rounds.

What kind of case or product question should I expect?

Candidates report questions like 'How would you improve a feature for hotel revenue managers?' or 'How would you prioritise a roadmap for independent hotels versus large chains?' These are product improvement and prioritisation cases, not abstract puzzles. Ground your answers in the actual workflow of hotel operators, pick clear metrics, and show a structured approach. Practice with a hospitality context rather than reaching for generic consumer tech examples.
