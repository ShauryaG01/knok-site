01 Overview

Overview

IDfy is one of India's leading platforms for identity verification and background checks. Banks, NBFCs, and large enterprises use IDfy for KYC, document verification, and pre-employment background screening. The DevOps team is responsible for keeping these pipelines fast, highly available, and compliant with data regulations.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

The questions below reflect patterns reported by DevOps candidates at identity-tech and fintech companies. Candidates who have interviewed at IDfy typically say security awareness and reliability thinking are weighted more heavily than at generic product companies, given the sensitivity of identity and background-check data.

  1. Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you built from scratch. What tools did you choose and why?
  2. IDfy handles Aadhaar, PAN, and background records. How do you ensure secrets and credentials never appear in logs or version control?
  3. How would you design a Kubernetes deployment for a document-verification service that must handle sudden traffic spikes during banking hours?
  4. What is your approach to zero-downtime deployments? Which strategy have you used (blue-green, canary, rolling) and where has it fallen short?
  5. IDfy integrates with government APIs that have unpredictable uptime. How do you build infrastructure that degrades gracefully when those dependencies go down?
  6. How would you set up full observability (metrics, logs, distributed traces) for a new microservice joining an existing production cluster?
  7. Describe your disaster recovery approach for a system that stores PII. How do you define RTO and RPO, and how do you test them?
  8. What is your experience with Infrastructure as Code? Walk us through a complex Terraform or Pulumi module and the tradeoffs you made.
  9. In a compliance-sensitive environment, how do you ensure every infrastructure change has a clear audit trail?
  10. Tell us about a production incident you owned end-to-end: the timeline, the fix, and the post-mortem actions.
  11. How do you maintain environment parity across dev, staging, and prod when the external APIs you depend on have no sandbox?
  12. IDfy serves BFSI clients with strict uptime expectations. How do you define, measure, and defend SLAs for a platform you own?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for every behavioral and situational question: Situation (brief context), Task (what you specifically owned), Action (concrete steps and tools), Result (what changed and why it mattered). The three examples below apply this to IDfy-relevant scenarios.

---

Q: Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you built from scratch.

*Situation:* Our team was deploying a payment-reconciliation service manually over SSH. Every release took several hours and deployments sometimes failed silently, leaving no one certain of the production state.

*Task:* I was asked to automate the full release process and make every deployment auditable and repeatable.

*Action:* I set up a GitHub Actions workflow with sequential stages for linting, unit tests, Docker image build, vulnerability scanning with Trivy, and push to ECR. I wrote Helm charts for the Kubernetes deployment and used ArgoCD for GitOps-style promotion across staging and production. Secrets were moved from the repository into AWS Secrets Manager and injected at runtime via the External Secrets Operator.

*Result:* Release time dropped from several hours to a short, repeatable window. Rollback to the previous version became a single ArgoCD operation. The team had full visibility into every deployment for the first time.

---

Q: Tell us about a production incident you owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* On a Monday morning, our background-verification API began timing out for a portion of incoming requests. Client teams started escalating within minutes of the issue starting.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and owned the incident from detection through resolution and post-mortem.

*Action:* I pulled the Grafana dashboards and spotted database connection pool exhaustion on one pod. Correlating with the deploy log, I traced it to a missing index introduced in that morning's release. I rolled back the release via ArgoCD, which cleared the backlog quickly. I then wrote a hotfix with the correct index, ran it through the full CI pipeline on a staging replica, and re-deployed.

*Result:* Full service was restored cleanly. The post-mortem introduced a mandatory slow-query check in the CI pipeline so the same class of issue cannot reach production undetected again.

---

Q: How do you manage secrets in a pipeline that processes identity documents?

*Situation:* A previous engineer had stored API keys for a government verification gateway in a .env file committed to the repository. This was flagged during an internal security review.

*Task:* I was asked to remediate the exposure immediately and put a process in place to prevent recurrence.

*Action:* I rotated all exposed credentials first. I then migrated secrets to HashiCorp Vault, integrated Vault's Kubernetes auth method so pods received short-lived tokens rather than static keys, and added a pre-commit hook using git-secrets to block credential patterns from entering version control. I also set up an automated weekly scan of the full git history using truffleHog.

*Result:* The next quarterly security review found zero exposed secrets. The pre-commit hook caught two accidental credential inclusions from other team members within the first month of rollout.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'how would you design' questions. Start by clarifying constraints before drawing any architecture. Ask about expected load, data sensitivity requirements, uptime targets, and compliance obligations. Then walk through your design layer by layer: compute, networking, storage, observability, and security controls. IDfy interviewers particularly value candidates who bring up compliance and audit logging without being prompted, since these are daily realities for the team.

For 'tell me about a time' questions. Use STAR. Keep Situation and Task to a few sentences combined. Spend the bulk of your answer on Action (concrete tools, commands, decisions) and Result (what changed and why it mattered to the business). Avoid vague results like 'things improved'; describe a specific, tangible outcome.

For 'how do you handle X' process questions. Structure your answer as: 'Here is my normal approach, here is where it breaks down in practice, and here is how I adapt.' This signals real-world maturity. Interviewers can tell the difference between someone who has done the thing and someone who has only read about it.

For debugging and incident questions. Think out loud and be systematic: 'First I would check X because it is the most likely cause given the symptoms. If that looks clean, I move to Y.' Interviewers at IDfy are evaluating your diagnostic reasoning and your calm under pressure, not just whether you land on the right answer.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

IDfy's product sits at the intersection of finance and identity data, making it one of the more compliance-driven engineering environments in India. Interviewers are not just checking tool familiarity. They are looking for several specific qualities.

Security as a first instinct. The strongest candidates weave secrets management, RBAC, and audit logging into every technical answer without being prompted. If you only bring up security after being asked directly, that signals shallow operational experience in a domain where it cannot be an afterthought.

Reliability engineering depth. IDfy's clients are banks and large enterprises with strict SLAs. Interviewers want to hear how you think about failure modes, graceful degradation, and recovery. Mentioning SLO targets, error budgets, or structured post-mortem formats shows the right level of seniority for the role.

Decision-making over tool listing. Knowing a tool is fine. Understanding why your team adopted it, where it struggles, and what you would replace it with shows genuine depth. Avoid walking through your resume tool by tool; talk about tradeoffs and decisions instead.

Clear cross-team communication. DevOps engineers at IDfy work closely with product and backend teams. Interviewers often check whether a candidate can explain a complex infrastructure decision to someone without a DevOps background, clearly and without jargon.

Genuine curiosity about the domain. Candidates who ask about IDfy's verification stack, the scale of daily KYC checks, or how the team handles DPDPA compliance tend to leave a stronger impression than those who treat the interview as a generic tech round.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical depth.
Review your CI/CD toolchain end-to-end. Build a small pipeline from scratch if you have not done so recently. Revisit Kubernetes fundamentals: pod lifecycle, resource limits, horizontal pod autoscaling, ingress controllers, and RBAC. Practice writing a Terraform module with remote state and variable validation.

Week 2: Security and compliance focus.
IDfy's environment makes this non-negotiable. Study secrets management (HashiCorp Vault or a cloud-native equivalent), Kubernetes network policies, container image scanning, and the basics of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Be able to explain why least-privilege access matters specifically in an identity-verification context, not just in general terms.

Week 3: System design and incident scenarios.
Practice designing a highly available microservice deployment on paper, including how you would handle a third-party government API going down mid-day. Structure at least two real incidents from your own experience as STAR stories. If you lack direct incident experience, work through public post-mortems from well-known companies and walk through how you would have approached each step.

Week 4: Company research and mock interviews.
Read about IDfy's product suite, covering their background verification, document check, and court-record screening offerings. Understand that their primary clients are in BFSI, which shapes every reliability and compliance requirement the team operates under. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating security as a separate topic. Many candidates answer a CI/CD question and then wait to be asked about security separately. At IDfy, security belongs inside every technical answer. Mention secrets scanning, RBAC, and image vulnerability checks as part of your standard pipeline description, not as an add-on.

Vague incident stories. 'I fixed a production issue once' is not enough. Interviewers want a clear timeline, the tool you used to detect the problem, the specific fix, and what you changed afterward to prevent recurrence. Prepare two or three incidents in STAR format before you walk in.

Overloading on tool names. Listing every tool you have touched signals shallow experience. Pick two or three tools you know deeply and be prepared to discuss their internals, failure modes, and the alternatives you considered. Talk about decisions, not a resume inventory.

Ignoring the business context. IDfy is not a generic product company. Their DevOps decisions are shaped by RBI KYC guidelines, enterprise SLAs, and data privacy regulations. Anchoring your answers to reliability, auditability, and compliance shows you understand where the product lives.

Not preparing your own questions. Candidates who ask nothing at the end signal low interest. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team's current infrastructure challenges, their on-call rotation, or what a typical release cycle looks like.

Underestimating the final round. Candidates report that IDfy's last discussion sometimes includes light technical questions alongside cultural-fit ones. Stay sharp through every round, not just the main technical ones.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does IDfy typically have for a DevOps Engineer?

Candidates report anywhere from 2 to 4 rounds, varying by seniority and team. A common path includes a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds, and a final discussion with a senior engineer or hiring manager. Treat any specific count you read online as a general guide, since the structure can shift based on the role level and who is hiring.

Is there a coding or DSA round in the IDfy DevOps interview?

Candidates do not typically report a heavy LeetCode-style DSA round for DevOps roles at IDfy. The technical focus is usually on CI/CD design, Kubernetes, cloud infrastructure, and security practices. Some teams include a short scripting or debugging exercise in Python or Bash, so brushing up on basic automation scripting is a worthwhile part of your prep.

Which cloud platform should I focus on when preparing?

IDfy is primarily reported to use AWS, so services like EKS, ECR, Secrets Manager, and CloudWatch are worth studying. Cloud-agnostic concepts such as container orchestration, IaC principles, and VPC networking matter more than memorising platform-specific APIs. Be ready to explain your reasoning for any tool or service choice rather than just naming it.

How important is knowledge of compliance and data privacy for this role?

More important here than at a typical product startup. IDfy processes Aadhaar data, PAN details, and employment background records, placing it under RBI KYC guidelines and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. You do not need to be a legal expert, but understanding why audit logs, access controls, and PII masking matter in this context will clearly set you apart from candidates who treat DevOps as purely a tooling exercise.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer at IDfy?

IDfy does not publicly disclose its pay bands. As a market reference, Glassdoor and industry surveys commonly place mid-level DevOps engineers (3-5 years of experience) in India in the 15-28 LPA range and senior engineers (6-9 years) in the 30-50 LPA range. Your actual offer will depend on your experience, the team's budget, and how you negotiate. Check levels.fyi and Glassdoor for data points closer to IDfy specifically.

How should I research IDfy before the interview?

Start with their core product areas: background verification, KYC document checks, digital document storage, and court-record screening. Their primary clients are banks, NBFCs, and large enterprises, which explains the strong emphasis on SLAs, compliance, and reliability you will encounter in every technical discussion. Check their LinkedIn engineering page for hints about their stack, and prepare at least one specific question about a product feature or infrastructure challenge you found genuinely interesting.
