01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

SpotDraft is a B2B legal tech company building contract lifecycle management software. Businesses use SpotDraft to draft, negotiate, sign, and store contracts, cutting down the back-and-forth that usually clogs up legal and business teams. The company serves customers across India and internationally and has been growing steadily through 2025 and 2026.

As of July 2026, SpotDraft has 19 open roles listed across functions including engineering, product management, sales, customer success, marketing, and legal operations. Whether you are a software engineer, a sales professional, or someone with a legal background wanting to move into tech, there is likely something worth applying to.

SpotDraft is at the growth stage, which means the company moves fast and expects people who can operate with some ambiguity. Teams are lean, so your work has direct visibility and impact from day one.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

SpotDraft's interview process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact steps vary by role and team. Here is what candidates commonly report.

Application and Resume Screen: The recruiting team reviews your application. A strong match between your skills and the job description matters here. Tailor your resume to highlight relevant experience in B2B SaaS, legal tech, or the specific function you are applying for.

Recruiter Call: A short screening call with someone from the HR or recruiting team. Expect questions about your background, why you are interested in SpotDraft, and your current situation (notice period, location, salary expectations). This is also your chance to ask about the role and team.

Skills Assessment or Technical Round: For engineering roles, this is usually a coding round or a take-home assignment. For product roles, expect a case study or product thinking exercise. Sales and customer success candidates may face a mock pitch or a scenario-based exercise. The assessment tests practical ability, not just theoretical knowledge.

Panel or Manager Interview: One or two rounds with the hiring manager and sometimes other team members. For engineering, this may include a system design discussion. For non-technical roles, expect deeper questions about past work, metrics you have owned, and how you handle specific situations. Behavioral questions using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) are common.

Culture or Leadership Round: Some candidates, especially for senior roles, go through a final conversation with a founder or senior leader. This round is less about hard skills and more about values, long-term thinking, and how you approach problems.

Offer: If all rounds go well, the recruiter reaches out with a verbal offer followed by a written one. Negotiation is possible, particularly if you have competing offers or strong market data to reference.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

SpotDraft looks for people who combine domain sharpness with an ownership mindset. A few qualities that come up repeatedly when candidates describe their experience:

Clarity of thinking. SpotDraft builds software for legal teams, where precision matters. Whether you are writing code, designing a feature, or talking to a customer, the company wants people who can communicate clearly and reason through problems without hand-holding.

Customer obsession. The product touches real workflows for legal and business teams. Candidates who can show they have thought deeply about end-user problems, not just the tech or the process, tend to stand out.

Comfort with ambiguity. Growth-stage startups do not have a playbook for every situation. SpotDraft wants people who can identify what needs to be done and move forward without waiting for perfect information.

Relevant domain knowledge. For technical roles, solid fundamentals in software engineering, system design, and modern tech stacks matter. For go-to-market roles, experience in B2B SaaS sales or customer success is a strong signal. A basic understanding of contracts or legal operations is a plus across all functions.

Collaboration and low ego. Teams at SpotDraft are cross-functional. The ability to work well across engineering, legal, sales, and product is valued more than individual brilliance that does not translate into team outcomes.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparation makes a real difference at SpotDraft, where interviews are practical and specific.

Research the product deeply. Sign up for a free trial or watch demo videos of SpotDraft. Understand what contract lifecycle management means, what pain points it solves, and how SpotDraft compares to alternatives. If you can speak intelligently about the product in your interview, you immediately stand out.

Know your own work in detail. Interviewers at SpotDraft tend to ask detailed questions about past projects. Be ready to walk through specific examples: what the problem was, what you did, what the outcome was, and what you would do differently. Vague answers about 'working in a team' do not land well here.

Prepare for practical assessments. Do not wait for the assessment round to start practising. Engineers should brush up on data structures, algorithms, and system design. Product managers should practise product critique and feature prioritisation exercises. Sales candidates should prepare a short pitch for a software product they have sold or used.

Tailor your resume to the role. With 19 open roles across the company, SpotDraft is hiring for multiple functions. Read each job description carefully, identify the three or four most important requirements, and make sure your resume directly addresses each one.

Prepare smart questions. Asking thoughtful questions in every round signals genuine interest. Good ones include: how does the team measure success for this role in the first six months, what does onboarding look like, and what is the biggest challenge the team is solving right now.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

SpotDraft has a culture shaped by its roots as a founder-led startup solving a real problem in legal tech.

Fast pace with purpose. The company moves quickly and teams are expected to deliver. Unlike some startups where speed means chaos, SpotDraft is focused on a specific problem space, which gives work a clearer direction.

Wide ownership early. People who join SpotDraft often mention getting responsibility faster than they expected. Because teams are lean, junior hires take on work that might take years to reach at a larger company.

Remote-friendly but collaborative. SpotDraft has embraced flexible work, with team members spread across cities. Async communication skills matter, but the culture still values collaboration and team cohesion.

Mission alignment. Legal teams in Indian companies have historically been underserved by technology. SpotDraft employees tend to feel connected to the mission of making legal work faster and less painful. If that problem genuinely interests you, you will fit in better than someone who sees it as just another SaaS company.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many jobs is SpotDraft currently hiring for?

As of July 2026, SpotDraft has 19 open roles across functions including engineering, product, sales, customer success, and marketing. The number changes as roles get filled and new ones open, so check their careers page or major job platforms regularly to catch fresh listings.

Does SpotDraft hire freshers or entry-level candidates?

SpotDraft does hire for early-career roles, particularly in engineering and sales. As a growth-stage company, they prefer candidates who can contribute quickly with minimal ramp-up. If you are a fresher, strong internship experience, personal projects, or a clear understanding of the SpotDraft product will help your application stand out.

How long does SpotDraft's hiring process take from application to offer?

The typical timeline from application to offer is a few weeks, though it can stretch longer for senior roles or during busy hiring periods. After each round, following up politely with your recruiter after a few business days is reasonable if you have not heard back.

What salary can I expect at SpotDraft?

SpotDraft does not publicly list salary bands, and compensation varies significantly by role, level, and function. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have some self-reported figures from employees, though the sample sizes for SpotDraft specifically are small. Research market rates for your role at B2B SaaS companies of a similar size and come prepared to discuss your expectations.

Is experience in legal or contracts required to work at SpotDraft?

It is not required for most roles, but it is a genuine advantage. For engineering and product roles, understanding how legal teams work helps you contribute faster. For sales and customer success roles, being able to speak a customer's language around contracts and compliance can be a real differentiator.

How should I follow up after an interview at SpotDraft?

Send a brief thank-you note to your recruiter within a day of each round, mentioning your continued interest and one specific thing you appreciated about the conversation. If you do not hear back within the timeline the recruiter mentioned, one polite follow-up email is appropriate. Avoid sending multiple follow-ups in quick succession.
