01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Inito is a Bengaluru-based femtech startup building a home fertility monitor that tracks key reproductive hormones, letting women understand their cycles without a clinic visit. Because the team is lean, each hire carries genuine weight and your contributions will be visible quickly. If you want to build products that matter to families across India and beyond, Inito is worth a serious look.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Inito's hiring process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact sequence can shift by role and seniority. Here is how it generally unfolds, based on publicly shared candidate accounts.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen

Inito reviews applications for relevant experience and mission alignment. A resume that speaks to product impact rather than just job duties tends to move faster at this stage. With 10 open roles live as of July 2026, the team is actively hiring, so applying promptly matters.

Stage 2: Recruiter or hiring manager call

This is a brief conversation to check basic fit, your interest in women's health, and compensation expectations. Be ready to explain why Inito specifically, not just 'healthtech in general.' The recruiter will also walk you through the next steps so you know what to expect.

Stage 3: Skills or take-home assessment

Engineering candidates commonly receive a coding assignment or a system design problem. Product and growth candidates may get a case study or a go-to-market brief. These assessments are usually open-ended, so showing your reasoning matters more than landing on a single 'correct' answer.

Stage 4: Technical or domain interview

A deeper, live conversation with the team you would join. For engineers, expect questions on data structures, system design (especially for mobile-connected hardware), and past project trade-offs. For product roles, expect prioritisation scenarios, user research questions, and how you define success metrics for a health product.

Stage 5: Culture and leadership round

A conversation with a founder or senior leader focused on values, ownership, and how you handle ambiguity. Inito moves fast, so they want to see that you can make decisions without waiting for perfect information.

Offer

Successful candidates typically hear back fairly quickly after the final round. Compensation at startups of this stage is commonly cited as a mix of fixed pay and ESOPs, so clarify both components before accepting.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Mission-first thinking. Inito is solving a problem that affects millions of women trying to understand their own bodies. They want people who genuinely care about that mission, not candidates who see this as a stepping stone to a bigger brand name. In interviews, they pay close attention to whether your curiosity about women's health is real or rehearsed.

Ownership and speed. Small teams cannot afford people who wait for direction. Inito looks for candidates who have taken end-to-end ownership of a project, made a call under pressure, and stood by the outcome. Concrete examples beat generic claims every time.

Cross-functional comfort. The product combines a physical device with a mobile app and a health data layer, so even specialist roles in engineering, product, and design are expected to understand adjacent areas. Show that you have worked across team boundaries before.

Data fluency. Whether you are in marketing, product, or engineering, being able to read data, form a hypothesis, and act on it is a common expectation. You do not need to be a data scientist, but you should be comfortable pulling numbers and questioning them.

Clear, written communication. Inito teams are small and meetings are few. They rely heavily on written tools to stay aligned. Candidates who can explain complex ideas simply tend to stand out in interviews and in the role itself.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product before anything else. If you can, try the Inito fertility monitor before your interview. Understanding the user experience first-hand gives you specific, credible things to say. At minimum, read through app store reviews and public user feedback to understand real pain points before you sit down with the team.

Know the market context. Women's health diagnostics in India are growing fast but still navigating awareness gaps. Read recent industry surveys or publicly reported news on femtech in India so you can speak intelligently about the space during your interviews.

Prepare three strong ownership stories. For any role, be ready to walk through three projects where you owned an outcome from start to finish, including what went wrong and what you would do differently. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but keep it conversational, not scripted.

Brush up on technical basics for your role. Engineers should revisit data structures, system design for mobile-connected devices, and API design. Product candidates should be ready to discuss prioritisation frameworks and how to define success metrics for a health product that has both a hardware and software component.

Prepare thoughtful questions. Ask about the biggest product challenge the team is working through, how engineering and product collaborate day to day, and what success looks like in the first few months on the job. Questions like these signal genuine interest and help you evaluate fit from your side as well.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Inito operates like a classic mission-driven startup: high ownership, low bureaucracy, and a genuine belief that the product can change lives. The team is small enough that your work is visible to the founders, which can be energising if you like direct feedback and real accountability.

Pace and expectations. The company moves fast and expects the same from its people. Priorities can shift as the product and market evolve, so adaptability is not optional. If you have only worked in large, process-heavy organisations, the absence of formal structure may take some adjustment in the early weeks.

Collaboration style. Inito teams work closely across functions. Engineers are expected to understand user problems, and product managers are expected to understand technical constraints. There is far less siloing than you would find at a large company, which means you learn faster but also need to communicate more proactively.

Learning environment. Because the team is small and the problem is complex (combining hardware, software, and health data), there is a steep learning curve early on. Most employees who share their experience publicly note that the variety of work keeps things interesting well beyond the initial ramp-up period.

Commitment to the user. Given that Inito's users are predominantly women navigating fertility questions, the team places genuine value on diverse perspectives in hiring and in how product decisions are made. This shows up in interview conversations, not just in stated company values.
