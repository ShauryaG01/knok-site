01 Overview

Overview

Agoda is a travel technology company under Booking Holdings, best known for hotel and travel bookings across the Asia-Pacific region. Its ML and AI teams work on personalization engines, dynamic pricing, search ranking, and fraud detection at a scale that touches travellers around the world every day. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 299 open roles at Agoda, showing the company is in a strong hiring phase.

ML Engineers at Agoda are expected to own the full model lifecycle: ideation, feature engineering, training, A/B testing, and production monitoring. The work is product-driven, meaning you will be expected to frame ML problems in terms of business impact, not just model metrics.

The interview process candidates report typically runs across four to six conversations spread over two to four weeks. Expect an initial recruiter call, one or two technical screens covering coding and ML fundamentals, a system design round, and a set of final interviews with the hiring manager and cross-functional stakeholders. Some candidates also report a short case study tied to a real Agoda product problem. Rounds are typically conducted over video call.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Agoda ML Engineer interviews, based on what candidates report across forums and review platforms:

  1. How would you design a hotel recommendation system to improve click-through rates for logged-in users?
  2. Walk us through how you would build a dynamic pricing model for travel inventory and how you would evaluate it in production.
  3. How do you handle class imbalance in a fraud or anomaly detection model?
  4. Explain how you would set up an A/B test to measure the impact of a new search ranking algorithm, including how you decide sample size and success metrics.
  5. What is the difference between collaborative filtering and content-based filtering? When would you pick one over the other for a travel platform?
  6. How would you reduce inference latency in a real-time ranking pipeline that must handle high traffic volumes with very tight response time budgets?
  7. How would you monitor a production ML model and detect data drift or performance degradation?
  8. Describe how you would design a feature store for a large-scale travel platform with many teams consuming the same features.
  9. Given a sparse user-item interaction matrix, what techniques would you use to improve recommendation quality for cold-start users?
  10. How would you build a search ranking model that balances relevance, hotel revenue contribution, and overall user experience?
  11. Tell me about a time you had to trade off model accuracy for inference speed or infrastructure cost.
  12. How would you explain the output of a complex ensemble model to a product manager who needs to act on it?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you improved a production model that was underperforming.

*Situation:* I was working on a user engagement model that predicted which users were likely to book within two days. After a few months in production, prediction quality had dropped noticeably and the product team was losing confidence in the system.

*Task:* I was asked to diagnose the drop and restore prediction quality without a full model rebuild if possible.

*Action:* I ran a feature drift analysis and found that one key input, the average session duration feature, had shifted significantly because the app had added a new onboarding flow that inflated session times for new users. I added a user-segment flag to separate new and returning users, retrained on a rolling window of recent data, and added automated drift alerts to catch similar issues faster in the future.

*Result:* Prediction quality recovered within one week of the fix going live. The automated alerts caught another drift event two months later before it affected downstream metrics.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you had to explain ML results to a non-technical stakeholder who needed to act on them.

*Situation:* Our demand forecasting model flagged a sharp expected drop in bookings for a specific hotel category in a region. The commercial team needed to decide whether to offer promotions before the drop, but they did not trust the model output.

*Task:* I had to explain the forecast and its confidence level in a way that a commercial director could use to make a real business decision.

*Action:* I built a one-page brief that showed the model's prediction alongside the actual booking trend for the two most similar historical periods in 2024 and 2025. I avoided technical terms and instead framed it as: 'the model is saying what we saw in similar situations before.' I also showed a simple confidence range so they could see best-case and worst-case scenarios.

*Result:* The commercial team ran a targeted promotion. The drop in bookings was smaller than the worst-case scenario, and the director later asked for a monthly briefing in the same format.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to trade off model accuracy for system constraints.

*Situation:* A ranking model I had trained was significantly more accurate than the system in production, but it used a large transformer-based architecture that could not meet the latency budget for real-time requests.

*Task:* I needed to deliver a model that improved on the baseline while staying within the latency constraint the infrastructure team had set.

*Action:* I used knowledge distillation to train a smaller student model on the outputs of the larger model. I also profiled inference to remove redundant features that added compute but minimal lift. The distilled model was far smaller than the original.

*Result:* The distilled model met the latency requirement while still outperforming the baseline on the main ranking metric. We shipped it within the planned release window.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For ML system design questions, use a Product-to-Pipeline structure: start by clarifying the business goal (what metric defines success for Agoda), define the ML task (classification, ranking, regression), walk through data sources and feature engineering, describe model selection and offline evaluation, then cover serving architecture and online evaluation via A/B testing. Interviewers want to see that you think in terms of business outcomes, not just model architecture.

For coding questions, think aloud as you write. Agoda interviews typically involve Python. Before coding, confirm the input and output contract and any edge cases. Candidates report questions covering array manipulation, graph traversal, and ML-adjacent problems like implementing gradient descent from scratch or writing a simple recommendation function.

For behavioral questions, use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but keep your Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on the Action and the Result. Agoda interviewers are reported to value candidates who show clear ownership, data-driven thinking, and the ability to move fast without cutting corners.

For trade-off questions (accuracy vs. latency, precision vs. recall, model complexity vs. maintainability), name the trade-off explicitly first, state which side you would lean toward given the specific business context, and explain your reasoning. Avoid vague answers like 'it depends' without immediately following up with what it actually depends on.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Agoda ML interview panels typically look for four things:

Product thinking. Can you frame an ML problem in terms of what Agoda's users and business actually need? Candidates who only think about model accuracy without considering latency, fairness, or revenue impact are typically screened out early.

Depth in core ML. You should be comfortable explaining gradient boosting, neural network training, regularization, and evaluation metrics without looking them up. For senior roles, depth in areas like ranking models, recommendation systems, or sequential models is expected.

Engineering maturity. Agoda runs ML at scale. Interviewers want to see that you understand production realities: data pipelines, model versioning, monitoring, and the cost of retraining large models.

Clear communication. Many Agoda teams are cross-functional and distributed across time zones. The ability to explain your reasoning to engineers, product managers, and data analysts alike is a real differentiator.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before the interview:

Revise ML fundamentals: bias-variance trade-off, regularization, tree-based models, and neural network basics. Practice coding in Python on a competitive programming platform, focusing on medium-difficulty problems involving arrays, trees, and dynamic programming.

One to two weeks before:

Practice ML system design using real Agoda products as prompts. For example: 'Design a hotel ranking system for the Agoda search results page.' Read up on how travel platforms approach recommendation, pricing, and fraud detection. Review A/B testing concepts including statistical significance, sample size estimation, and common pitfalls like novelty effects.

Three to five days before:

Prepare four to six STAR stories from your work history covering: improving a model in production, working across teams, making a data-driven decision under uncertainty, and handling a technical failure. Research Agoda's recent product updates and publicly reported ML initiatives so you can tie your answers to real context.

The day before:

Do a mock interview with a peer or an AI practice tool. Test your video and audio setup. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer about the team's current ML stack, deployment practices, or how they measure model impact in production.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to model choice before defining the problem. A common pattern candidates report is immediately saying 'I would use XGBoost' without first clarifying what metric defines success or what data is available. Always anchor your answer to the business goal.

Over-engineering in system design. Candidates sometimes propose overly complex architectures to signal technical depth. Agoda interviewers are reported to prefer practical, scalable solutions over impressive but fragile ones. Start simple, then add complexity only when you can justify it.

Ignoring production concerns. Describing only the training pipeline without addressing monitoring, retraining triggers, or rollback strategy is a common gap. ML in production is very different from ML in a notebook.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'my team improved the model' instead of describing exactly what you personally designed, changed, or decided leaves interviewers with no signal on your individual contribution. Be specific about your own actions and the measurable outcome.

Not asking clarifying questions. Agoda system design questions are intentionally open-ended. Jumping in without asking about scale, latency requirements, or available data signals poor problem-solving habits.

Neglecting the A/B testing layer. For a company like Agoda that runs many experiments continuously, not knowing how to properly evaluate a model in a live environment is a significant gap. Make sure you can discuss experimentation concepts, including guardrail metrics and experiment duration, fluently.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Agoda ML Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report four to six rounds spread over two to four weeks. This typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two technical phone screens, a system design round, and a final set of interviews with senior engineers and the hiring manager. Some candidates also report a case study tied to an Agoda product problem, though this varies by team and seniority level.

What programming language should I prepare in for Agoda ML interviews?

Python is the standard language candidates report using in Agoda ML engineering interviews. You should be comfortable writing clean, working Python for both algorithmic problems and ML-adjacent tasks like implementing a simple model or a data transformation pipeline. Familiarity with libraries like NumPy, pandas, and scikit-learn is useful background, though you will typically not need to import them during coding rounds.

Does Agoda ask ML theory questions or focus more on system design?

Candidates report both types across different rounds. Early rounds tend to test ML fundamentals including model evaluation, regularization, and common algorithms. Later rounds shift toward system design, where you are asked to architect a full ML pipeline for a product problem like search ranking or personalization. Coming prepared for both is strongly recommended.

How important is domain knowledge in travel tech for this role?

You do not need prior travel industry experience to interview successfully at Agoda. Interviewers care more about your ability to quickly understand a business problem and map it to an ML solution. That said, spending a few hours understanding how hotel ranking, dynamic pricing, and travel recommendations work will help you give more grounded answers and ask sharper questions during the interview.
