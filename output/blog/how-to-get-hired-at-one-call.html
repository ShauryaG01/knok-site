03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at One Call typically unfolds in three to four stages, though the exact number of rounds can vary by role and location.

Stage 1: Application and resume screening. The HR team reviews applications against the job description. Tailoring your resume to highlight relevant experience, whether in healthcare coordination, software development, or operations support, improves your chances of moving forward.

Stage 2: HR or recruiter call. A short call, usually around half an hour, covers your background, notice period, salary expectations, and basic role fit. Expect questions about why you are interested in the healthcare or managed care space.

Stage 3: Technical or functional round. Depending on the role, this round tests job-specific skills. Tech candidates can expect questions on their core stack, problem-solving, and sometimes a coding exercise. Operations and support roles may involve scenario-based questions about handling escalations or coordinating across teams.

Stage 4: Manager or panel interview. A senior manager or a small panel assesses your communication, situational judgement, and cultural fit. This is also your chance to ask questions about the team, workflows, and growth paths.

Some roles, especially senior or specialist positions, add a fifth round with HR leadership or a competency assessment. One Call currently has 13 open positions across different functions, so the exact process may differ slightly depending on which team you are joining.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

One Call values a specific combination of technical competence and soft skills, shaped by the nature of its work in healthcare coordination.

Domain awareness. Familiarity with the US healthcare system, workers' compensation, or managed care is a strong differentiator. You do not need to be an expert, but candidates who understand concepts like care coordination, network management, or medical billing tend to stand out from the pool.

Communication skills. Because many roles interface with US-based clients, partners, or internal teams, clear and professional English communication, both written and spoken, matters a great deal. This applies even to technical roles where cross-functional collaboration is part of the job.

Process orientation. One Call handles complex, multi-party workflows. Candidates who can demonstrate that they follow processes carefully, flag gaps proactively, and work well within structured systems perform well in interviews.

Technical proficiency. For tech roles, competence in the relevant stack is table stakes. For operations roles, comfort with tools like CRMs, case management systems, or data reporting platforms is commonly expected.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the company and industry. Spend time understanding what workers' compensation means and how managed care organisations fit into that picture. Reading publicly available material on One Call's services will give you credible talking points in your interview.

Tailor your resume to the job description. Match your experience directly to the key requirements listed. Highlight outcomes, not just responsibilities. If the role mentions reducing turnaround time, describe a situation where you delivered exactly that.

Prepare STAR-format answers. One Call interviewers frequently use behavioural questions. Structure your answers around Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Prepare examples that show problem-solving, cross-team collaboration, and handling pressure.

Brush up on technical skills. If you are applying for a tech role, review data structures, system design basics, and any tools mentioned in the job description. For operations roles, be ready to walk through how you have managed complex cases or escalations in past jobs.

Ask thoughtful questions. Towards the end of the interview, asking about team structure, success metrics for the role, or what challenges the team is currently navigating shows genuine interest and preparation. It also helps you assess whether the role is the right fit for you.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

One Call operates in a mission-driven space, helping injured workers access the care they need, and this shapes the internal culture in meaningful ways.

Service at the core. The company's work has a direct human impact. Employees frequently describe a sense of purpose tied to helping people navigate difficult health situations. This is not a culture where output is purely transactional.

Structured and process-driven. Given the regulated nature of healthcare and workers' compensation, One Call maintains clear processes and compliance expectations. If you thrive in an environment with defined workflows and clear accountability, you will likely find the culture a good fit.

Collaborative across functions. Roles at One Call often require coordination across clinical, operations, and technology teams. A willingness to work across boundaries and communicate proactively is valued at every level.

Growth and domain expertise. The company offers exposure to a specialised and growing sector of global healthcare services. For Indian professionals, this can translate into valuable domain expertise that opens doors in the broader healthcare technology and services industry.
