01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Speechmatics is a Cambridge-based speech AI company building automatic speech recognition (ASR) technology trusted by enterprises, broadcasters, and developers worldwide. Their platform supports a wide range of languages and accents, making it central to the growing market for conversational AI and audio intelligence.

As of mid-2026, Speechmatics has 12 open roles across software engineering, machine learning research, product, sales, and customer success. The company is in an active growth phase, so new positions appear regularly. Roles are primarily based in Cambridge and London, with some hybrid or remote options depending on the team and function.

For Indian professionals, this is a company worth targeting if your background touches machine learning, audio processing, backend engineering, Python-based systems, or enterprise SaaS sales. The hiring process is structured and typically spans three to five rounds, with a strong emphasis on technical depth, ownership mindset, and clear communication.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The stages below reflect publicly reported candidate experiences. The exact sequence can vary by role and team.

Application and CV screen
Apply through the Speechmatics careers page. Your CV is reviewed by a recruiter or hiring manager. Highlight specific experience in speech, audio, ML, or software engineering relevant to the role you are targeting. Generic CVs tend to get filtered out quickly at specialist AI companies.

Recruiter call
If shortlisted, you are invited to a short introductory call with a recruiter. Expect questions about your background, your motivation for joining Speechmatics, your current notice period, and your location or remote work situation. Use this call to ask about team size, role scope, and the hiring timeline.

Technical or functional screen
For engineering and ML roles, this is usually a take-home exercise or a live coding session. Topics commonly include algorithms, data structures, system design, and, for specialist roles, concepts around audio processing or model evaluation. For non-technical roles, a case study or role-specific task is more typical at this stage.

Panel or technical interview
A deeper round with one or two engineers or research leads. For ML roles, expect discussion of model architectures, training strategies, evaluation metrics, and how you approach research problems in a production setting. For software engineering roles, system design and code quality tend to be the focus.

Final round or leadership conversation
A conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional panel. Expect questions about how you collaborate across teams, handle ambiguity, and drive end-to-end outcomes. Some candidates report a short presentation of past work at this stage.

Offer
Successful candidates receive a verbal offer from the recruiter, followed by a written offer letter. There is generally room to negotiate, particularly on compensation components and remote work arrangements.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Speechmatics looks for people who combine technical rigour with a bias toward shipping real outcomes. Here is what tends to matter across role types.

For engineers and ML researchers
Strong fundamentals in software engineering or machine learning are table stakes. Domain familiarity with speech or audio processing is a genuine advantage, but generalist engineers with solid Python or C++ skills are considered. Published research, open-source contributions, or prior work with audio ML frameworks can significantly strengthen your profile for research-facing roles.

For product and go-to-market roles
A clear grasp of enterprise SaaS dynamics, strong written and verbal communication, and the ability to translate technical capability into customer value. Prior experience in AI, developer tools, or media technology markets is a bonus.

Across all roles
Speechmatics values people who take ownership, move without waiting for permission, and communicate clearly across functions. Comfort with ambiguity matters here. This is a growth-stage company and processes are still being built. If you thrive only in structured, slow-moving environments, this may not be the right fit.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Explore the product before your interview
Speechmatics offers a developer API and trial access. Being able to speak concretely about their transcription quality, language support, or API design signals genuine interest and sets you apart from candidates who only skimmed the website.

Refresh your speech and audio ML fundamentals
Even for generalist engineering roles, expect at least a conversational-level question about how modern speech recognition works. Familiarise yourself with acoustic models, language models, CTC loss, and how large audio models have evolved in recent years through 2026.

Practise system design for audio workloads
For engineering roles, practise designing scalable audio processing pipelines or real-time transcription systems. Think through latency, accuracy trade-offs, and how you would handle edge cases like background noise, overlapping speakers, or code-switching across languages.

Prepare STAR-format behavioural answers
Speechmatics interviewers commonly ask behavioural questions alongside technical ones. Prepare clear, specific stories around: shipping something complex under pressure, resolving a technical disagreement, and improving a system or product in a way that had visible impact.

Ask sharp, informed questions
Prepare two or three questions that show you have done your homework, such as how the research team feeds into the product roadmap, or how the company thinks about accuracy versus latency trade-offs for different customer segments.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Speechmatics describes itself as a mission-driven company focused on making speech technology work across all languages and accents, including those typically underserved by mainstream ASR systems. The culture reflects its Cambridge deep-tech roots: precise, intellectually driven, and collaborative.

Ownership and directness
Teams are small enough that individual contributions are visible. Employees are expected to bring solutions rather than just surface problems, and to move forward without waiting for top-down direction. Directness in communication is valued, and feedback tends to be specific rather than softened.

Technical rigour
The company has strong academic roots and that shows in how problems get approached. Whether you are writing code, a product spec, or a customer-facing document, precision and clarity matter. Vague thinking tends to get pushed back on quickly, both in interviews and in day-to-day work.

Diverse voices, by design
There is something fitting about a speech AI company that genuinely invests in supporting underrepresented languages. That mission translates internally into a team that spans multiple nationalities and backgrounds, with a stated commitment to inclusion.

Growth-stage pace
With 12 open roles currently active and an expanding enterprise customer base, Speechmatics is building fast. Expect some process ambiguity, evolving team structures, and the kind of learning curve that comes with a company scaling its go-to-market alongside its technology.
