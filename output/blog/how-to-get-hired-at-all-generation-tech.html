03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at All Generation Tech typically moves through four to five stages, though the exact structure can vary by role and seniority level. Here is what candidates commonly experience, described honestly and without over-promising on specifics.

Stage 1: Application and Resume Shortlisting. Your resume is the first filter. With 20 open roles at the company, there are real opportunities but also more applicants than available slots. A resume that mirrors the language and requirements in the job description gets through the screening faster than a generic one. Highlight your most relevant skills and keep the format clean.

Stage 2: HR Screening Call. Once your profile clears the initial review, HR typically calls to confirm basic details: your current role, experience level, location preference, and notice period. Communication matters here. Speak clearly, be honest about your expectations, and have your expected CTC range ready because the question almost always comes up.

Stage 3: Technical Round (one or two rounds). This is where your core skills are tested. For software and engineering roles, expect questions on data structures, algorithms, or the specific frameworks listed in the job description. For QA roles, test design and automation concepts are common. For business analyst or project management roles, expect scenario-based questions that test how you think through real problems rather than pure theory. Senior roles often go through two technical rounds, with the second going deeper into system design or domain expertise.

Stage 4: Managerial or Panel Discussion. A senior team member or a small panel asks about your work history, how you handle challenges, and how you collaborate. Behavioural questions are common here. Structured answers using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) work well. Come ready with real examples from your experience.

Stage 5: HR Discussion and Offer. The final conversation covers compensation, joining date, and any remaining logistics. Know your current CTC, your expected CTC, and your negotiables before this call. Negotiating is standard and expected, so do not undersell yourself.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Technology services companies hire for a mix of technical ability and interpersonal skills. At All Generation Tech, interviewers are typically checking for several things across every round.

Technical depth in your core area. You should know your tools well enough to apply them, not just describe them. If you are a developer, that means writing working code and explaining your reasoning. If you are in QA or a functional role, it means demonstrating your process, not just listing tools you have used.

Clear communication. Many roles in this space involve working with multiple stakeholders or clients. The ability to explain your thinking, ask the right questions, and listen well is noticed from the very first HR call. Do not mistake 'technical interview' for 'only technical things matter': communication runs through every round.

Problem-solving mindset. Interviewers watch closely when you hit a question you do not immediately know the answer to. Thinking out loud, asking clarifying questions, and working through the problem methodically all signal maturity. Getting stuck is fine. Freezing silently is not.

Ownership and follow-through. Technology services work involves deadlines and client commitments. Interviewers look for evidence that you take responsibility for your work, own your mistakes, and deliver on what you commit to. Prepare examples that show this clearly.

Genuine interest in the role. Candidates who have researched the company and ask thoughtful questions leave a better impression than those going through the motions. Showing that you actually want this job at this company, not just any job anywhere, comes through clearly to experienced interviewers.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research All Generation Tech before any interview. Understand what the company does, the types of clients or projects it handles, and the specific role you are applying for. The question 'Why do you want to work here?' will come up, and a well-informed answer is far more convincing than a vague one.

Customise your resume for each role. With 20 open roles at the company spanning different functions, requirements vary significantly across positions. Read the job description carefully and edit your resume to highlight the experience most relevant to that specific role. A resume written for one job and sent to ten different ones rarely clears the first filter.

Practise your core technical skills. For software roles, revisit data structures, algorithms, and any languages or frameworks mentioned in the job description. For non-technical roles, review domain concepts and prepare case studies from your actual work. Practise explaining your reasoning out loud, not just in your head.

Build three to five strong STAR stories. Choose examples from your career that show problem-solving under pressure, teamwork, handling conflict, and delivering results. Practise telling these stories concisely. You will use them in the managerial and panel rounds, and they make your answers concrete rather than abstract.

Know your compensation numbers before the HR call. Research what people in similar roles commonly report on Glassdoor or AmbitionBox. Go in with a range, not just a single number. Know your current CTC and notice period precisely.

Prepare questions to ask the panel. Questions like 'What does success look like in this role over the first few months?' or 'How is the team structured?' show genuine interest. Having no questions at the end of a round signals that you are not thinking seriously about the role.

Follow up after each round. A short, professional message to your HR contact after an interview keeps you visible and reinforces that you are serious. It takes two minutes and most candidates skip it entirely.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Technology services companies tend to build their culture around delivery, teamwork, and staying current with changing technology. Based on its industry positioning, All Generation Tech is likely to reflect several of these characteristics.

Collaboration and team delivery. Projects in this space are rarely solo efforts. Developers, QA engineers, BAs, and project managers work closely together on shared goals. People who communicate well, share knowledge generously, and do not hoard credit tend to thrive. If you prefer working independently with minimal overlap, this type of environment may require some adjustment.

Client orientation. Even roles that do not interact directly with clients are shaped by client commitments and timelines. Understanding the downstream impact of your work, and caring about quality and delivery, fits the culture well.

Continuous learning. The technology landscape changes fast, and companies in this space value people who invest in staying current. Whether through certifications, self-study, or internal training, showing that you learn proactively is a positive signal both during interviews and on the job.

Structured, process-driven environment. Candidates who have worked at similar companies commonly describe a culture where timelines, processes, and documentation matter. Being organised and reliable tends to be noticed and rewarded.

For a ground-level view of what working at All Generation Tech actually feels like day to day, reading recent employee reviews on Glassdoor or AmbitionBox is worth the time before your interview. It gives you talking points and helps you decide whether the environment is the right fit for you.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does All Generation Tech typically have?

Most candidates go through four to five rounds: resume screening, an HR call, one or two technical rounds, a managerial or panel discussion, and a final HR offer conversation. The exact number depends on the role and your seniority level. Senior or specialist positions tend to have more rounds with deeper evaluation at each stage.

What is the interview experience like for freshers with no work experience?

For freshers, the process is usually shorter and focuses on fundamentals rather than work history. Expect a technical round covering core concepts in your domain (data structures and algorithms for developers, testing basics for QA), followed by an HR discussion. Interviewers for fresher roles prioritise your learning attitude, communication skills, and how confidently you explain your college projects or internship work. Prepare your academic projects well because those become your main examples.

How long does it take to hear back after applying?

At most technology services companies, the process from application to offer commonly spans two to four weeks, though this varies by how many rounds the role involves and how quickly panels get scheduled. With 20 open roles at All Generation Tech right now, the team is actively hiring, which can sometimes speed up timelines. Following up politely with your HR contact after each round is a reasonable way to stay on their radar without being pushy.

What salary should I expect, and how do I negotiate?

Compensation varies by role, city, and years of experience, so it is worth checking Glassdoor or AmbitionBox for All Generation Tech specifically, or looking at what similar technology services companies publicly report for comparable positions. Go into the HR call with a range in mind rather than a single number, and anchor it in current market data rather than gut feel. Negotiating politely and professionally is completely normal and expected.

Is there a coding test or take-home assignment in the process?

For software development and QA roles, a coding test or online assessment is common at technology services companies. The format varies: some roles use a timed platform test, others involve a small take-home task completed over a day or two. Practise on platforms like LeetCode or HackerRank at a difficulty level that matches the role, and pay attention to the job description because it usually signals how technical the evaluation will be.

How do I make sure I do not miss new openings at All Generation Tech?

Checking the company careers page and major job portals regularly helps, but it is easy to miss a listing that goes live and fills within days.
