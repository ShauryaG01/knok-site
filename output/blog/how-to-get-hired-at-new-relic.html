01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

New Relic is a US-based observability and monitoring platform used by engineering teams worldwide to track application performance, infrastructure health, and logs in one place. The company went private in 2024 after a buyout by Francisco Partners and TPG, and it continues to grow its engineering and go-to-market teams globally, including a significant presence in India.

As of early July 2026, New Relic had 72 open roles across engineering, sales, customer success, and technical support. Indian professionals will find the most openings in software engineering (backend, fullstack, SRE/DevOps), sales, and technical account management. Roles span individual contributor positions all the way to senior and staff levels.

What makes New Relic a compelling target is the observability market itself, which is expanding fast as more companies shift to microservices and cloud-native architectures. Engineers here work on distributed systems that handle enormous data volumes daily, and the problems are genuinely complex. If you want depth in how production software behaves at scale, this is a good environment for that.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

New Relic's hiring process typically runs in four to five stages, though the exact format varies by role, level, and team. The following is a general picture based on publicly reported candidate experiences.

Recruiter or HR screen. A brief call covering your background, what you are looking for, and basic role fit. This is the right moment to ask about team structure and compensation range before committing time to the full process.

Hiring manager call. A conversation with the engineering manager or team lead. Expect questions about past projects, how you handle technical trade-offs, and what draws you to observability or this specific domain.

Technical assessment. For engineering roles, this is usually a take-home assignment or a live coding session on a shared editor. Problems tend to focus on data structures, algorithms, and real-world debugging scenarios. Senior roles often include a system design component here or in a dedicated follow-up round.

Virtual panel (on-site equivalent). A set of back-to-back video interviews covering deep technical questions, a debugging or 'break things' scenario that tests how you reason about production systems, and cross-functional conversations with product managers or adjacent team members.

Offer and reference checks. If the panel goes well, HR presents a verbal offer. Reference checks may run in parallel. The full cycle from first contact to offer commonly takes three to six weeks, based on publicly reported timelines.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

New Relic hires people who care about how software runs in production, not just how it is built. A few qualities consistently stand out in candidates who clear the process.

Strong fundamentals. Solid grounding in data structures, algorithms, and distributed systems is expected for engineering roles. New Relic's systems handle very high data volumes, so understanding trade-offs in storage, querying, and scalability is directly relevant to the day-to-day work.

Observability mindset. Even without prior experience with New Relic's own product, familiarity with metrics, logs, traces, APM (application performance monitoring), and SRE practices gives you a real edge. Candidates who naturally ask 'what happens after deploy' rather than just 'does the code work' tend to resonate with interviewers here.

Customer-first thinking. New Relic serves engineering teams at large enterprises. Candidates who can connect technical work to business impact, whether in engineering or in sales roles, stand out from the pool.

Collaboration under ambiguity. Engineering at New Relic is cross-functional. Interviewers will probe how you handle disagreement, share context across teams, and give and receive candid feedback on code and design decisions.

For non-engineering roles. Sales and customer success candidates should be comfortable in technical conversations, understand SaaS metrics, and show experience with consultative selling or managing accounts for technical buyers.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A few things make a real difference when preparing for New Relic interviews.

Use the product before your first round. New Relic offers a free tier. Spending time on it before any interview helps you answer 'why New Relic' questions naturally and demonstrates genuine interest. Try setting up a simple APM trace or a custom dashboard before your hiring manager call.

Brush up on distributed systems. For engineering roles, revisit concepts like consistent hashing, database indexing, time-series data storage, and horizontal scaling. These topics surface regularly in system design rounds at this company specifically.

Practice debugging, not just building. New Relic's technical rounds often include scenarios where something is broken and you need to diagnose it. Practice walking through a problem aloud, forming hypotheses, and narrowing down root causes step by step rather than jumping to solutions.

Prepare behavioural stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for questions about conflict, failure, or cross-team work. Have two or three solid examples ready that show ownership and clear impact from your past roles.

Research the observability market. Knowing how New Relic competes, what its customers care about, and where the industry is heading shows context beyond just a job change. This topic comes up in hiring manager and cross-functional rounds.

Know your numbers from past work. Specific figures from your own experience, such as the traffic scale you handled or the latency gains you delivered, are far more convincing than vague claims about 'large-scale systems'.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

New Relic's culture comes through consistently in how employees and ex-employees describe the company across publicly reported reviews.

Ownership culture. Teams own their services end to end, from writing the code to monitoring it in production using New Relic's own platform. This creates a tight feedback loop between building and operating software. Engineers who like autonomy find this energising. It can feel demanding if you prefer clear handoffs between build and ops functions or a lot of process structure.

Remote-first practices. Since going private in 2024, New Relic has continued with a distributed workforce. India-based team members often collaborate with counterparts in the US and Europe, so async communication skills matter as much as what you ship.

Learning and growth emphasis. The company invests in internal learning programs and encourages engineers to work across the stack. Multiple publicly reported reviews highlight good access to training resources and a culture of knowledge sharing between teams.

Direct feedback norms. New Relic has historically valued transparent, candid communication. This shows up in code reviews, design discussions, and retrospectives. If you prefer a softer feedback style, it is worth asking your interviewers how feedback actually works on their specific team during the interview process itself, before you accept an offer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does New Relic hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

New Relic typically hires experienced professionals for most India-based roles, especially in engineering and sales. Entry-level hiring does happen through specific graduate programs or internship-to-full-time pipelines, but these openings are less common and more competitive. If you are a fresher, target intern or associate-level listings specifically and lead your application with strong fundamentals and any project work involving cloud tools, monitoring, or distributed systems.

What salary can I expect at New Relic in India?

Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest software engineering compensation in India at New Relic ranges from around 20 LPA for mid-level roles to 40 LPA or above at senior and staff levels, though sample sizes on these platforms are small and actual offers vary by team, negotiation, and location. The recruiter screen is the right moment to ask for a confirmed band for your specific role before investing time in the full process.

How important is prior observability experience for engineering roles?

You do not need prior experience specifically with New Relic's product, but familiarity with APM tools, logging pipelines, or distributed tracing is a genuine advantage. Interviewers care about whether you think in terms of production systems, not just development. If you have used Prometheus, Grafana, Datadog, or even basic AWS CloudWatch in past roles, highlight that clearly in your resume and bring it up early in the recruiter screen.

Is the interview process the same for remote and Hyderabad-based roles?

The interview structure is broadly similar for both, as New Relic conducts all rounds virtually regardless of where the role is based. The difference comes after the offer: office-based roles may have expectations around periodic in-person attendance. Ask your recruiter about the specific team's norms on in-office days before you reach the offer stage, not after.

How long does it take to hear back after applying?

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, response times after applying range from about one week to over a month, depending on how urgently the role is being filled and the volume of applications received. If you apply and hear nothing after two weeks, a polite follow-up to the recruiter on LinkedIn is a reasonable next step. A referral from a current New Relic employee significantly speeds up the initial screening stage.

Can I reapply if I fail one of the rounds?

New Relic generally allows candidates to reapply after a waiting period, commonly cited by candidates as six months to a year after a rejection. If you did not clear a technical round, ask the recruiter for feedback, though they may not always share specifics. Use the gap to close the gaps that likely caused the rejection, build something that shows your growth, and reapply with concrete evidence of what has changed since your last attempt.
