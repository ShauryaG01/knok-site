02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect what candidates report being asked in zippee QA interviews. The focus is typically on delivery-specific scenarios, automation thinking, and how you handle pressure.

  1. Walk me through how you would write a test plan for a new 'Track My Order' feature in the zippee app.

  10. How would you set up a CI/CD test pipeline for a mobile app like zippee?
  11. A developer tells you the bug you raised is 'working as designed.' How do you handle that situation?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A critical bug is live in production. Customers are seeing incorrect order totals at checkout. Walk us through your steps.

*Situation:* In my previous role at a retail-tech company, we had a similar incident where a discount calculation error was applying coupons incorrectly during a sale event.

*Task:* I needed to quickly assess the scope, communicate to stakeholders, and help the team move toward a fix without adding more confusion to an already stressful situation.

*Action:* I first reproduced the bug on a test device to confirm it was real and consistent. I logged a detailed bug report immediately, with steps to reproduce, screenshots, and affected order IDs. I then checked whether the issue was limited to one payment method or universal, to give the dev team a focused starting point. I tagged it P0 and escalated to the engineering lead and product manager within minutes. While the dev team investigated, I ran a quick smoke test on related checkout flows to map the blast radius.

*Result:* The root cause turned out to be an API response being cached incorrectly. It was patched and deployed the same day. My upfront scoping reduced back-and-forth and helped the team isolate the fix faster.

---

Q: How do you decide what to automate versus what to test manually?

*Situation:* At a previous company, our QA team was small but the release cycle was weekly. The manual regression suite was too large to run reliably before each release.

*Task:* I was asked to build an automation strategy that would give the team confidence without burning bandwidth on brittle scripts.

*Action:* I applied a straightforward filter: automate tests that are high-frequency, stable, and regression-prone (login flows, checkout, order placement). Keep manual testing for exploratory work, new features with changing requirements, and UI-heavy flows that break with minor design updates. I also factored in maintenance cost, because complex scripts that break every sprint add more work than they save.

*Result:* We automated the core regression suite over two sprints, cutting manual regression time significantly. The team could then focus manual effort on new feature testing, which is where the most impactful bugs were being found.

---

Q: A developer says your bug is 'by design.' How do you handle it?

*Situation:* I once raised a bug where the app allowed users to place an order for an out-of-stock item with no warning. The developer closed it because the product manager had not explicitly specified a warning message in the requirements.

*Task:* I needed to advocate for the right user experience while keeping the working relationship with the developer intact.

*Action:* I came back with concrete user impact: a user placing an order only to have it cancelled later damages trust and increases support tickets. I pulled in the product manager to clarify intent and framed it not as 'the code is wrong' but as 'the requirement may have a gap.' We had a short three-way discussion and agreed it was an unintentional oversight.

*Result:* The fix was scoped as a small story and shipped in the next sprint. After that, I made it a habit to flag such gaps during requirement review, before any code was written.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For scenario and process questions, use STAR. Structure your answer as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (one to two sentences each). Spend most of your answer on the Action and be specific about the tools, steps, and decisions you made. End with a clear, honest Result.

For 'how would you test X' questions, use a structured test design approach:

  1. Clarify scope: what does 'done' look like for this feature?
  2. Identify test types needed: functional, edge-case, negative, performance, security.
  3. List the key user journeys or API contracts to validate.
  4. Note what test data or environment setup is required.
  5. State what you would automate and why.

This approach shows methodical thinking, which is exactly what QA interviewers reward.

For conflict or disagreement questions: Lead with impact on the user or business, not with 'I was right.' Show that you bring evidence (logs, user reports, test results) rather than opinions, and that you involve the right stakeholders calmly and constructively.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Zippee interviewers are typically looking for a few specific qualities in QA candidates.

Ownership mindset. Quick commerce moves fast. Interviewers want to see that you treat quality as your personal responsibility, not just a checklist before sign-off.

Testing for real delivery scenarios. Generic test cases for a login form will not impress here. Show that you think about what could go wrong in a delivery context: GPS inaccuracies, payment gateway timeouts, inventory sync delays, peak-load failures during sale events.

Automation fluency. You do not need to be a full-stack developer, but candidates report that familiarity with at least one automation framework (Selenium, Appium, Pytest, RestAssured) and basic API testing with Postman is expected at mid and senior levels.

Communication and collaboration. QA sits at the intersection of product, engineering, and operations. Interviewers look for people who can explain a bug clearly to a non-technical stakeholder and push back constructively on a developer without creating friction.

Speed without sloppiness. A company doing instant delivery expects its QA team to work quickly. Show that you can triage, prioritise, and release with confidence under pressure.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Fundamentals and company context

Review core QA concepts: test types (unit, integration, system, UAT), SDLC and STLC, bug life cycle, and test case design techniques like boundary value analysis and equivalence partitioning. Download the zippee app, place a test order, and map out the key user journeys you would need to test. Think about what could break at each step.

Week 2: Technical and automation prep

Practice writing test cases for API endpoints using Postman (order placement, delivery tracking, payment confirmation). If you list Selenium or Appium on your resume, revise the basics so you can talk through a real script you have written. Brush up on SQL queries for test data validation and review how CI/CD pipelines work and where QA fits in.

Week 3: Mock interviews and behavioural prep

Practice answering scenario questions using the STAR format. Prepare at least one story for each of these themes: a production bug you caught or reported, a time you improved a testing process, and a conflict with a developer you resolved well. Record yourself or run a mock session with a peer to check your clarity and pacing.

Before the interview

Check zippee's recent product updates or news so you can reference current context in your answers. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer, for example about how QA is involved in the planning stage, the current state of automation coverage, or what a typical release cycle looks like.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Writing vague test cases. Saying 'I would test the checkout flow' is not enough. Interviewers want specific scenarios: what happens if a coupon expires mid-session, or if the delivery pin-code changes after the cart is loaded?

Ignoring non-functional testing. Many candidates focus only on functional bugs. For a delivery platform, performance under load, app behaviour on a slow mobile network, and security of payment data are equally important. Mention these even if not asked directly.

Treating bugs as failures. Finding bugs is the job, not a problem. Avoid framing your experience as 'luckily there were not many bugs.' Talk about bugs you found, reported clearly, and helped fix.

Over-claiming automation skills. If you say you 'own the automation framework' but cannot explain a Page Object Model or write a basic test script on the spot, it becomes a red flag. Be accurate about your level and frame any gaps as areas you are actively building.

Not asking any questions. Candidates who ask nothing come across as disengaged. Prepare questions that show genuine curiosity about how QA works at zippee, not just questions about salary or joining date.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the zippee QA interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves multiple rounds, often starting with an HR screen, followed by a technical round covering manual testing concepts and sometimes a live test-case writing exercise, then a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager. The exact number varies by role level and team. Treat each round as a chance to show both your technical depth and your communication style.

Does zippee ask live coding questions in QA interviews?

Candidates at mid and senior levels report that automation-related tasks sometimes appear, such as writing a basic Selenium script or explaining how you would structure a test framework. Pure algorithm-style coding questions are less commonly reported for QA roles. Having basic scripting comfort in Python or Java is helpful, so focus on writing clear and maintainable test code rather than preparing for competitive programming problems.

What salary can I expect for a QA Engineer role at zippee?

Based on knok jobradar data, QA Engineer salaries in India range from 4-9 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 9-17 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 17-30 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 28-45+ LPA at lead level. Actual offers depend on your specific experience, the team you join, and how you negotiate. For verified compensation data at specific companies, checking Glassdoor or levels.fyi gives a more precise picture.

Is there a take-home assignment in the zippee QA interview?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home task, typically involving writing test cases for a feature or identifying bugs in a sample application. Not every hiring track includes this, so ask the recruiter during the HR call what the full process looks like for your specific role. If a take-home is assigned, treat it like real work: write clear bug reports, organise your test cases logically, and add a short note explaining your thinking and coverage decisions.
