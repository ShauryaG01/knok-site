01 Overview

Overview

Grab is Southeast Asia's leading superapp, covering ride-hailing, food delivery, digital payments, and financial services. Its India engineering hubs are active hiring centres, and Technical Program Manager roles sit at the heart of how Grab ships large, cross-functional programs across these product lines.

A TPM at Grab owns end-to-end delivery of complex, cross-functional engineering programs, from planning and dependency management to shipping at scale. The interview process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, two to four technical and behavioural rounds with senior engineers, TPMs, and product managers, and a final leadership discussion. Candidates report that Grab's values of Heart, Hunger, and Honour come up in nearly every round, alongside technical depth and program management rigour.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates report most frequently at Grab TPM interviews:

  1. Walk me through a program you drove end-to-end at scale. What was your exact role, and what did you ship?
  2. How do you manage dependencies across five or more engineering teams with competing priorities?
  3. Describe a time a project went off track. How did you spot the risk early, and what did you do to recover?
  4. How do you influence engineers and product managers who do not report to you?
  5. Tell me about a time you had to push back on a senior stakeholder's requirement. How did you handle it?
  6. Grab operates across multiple Southeast Asian markets. How have you handled programs with regional complexity, localisation needs, or varied infrastructure constraints?
  7. How do you build and maintain a program roadmap when requirements keep changing mid-execution?
  8. Describe a situation where you had to make a key decision with incomplete data. What was your process?
  9. How do you define and track success metrics for a large engineering program?
  10. Tell me about a time you improved engineering team velocity or delivery process without holding direct management authority.
  11. Grab's superapp model means a single feature can touch payments, maps, identity, and logistics at once. How do you manage integration risk across such interconnected systems?
  12. How do you handle a situation where two senior engineering leaders disagree on a technical approach that is blocking your program?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a program you drove end-to-end at scale.

*Situation:* Our payments team was building a new checkout flow requiring integration with three backend services, a fraud detection vendor, and the mobile platform team, all in parallel.

*Task:* I was the TPM responsible for shipping the feature across Android, iOS, and web within a single quarter.

*Action:* I created a dependency map on day one and ran a weekly cross-team sync with clear owners and logged blockers. When the fraud vendor delayed their API documentation by two weeks, I worked with engineering leads to build a stub layer so parallel development could continue without waiting.

*Result:* We shipped on time with zero P0 issues in the first month. The product team publicly reported the rollout as exceeding their adoption target.

---

Q: Describe a time a project went off track. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* A platform migration I was managing fell three weeks behind because a third-party data provider changed their schema without notice.

*Task:* I needed to recover the schedule and keep leadership informed without creating panic or losing team trust.

*Action:* I called an immediate working session with the affected team, mapped what was truly blocked versus what could proceed in parallel, and re-sequenced work to recover two of the three lost weeks. I sent a concise weekly update to leadership with a clear recovery plan and honest risk flags.

*Result:* We launched one week later than originally planned. The post-mortem led to a new schema-change alert process that two other teams later adopted.

---

Q: How do you influence engineers and product managers who do not report to you?

*Situation:* I needed a senior infrastructure engineer to prioritise a capacity upgrade for my program, but her manager had already committed her time to a different team for that sprint.

*Task:* I had to get the work done without escalating unnecessarily or damaging the working relationship.

*Action:* I met with her directly to understand her constraints first, then worked with my engineering lead to carve the task into a smaller piece she could fit into a lower-demand sprint. I also volunteered to take over the documentation work she would normally own, to free up her time.

*Result:* The upgrade shipped in time. She later mentioned me as a collaborative partner in a team retro. Influence without authority means making it easy for the other person to say yes.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences) so most of your airtime is on Action and Result. Grab interviewers are listening for specifics, not summaries.

For program management process questions, lead with your approach, then immediately anchor it with a real example. If asked how you track risks, say 'I maintain a risk register updated weekly with owner and mitigation,' and then illustrate with a story. Abstract frameworks without examples read as theoretical.

For 'influence without authority' questions, Grab interviewers specifically look for evidence that you listened first and found a shared goal. Open with how you understood the other person's constraints before explaining what you did. This maps directly to Grab's Heart value.

For 'metrics and success' questions, structure your answer across three layers: input metrics (what teams control), output metrics (what ships), and outcome metrics (what users experience). This kind of systems thinking is what Grab expects of senior TPMs.

For ambiguity questions, name the information you did not have, explain how you decided what was 'good enough to proceed,' and show you documented your assumptions and revisited them. Grab operates in fast-moving markets, so showing you can move forward without perfect clarity is a strong positive signal.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Grab TPM interviewers are looking for three things above all.

Proven program delivery. You have shipped complex, multi-team programs and can talk about them with specifics, not generalities. Vague answers like 'we drove alignment' without naming what shipped, when, and to what outcome will not score well.

Structured communication. TPMs at Grab write documents for engineers, present to VPs, and run cross-team syncs all in the same week. Interviewers are watching how you organise your answers, how clearly you explain tradeoffs, and whether you tailor your language to your audience.

Cultural alignment. Grab's stated values of Heart (care for teammates and users), Hunger (bias for action and growth), and Honour (integrity and accountability) are not decorative. Candidates report that interviewers probe these explicitly and also watch for them in how you describe past behaviour toward colleagues and stakeholders.

Interviewers also pay close attention to how you handle ambiguity. Grab's superapp model means TPMs regularly work across domains they did not grow up in, such as mapping, fintech, and logistics. Showing that you ask good questions, pull in the right people, and move forward without waiting for perfect clarity is a strong positive signal.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Research and story-building

Study Grab's product surface: superapp, GrabPay, GrabFood, GrabMaps, and financial services. Read their engineering blog and any publicly reported product launches or engineering challenges. Write out five to six STAR stories covering at minimum: a large cross-team program you personally owned, a missed or recovered deadline, a stakeholder conflict you navigated, a decision made under uncertainty, and a process improvement you initiated.

Week 2: Practice and depth

Run mock interviews with a peer, or record yourself answering the 12 questions listed above. Practice trimming each answer to under three minutes while keeping the specifics. Review your fluency with program management tools: roadmaps, risk registers, OKRs, and dependency graphs. Be ready to describe how you have used each, not just that you know what they are.

Week 3: Final polish

Research Grab's current priorities through their investor updates and recent press coverage. Prepare two to three questions per interviewer that show genuine curiosity about their team's programs and challenges. Do a final pass on your resume to confirm every program listed has a clear, stated outcome. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about your own contribution. Saying 'we shipped X' without explaining what you personally drove will cost you. Interviewers need to know your specific role, not the team's collective effort.

Skipping the result. Many candidates give strong Action answers and then forget to state the outcome. Always close the loop: what shipped, what metric moved, what happened next.

Treating Grab like any other tech company. Grab operates across Southeast Asian markets with varied infrastructure, regulations, and user behaviours. Showing no awareness of this signals insufficient preparation.

Over-engineering process answers. TPMs who describe elaborate frameworks with no examples come across as theoretical. Ground every framework in a real story from your own experience.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask nothing leave interviewers with no signal about curiosity or cultural fit. Prepare at least two thoughtful questions per round, grounded in research you have actually done.

Underestimating the cultural dimension. Grab's Heart, Hunger, and Honour values are evaluated seriously. Having no story ready for each value is a visible gap.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Grab TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager call, and two to four technical and behavioural rounds with senior ICs, TPMs, and product managers. A final round with a director or VP is common for senior-level roles. The total process typically spans three to five weeks, though timelines vary by team and location.

Does Grab ask coding questions for TPM roles?

Typically, no. TPM interviews at Grab are not expected to include live coding. However, candidates report that technical system design discussions do come up, particularly around how you would identify risks in a large integration or reason about distributed system tradeoffs. Strong engineering fluency helps even if you are not writing code.

How important is Southeast Asia market knowledge for a Grab TPM role?

Very important. Grab's business spans diverse markets from Indonesia to Singapore, and interviewers commonly probe for awareness of regional complexity, including payment localisation, regulatory variation, and low-bandwidth user environments. You do not need to be a regional expert, but showing genuine curiosity and prior exposure to multi-market programs is a meaningful advantage.

What salary can I expect as a TPM at Grab in Bangalore?

Grab does not publicly publish India salary bands. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi show a range that varies by level and years of experience, but treat those as reference points rather than guarantees. Come into salary discussions with your current compensation, the level you are interviewing for, and a clear view of the total package including equity and benefits.

How does Grab evaluate for culture fit?

Grab's stated values are Heart, Hunger, and Honour. In practice, candidates report that interviewers look for people who are proactive (Hunger), genuinely care about teammates and users (Heart), and are transparent when things go wrong (Honour). Preparing one STAR story that illustrates each value is a practical way to be ready for this dimension of the process.

Is it worth applying if I have never worked at a superapp or consumer internet company?

Yes. Candidates from enterprise software, fintech, and infrastructure backgrounds do get hired at Grab. The key is demonstrating that your program management skills transfer: multi-team coordination, stakeholder management, risk identification, and delivering to real users under pressure. Frame your experience in terms of complexity and scale, and show that you understand what makes consumer product delivery different from what you have done before.
