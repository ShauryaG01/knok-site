01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Intangles is a Pune-based fleet intelligence company that applies AI, IoT, and data analytics to help transport and logistics businesses run their fleets smarter. From predictive maintenance alerts to driver behaviour scoring, the product sits at the crossroads of hardware, software, and real-time data. As of mid-2026, Intangles has 26 open roles across engineering, data, product, sales, and customer success.

The company is in an active growth phase, which is good news for job seekers. Teams are lean, so new hires tend to pick up meaningful work quickly rather than spending months shadowing seniors. If you are drawn to building things with a visible, physical-world impact, and you enjoy working in a startup-style environment where priorities can shift fast, Intangles is worth targeting seriously.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Candidates at Intangles typically go through four to five stages, though the exact sequence can vary by role and team. Here is what most applicants report experiencing.

Stage 1: Application and resume shortlist
Intangles posts on job boards and its own careers page. A recruiter or HR team member reviews resumes for relevant domain experience, particularly in IoT, embedded systems, data engineering, or SaaS sales depending on the role. Tailoring your resume to the specific job description improves your shortlist chances significantly.

Stage 2: HR screening call
If shortlisted, you will typically have a brief call with someone from HR or talent acquisition. Expect questions about your current role, notice period, location preference, and salary expectations. This is also your chance to ask about team size, reporting structure, and work-from-office expectations.

Stage 3: Technical assessment or take-home task
For engineering and data roles, a coding or written assessment usually follows. This may be an online test covering data structures, SQL, or domain-specific problems such as time-series data handling or embedded C. Product and sales roles may skip this stage entirely.

Stage 4: Technical interview (one or two rounds)
This is the most substantive part of the process. A senior engineer or team lead goes deeper into your past projects, system design thinking, and problem-solving approach. For data roles, expect questions on ML pipelines, feature engineering, and model deployment. For backend roles, expect questions on APIs, scalability, and database design. Candidates report that interviewers appreciate practical, real-world reasoning over textbook answers.

Stage 5: Managerial or leadership round
Before an offer, many candidates meet a manager or a founder-level person. This round focuses on motivation, how you handle ambiguity, and whether your working style fits the team. Interviewers often ask why you want to work in fleet tech specifically, so have a genuine answer ready.

The total timeline from application to offer is commonly reported as a few weeks, though it can stretch longer for senior or specialised roles.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Intangles hires for a specific mix of skills and mindset. The technical bar is real, but so is the culture fit check.

Relevant domain knowledge is a plus but not always required. Experience with IoT platforms, vehicle telematics, logistics software, or industrial data systems will make your profile stand out. That said, strong generalist engineers and data professionals who can pick up domain context quickly are also hired regularly.

Hands-on problem solving over theoretical knowledge. Intangles builds products that interact with physical hardware in the field, where edge cases are common and real-time reliability matters. Interviewers want to see that you can think through failure modes and arrive at pragmatic solutions, not just describe them.

Comfort with ambiguity. As a growth-stage company, Intangles does not always have fully defined requirements. Candidates who have thrived in similar environments, and can point to concrete examples, tend to do well in interviews.

Communication and ownership. Because teams are small, there is no room to hand off problems to someone else. Interviewers look for people who take end-to-end ownership of their work and can communicate progress clearly to non-technical stakeholders like fleet operators or sales teams.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product deeply before any interview. Spend time understanding what Intangles actually does: how fleet telematics works, what problems fleet operators face every day, and how AI or IoT addresses those problems. If you have worked near similar technology (GPS tracking, CAN bus data, predictive maintenance), bring it up specifically with examples.

Brush up on your core fundamentals. For engineering roles, revise data structures, SQL queries, and system design basics. For data roles, revisit concepts like time-series analysis, anomaly detection, and model evaluation metrics. The assessment is practical in nature, not trick-question-heavy.

Prepare two or three strong project stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to talk about past work where you solved a hard technical or business problem. Quantify your impact wherever the data supports it.

Have good questions ready for each round. Ask about the team's current technical challenges, how the product roadmap is evolving, and what a successful first six months looks like in the role. This signals genuine interest in the work, not just the job title.

Sort your documents early. Have offer letters, recent payslips, and relieving letters from previous employers ready before you get an offer. Startups in growth mode often move fast once they decide to hire, and delays on your end can stall the process or cause them to move to the next candidate.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Intangles has the culture of a product-focused startup with a clear real-world mission: making road transport safer, more efficient, and more data-driven. A few things stand out about the working environment.

Ownership culture. Employees are expected to own problems end-to-end rather than passing work across team boundaries. This suits people who prefer autonomy and dislike bureaucratic handoffs. If you are used to clearly scoped tickets and a handover process, adjusting to this style will take some time.

Fast feedback loops. Because the product connects to real vehicles and fleets, results are often visible quickly. Engineers can see whether a new model or feature is working in near-real-time, which many find genuinely motivating compared to building features that are hard to measure.

Mission alignment matters. Intangles is not just building SaaS for its own sake. The work connects to road safety, fuel efficiency, and logistics costs, all of which are significant issues in the Indian economy. Candidates who are excited by that purpose tend to integrate better with existing teams.

Growth-stage dynamics. Expect a degree of flux: priorities shift, processes are still being built, and wearing multiple hats is normal. This is a strength or a challenge depending on what you are looking for in a role. Going in with clear eyes about this sets the right expectations.

Collaboration across functions. Given the product complexity (hardware, firmware, cloud, analytics, customer success), teams communicate across functional lines regularly. Comfort with cross-functional work is valued and often tested during the interview process.
