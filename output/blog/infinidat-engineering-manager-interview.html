03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a major technical decision you led in an infrastructure or storage product.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a financial services client was running nightly backups on a legacy SAN-based system. The backup window was stretching well beyond their contracted maintenance period, which was becoming a serious contract risk.

*Task:* As Engineering Manager, I was responsible for evaluating our options and recommending a path forward without disrupting live operations.

*Action:* I formed a small working group of engineers, ran a proof-of-concept comparing two alternative architectures, and documented trade-offs across cost, risk, and recovery time. I then presented a phased migration plan to the VP of Engineering and the client's infrastructure lead, with clear rollback steps at each phase.

*Result:* We cut the backup window significantly. The client renewed their contract, and the migration playbook we created was later adapted for two more enterprise accounts.

---

Q: How do you handle it when your team disagrees with a roadmap decision?

*Situation:* My team was asked to deprioritise a reliability improvement they had flagged as a risk, in favour of delivering a new feature for an upcoming industry event.

*Task:* I needed to address the team's concerns honestly without undermining a business decision already made at the VP level.

*Action:* I ran a team session where we documented the risks in a shared risk register with clear severity ratings. I escalated a concise summary to the VP of Engineering with a proposed compromise: one engineer would continue the reliability work as part-time focus while the rest of the team moved to the feature. I also committed to revisiting it in the next sprint planning cycle.

*Result:* The feature shipped on time for the event. The reliability fix was completed two sprints later. The team felt heard, and we avoided what could have been a serious incident down the line.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you managed underperformance on your team.

*Situation:* A senior engineer on my team had been consistently missing sprint commitments and the quality of their code reviews had declined over two quarters.

*Task:* I had to address this directly and fairly, while making sure the rest of the team was not indefinitely carrying the shortfall.

*Action:* I had a direct one-on-one conversation first, without assumptions. I discovered they were dealing with significant personal stress outside work. I worked with our HR partner to put together a structured improvement plan with fortnightly check-ins and clear milestones. I also paired them with a supportive senior peer to help them work through the technical backlog.

*Result:* Within a couple of months, their delivery was back to standard. They went on to lead a critical data migration module later that year. The structured approach also gave me a template I reused with another team member the following year.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions (the majority of rounds): Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation brief (two or three sentences), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what the team did collectively), and always close with a measurable or observable Result.

For 'how would you approach' questions: Use a layered structure. Start by saying what information you would gather first (context before conclusions). Then walk through your options with trade-offs. End with how you would decide and how you would communicate the decision. INFINIDAT interviewers typically value candidates who show they do not jump to conclusions in complex technical environments.

For technical depth questions: Even as a manager, you are expected to have an informed opinion on engineering decisions. Use a 'problem, options, recommendation' structure. State the problem clearly, name two or three realistic approaches with their trade-offs, then state your recommendation and why. You do not need to know INFINIDAT's internal stack, but candidates report that showing familiarity with storage concepts such as RAID, replication, data tiering, and NVMe strengthens your credibility.

For cross-functional or stakeholder questions: Show that you can hold two things at once: your team's needs and the business reality. A good answer names the stakeholder, the tension, and the specific step you took to bridge it, not just 'I communicated clearly.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

INFINIDAT builds enterprise storage that customers depend on for mission-critical workloads. Interviewers are looking for Engineering Managers who can lead in that environment specifically. Based on patterns candidates report, here is what tends to matter most:

Technical credibility without micromanagement. You should be able to engage meaningfully in architectural discussions and call out risks, but your examples should show you trusted your team to execute rather than doing the work yourself.

Experience with reliability and high-availability systems. Even if you have not worked in storage specifically, experience leading teams that owned uptime, SLAs, or on-call processes is valued. Interviewers want to know you understand the cost of downtime.

Clear people management instincts. Expect questions on hiring, performance management, and team growth. Vague answers like 'I foster a collaborative culture' do not land well. Use specific examples with real stakes.

Cross-functional maturity. INFINIDAT's Engineering Managers typically work with product, sales engineering, and customer success. Show that you can represent your team's constraints clearly to non-technical stakeholders without creating friction.

Bias toward outcomes, not activity. Interviewers respond well to answers that frame success in terms of what changed for the customer, the product, or the team, not just what process you ran.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before your interview:

Research INFINIDAT's product line, specifically InfiniBox and InfiniGuard. Read their public case studies and any recent press about enterprise storage trends such as flash adoption, AI storage demands, and ransomware protection. You do not need to memorise specs, but you should be able to discuss why storage performance and reliability matter to enterprise customers in plain terms.

Prepare eight to ten STAR stories. Cover these themes at minimum: a major technical decision you led, a time you handled a high-severity incident, a difficult people management situation, a time you pushed back on a business decision, and a time you delivered under pressure. Each story should have a clear result you can state in one or two sentences.

Practice out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself or do mock interviews with a peer. INFINIDAT interviews are typically conversational, and candidates who rehearse only silently often find they ramble when speaking in the actual round.

One week before: Review the job description line by line and map each responsibility to one of your prepared stories. Note any gaps and prepare a bridging answer. For example: 'I have not worked directly in storage, but here is the closest relevant experience I can draw on.'

Day before: Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for each interviewer. Good questions at INFINIDAT typically focus on engineering culture, how the team measures success, or how they handle the tension between innovation and stability in a mature enterprise product.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague on the technical side. Engineering Manager roles at a storage product company like INFINIDAT require you to engage with technical trade-offs. Answers like 'I let my engineers decide the technical approach' without any follow-up on how you guided or evaluated that decision come across as disengaged.

Centering yourself instead of the team. Using 'I' throughout your answers is fine for your own decisions, but your best examples will also show how you enabled, coached, or cleared the path for your team. Interviewers want to see multiplier behaviour.

No measurable results. 'The project was a success' is a weak close. Even approximate outcomes such as reduced time, improved reliability, or team growth make your stories credible. If you genuinely cannot share specifics, explain why and describe the qualitative outcome clearly.

Underpreparing on people management questions. Many candidates who come from a strong IC background spend all their prep time on technical scenarios. INFINIDAT typically dedicates significant interview time to leadership and team management, so balance your preparation across both areas.

Asking no questions, or only asking about compensation. Not asking questions signals low interest. Asking only about salary signals you have not thought about the role itself. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team, the product roadmap, or the engineering challenges ahead.

How many rounds does the INFINIDAT Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes three to five rounds. These commonly cover an initial recruiter or HR screening, one or two technical or system design discussions, and one or more leadership and behavioral rounds with senior managers or directors. A final round with a hiring director or VP is also commonly reported, though the exact structure can vary by team, so it is worth asking your recruiter for the expected format after your first call.

Do I need a background specifically in storage technology to be a strong candidate?

Not necessarily, but candidates report that familiarity with storage concepts such as replication, RAID, NVMe, and data tiering helps establish credibility in technical discussions. If your background is in a related area like infrastructure, networking, or systems software, you can bridge the gap by studying INFINIDAT's public product documentation. Being ready to discuss how your experience applies to high-availability, enterprise-grade systems typically goes a long way.

Based on knok jobradar data for Engineering Manager roles in India, Manager-level positions typically fall in the 35-60 LPA range, Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range, and Director-level roles at 90-150+ LPA. How much coding or system design should I expect in an EM interview at INFINIDAT?

Candidates report that Engineering Manager interviews at INFINIDAT focus more on system design and architectural discussion than on live coding. You are unlikely to be asked to write code on a whiteboard, but you may be asked to walk through a design decision, evaluate a trade-off, or discuss how you would approach a technical problem at a high level. Brushing up on distributed systems basics and storage architecture concepts is more useful than practicing algorithm-heavy problems.

How is INFINIDAT different from other storage companies to prepare for?

INFINIDAT positions itself on reliability and performance at petabyte scale, serving large enterprises where downtime has real financial consequences. This means interviewers tend to probe deeply on how you handle incidents, how you build resilient teams, and how you manage the pressure of enterprise customer commitments. Candidates who can speak specifically to high-availability environments and enterprise customer dynamics tend to stand out compared to those with only startup or SMB experience.
