01 Overview

Overview

Quicksell is a B2B commerce platform that helps businesses sell through WhatsApp catalogs, manage orders, and run digital storefronts. As of mid-2026, Quicksell has 10 open roles, with Solutions Engineer being a key hire as the company scales its enterprise client base.

A Solutions Engineer at Quicksell sits between sales and engineering. You run product demos, design and execute proof-of-concepts for enterprise clients, support API integrations, and act as the technical voice of the customer inside the company. Candidates report that the interview process typically includes an initial HR or recruiter screen, one or two technical or product-focused rounds, and a final discussion with a senior stakeholder or founder.

The role rewards people who can simplify complexity, build trust with non-technical buyers, and move quickly when a client has a problem. If you come from a SaaS sales engineering, pre-sales, or customer success background with some API literacy, you are in a strong position.

Most Asked Questions

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a deal you helped save. What was your role and what made the difference?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a retail client was a few weeks into a pilot and told their account manager they were planning to drop us for a competitor. The account manager looped me in immediately.

*Task:* I owned the technical relationship and needed to understand why confidence had dropped before the client made a final decision.

*Action:* I set up a call with their IT lead and the store manager the same day. I discovered that webhook events were firing out of order, causing their inventory dashboard to show stale data. I replicated the issue in our sandbox, wrote a workaround using a queue, and walked their developer through the fix on a screen-share before the end of that week. I also built a short summary doc they could share internally to show their leadership team that the issue was resolved.

*Result:* The client stayed, completed the pilot, and signed a full contract the following month. The account manager told me it was the fastest technical turnaround she had seen.

---

Q: How do you structure a proof-of-concept for an enterprise client?

*Situation:* A large FMCG distributor wanted to test catalog-based ordering across a small set of their wholesale buyers before committing to a full rollout.

*Task:* I was asked to design and run a structured pilot with clear success criteria agreed upfront.

*Action:* I started with a scoping call to agree on measurable success criteria with the client's project lead: order volume through the catalog, reduction in back-and-forth for order confirmation, and buyer adoption rate. I set a fixed pilot window, defined weekly check-ins, and created a shared tracker both sides could see. When a payment integration issue surfaced in the first week, I escalated it internally and kept the client updated with a daily one-line status note rather than waiting for the weekly call.

*Result:* Most of the success metrics were exceeded by the end of the pilot, and the client used our shared tracker as the basis for their internal business case to get budget approved.

---

Q: How do you explain a REST API or webhook to a business owner who has never heard those terms?

*Situation:* A mid-market clothing brand wanted to connect orders to their existing ERP, but their decision-maker was the owner, not a technical person.

*Task:* I needed to get her comfortable enough to approve the integration budget without overwhelming her with jargon.

*Action:* I used an analogy: 'Think of the API like a waiter. Your ERP is the kitchen, Quicksell is the table. The waiter takes the order from the table and brings it to the kitchen without the chef and the customer ever needing to talk directly. A webhook is like the kitchen bell, it tells the waiter the food is ready.' I then showed her a one-page diagram with her brand's logo and the Quicksell logo connected by an arrow labelled 'orders flow here automatically.' I kept the technical spec in a separate document for her IT vendor.

*Result:* She approved the integration in the same meeting and told me it was the first time a vendor had explained it in a way that made sense to her.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioural question. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief and spend most of your time on Action and Result. Interviewers at product-led startups like Quicksell care most about what you personally did and what changed because of it.

For technical explanation questions, use the Analogy-then-Diagram method. Start with a real-world analogy your listener already understands, then offer to show a simple diagram or walk through a screen-share. This signals you can translate between business and engineering worlds, which is the core of a Solutions Engineer's job.

For deal or client scenarios, lead with the customer's problem, not your process. Instead of saying 'I followed our POC framework,' say 'The client needed to show their CFO that order errors would drop before they could get budget.' Interviewers want to see customer empathy first, methodology second.

For product gap questions, use the Redirect framework. Acknowledge the gap honestly, pivot to what Quicksell does exceptionally well for this client's specific need, and offer a workaround or roadmap context if you have it. Never dismiss the competitor or oversell a feature that does not exist yet.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without over-engineering. You do not need to be a full-stack developer, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, setting up a basic webhook, and debugging a failed integration call. Candidates report that interviewers ask about real technical problems, not whiteboard algorithms.

Customer empathy. Quicksell sells to business owners and operations teams who are not always technical. Interviewers want evidence that you can build trust with a non-technical buyer and translate their pain into something engineering can act on.

Speed and ownership. As a startup, Quicksell moves quickly. Stories that show you diagnosed a problem fast, took ownership without being asked, and kept the client informed will land better than polished but slow processes.

Product thinking. Solutions Engineers at early-stage companies often feed product decisions. Interviewers appreciate candidates who can explain what a client asked for, what the underlying need actually was, and how they communicated that to the product team.

Clear, jargon-free communication. If you use terms like 'webhook' or 'payload' in an answer, define them briefly. This shows you can adjust your language for the audience, which is exactly what the job requires every day.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Learn Quicksell's product deeply. Sign up for a free or trial account if available, build a sample catalog, and walk through the buyer and seller flows. Note where integrations happen and what a typical enterprise client would need to customise.

Step 2: Prepare a set of key client stories. Pick situations where you ran a technical demo, diagnosed an integration issue, handled a difficult client conversation, collaborated with product or engineering, and saved or expanded a deal. Map each to a STAR structure before your interview.

Step 3: Brush up on API and webhook fundamentals. You should be able to explain what a REST API call does, what a webhook is, and how to read a basic JSON response. Practise explaining these out loud to a friend who is not technical.

Step 4: Research Quicksell's market. Know who their typical customers are (D2C brands, FMCG distributors, retail chains), who their competitors are, and what problems WhatsApp commerce solves versus traditional e-commerce. Indian business news and LinkedIn company pages are good starting points.

Step 5: Prepare smart questions to ask. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest and help you evaluate the role. Good ones include: 'What does success look like in the first few months for someone in this role?' and 'How does the Solutions Engineering team currently hand off feedback to the product team?'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the business context. Many candidates jump straight into technical detail without first connecting it to the customer's business problem. Always frame your answer around what the client was trying to achieve commercially.

Being vague about your personal contribution. In STAR answers, replace 'we' with 'I' wherever you were the one driving the action. Interviewers cannot assess you on what your team did.

Over-promising on product gaps. When a prospect asks about a missing feature, saying 'that is on the roadmap' without knowing for sure is a trust-breaker. It is better to say 'I will check with the product team and come back to you with a clear answer.'

Ignoring the non-technical stakeholder. Solutions Engineers talk to IT leads and CFOs in the same week. If all your stories involve only technical counterparts, interviewers may doubt your ability to sell upward.

Not having outcomes ready. Even if exact figures are not public, be ready to describe results in directional terms: 'order errors dropped significantly,' 'the client expanded to a second business unit,' 'the deal closed ahead of the original timeline.' Vague answers with no result make STAR stories feel incomplete.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing signal low curiosity or low interest. Prepare at least a couple of genuine questions about the role, the team, or the product direction.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Is a coding interview part of the Quicksell Solutions Engineer process?

Candidates report that Quicksell's SE interviews typically focus on technical problem-solving in context rather than pure coding. You may be asked to read an API response, debug a sample integration, or walk through how you would set up a webhook. Writing full code from scratch is less common, but comfort with JSON, basic scripting, and API tools like Postman is a practical advantage.

What salary range should I expect for this role at Quicksell?

Quicksell does not publicly list salary bands for Solutions Engineers. Publicly reported ranges for SE roles at Indian B2B SaaS startups of comparable size vary widely by experience level, so check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for community-sourced data points before your negotiation. Go into the conversation with a clear number based on your current CTC, your market research, and the total compensation structure including fixed pay, variable, and any ESOPs on offer.

How many interview rounds does Quicksell typically have for a Solutions Engineer?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screening call, one or two technical or product-scenario rounds, and a final discussion with a senior leader or founder. The exact number of rounds can vary based on the seniority of the role and the hiring manager's preference. Treat each round as an opportunity to ask questions that help you evaluate the role, not just to answer theirs.

Do I need prior experience with WhatsApp Business API to apply?

Familiarity with WhatsApp Business API is useful but candidates report it is not always a hard requirement at the application stage. Quicksell typically values general API literacy, strong client-facing experience, and the ability to learn a new product quickly. If you have not worked with WhatsApp API directly, spend time before the interview exploring how it works at a conceptual level and how Quicksell uses it in their platform.

How important is sales experience versus technical experience for this role?

Solutions Engineering sits at the intersection of both, and interviewers at Quicksell typically want evidence of each. Strong candidates can tell client-facing stories covering demos, POCs, and stakeholder management, and also speak credibly about integrations and troubleshooting. If your background skews heavily technical, practise framing your work in terms of business outcomes. If it skews heavily towards sales, be ready to demonstrate hands-on comfort with APIs and integration workflows.

What is the best way to research Quicksell before the interview?

Start with Quicksell's own product: sign up, build a sample catalog, and trace the order flow from buyer to seller. Read recent coverage in Indian business and startup media to understand who their customers are and how the product has evolved. Check LinkedIn to see what backgrounds current team members come from, and look at all open roles, not just the one you applied for, since they signal which parts of the business are growing fastest right now.
