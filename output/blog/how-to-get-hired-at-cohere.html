01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Cohere is one of the most closely watched enterprise AI companies right now. They build large language models and natural language processing APIs that help businesses power search, summarisation, classification, and conversational tools at scale. Unlike consumer-facing AI products, Cohere is squarely focused on the enterprise market, which means their teams care deeply about reliability, security, and real business outcomes rather than flashy demos.

As of mid-2026, Cohere has 135 open roles across machine learning, software engineering, solutions engineering, sales, and corporate functions. This volume of hiring signals a company in a significant growth phase. Many roles are remote-friendly, which makes them genuinely accessible to strong candidates based in India. If you are an ML engineer, a backend engineer, or an enterprise-facing professional with an interest in applied AI, Cohere is actively looking for people.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Cohere's hiring process typically runs four to six weeks from first recruiter contact to offer, though timelines vary by role and team. Here is what most candidates report going through, stage by stage.

Recruiter screen. A talent partner calls to confirm your background, check your interest, and explain the process. This round usually runs thirty to forty-five minutes. Come ready to explain why Cohere specifically, not just 'I want to work in AI.' Ask about team size, what the first ninety days look like, and how success is measured.

Hiring manager or senior IC screen. This is a deeper conversation with the person who owns the role. For engineering positions it often includes a light technical discussion or a walkthrough of your past projects. For go-to-market roles it leans toward your domain experience and how you have handled enterprise customers. Expect forty-five to sixty minutes.

Take-home assessment or technical test. Many engineering and research roles include a take-home project or a timed online assessment. Commonly reported topics include Python coding, data structures, and NLP or ML problem-solving. Budget two to four hours and focus on clean, well-documented work rather than an ambitious but half-finished solution. Explain your trade-offs in comments or a short write-up.

Full interview loop. This is the core evaluation, usually held virtually over a half day. Expect three to five rounds covering technical depth, system design, and behavioural questions. Research roles may include a paper discussion or a short presentation. Solutions engineering roles often add a mock customer scenario where you map a business problem to an AI solution.

Reference checks and offer. If the loop goes well, Cohere moves quickly to references. Offers include base salary, equity, and benefits. Negotiate on all components, not just base.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Cohere hires people who are genuinely excited about applied AI, not just AI in the abstract. Here is what tends to matter across different role types.

ML and research engineers need strong fundamentals in transformers, fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and embeddings. Hands-on experience with Python and frameworks like PyTorch or JAX matters. The ability to turn a research idea into a working prototype is valued as much as theoretical depth.

Software engineers need solid backend skills, comfort with distributed systems, and experience building large-scale APIs. Because Cohere serves enterprise customers, reliability and security awareness are taken seriously from day one.

Solutions engineers and technical sales professionals need to understand a customer's business problem and map it clearly to an AI solution. Prior experience selling or deploying developer tools, APIs, or data platforms is a strong signal.

Across all roles, Cohere looks for clear communication, an ownership mindset, and comfort with ambiguity. They move fast and expect people to figure things out independently. The ability to write well and explain complex ideas in plain language is valued everywhere, not just in customer-facing positions.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Start with the product. Sign up for Cohere's API (they have a free tier for developers) and build something with it. Even a small prototype using their embed, generate, or rerank endpoints gives you concrete things to discuss in interviews and signals genuine interest.

Brush up on NLP and LLM fundamentals. Review transformer architecture, tokenisation, embedding spaces, fine-tuning approaches, and RAG pipelines. Be ready to explain trade-offs between approaches, not just describe them. Cohere interviewers often ask 'why did you choose this over that' rather than 'define this term.'

Practise system design with an AI angle. For senior engineering roles, prepare to design systems that serve ML models at scale: think about latency, throughput, caching, and failure modes. LeetCode-style coding still matters, but Cohere leans more toward practical problem-solving than puzzle questions.

Prepare strong behavioural stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for questions about ownership, ambiguity, and cross-functional work. Have two or three stories ready that show you shipped something difficult and learned from it.

Read Cohere's public writing. Their engineering blog, research papers, and product announcements give you vocabulary and context. Mentioning a specific paper or product decision in conversation shows you did more than skim the careers page.

Tailor your resume for each role. Quantify your impact using numbers from your own work history, connect your past experience to what Cohere is building, and keep it to one page if you have under ten years of experience.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Cohere describes itself as mission-driven, focused on making AI genuinely useful for enterprises rather than winning benchmark competitions. The culture is fast-paced, technically demanding, and relatively flat in hierarchy. People are expected to take ownership of their work from day one and are not handed a detailed playbook.

One distinctive feature is how closely research and engineering work together. At Cohere, researchers and product engineers often sit in the same team rather than in separate silos, which means ideas move from paper to product faster than at larger companies. If you enjoy that kind of close collaboration, it is a meaningful cultural fit signal worth mentioning in your interviews.

The company values directness. Feedback is given openly, and people are expected to push back constructively rather than nod along. If you come from a more hierarchical work culture, it is worth mentally preparing for a flatter and more candid environment where junior engineers are expected to have and voice opinions.

Cohere has a distributed global team with strong presence across North America and Europe, and growing representation in India. Remote candidates are genuinely considered for most engineering and research roles, though some leadership and go-to-market positions prefer presence near a hub city.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Cohere hire candidates based in India, or only from North America?

Cohere hires globally and has remote-friendly policies for most engineering and research roles. Indian candidates have been hired for both fully remote and relocated positions. That said, some go-to-market and leadership roles may specify a location preference, so check the individual job listing carefully before applying.

How long does Cohere's interview process typically take from application to offer?

Most candidates report a process of four to six weeks from first recruiter contact to offer, though this varies by role and how quickly both sides move. Following up with your recruiter after each stage to confirm next steps is a practical way to keep things moving and show continued interest.

What programming languages and tools does Cohere commonly use?

Python is the primary language for ML and research roles. Backend services commonly use Go or Python. For ML positions, familiarity with PyTorch or JAX comes up frequently in job descriptions. Infrastructure and platform roles often mention Kubernetes and major cloud platforms such as AWS, GCP, or Azure.

Is prior LLM experience required, or can I join as a strong generalist engineer?

For ML and research engineering roles, hands-on LLM experience is a strong advantage and often expected at the senior level. For software engineering, solutions engineering, or corporate roles, domain-specific LLM experience is less critical. Cohere does hire strong generalists and invests in teaching the domain, but genuine curiosity about AI is expected across all functions.

How should I approach the take-home assessment to stand out?

Read the brief carefully and resist the urge to over-engineer your solution. Cohere assessments typically evaluate your reasoning process as much as the final output, so document your thinking, explain the trade-offs you made, and submit something clean and working rather than an ambitious but incomplete prototype. A short write-up alongside your code goes a long way.

Can I negotiate the offer, and what should I focus on?

Yes, and you should. Cohere offers typically include base salary, equity (stock options or RSUs), and benefits. Candidates commonly cite levels.fyi as a reference point for benchmarking equity and total compensation ranges at AI-focused companies. Be specific about what you are asking for and why, and negotiate all components rather than focusing only on base salary.
