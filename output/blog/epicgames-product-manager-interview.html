01 Overview

Overview

Epic Games is the studio behind Fortnite, Unreal Engine, and the Epic Games Store. As a company, it sits at the intersection of consumer gaming, developer tooling, and a creator economy through platforms like UEFN (Unreal Editor for Fortnite) and the Fab marketplace. This makes the PM role unusually broad: you could be working on live-service game features, B2B developer tools, or a two-sided marketplace for digital assets.

As of July 2026, Epic Games has 123 open roles listed on knok jobradar. PM interviews at Epic typically span multiple rounds covering product sense, analytical thinking, and execution. Candidates report that Epic values PMs who think like a gamer, reason with data, and navigate complex stakeholder environments, including large creative communities with strong opinions.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Epic PM interviews focus on gaming product intuition, marketplace dynamics, developer tooling, and community management. Candidates report encountering questions like these:

  1. How would you improve the Epic Games Store to better compete with Steam?
  2. Fortnite serves both hardcore gamers and casual social players. How do you build a product roadmap that serves both without alienating either?
  3. How would you prioritize features for Unreal Engine's next major release, given that your users range from solo indie developers to large AAA studios?
  4. Design a feature that helps independent creators discover and use Fab (Epic's digital asset marketplace) for the first time.
  5. How would you measure the success of a new in-game economy feature in Fortnite, such as a new item category?
  6. Epic's publicly reported revenue split for the Epic Games Store differs from the standard industry model. As a PM, how would you defend or evolve this policy?
  7. Tell me about a time you managed a product used by a large and vocal community. How did you handle conflicting feedback?
  8. A major game launches on the Epic Games Store with a critical bug causing player crashes. You are the PM. What do you do in the first hour?
  9. How would you define and set quarterly OKRs for the Epic Games Store's growth team?
  10. What metrics would you use to evaluate the health of Epic's creator economy, specifically UEFN and the Fab marketplace?
  11. How would you build a new onboarding experience for studios that are new to Unreal Engine but experienced in other game engines?
  12. Epic is expanding into mobile. How would you adapt the Epic Games Store for a mobile-first audience in markets like India?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you managed a product used by a large and vocal community.

*Situation:* I was PM for a fantasy sports platform with a highly engaged user base of cricket fans. Before every IPL season, our community forums would flood with feature requests, complaints, and competing opinions.

*Task:* I needed to decide which feedback was signal versus noise, and build a roadmap that addressed the most important user needs without derailing engineering priorities.

*Action:* I categorised all inbound feedback into three buckets: bugs, usability issues, and new feature requests. I then ran a community survey to understand which issues had the broadest impact. I also set up fortnightly calls with power users to go deeper. This gave me a prioritised list I could defend to both the community and the engineering team.

*Result:* We shipped the top usability fixes within the quarter, which reduced negative app store reviews noticeably. The community felt heard, retention improved in the following season, and the process became our standard for community-informed roadmapping.

---

Q: How would you prioritize features for Unreal Engine's next major release?

*Situation:* I have just joined the Unreal Engine team and been asked to define priorities for the next major version. My users range from solo indie developers to large AAA studios and enterprise clients in film and architecture.

*Task:* I need a prioritization framework that balances very different user types, each with different technical needs and business pressures.

*Action:* I would start by segmenting users into cohorts (indie, mid-tier studio, AAA, enterprise) and running discovery interviews with representatives from each. I would map feature requests against two axes: breadth of impact (how many developers are blocked without this?) and depth of impact (how severely does it break their workflow?). I would also review public developer forums and feature request trackers for patterns, then validate top priorities with a small beta partner group before locking the roadmap.

*Result:* This approach surfaces features that unblock the most developers rather than amplifying the loudest voices. It also builds trust with the developer community because they see their feedback reflected in actual releases.

---

Q: How would you measure the success of a new in-game economy feature in Fortnite?

*Situation:* Epic has just launched a new category of cosmetic items in Fortnite, and I am the PM responsible for it.

*Task:* I need to define what 'success' looks like for this feature at launch and at the three-month mark.

*Action:* I would define a layered metrics hierarchy. At the top: business impact (did this feature grow V-Bucks revenue without cannibalising the existing item shop?). Below that: engagement metrics (are players equipping, showcasing, or discussing the new items?). Below that: health metrics (are we seeing fraud signals or community backlash about pay-to-win perceptions?). I would set targets for each metric before launch and review them weekly in the first month.

*Result:* This framework stops the team from celebrating a short-term revenue spike that comes at the cost of player trust. It also gives clear signals for when to iterate versus when the feature is stable.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and improvement questions (like improving the Epic Games Store): Use the Goals, Users, Problems, Solutions flow. Start by clarifying Epic's goals for the product. Then define user segments, identify their biggest pain points, brainstorm solutions, prioritise by impact and feasibility, and recommend metrics to track success.

For prioritization questions (like Unreal Engine feature decisions): Use an impact-versus-effort matrix. Segment users first, then score each request against: (a) how many users it affects, (b) how severely they are affected without it, and (c) how complex it is to build. Prioritise high-impact, lower-complexity items unless there is a strategic reason to tackle a harder problem first.

For metrics and success measurement questions: Use a layered approach. North Star metric (the one number that best reflects the product's core value), supporting metrics (engagement, retention, revenue), and guardrail metrics (things you must not break, such as player trust or developer satisfaction). Always define metrics before launch, not after.

For crisis and incident questions (like a launch bug): Use a structured incident response: (1) understand scope and player impact, (2) communicate to affected users and internal stakeholders, (3) coordinate with engineering on a fix timeline, (4) decide whether to roll back or patch forward, (5) run a post-mortem and share learnings. The PM's role in an incident is coordination and communication, not debugging.

For behavioral and leadership questions: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action, specifically what you did personally. Anchor the Result in a concrete outcome, even if you do not have an exact number to cite.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Epic Games looks for PMs who go beyond generic product frameworks and show genuine understanding of gaming, creator, and developer communities. Here is what stands out in candidates:

Gaming product intuition. Can you think like a player and a developer at the same time? Epic's products serve consumers (Fortnite players) and builders (Unreal Engine developers, UEFN creators). Candidates who can articulate trade-offs between these audiences signal real product depth.

Data-driven decision making. Interviewers want to see you define metrics before you design solutions, not after. Be specific about what you would measure, what a good result looks like, and what a warning sign looks like.

Community empathy without being community-captured. Epic's games and tools have passionate, vocal communities. Interviewers want to see that you can listen to and respect community feedback while still making hard calls the community might not always agree with.

Ownership and execution under ambiguity. Epic PMs often work in fast-moving environments such as live-service games and major engine releases. Candidates report that interviewers respond well to stories that show you drove something to completion under pressure, not just that you 'contributed to' a team.

Marketplace and ecosystem thinking. The Epic Games Store and Fab marketplace are two-sided platforms. Understanding how to balance the interests of developers and players, or asset creators and buyers, is a strong differentiating signal.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand Epic's product landscape

Play Fortnite for a few sessions. Navigate the Epic Games Store as both a buyer and a browser. Explore the Fab marketplace. Read publicly available articles about Epic's business strategy, creator tools, and developer relations. Your goal is to form concrete opinions, not just collect facts, about what is working and what could be better.

Week 2: Practice product sense and metrics questions

Pick two Epic products (such as the Epic Games Store and UEFN) and run yourself through the full product design flow for each: goals, users, pain points, solutions, metrics. Time yourself so your answers stay concise, because candidates report that Epic interviews are structured and time-bound.

Week 3: Behavioral prep

Write out STAR answers for the following themes: handling community conflict, making a difficult prioritization call, shipping under pressure, and recovering from a failure. Ground each story in real work you have done. Thin stories without specific actions will not land well.

Week 4: Mock interviews and review

Do at least two full mock interviews with a peer, ideally someone familiar with gaming, developer tooling, or marketplace products. Ask them to push back on your assumptions. Replace any vague claim ('I improved engagement') with a specific action ('I ran a community survey, identified the top pain points, and shipped fixes within the same quarter').

Ongoing: Stay current

Check gaming industry news and Epic's official newsroom for announcements. Candidates who reference recent product changes (a new Fortnite season mechanic, a Fab marketplace update, a new Unreal Engine feature) signal genuine interest, not just interview prep. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being generic with gaming examples. If you cite a gaming product in your answer, anchor it to Epic's ecosystem specifically. Saying 'I would improve player retention' without tying it to Fortnite's distinct player types (competitive players, casual social players, creative mode builders) signals a lack of preparation.

Ignoring the developer side. Many candidates focus only on Fortnite and forget that Epic is also a major B2B company (Unreal Engine, MetaHuman, UEFN). If an interviewer asks about Epic's product strategy and you only discuss consumer gaming, you are missing half the picture.

Metrics without guardrails. Listing only positive metrics (active users, revenue, conversion) without naming what you would protect (community sentiment, developer trust, game quality standards) signals immature product thinking. Epic's products depend heavily on community health.

Weak STAR structure. Many candidates spend too long on Situation and Task and rush through Action and Result. The interviewer wants to know what you specifically did, not what your team accomplished or what the problem was.

Treating community feedback as a direct backlog. Saying 'I would listen to what users ask for and build it' is a red flag. Show that you use community input as one signal among many, not as a direct product spec.

Not knowing Epic's business model. Epic's publicly reported revenue arrangements with developers differ from standard industry norms. Not being aware of this, even at a high level, suggests you have not done basic research on the company before interviewing.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Epic Games PM interview typically have?

Candidates report that Epic's PM process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager interview, and a panel of interviews covering product sense, analytical thinking, and behavioral questions. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and seniority level. It is best to ask your recruiter for the specific format when you receive the interview invite.

Does Epic Games hire Associate PMs or entry-level PMs in India?

Epic Games does have entry-level PM roles, though candidates report that most openings in India tend to be for experienced PMs given the nature of their India operations. Associate PM roles (typically in the 12-20 LPA band per knok jobradar data) do exist but are less common than mid-to-senior level openings. Check Epic's careers page directly for current listings filtered by level.

Do I need to be a gamer to become a PM at Epic Games?

You do not need to be a hardcore or competitive gamer, but you do need genuine familiarity with Epic's products, especially Fortnite and the Epic Games Store. Candidates report that interviewers can tell the difference between someone who has actually used the product and someone who has only read about it. Playing Fortnite even casually and exploring the Epic Games Store before your interview is a practical and important step.

How important is a technical background for Epic Games PM roles?

Epic's products (Unreal Engine, UEFN, the store backend) are technically complex, and a basic understanding of how game engines or software development workflows operate will help in interviews. You do not need to be an engineer, but candidates report that interviewers appreciate PMs who can engage meaningfully with technical trade-offs rather than deferring everything to engineering. Strong product sense and communication skills matter more than a specific degree.

What is the salary range for a PM at Epic Games in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for PMs with three to six years of experience, 40-60 LPA at the Senior PM level, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PM roles. These are indicative ranges and actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and how you negotiate.

How do I prepare if I come from a non-gaming background?

Focus on transferable PM skills such as marketplace dynamics, developer tools, and consumer product design, and map them explicitly to Epic's products. Spend time genuinely using Fortnite, the Epic Games Store, and the Fab marketplace so you have firsthand observations to draw on in interviews. Candidates from fintech, edtech, and developer tools backgrounds have made this transition successfully by showing strong product fundamentals and genuine curiosity about gaming.
