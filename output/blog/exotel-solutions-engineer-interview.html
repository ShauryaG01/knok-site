01 Overview

Overview

Exotel is one of India's leading CPaaS (cloud communications) platforms, powering customer engagement for banks, logistics firms, e-commerce companies, and startups across India and Southeast Asia. A Solutions Engineer at Exotel sits squarely at the intersection of sales and technical delivery. Your core job is to understand a prospect's existing communication setup, show how Exotel's APIs and products fit that setup, and support smooth implementation once the deal is signed.

The role requires a mix of API fluency, telecom domain knowledge, and the ability to run compelling demos to audiences ranging from a startup's single developer to an enterprise CTO. Candidates who treat this as purely a sales role, or purely a technical role, tend to struggle. You need both.

As of July 2026, Exotel has 65 open roles listed on knok jobradar, making it one of the more active hiring companies in the Indian communications space. Across all companies hiring Solutions Engineers in India right now, Bangalore leads with 55 open positions, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5), out of 1,270 total openings nationally.

The interview process typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical round, a solutions or demo round, and a final discussion with leadership. Candidates report variations depending on the seniority of the role. Preparation that blends product knowledge, integration troubleshooting, and crisp storytelling will put you in a strong position.

  11. A customer's dev team is stretched thin and cannot complete the integration. How do you help without writing their production code for them?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A customer's call drop rate has spiked after going live on Exotel's API. How do you troubleshoot this?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a retail client went live on cloud telephony and reported a rise in dropped calls, with their support team fielding escalations from end customers.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly, since the account was at risk and the client's team was under pressure.

*Action:* I pulled the call logs and filtered for failed-call events. I looked at whether the pattern was time-based, which would suggest network congestion or a rate-limit issue, or tied to specific numbers, which would point to a routing problem. The drops were concentrated on a single virtual number during the morning peak. I reviewed the concurrency configuration and found the number had a cap that was lower than their actual call volume at peak. I raised the concurrency limit, set up a second number as overflow, and asked the client to monitor the next morning's peak window.

*Result:* Drops stopped within the same day's peak window. The client's support queue cleared, and we turned the incident into a reliability case study for the account review.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you convinced a skeptical technical buyer.

*Situation:* During a pre-sales cycle at a previous role, the prospect's lead developer was convinced that an on-premise PBX was more reliable than a cloud solution. He pushed back hard in the first technical call.

*Task:* I had to address his concerns credibly without dismissing them, because a dismissive response would have ended the conversation.

*Action:* Instead of jumping to a counter-argument, I asked him to walk me through the failure scenarios that worried him most. He cited SIM blocking issues he had read about with cloud telephony providers. I acknowledged that this is a real industry problem, then walked him through Exotel's number rotation logic and how call routing failover works in practice. I offered a sandboxed proof of concept so his team could test the failover behavior themselves rather than taking my word for it.

*Result:* The developer ran the POC over a couple of weeks, found the failover behavior solid, and gave his internal sign-off. The deal moved to procurement shortly after.

---

Q: How would you scope a POC for a bank automating loan repayment reminders via call and SMS?

*Situation:* In a previous engagement, a mid-sized NBFC wanted to replace manual reminder calls with an automated flow, but their compliance team had concerns about TRAI regulations around automated outbound calls.

*Task:* I needed to design a POC that was technically meaningful for their dev team while also giving the compliance team enough to evaluate.

*Action:* I started by mapping their existing workflow: agents dialing from a list and recording outcomes manually. I proposed a phased POC. In the first phase, we would use Exotel's outbound dialer API to trigger calls from a sample customer list, with a pre-recorded message and a keypress option for customers to connect to a live agent. In parallel, I prepared a short note on TRAI's regulations on automated calls so their compliance lead could review it alongside the demo. I scoped the sample small so results would be visible quickly without requiring a full integration.

*Result:* The compliance team approved the call flow, the dev team integrated successfully within the POC window, and the bank expanded the rollout to their full repayment reminder workflow in the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For troubleshooting questions: Use a structured diagnostic approach. Start with logs and data before forming hypotheses. Narrow from broad (is it all calls or specific numbers?) to specific (is it a config issue, a network issue, or a code issue?). Always state what you checked, what you ruled out, and what you changed. Interviewers want to see methodical thinking, not lucky guesses.

For product explanation questions: Lead with the customer's problem, not Exotel's feature list. 'You want your agents to always reach customers, even when numbers get flagged as spam. Here is how Exotel handles that' lands better than 'Exotel has a feature called number rotation.' Match the explanation depth to the audience: developer-level detail for tech buyers, outcome-level framing for business stakeholders.

For competitive differentiation questions: Avoid directly criticising a competitor. Instead, anchor on what the specific customer values most. 'If reliability during high-volume campaigns is the priority, here is how Exotel's infrastructure approaches that' is more credible than a generic 'we are better' claim. Know Exotel's publicly stated strengths: domestic Indian infrastructure, enterprise integrations, and support responsiveness.

For stakeholder management questions: Use a listen-first structure. Name the concern, validate it, then address it with evidence or a concrete next step. Offering a POC, a sandbox, or a joint call with a technical counterpart shows you solve problems rather than talk past them.

For process and scoping questions: Show that you think in phases. A POC should be small enough to run quickly and specific enough to answer the customer's actual question. Name the success criteria upfront so everyone agrees on what 'it worked' means.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Exotel's Solutions Engineer role attracts candidates from diverse backgrounds: telecom, SaaS sales engineering, and software development. Interviewers typically look for a few specific signals, based on what candidates report from recent hiring rounds.

Technical credibility without arrogance. You do not need to be a software engineer, but you do need to understand APIs at a working level: how REST calls function, what a webhook does, how authentication flows like OAuth or API keys work. If you have built integrations yourself, describe them. If you have not, be honest and explain how you learn new technical concepts quickly.

Customer empathy. The best answers show that you think from the customer's perspective first. Why does this feature matter to them? What are they worried about? What does success look like for their business? Answers that lead with product features and bolt on customer context as an afterthought tend to score lower.

Calm under pressure. The 'API error in the demo' question is a proxy for 'can you stay composed when something goes wrong in front of a customer?' The right answer shows that you acknowledge the situation, stay professional, have a fallback plan, and follow up. Panic or blame-shifting are red flags.

India-specific domain knowledge. Exotel's customers are primarily Indian businesses, so awareness of TRAI regulations, the Indian telecom stack (DLT registration for SMS, pesky-call regulations for outbound), and common integration challenges for Indian enterprise software is a real differentiator.

Business sense. You are part of the revenue engine. Interviewers want to see that you understand deal stages, know when to escalate versus handle yourself, and think about customer outcomes beyond the technical handoff.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Know the product deeply. Spend time on Exotel's developer documentation. Understand the core APIs: outbound calls, inbound call handling, IVR flows, SMS, and webhooks. If you can set up a free trial and make a test call, do it. Walking into the interview having actually used the product is a strong signal.

Step 2: Map the customer landscape. Exotel's publicly stated customer base spans logistics, BFSI, healthcare, and e-commerce. For each vertical, think about one or two communication problems they commonly face and how Exotel's products address them. This preparation pays off in product explanation and demo design questions.

Step 3: Brush up on telecom basics. Know the difference between SIP, PSTN, virtual numbers, and cloud numbers. Understand what DLT registration means for SMS in India. Read at least the summary of TRAI's regulations on automated and commercial calls. You do not need to be a telecom engineer, but you should be able to discuss these concepts comfortably.

Step 4: Prepare your STAR stories. You will need several strong stories: one about troubleshooting a technical problem with a customer, one about winning over a skeptical stakeholder, and one about scoping or delivering a POC. Write them out in advance and practice keeping each one brief and crisp.

Step 5: Research the competitive landscape. Know Exotel's commonly cited competitors (Knowlarity, Ozonetel, Tata Communications, global players like Twilio). Understand, at a high level, how Exotel positions itself differently. You will not be expected to have proprietary intel, but having an informed point of view shows you take the market seriously.

Step 6: Prepare your questions. Asking good questions signals genuine interest. Ask about the typical customer profile for the team you would join, how the SE team coordinates with implementation post-sale, and what the onboarding ramp looks like for a new SE.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it as a pure sales interview. Solutions Engineers are evaluated on technical depth. Candidates who give vague, feature-level answers without showing they can get into the details of an API or integration tend to get filtered early.

Treating it as a pure technical interview. Going too deep into code without connecting back to the customer's problem is equally risky. If you are explaining webhooks at length without mentioning why they matter to the customer, you have lost the room.

Not knowing Exotel's products. Walking in without having read the documentation or tried the product is a significant missed opportunity. Many candidates show up knowing cloud telephony generically but cannot speak to how Exotel specifically handles IVR flows or call recording. This is easy to fix before your interview.

Using invented numbers in demos or answers. If you do not know a specific figure, say so and explain how you would find it. Fabricating metrics in a demo or case study damages your credibility instantly with a technical interviewer.

Panicking at curveball questions. The 'API error mid-demo' scenario catches many candidates off guard because they rehearse for smooth scenarios only. Practice out loud: what do you say, what do you do, how do you keep the customer engaged while you troubleshoot?

Skipping the India-specific context. Candidates with experience in global SaaS sometimes underestimate how important TRAI compliance and Indian enterprise integration patterns are to Exotel's customers. If you come from a global background, show that you have done your homework on the Indian telecom regulatory environment.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Exotel Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically spans multiple rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical round covering APIs and telecom basics, a solutions or demo round where you walk through a customer scenario, and a final discussion with a hiring manager or senior leader. The exact structure can vary by seniority and team, so it is worth asking your recruiter to confirm the process after you clear the first round.

Do I need to know how to code to pass the Exotel SE interview?

You do not need to write production-level code, but you should be comfortable reading and discussing API calls, understanding request and response structures, and explaining what a webhook does and how it is configured. Candidates who have built small integrations or worked with REST APIs in any context, even personal projects, tend to find the technical round more comfortable. If your background is non-technical, spend time on Exotel's developer documentation before the interview.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer at Exotel?

Exotel does not publish official salary bands publicly. Compensation for Solutions Engineer roles at Indian SaaS companies varies widely by experience and team, and the commonly cited ranges on platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi reflect this wide variation. It is best to ask your recruiter for the band early in the process so you can assess fit before investing time in later rounds.

How important is telecom domain knowledge versus general SaaS experience?

Both matter, but telecom domain knowledge is a differentiator at Exotel specifically because their customers regularly ask about TRAI compliance, DLT registration for SMS, and call quality on Indian networks. Candidates with general SaaS SE experience can absolutely succeed, but they need to invest time in understanding the India-specific telecom context before the interview. Showing that you have done this homework goes a long way with interviewers.

Is there a demo or live coding component in the Exotel SE interview?

Candidates report that the solutions round often involves walking through a customer scenario, which may include sketching an architecture or showing how you would demonstrate a specific product feature. A traditional live coding test is less common, but you may be asked to read through an API call or explain how you would set up a particular integration. Preparing a mock demo walkthrough for a realistic customer use case (logistics, BFSI, or e-commerce) is solid practice.

How competitive is it to get a Solutions Engineer role at Exotel right now?

As of July 2026, Exotel has 65 open roles across functions, making it one of the more actively hiring companies in the Indian cloud communications space. Solutions Engineer roles attract candidates with a mix of technical and commercial skills, which is a narrower profile than a pure developer or a pure sales role. Standing out typically requires combining strong product knowledge, real customer-facing examples, and a solid understanding of the Indian telecom landscape.
