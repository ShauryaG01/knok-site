02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates at AI-product companies similar to Nablon AI report questions across three themes: machine learning fundamentals, applied problem-solving, and communication of results.

  1. Walk me through a machine learning project you are most proud of. What problem did it solve and how did you measure success?
  2. How would you design a recommendation or ranking model for an AI product from scratch?
  3. Explain overfitting. How did you detect and handle it in a project?
  4. You built a model that performs well offline but poorly in production. How do you debug it?
  5. Describe your experience with large language models or generative AI. Have you fine-tuned or used them for a business task?
  6. How do you choose between a simple model like logistic regression and a more complex neural network for a classification problem?
  7. A stakeholder tells you 'the model is not working.' What do you investigate first?
  8. Walk me through how you handle messy, real-world data before modelling.
  9. How do you evaluate a model when the target classes are heavily imbalanced?
  10. Tell me about a time your analysis changed a business decision. What was the outcome?
  11. How do you keep up with new research and tools in machine learning?
  12. What excites you about working at an AI-first company like Nablon AI, and how does your background connect to what they are building?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a machine learning project you are most proud of.

*Situation:* My team was dealing with high churn in a subscription product. We had rich user behaviour data but no model to predict who was at risk of leaving.

*Task:* I was asked to build a churn prediction system that the product team could act on within a short window of a user showing early signals.

*Action:* I pulled several months of event data, engineered features around login frequency, feature usage drop-off, and support ticket patterns. I trained a gradient boosting model, tuned the decision threshold to favour recall (since missing a churner was more costly than a false alarm), and worked with the backend team to serve scores via an internal API.

*Result:* The model flagged at-risk users early enough for a targeted retention campaign. The product team reported a meaningful improvement in 30-day retention for the flagged segment.

---

Q: You built a model that performs well offline but poorly in production. How do you debug it?

*Situation:* A fraud detection model I shipped had strong offline metrics but started generating too many false positives in production within the first few weeks of launch.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause quickly, since every false positive was a blocked transaction and a frustrated customer.

*Action:* I compared the distribution of input features in training data versus live data and found significant drift in one payment-channel feature that had changed after a UI update. I added distribution monitoring, retrained on more recent data, and set up a regular drift check going forward.

*Result:* False positives dropped back to acceptable levels. The monitoring system caught additional drift events in subsequent quarters before they affected users at scale.

---

Q: Tell me about a time your analysis changed a business decision.

*Situation:* Leadership was about to invest in expanding a feature that showed high engagement on the standard dashboard.

*Task:* I was asked to validate the business case before the budget was approved.

*Action:* I segmented the engagement data and found that usage was concentrated in a small group of power users, not the broader base. A cohort analysis showed that new users rarely returned to the feature after the first week. I presented this alongside a cost-benefit breakdown for the proposed expansion.

*Result:* The team paused the expansion, ran a targeted user research sprint, and redesigned the onboarding flow. Retention for that feature improved in the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences combined). Spend most of your time on Action, since that is where interviewers see your thinking. End with a concrete Result, even if it is 'the team reported a meaningful improvement' when exact figures are unavailable.

Structured walkthrough for technical questions. Restate the problem in your own words before solving it. State your assumptions clearly. Describe your approach step by step and call out trade-offs ('I chose X over Y because...'). End with how you would validate or monitor the solution.

Think out loud for ambiguous questions. Interviewers at AI-product companies want to see how you break down a vague problem. Saying 'I would first clarify whether we care more about precision or recall here' before diving into an answer shows product sense alongside technical skill.

Be honest about numbers. If you do not have an exact metric, say 'the team reported a meaningful improvement' rather than inventing a figure. Fabricating results is a quick way to lose credibility with technical interviewers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Nablon AI is an AI-product company, so interviewers typically look for candidates who can work across the full pipeline: data preparation, modelling, evaluation, and production deployment. Building a model is table stakes. Keeping it working in production, and explaining its outputs to non-technical stakeholders, is what tends to differentiate candidates.

ML fundamentals. Expect questions on bias-variance trade-off, regularisation, model selection, and evaluation metrics. Know why accuracy alone can mislead on imbalanced datasets and be ready to discuss alternatives like precision, recall, F1, or AUC.

Modern AI familiarity. Given the company's focus, candidates report questions on large language models, prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, and when fine-tuning is worth the cost versus prompt engineering alone. You do not need to have trained a foundation model from scratch, but you should be able to discuss how you have used or evaluated these tools.

Clear communication. Data Scientists at AI-product companies work closely with product managers and engineers. Interviewers want to see that you can translate model output into a business recommendation without burying the insight in technical jargon.

Ownership. Candidates who describe not just what they built but the outcome it drove, the problems they encountered, and how they resolved them tend to do better than those who narrate steps without stakes.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Refresh core ML concepts (week 1). Revisit supervised and unsupervised learning, common algorithms (gradient boosting, neural networks, k-means), and evaluation metrics. Practise explaining these out loud without jargon, the way you would to a product manager.

ML in production (weeks 1-2). Review feature drift, model monitoring, A/B testing, and basic serving infrastructure. These topics come up regularly at AI-product companies and are often where candidates fall short.

Modern AI tools (week 2). If you have not worked with LLMs directly, spend time with publicly available APIs and documentation. Understand context windows, temperature, RAG, and when fine-tuning makes sense versus prompt engineering.

Prepare your stories. Write down 5-6 projects in STAR format. For each, note the business problem, your specific role, the technical approach, and the outcome. Include at least one story where something went wrong and how you recovered.

Research Nablon AI. Read their publicly available product materials, blog posts, and the job description carefully. Tailor at least one story to something relevant to what they are building. Prepare a few thoughtful questions for the interviewer about the team's current challenges.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to a solution without scoping. When asked an open-ended question like 'how would you improve our recommendation system?', candidates who name an algorithm immediately tend to score lower than those who first ask about data availability, business constraints, and success metrics.

Treating evaluation as an afterthought. Many candidates describe a solid modelling approach but cannot explain how they would know if it worked. Always have an evaluation strategy ready before you finish describing your model.

Vague interest in the company. Saying 'I want to work here because it is an AI company' is too generic. Connect your interest to something specific about Nablon AI's product or problem domain.

Underestimating communication skills. Technical accuracy matters, but AI-product companies often include a non-technical stakeholder in later rounds. Practise explaining your work to someone outside data science before the interview.

Not preparing questions. Candidates who say they have no questions signal low interest. Prepare at least a couple of specific questions about the team's current work, technical stack, or biggest challenges.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Nablon AI Data Scientist interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds. This usually includes a recruiter or HR screening call, a technical assessment (take-home problem or live coding), one or two technical interviews with the data science or engineering team, and a final discussion with a manager or senior leader. The exact structure can vary, so confirm with your recruiter at the start of the process.

What technical skills does Nablon AI focus on for Data Scientist roles?

Based on candidate reports and the nature of AI-product companies, the focus is typically on machine learning fundamentals (model selection, evaluation metrics, handling imbalanced data), Python and SQL, and familiarity with large language models and generative AI tools. Candidates also report questions on model deployment and monitoring, not just model building.

How should I approach a take-home assignment?

Read the brief carefully and restate the problem before you start coding. Document your assumptions, choices, and trade-offs as you go. Interviewers care as much about your reasoning process as your final model. Submit clean, readable code alongside a short write-up explaining your approach. If time allows, include a section on what you would do differently with more data or more time.

What salary can I expect for a Data Scientist role at Nablon AI?

Nablon AI does not publicly list its salary ranges. Broader market data from knok jobradar shows Data Scientist salaries in India typically range from 8-16 LPA at entry level, 18-30 LPA at mid-level (3-5 years), and 30-48 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). Actual offers depend on your experience, skills, and negotiation, and Nablon AI's numbers may differ from these market benchmarks.

Do I need LLM or generative AI experience for this role?

Given Nablon AI's AI-product focus, familiarity with large language models is a clear advantage. You do not need to have trained one from scratch, but be ready to discuss LLMs at a conceptual level, share any hands-on experience you have, and speak to topics like prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, and fine-tuning trade-offs. Candidates who can bridge classical ML and modern AI tools tend to stand out.

How do I stand out in the final round or manager discussion?

Final rounds typically shift focus from algorithms to how you think about problems, work with stakeholders, and handle ownership. Come prepared with specific examples of driving a decision or recovering from a setback. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team's current challenges and product direction. Showing genuine, specific curiosity about what Nablon AI is building is consistently cited as a differentiator at this stage.
