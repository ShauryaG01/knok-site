01 Overview

Overview

Adyen is a global payments technology company whose infrastructure handles transactions for major merchants around the world. Their engineering culture centres on reliability and full ownership: the team that builds a service typically runs it in production as well. With 253 open roles at Adyen as of mid-2026, they are actively expanding across technology teams, and DevOps engineers are central to that growth.

The interview process typically spans several rounds. Candidates report an initial recruiter call, followed by one or two technical rounds covering cloud infrastructure, CI/CD pipelines, observability, and reliability concepts. A behavioural round, usually with a senior engineer or engineering manager, focuses on ownership, incident handling, and cross-team collaboration. Some candidates also report a system design discussion specific to payments infrastructure.

Because Adyen operates in a regulated payments environment, expect questions that touch on security and compliance alongside the standard DevOps topics. 02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Adyen DevOps interviews, based on what candidates have shared publicly. Prepare concrete examples and real metrics from your own work for each one.

  1. Walk me through how you would design a highly available payment processing pipeline. How would you ensure no transaction is lost during a partial outage?
  2. Adyen serves merchants globally. How do you approach multi-region deployments and handle regional failover without impacting live transactions?
  3. Describe your hands-on experience with Kubernetes at scale. How have you handled resource limits, pod scheduling, and cluster upgrades in production?
  4. How would you build a CI/CD pipeline for a service that must operate under PCI-DSS compliance requirements?
  5. Tell me about a significant production incident you personally owned, from first alert through to postmortem. What changed afterward?
  6. How do you manage secrets and credentials in a microservices environment that undergoes regular security audits?
  7. Walk me through your approach to infrastructure as code. Which tools have you used, and how do you detect and correct state drift?
  8. How do you design monitoring and alerting for a system where even a brief degradation affects thousands of live transactions?
  9. Describe how you have executed a database migration with zero downtime on a high-traffic service.
  10. How do you handle capacity planning when merchant traffic spikes are sudden and unpredictable?
  11. Adyen follows a 'build it, run it' philosophy. How have you taken full end-to-end ownership of a service, including on-call responsibility?
  12. How do you balance deployment velocity with the risk controls required in a regulated payments environment?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a significant production incident you owned from detection to postmortem.

*Situation:* Our payment gateway microservice started returning intermittent errors during peak evening traffic. The alert fired and the error rate climbed steeply within minutes, affecting live transactions.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer I was responsible for restoring normal service, keeping stakeholders informed, and then leading the postmortem to prevent a repeat.

*Action:* I used our distributed tracing tool to narrow the failure to a single downstream database replica that had fallen behind on replication. I rerouted traffic to the primary, which brought errors to zero within a few minutes. I posted status updates to the internal channel at regular intervals until we declared resolution. The next day I ran the postmortem and identified a long-running analytics query sharing the same replica instance as the root cause. We separated read workloads onto a dedicated replica and added a replication lag alert.

*Result:* That failure pattern has not recurred. The new replication lag alert has caught similar conditions twice before they became user-facing incidents.

---

Q: How would you build a CI/CD pipeline for a PCI-DSS compliant service?

*Situation:* My previous team deployed a card tokenisation service manually over SSH. It was slow, error-prone, and auditors flagged it as a compliance risk during a review.

*Task:* Design and implement an automated pipeline that met both our release cadence needs and auditor requirements for access control, logging, and change traceability.

*Action:* I set up a pipeline with four quality gates: static code analysis, a container image vulnerability scan, a policy-as-code check for infrastructure changes, and an automated integration test suite against a sandboxed environment. Every production deployment required a signed approval from a second engineer, logged and immutable. Secrets were pulled from our vault at runtime and never stored in the pipeline configuration.

*Result:* We passed the next PCI audit with no findings related to the deployment process. Deployment frequency increased significantly because the manual risk anxiety was gone and engineers trusted the gates.

---

Q: Describe how you handled capacity planning for an unpredictable traffic spike.

*Situation:* One of our largest merchant clients ran a flash sale without notifying us in advance. Their transaction volume jumped to several times the usual peak within minutes and our services started showing latency increases.

*Task:* Our autoscaling was configured for gradual ramp-up and was not reacting fast enough. I needed to stabilise the service immediately and then fix the underlying scaling configuration.

*Action:* I manually scaled up the critical processing pods to stop the latency climb. In parallel I updated the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler to respond to a combination of CPU utilisation and a custom queue-depth metric, which reacts faster than CPU alone. I also worked with the account team to put in place a notification process so infrastructure gets advance warning before any planned high-volume merchant event.

*Result:* The immediate incident was resolved quickly. The updated autoscaling handled two subsequent large merchant events automatically with no manual intervention needed.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions: Use the STAR structure. Situation (one or two sentences of context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, in first person), Result (a concrete outcome with a metric if possible). Adyen interviewers probe for ownership, so be explicit about what 'you' did versus what 'the team' did.

For technical design questions: A reliable structure is: Requirements (functional and non-functional), Scale constraints (traffic, data volume, latency targets), Component choices with reasoning, Failure modes and how you handle each, and Security or compliance considerations. For a payments company, bring up failure modes and data integrity before the interviewer has to ask.

For incident and debugging questions: Walk through it as a timeline: how you detected the issue, how you narrowed it down, the immediate mitigation, the permanent fix, and what monitoring or process change prevents recurrence. Adyen values postmortem culture, so never skip the last step.

For 'how do you approach X' questions: State your default approach, name a specific tool or technique, give a real example where it worked, then mention a tradeoff or a situation where you would choose differently. This combination shows both practical experience and judgment.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Reliability as a first instinct. Adyen's infrastructure is revenue-critical. Interviewers look for engineers who naturally think about failure modes, degradation, and recovery before they talk about features or speed.

Real ownership. The 'build it, run it' culture means they want people who have actually been on-call for services they built, not just handed them off to a separate ops team. Expect follow-up questions like 'what was your on-call rotation like?' or 'what was the last alert you personally investigated?'

Security awareness in context. Because Adyen is a payments platform, security is not an afterthought. Candidates who naturally weave PCI-DSS thinking, secrets management, and least-privilege principles into their technical answers stand out from those who treat security as a separate concern.

Clarity over jargon. Adyen engineers work across many nationalities and time zones. Interviewers value candidates who explain their reasoning step by step rather than name-dropping tools without context.

Specificity. Vague answers like 'I used best practices' rarely land well. Prepare stories with actual numbers, timelines, and outcomes from your own work. 'We reduced mean time to recover from roughly forty minutes to under ten' is far more convincing than 'we improved our incident response.'

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: research and foundations
Read Adyen's engineering blog (search 'Adyen Tech Blog') to understand the scale they operate at and the kinds of problems their teams solve. Note any tools or architectural patterns they mention. Review core concepts: Kubernetes internals, Terraform or your infrastructure-as-code tool of choice, CI/CD pipeline security, and distributed tracing.

Week 2: story preparation
Write out four or five STAR stories that cover: a production incident, a CI/CD improvement, a security or compliance challenge, a capacity or scaling problem, and a time you improved reliability metrics. Practice saying each one out loud in under three minutes. Time yourself.

Week 3: technical depth
Practice one system design problem per day focused on payment-adjacent scenarios: high-availability queuing, idempotent transaction processing, and zero-downtime database migrations. Brush up on PCI-DSS basics. Even a surface-level understanding of the requirements signals the right mindset to interviewers.

Before the interview
Prepare two or three questions for the interviewer. Questions about their incident review culture, how they balance deployment speed with compliance controls, or how on-call is structured, show genuine interest and preparation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic DevOps answers without payments context. Saying 'I would set up Kubernetes' is far less compelling than 'I would set up Kubernetes with network policies and pod security standards, because in a payments environment, lateral movement between services is a serious risk.' Show that you have thought about the domain.

Treating incidents as embarrassing. Some candidates avoid incident stories or gloss over them quickly. Adyen interviewers specifically want to hear about things that went wrong. A well-structured incident story, including what you personally learned and changed, is a strong positive signal.

Not having numbers. 'We improved performance' is forgettable. Concrete metrics from your own work, even approximate ones, stick in an interviewer's memory. Prepare to quantify the impact of your actions.

Skipping the 'run it' half of 'build it, run it'. Candidates who have only built pipelines and handed services off to a separate ops team often struggle with on-call and production ownership questions. If your experience has gaps here, be honest and show that you understand what the role actually involves.

Over-preparing for one specific tool. Adyen uses a variety of infrastructure tools and the stack evolves. Interviewers care more about how you reason through a problem than whether you have used their exact toolchain. Focus on transferable concepts and judgment, not tool-specific commands.

Not asking questions. Arriving with nothing to ask signals that you have not researched the company or thought seriously about the role. Prepare at least two genuine questions.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Adyen DevOps interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to five rounds. This usually includes a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering infrastructure and DevOps concepts, a system design or case discussion, and a behavioural round. The exact structure can vary by team and hiring location, so confirm with your recruiter after the first call.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer role at Adyen in India?

Market data for DevOps Engineers in India shows 15-28 LPA for mid-level profiles (3-5 years of experience) and 30-50 LPA for senior profiles (6-9 years). Adyen is considered a premium employer, and Glassdoor and levels.fyi data for global fintech companies in this segment commonly shows figures above the broad market median. Specific Adyen India compensation figures are limited in public data, so treat the market bands as a starting point and research current ranges before your compensation discussion.

Do I need a PCI-DSS certification to apply?

No formal certification is required. What matters is familiarity with the core principles: data classification, access controls, audit logging, and keeping cardholder data environments isolated. Candidates who can speak to how they have applied these ideas in past roles, even informally, consistently do better than those who treat compliance as someone else's responsibility.

What tools and technologies should I focus on for preparation?

Based on what Adyen publicly shares about their engineering, focus on Kubernetes, CI/CD tooling (any major platform is fine), infrastructure as code (Terraform is widely cited in their blog), observability stacks covering metrics, logs, and distributed tracing, and major cloud platforms. Deep, demonstrable knowledge in one or two areas is more impressive than surface-level familiarity across everything.

How important is the 'build it, run it' culture in the interview process?

Very important. Adyen publicly emphasises that engineering teams own their services in production, end to end. Interviewers will probe for on-call experience, incident ownership, and whether you have actually operated services rather than just built them. Prepare specific stories about production incidents you personally owned and resolved, including what you changed afterward to prevent recurrence.

Is there a coding round for DevOps Engineer roles at Adyen?

Candidates report that algorithmic coding is not the primary focus, but some rounds include scripting tasks, typically Python, Bash, or Go, or infrastructure-as-code exercises. You may be asked to write or review a Terraform module, a Dockerfile, or a deployment script. Practise writing clean and readable scripts rather than optimising for algorithmic complexity.
