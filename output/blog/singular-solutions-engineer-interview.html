01 Overview

Overview

Singular is a mobile attribution and marketing analytics platform. Their Solutions Engineers sit at the crossroads of technical depth and client communication. You will typically work with marketing teams, developers, and product managers at client companies to make sure their mobile measurement, attribution, and reporting are set up correctly and delivering real value. The role combines pre-sales support, post-sales implementation, and ongoing technical advisory work.

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical round focused on APIs and integrations, a case study or presentation component, and a final conversation with hiring managers or a cross-functional panel. The exact number of rounds may vary, so ask your recruiter upfront what to expect. As of July 2026, Singular has 18 open Solutions Engineer roles tracked on knok jobradar.

This guide covers the questions most commonly asked, how to structure strong answers, and what will help you stand out from other candidates.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A client says their install numbers in Singular do not match their internal dashboard. How do you troubleshoot this?

*Situation:* At my previous role as a solutions consultant for a SaaS analytics platform, a mid-sized gaming app client came to me three weeks after go-live reporting that their install numbers were noticeably lower in our platform than in their internal BI system.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause quickly, restore the client's confidence, and prevent incorrect data from influencing their campaign spending decisions.

*Action:* I asked the client to share both data sets with timestamps and traffic sources labelled side by side. I checked whether they were comparing the same attribution window, the same event definition (first open vs. install), and the same timezone settings. I then reviewed their SDK integration logs and found they were on an older SDK version that did not fire correctly on first open for a specific segment of devices. I looped in our engineering team for a patch and walked the client through the reconciliation line by line so they understood exactly where the gap came from.

*Result:* We resolved the discrepancy within two business days. The client went on to expand their contract the following quarter, and the incident became a standard checklist item in our onboarding process to prevent similar issues.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you successfully onboarded a technically complex client.

*Situation:* I was assigned to onboard a fintech super-app with operations across multiple countries. They had separate development teams per region, different attribution requirements per market, and a security team that wanted to review every API endpoint before signing off.

*Task:* My goal was to complete the technical integration, get all their campaigns tracked correctly across regions, and obtain security sign-off, all within a six-week deadline set by their leadership.

*Action:* I built a shared project tracker with each team lead, ran weekly syncs in two timezone blocks, and wrote custom integration documentation specific to their React Native stack. I proactively reached out to their security team in week one and shared our compliance documentation before they asked for it, which removed a major bottleneck from the schedule.

*Result:* We completed the full integration in five weeks, one week ahead of schedule. The client's head of growth told my manager it was the smoothest vendor onboarding they had experienced.

---

Q: Describe a time you turned a frustrated or at-risk client into a satisfied one.

*Situation:* A large e-commerce client threatened to leave after a reporting bug caused incorrect ROAS figures to appear in their weekly board presentation. Their CMO was directly affected and was not happy.

*Task:* As the Solutions Engineer on the account, I needed to contain the situation immediately, fix the error, and rebuild trust with a very senior stakeholder.

*Action:* I called the client within the hour, acknowledged the issue without making excuses, and committed to a written incident report by end of day. I worked with our product team to confirm the bug was isolated to a specific date filter logic. I then prepared corrected data with a plain-language explanation of what went wrong and arranged a joint call between our VP of Customer Success and their CMO.

*Result:* The client stayed. Their CMO said the speed and transparency of our response exceeded what they had expected from a vendor. They later agreed to participate as a reference customer.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical troubleshooting questions: Use a structured diagnostic approach. State what information you would gather first (data samples, SDK logs, timestamps), then describe the layers you would check (SDK version, API configuration, partner feed settings), then explain how you would communicate progress to the client while the investigation is ongoing. Interviewers want to see methodical thinking, not just the right answer.

For client escalation questions: Lead with empathy before jumping to action. Show that you acknowledge the client's frustration first. Then briefly describe how you would triage severity, who you would involve internally, and how you would keep the client informed throughout the resolution process.

For product knowledge questions: Connect your answer to business impact. If asked about SKAdNetwork, attribution windows, or cost aggregation, frame your answer in terms of what the client is actually trying to achieve (measure ROAS, make budget decisions, justify spend to leadership) rather than reciting technical definitions. This signals you think like a Solutions Engineer, not just a support agent.

For competitor or 'why Singular' questions: Be specific. Mention Singular's unified marketing analytics approach, cross-channel cost aggregation, and creative analytics as differentiators. Candidates who show genuine product research stand out clearly from those giving generic answers.

General rule for all answers: Keep answers focused and clear. Solutions Engineer interviews typically reward candidates who can simplify complexity for clients, not those who impress by making things sound complicated.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Singular's interviewers are typically looking for three qualities working together: technical credibility, client empathy, and business awareness.

Technical credibility means you can hold a real conversation with a developer or data engineer about SDKs, APIs, and data pipelines. You do not need to be a software engineer, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding data flow end to end, and diagnosing integration issues without needing an engineer next to you at all times.

Client empathy means you genuinely understand that clients are under pressure from their own stakeholders. Solutions Engineers who can translate technical complexity into plain language that a marketing director can act on are far more valuable than those who stay in technical jargon throughout every conversation.

Business awareness means connecting your work to outcomes. When you fixed an integration issue, what was the business impact? When you recommended a workaround, why was it the right trade-off for that client at that time? Candidates who link technical work to retention, revenue, or efficiency tend to perform better in this process.

Candidates also report that Singular values self-starters who can manage multiple client relationships simultaneously without needing constant direction from a manager.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product and industry grounding. Use Singular's public documentation, blog, and product pages to understand their attribution model, SDK structure, cost aggregation features, and creative analytics offering. Separately, read up on SKAdNetwork, ATT (App Tracking Transparency), and how iOS privacy changes have shifted mobile attribution in recent years. Knowing this context lets you give specific, credible answers rather than generic ones.

Week 2: Technical practice. Practice explaining REST APIs, webhooks, and data pipelines in plain language as if you are talking to a marketing manager. If you have used Postman before, revisit it. Work through a mock troubleshooting scenario where install counts do not reconcile between two systems and time yourself explaining the steps.

Week 3: Story preparation. Write down six to eight stories from your own experience using the STAR format. Cover at minimum: a complex onboarding, a client escalation you resolved, a time you worked with a product or engineering team on a client request, and a time you had to push back on a feature request while keeping the relationship intact.

Before the interview. Research any recent Singular news, partnerships, or product launches. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for your interviewer that show you have done your homework, not generic questions you could ask any company.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Staying too technical without connecting to the client. Solutions Engineer interviews are not pure engineering interviews. Candidates who answer every question at the API or SDK level without ever mentioning the client's experience tend not to move forward.

Being vague on troubleshooting. Saying 'I would look into the data' is not enough. Walk through the actual steps: what you check first, what tools you use, who you loop in internally, and how you communicate with the client while the investigation is happening.

Not researching Singular's specific products. Generic answers about loving technology and clients stand out for the wrong reason. Know what Singular actually does, who their commonly cited competitors are in the MMP space, and what their attribution approach offers that others do not.

Underestimating the case study or presentation round. Candidates report this is the round where many people lose the offer. Practice presenting a mock client scenario out loud rather than just thinking through it in your head. Timing yourself helps significantly.

Waiting for perfect information before acting. Interviewers typically want to see that you can make a reasonable first move with incomplete information and then course-correct as you learn more. Candidates who respond to every scenario with 'I would need more information before I could do anything' can come across as passive rather than proactive.

How many interview rounds does Singular typically have for the Solutions Engineer role?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds, and a final round with a hiring manager or a cross-functional panel. Some candidates also report a case study or presentation component in between. The exact structure can vary by location and level, so ask your recruiter at the start what the full process looks like for your specific opening.

What technical skills does Singular look for in a Solutions Engineer?

Based on what candidates report, Singular looks for comfort with REST APIs, mobile SDK integrations, and data troubleshooting. You do not need to be a software developer, but being able to read API documentation, run basic queries, and debug integration issues without hand-holding is expected. Familiarity with mobile attribution concepts like SKAdNetwork, ATT, and ROAS measurement is a strong advantage going into the technical rounds.

Is prior experience with Singular's platform required to get the job?

No prior experience with Singular specifically is required, but candidates who demonstrate they have researched the platform tend to perform better. Understanding how mobile attribution works, who Singular's publicly known competitors are in the MMP space, and what differentiates Singular's unified analytics approach will help you give more specific, credible answers throughout the process.

How important is client-facing experience for this role?

Very important. The Solutions Engineer role at Singular is heavily client-facing, and interviewers will probe for real examples of how you have handled difficult clients, complex onboardings, and escalations. If you have managed technical accounts, led implementation projects, or worked in pre-sales support, prepare concrete stories from those experiences in STAR format well before your first round.
