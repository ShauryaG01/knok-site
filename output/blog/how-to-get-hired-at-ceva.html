01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Ceva is a semiconductor IP company. Instead of building physical chips, Ceva designs and licenses processor cores, wireless connectivity platforms, and AI/ML accelerator technologies to chip makers worldwide. Think of them as the company that sells the 'blueprints' that power chips inside smartphones, hearing aids, IoT devices, and wearables. This makes Ceva a niche but influential player in the global semiconductor ecosystem.

As of mid-2026, Ceva has 184 open roles spanning engineering, product, business development, and operations. Most of the hiring demand sits in areas like DSP (digital signal processing), VLSI/RTL design, embedded software, AI inference optimization, and wireless protocols (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, 5G). If your background is in semiconductor IP, chip architecture, or embedded systems, Ceva is worth a close look.

For Indian candidates, Ceva operates primarily from its headquarters in Israel and offices in the US and Europe. India-specific roles do appear in their listings from time to time, mainly in engineering and support functions. Check their careers page regularly to catch roles as they open.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Ceva hiring process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact structure varies by role, team, and location. Here is what candidates commonly report.

Application screening: The recruiter reviews your resume for fit, paying close attention to your domain background in semiconductor IP, DSP, embedded systems, or wireless. Tailoring your resume to the specific role description makes a real difference at this stage.

Recruiter call: A short introductory call to confirm your background, discuss the role scope, and cover basics like notice period and location preference. This is also your chance to ask about team structure and day-to-day responsibilities.

Technical screening: For engineering roles, this is often a one-on-one technical interview or a focused take-home problem. DSP engineers can expect signal processing theory, filter design concepts, and algorithm questions. RTL/VLSI candidates typically face questions on Verilog or SystemVerilog, timing closure, and verification methodology. Embedded engineers get tested on C, RTOS concepts, memory management, and hardware-software interfacing.

Technical panel interviews: For senior or specialized roles, there are commonly two to three technical interviews with different team members. These cover architecture discussions, design trade-offs, and deep problem-solving in your domain. Ceva panels are known to probe fundamentals rigorously, so surface-level answers are not enough.

Hiring manager and HR round: A final conversation covering your past projects, how you collaborate across teams, and your career direction. Compensation discussion usually happens here or shortly after with the recruiter.

The total timeline from application to offer commonly runs three to six weeks. Senior roles or positions that require panels across multiple time zones can stretch longer.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Ceva hires for depth over breadth. Because their work lives in specialized territory like DSP cores and wireless IP, they need people who genuinely understand the underlying technology, not just the tools built on top of it.

Technical depth: Strong fundamentals in your domain matter most. Signal processing math, digital design principles, embedded C, or AI inference optimization all come up depending on the role. Ceva values engineers who can explain why a design decision was made, not just what was done.

Domain experience: Prior work at semiconductor companies, IP licensing firms, or chip design teams is a strong plus. Familiarity with wireless standards (Bluetooth LE, Wi-Fi 6 or 7, 5G NR) or experience with AI/ML model optimization for edge deployment is increasingly valued.

Ownership mindset: Ceva operates as a lean IP company, which means individuals often carry significant responsibility. They look for people who track their own deliverables, communicate proactively, and do not wait to be told what to do next.

Cross-cultural communication: With teams across Israel, the US, Europe, and Asia, the ability to work clearly across time zones, especially in written form, is genuinely important. Clear async communication is a real skill Ceva actively looks for.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know your fundamentals cold. Ceva interviews are known to go deep. If your role involves DSP, revisit convolution, Z-transforms, filter design, and FFT. For RTL roles, be solid on synthesis, timing constraints, and verification basics. For embedded roles, know your RTOS, memory hierarchy, and C inside out.

Study Ceva's IP portfolio. Before your interviews, spend time on Ceva's website understanding their main product lines: the CEVA-X DSP cores, RivieraWaves wireless platforms, and CEVA-NeuPro AI/ML IP. Being able to connect your past experience to their actual product areas shows genuine interest and helps the conversation move faster.

Prepare project walkthroughs. For every significant project on your resume, be ready to explain the problem, your technical approach, the trade-offs you considered, and the outcome. Ceva panels typically probe the 'why' behind technical decisions, so prepare to go beyond the surface of each example.

Research the role context. Whether the role is in silicon validation, AI model optimization, or protocol stack development, look at industry discussions on LinkedIn or technical forums to understand current challenges in that space. It gives you better questions to ask and signals real preparation to the interviewer.

Think at the system level. Even if your role is narrowly scoped, interviewers appreciate candidates who understand how their piece fits into the broader chip or product ecosystem. Being able to zoom out from your specific domain shows engineering maturity.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Ceva operates as a global IP licensing company, and that shapes its culture in a few specific ways.

Technical culture: Engineering sits at the core of what Ceva does. The culture rewards people who care deeply about technical quality and can defend their design choices. You will likely work alongside engineers who are highly specialized and expect the same level of depth from their colleagues.

Lean and ownership-oriented: As an IP company rather than a large product company, Ceva teams tend to be smaller and more focused. This means less bureaucracy and more individual ownership, but also less hand-holding. People who thrive here generally prefer having real responsibility over their own domain rather than working inside a large, structured hierarchy.

Global collaboration: With headquarters in Israel and teams across the US, Europe, and Asia, working across time zones is routine. Written communication skills and async work habits matter here more than at a single-location company. If you can write a clear, concise technical update that makes sense to a colleague in a different time zone, that is a genuine advantage.

Impact through foundational work: Ceva's technology ends up inside connected devices across many product categories, from hearing aids to smartphones to IoT sensors. Many employees cite this real-world reach as meaningful, even though the output is an IP license rather than a consumer product.

Active growth areas: Ceva has been expanding its AI and wireless IP offerings through 2024-2026, which means there is active work in newer technology areas alongside their established DSP business. Engineers joining now can contribute to IP that will ship inside products over the coming years.

Does Ceva hire freshers or only experienced engineers?

Ceva's open roles lean heavily toward experienced engineers, especially for technical positions requiring specialized semiconductor or DSP knowledge. Some teams do hire recent graduates for research or junior engineering roles, particularly through campus programmes or internship-to-hire paths. If you are a fresher with a strong academic background in VLSI, signal processing, or embedded systems, it is worth applying directly to junior-level openings and making your thesis or project work the centerpiece of your application.

What kind of work will I actually do at Ceva?
