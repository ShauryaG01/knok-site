01 Overview

Overview

IDfy is a Bengaluru-based identity verification and risk intelligence company. Their platform powers KYC, background checks, and fraud prevention for banks, NBFCs, fintechs, and insurance companies across India. A Technical Program Manager (TPM) at IDfy sits at the crossroads of engineering, product, compliance, and client delivery, making sure complex programs stay on track in a highly regulated environment.

IDfy currently has 13 open roles across functions. Across all of India, knok's job radar tracked 313 TPM openings as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 41 positions, followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), and Hyderabad (12). Competition is real, so preparation matters.

The TPM role here is not a generic project management job. You will be working with teams building products that touch government databases, telecom APIs, and bank systems. Compliance timelines, vendor dependencies, and multi-stakeholder coordination are core parts of the work, not edge cases.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are commonly reported by candidates interviewing for TPM roles at identity verification and fintech companies. IDfy's specific process typically includes behavioural, technical, and program management rounds.

  1. How would you manage a program where engineering timelines conflict with a compliance or regulatory deadline?
  2. IDfy's products handle sensitive identity data. How do you ensure data privacy is treated as a program constraint, not an afterthought?
  3. Walk us through how you have coordinated between product, engineering, and business teams on a large, cross-functional program.
  4. How do you track dependencies across multiple teams when a critical feature has many moving parts?
  5. Tell us about a time you had to de-risk a program mid-flight. What signals did you catch early and what did you do?
  6. How would you handle a situation where a key third-party provider (such as a government API or telecom data source) goes down during a critical release?
  7. IDfy serves regulated industries. How do you keep compliance requirements visible and actionable across sprint cycles?
  8. How do you communicate program status to leadership when things are not on track?
  9. Describe how you prioritise competing requests from multiple business stakeholders with different urgency levels.
  10. What metrics do you use to measure program health, and how do you surface them for different audiences?
  11. How would you approach launching a new feature across multiple banking clients who each have different integration needs and timelines?
  12. Tell us about a program where you had to balance speed with quality. How did you make that call, and what was the outcome?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you manage a program where engineering timelines conflict with a compliance deadline?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were building an API integration for a major NBFC client. Engineering estimated they needed additional sprints, but the regulatory deadline was fixed.

*Task:* I needed to find a path that met the compliance deadline without shipping a product that broke under real usage.

*Action:* I mapped every feature against the actual compliance requirements and separated what was legally mandatory from what was 'nice to have' for launch. I worked with the product lead to defer non-mandatory features to a follow-up release, and coordinated with engineering to focus all sprint capacity on the compliance-critical path.

*Result:* We shipped on the deadline with full compliance coverage. The deferred features went live in the next sprint without any client-facing impact. The approach became a reusable template for similar deadline conflicts.

---

Q: How do you communicate program status to leadership when things are not on track?

*Situation:* A verification program I was running hit a critical blocker when a third-party data provider had an unplanned outage.

*Task:* I had to keep leadership informed and protect the client relationship without creating panic or wasting time in unstructured calls.

*Action:* I sent a concise status note covering what happened, the impact, and three options we were evaluating with a recommended path. I then set up a focused call so stakeholders could align quickly. In parallel, I was already coordinating with the vendor's account team on a workaround.

*Result:* Leadership made the call fast, the client was informed proactively, and we recovered with minimal delay. The client later said the transparent communication made them trust us more, which supported the contract renewal.

---

Q: Walk us through how you have coordinated across product, engineering, and business teams on a complex program.

*Situation:* We were launching a new KYC flow that touched our mobile SDK, backend APIs, and the client's internal onboarding system.

*Task:* I had to keep three internal teams and one client team aligned across different sprint cycles and release windows.

*Action:* I created a single shared tracker visible to all teams, ran a weekly cross-team sync, and maintained a live dependency map that flagged blockers before they hit the critical path. Whenever a decision needed sign-off, I routed it to the right person the same day rather than letting it sit in a queue.

*Result:* The launch went out on the planned date. Post-launch, we had fewer integration issues than comparable past launches, which the engineering lead attributed directly to the dependency tracking process.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result): This is the baseline for behavioural questions. Keep the Situation brief (one or two sentences), spend most of your words on Action (what you specifically did), and end with a concrete Result. Vague results like 'it went well' do not land.

MoSCoW prioritisation: When asked how you prioritise, walk the interviewer through Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, and Won't Have. IDfy interviewers especially want to see that you can separate compliance-mandatory scope from everything else under pressure.

Risk radar: For de-risking questions, describe how you identify risks early (dependency maps, vendor health signals, stakeholder silence), how you score them on likelihood and impact, and how you escalate before a risk becomes a blocker.

Communication triangle: For stakeholder questions, show that you tailor the same update differently for engineering (blockers and next actions), product (feature and timeline impact), and leadership (business risk and cost). One status update reworded for three audiences is a sign of maturity.

Scope negotiation: For timeline conflict questions, show the tradeoff clearly. Fewer features on time beats all features late in a compliance-driven business. Frame this as a deliberate decision with stakeholder sign-off, not a quiet compromise.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

IDfy operates in a space where a missed compliance deadline or a data leak has direct legal consequences for their clients. Interviewers are not just checking if you can manage a backlog. They want to see:

Domain awareness. You do not need to be a KYC expert on day one, but you should know what RBI KYC guidelines are, why the DPDP Act matters to their clients, and why government API availability is a real program risk.

Structured communication. Can you write a status update that makes a decision easy? Can you run a cross-team sync without it turning into a complaint session? Show examples, not principles.

Risk instinct. Candidates who talk only about what went right miss the point. IDfy wants to hear how you spotted a risk early and what you did before it became a fire.

Client orientation. Many programs at IDfy have a direct client impact. Interviewers want to see that you understand the difference between an internal delay and a client-facing one, and that you act accordingly.

Comfort with external dependencies. A significant portion of IDfy's platform relies on government databases, telecom APIs, and third-party data providers. Show that you know how to manage programs where critical dependencies are outside your control.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Context and content

  • Read IDfy's product pages to understand their core offerings: identity verification, background checks, and risk intelligence.
  • Study the basics of RBI KYC guidelines and the DPDP Act so you can speak to compliance constraints intelligently.
  • Map your past experience to the scenarios in the question list above. Identify your strongest STAR stories covering compliance pressure, vendor risk, and multi-team coordination.
  • Research IDfy's clients and the verticals they serve (BFSI, gig economy, insurance, government) to ground your answers in their context.

Week 2: Practice and polish

  • Write out STAR answers for all the questions above. Time yourself. Aim for under two minutes per answer.
  • Practice explaining a complex program in plain language to a non-technical listener, then extend to a fuller version with tradeoffs.
  • Prepare a few questions to ask your interviewer: about team structure, how program success is measured, and the biggest current challenge the team is solving.
  • Review IDfy's LinkedIn page and recent press for product launches or partnerships announced in 2025 or 2026.
07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating compliance as someone else's problem. IDfy's clients are banks and NBFCs. Every program you run will have regulatory implications. Candidates who frame compliance as a separate workstream raise red flags with interviewers.

Vague stakeholder management answers. Saying 'I kept everyone aligned' without explaining how (which cadence, which tool, which escalation path) does not convince anyone. Be specific.

Leading with tools instead of outcomes. Mentioning Jira, Confluence, and status dashboards is fine, but interviewers want to hear what those tools produced. Lead with the outcome, then explain the method.

Not preparing questions for the interviewer. IDfy interviewers typically leave time for your questions. Candidates who ask nothing signal low curiosity or low preparation.

Ignoring vendor and third-party dependencies. IDfy's platform integrates with government databases, telecom providers, and bank systems. Programs here often have external dependencies outside your control. Candidates who answer only about internal team dynamics miss a core part of the job.

Over-indexing on methodology. Reciting Agile or SAFe frameworks without showing judgment (when to use them, when to skip ceremony) reads as textbook knowledge, not lived experience.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the IDfy TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening, one or two program management or case rounds, and a final leadership or culture conversation. The exact number varies by team and seniority level. Confirm the process with your recruiter at the start so you can prepare the right material for each stage.

What background is most useful for a TPM role at IDfy?

Experience in fintech, BFSI, or regulated SaaS is a clear advantage. Candidates with backgrounds in KYC platforms, identity verification, or API-heavy products tend to receive strong feedback. That said, solid program management fundamentals from any technical domain can translate well if you can connect your experience to IDfy's compliance-driven context.

Is coding or system design expected in IDfy TPM interviews?

Typically, TPM interviews at fintech companies like IDfy focus on program management, cross-team coordination, and systems thinking rather than live coding. You may be asked to evaluate a technical design or discuss an API architecture, so comfort with backend concepts and REST APIs helps. Confirm the exact format with your recruiter before the interview.

What salary can I expect for a TPM at IDfy?

IDfy does not publicly disclose salary bands. How should I research IDfy before my interview?

Start with their official product documentation and any recent coverage in Mint, Economic Times, or FinancialExpress. Check their LinkedIn page for recent hires and announcements. Understanding who their clients are (banks, NBFCs, insurance companies, gig platforms) and which regulations govern those clients (RBI KYC guidelines, DPDP Act, IRDAI) will give you strong context to weave into your answers.
