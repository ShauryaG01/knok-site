01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Locus is a Bengaluru-based logistics intelligence company that helps enterprises plan smarter last-mile delivery, optimize routes, and automate dispatch decisions using AI. Their platform is used by large players in e-commerce, FMCG, and retail to handle delivery at scale across India and internationally.

As of mid-2026, Locus has 18 open roles across engineering, product, data science, sales, and customer success. The team is relatively lean given the complexity of what they build, so each hire is expected to contribute meaningfully from early on.

Hiring at Locus is selective. The process rewards candidates who understand the problem space, communicate clearly, and can show real impact in their previous roles. Applications are accepted through their careers page and via major job platforms.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Locus interview process typically spans four to five rounds, though the exact structure depends on the role and seniority level. Here is what candidates commonly report across engineering, data science, and business tracks:

Application and resume screen. The talent team filters for relevant domain experience and technical depth. For engineering and data science roles, they pay attention to the tools you use (Python, SQL, Java, and Go are frequently mentioned) and the scale of problems you have solved. A resume that references optimization, logistics, or data-intensive systems tends to perform better at this stage.

Online assessment or take-home exercise. Most technical candidates go through a short coding or problem-solving test before speaking to anyone. Problems are typically grounded in algorithms and data structures. Data science candidates may also see a scenario involving time-series data or constraint-based optimization.

Technical interview (one or two rounds). This is a video or in-person conversation with a senior engineer or the hiring manager. Expect a deep-dive into past projects, follow-up questions on your design decisions, and, for senior engineering roles, a system design exercise. Data science candidates commonly face questions on model evaluation, feature engineering, and SQL.

Cross-functional or culture interview. Many candidates describe a round with someone from outside their direct team, such as a product manager or a team lead. This round is less about technical depth and more about how you collaborate, communicate trade-offs, and handle ambiguity under pressure.

Leadership round (senior and specialist roles). Senior hires often have a final conversation with a VP or a founder. It tends to be a strategic discussion about your thinking process, how you have built or scaled something, and how you approach problems where the answer is not yet clear.

Offer stage. Candidates who clear the final round typically receive a written offer relatively quickly. Benchmarking your salary expectations against Glassdoor or levels.fyi before this stage is strongly recommended.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Locus sits at the intersection of AI and real-world logistics, and the traits they value reflect that combination:

Technical depth over breadth. For engineering and data science roles, they want people who can explain why they chose a particular algorithm or model, not just that they used it. Interviewers commonly probe the reasoning behind your decisions.

Domain curiosity. A logistics background is not required, but showing genuine interest in how route optimization or constraint-based dispatch works signals that you will ramp up quickly. Reading their blog before interviews is a simple way to demonstrate this.

Ownership mindset. Locus is a fast-moving company with a lean team. They look for people who spot problems and drive solutions without being asked. This theme comes up across both technical and leadership rounds.

Clear communication. The product touches operations teams, engineering, and business stakeholders simultaneously. The ability to explain complex ideas in plain terms is consistently valued across roles.

Comfort with messy problems. Real-world logistics data is imperfect and constraints shift constantly. Candidates who have thrived in ambiguous environments, where requirements were unclear or changed mid-project, tend to fit the culture well.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the product before any round. Read Locus's blog and any publicly available case studies. Understanding what dispatch optimization actually means in practice, not just in theory, helps you frame your answers in language that resonates with interviewers.

Revise the right fundamentals. For engineering roles, focus on graph algorithms (shortest path problems and constraint optimization), system design for high-throughput services, and distributed systems basics. For data science roles, prioritize ML model evaluation, feature engineering, and clean SQL.

Prepare structured project stories. Pick two or three projects where you solved a hard problem, made a trade-off under pressure, or delivered clear impact. Be ready to walk through the problem, your approach, what you would do differently today, and what the outcome was.

Practice explaining trade-offs. Locus interviewers are commonly reported to ask 'why' rather than just 'how.' Saying 'I chose approach A over B because of constraint C' is more impressive than simply describing what you built.

Research the competitive landscape. Knowing who competes with Locus in the logistics intelligence space shows commercial awareness. This matters especially for product and business roles.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Locus describes its culture as driven by impact, intellectual honesty, and a willingness to solve genuinely hard problems. A few themes come through consistently in publicly available employee reviews:

Fast pace with a high bar. The team moves quickly and expectations are clear from the start. People who prefer well-defined tasks tend to find the pace uncomfortable. Those who enjoy figuring things out under pressure tend to thrive.

Candid and collaborative. Feedback at Locus is commonly described as direct. If something is not working, people say so, and this is treated as a sign of respect rather than criticism. Cross-team collaboration is a regular part of how work gets done.

Learning-heavy environment. Because logistics optimization is a specialized domain, there is a strong culture of internal knowledge sharing between engineers, data scientists, and product managers. New joiners are expected to ask questions and engage with the domain actively.

Visible real-world impact. Many employees cite the tangible nature of the work as a reason they joined. Seeing a delivery route you optimized play out across thousands of shipments daily is a concrete kind of impact that is harder to find at pure software companies.

Work-life balance. Publicly reported reviews on platforms like Glassdoor are mixed on this front, which is typical for a growth-stage B2B SaaS company. Crunch periods exist, particularly around major product launches or large client onboarding cycles.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does Locus currently have?

As of mid-2026, Locus has 18 open roles. These span engineering, product, data science, sales, and customer success functions. The mix shifts as the company's priorities evolve, so checking their careers page or a job aggregator regularly is the best way to stay current on new openings.

What salary can I expect at Locus?

Locus does not publish fixed pay bands publicly. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list commonly cited ranges for engineering and product roles at comparable B2B SaaS companies in Bengaluru, and these are the most reliable benchmarks to use before entering salary discussions. Publicly reported accounts suggest that compensation at mid-to-senior levels commonly includes a fixed component, variable pay, and ESOPs.

Do I need prior logistics or supply chain experience to apply?

No, prior logistics experience is not a strict requirement for most roles. Locus hires strong engineers, data scientists, and product managers from a wide range of industries. That said, candidates who have taken time to understand the domain, even through self-study, tend to perform better in interviews and ramp up faster after joining.

How long does the Locus interview process typically take?

The full process from application to offer commonly spans several weeks for mid-level roles. Senior or specialist roles can take longer, especially when multiple stakeholders are involved in the final decision. Staying responsive and following up politely after each round can help keep the process moving at a reasonable pace.

Does Locus hire remotely or primarily from Bengaluru?

Locus is headquartered in Bengaluru, and most roles are based there. Some roles in sales and customer success may offer flexibility depending on client geography. It is worth asking the recruiter about the specific arrangement during the initial screening call, as remote and hybrid policies have evolved since 2024.

What is the best way to stand out when applying to Locus?

Tailoring your resume to highlight experience with scale, data-intensive systems, or optimization problems gives you a stronger chance of clearing the initial screen. A referral from a current employee can also speed up the process. Preparing well-structured project stories before any round, rather than improvising on the day, is one of the most reliable ways to perform well in Locus interviews.
