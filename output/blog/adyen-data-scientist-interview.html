01 Overview

Overview

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you built or improved a model already running in production.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a churn prediction model that had been live for 18 months without updates. Business teams had largely stopped acting on its output because precision had degraded noticeably over time.

*Task:* I was asked to diagnose why the model was underperforming and either fix or replace it before the next quarterly planning cycle.

*Action:* I ran a data drift analysis and found that two key features, average session length and payment frequency, had shifted significantly after a product redesign six months earlier. I retrained the model with refreshed feature definitions, added a monthly automated retraining pipeline, and set up monitoring alerts to flag when feature distributions drifted beyond a set threshold.

*Result:* Precision improved meaningfully on the holdout evaluation set, and the business team resumed acting on the model's output within the first quarter after relaunch. The monitoring pipeline also caught a smaller drift event two months later before it caused a visible performance drop.

---

Q: A merchant's authorization rate dropped suddenly. Walk us through your investigation.

*Situation:* During a scheduled weekly review, an analyst flagged that a large retail merchant had seen a sharp drop in authorization rates over a 48-hour window.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly, since every declined transaction meant lost revenue for the merchant and risk to the platform's reputation.

*Action:* I sliced the data across card networks, issuing banks, transaction types, and geographies. The drop was concentrated in a single card network for high-value transactions only. I cross-referenced with a changelog of recent model updates and found that a feature weight adjustment in our risk scoring model was over-flagging this merchant's high-value transactions as suspicious. I escalated to the risk team with the specific segment breakdown and a proposed threshold adjustment.

*Result:* The team rolled back the specific parameter change, and authorization rates recovered within a few hours. I also documented the investigation process as a reusable runbook so the next analyst facing a similar issue could move faster.

---

Q: How do you handle a highly imbalanced fraud dataset?

*Situation:* While building a transaction fraud classifier, I found that fraud labels made up a very small fraction of our training data. Naive models were defaulting to predicting 'not fraud' almost every time and still showing high accuracy, which was completely misleading.

*Task:* I needed a model that caught a meaningful proportion of fraud without generating so many false positives that legitimate transactions would be blocked.

*Action:* I experimented with SMOTE oversampling, adjusting class weights in the loss function, and threshold tuning based on the precision-recall curve. I switched the primary evaluation metric from accuracy to F1 score and area under the precision-recall curve, which gave a far more honest picture of model quality for a rare-event problem.

*Result:* The threshold-tuned, class-weighted XGBoost model gave the best business trade-off between fraud catch rate and false positive rate. I presented the full precision-recall curve to the product team so they could choose an operating point that matched their own risk tolerance, rather than me making that call unilaterally.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical ML questions, structure your answer as: problem framing, data and feature choices, model selection and rationale, evaluation metric aligned to business goals, then deployment and monitoring. Adyen cares about real-world constraints like scoring latency, data sparsity for new merchants, and regulatory requirements. Always tie technical choices back to a business outcome, not just a benchmark number.

For SQL questions, think aloud as you write. State your assumptions upfront, name CTEs clearly, and call out edge cases such as nulls, duplicate rows, and time zone handling in transaction timestamps. Adyen engineers value readable, well-structured SQL over clever one-liners that are hard to maintain.

For investigation and product questions, use a structured diagnostic approach: define what normal looks like, identify where the anomaly is concentrated across segments, time windows, and channels, form a ranked list of hypotheses, and test the most probable one first. This shows the systems-level thinking Adyen values in their data teams.

For behavioral questions, use STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Result honest and grounded. If the outcome was mixed, say so and explain what you learned. Adyen reportedly values directness over polished, too-good-to-be-true stories, so avoid inflating outcomes.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Business grounding. Can you explain why the metric you chose matters to Adyen's business? Their core concerns are transaction volume, authorization rates, and fraud losses. Candidates who connect model choices to these outcomes consistently stand out from those who treat the interview as a purely academic exercise.

Ownership mindset. Adyen's culture emphasizes individual accountability and long-term thinking. Interviewers want to hear how you drove something end-to-end: scoping, building, deploying, and monitoring. Contributing to a team project is not the same as owning one.

Clear communication. Expect at least one moment in the process where you must explain a technical finding to a non-technical stakeholder. Practice framing model trade-offs in business language without jargon. Saying 'precision went up' means less than saying 'we blocked fewer good transactions while catching the same number of fraudulent ones.'

Intellectual honesty. Adyen reportedly prefers candidates who say 'I am not sure, but here is how I would find out' over those who bluff through gaps. Naming the limits of your approach is a strength in their culture, not a weakness.

Depth in at least one domain. Whether it is fraud detection, experimentation, real-time analytics, or NLP, interviewers want to see that you have gone beyond surface-level knowledge in an area relevant to payments data science. Breadth alone is not enough at this level.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical foundations. Revisit probability, distributions, and hypothesis testing. Practice SQL on transactional datasets focusing on window functions, CTEs, and aggregations. Review tree-based ML algorithms in depth, and study how class imbalance affects model training, evaluation, and threshold selection.

Week 2: Payments domain knowledge. Read publicly available material on how payment authorization works, what churn looks like in a two-sided marketplace, and how fraud models are evaluated differently from standard classifiers. Understanding Adyen's product at a basic level, before your first round, is a real differentiator. Their engineering blog is a good starting point.

Week 3: Case practice and behavioral prep. Run through at least three investigation-style case questions out loud, ideally with a peer who can push back with follow-up questions. Write down 4-5 STAR stories covering model ownership, stakeholder conflict, and handling data quality issues. Practise SQL under realistic time pressure.

The week before. Review Adyen's public engineering blog and any recent product announcements. Check their careers page to understand which teams are hiring and what problem areas they are focused on. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Jumping to model choice before framing the problem. Saying 'I would use XGBoost' before defining the objective, the label, and the success metric signals shallow thinking to Adyen interviewers. Always frame the problem first.
  1. Ignoring business constraints. In payments, a highly accurate model that scores transactions too slowly is not usable for real-time authorization. Always mention latency, interpretability, and regulatory constraints alongside accuracy metrics.
  1. Treating the behavioral round as a formality. Adyen's culture rounds carry real weight in the final decision. Candidates who arrive without concrete STAR stories often get passed over even when their technical scores are strong.
  1. Generic answers for domain-specific questions. Treating a fraud detection question as a standard binary classification problem misses the payments-specific nuances: adversarial concept drift, the asymmetric cost of false positives, and the need for real-time scoring under strict latency budgets.
  1. Not asking clarifying questions. Adyen interviewers reportedly appreciate candidates who pause to ask 'what does success look like here?' before diving into an answer. It signals structured thinking and mirrors how their teams actually work.
  1. Overclaiming results. Asserting that a model 'drove significant revenue growth' without being able to explain the attribution methodology is a red flag. Be precise about what you measured, how you isolated the impact, and what assumptions you made.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Adyen data scientist interview typically have?

Candidates report typically three to five rounds: a recruiter call, one or two technical rounds covering ML and SQL, a case study or take-home assignment, and a final behavioral or values-fit round. The exact structure varies by team and seniority level. Confirm the specific format with your recruiter before you start preparing so you allocate your time correctly.

Does Adyen give a take-home assignment for data scientist roles?

Many candidates report receiving a take-home case study, typically involving a dataset from a payments or e-commerce context. You usually have a few days to complete it. Focus on clear reasoning and business framing rather than building the most complex model. Reviewers typically care more about how you communicate your choices and their trade-offs than about raw performance numbers.

What SQL level does Adyen expect in the interview?

Candidates report questions involving window functions, multi-table joins, CTEs, and aggregations on transactional data. You should be comfortable writing clean, readable queries and explaining your logic step by step as you write. Edge cases like null handling, duplicate rows, and time zone issues in transaction timestamps come up regularly, so practise calling those out proactively.

Is Python or R preferred at Adyen for data science roles?

Python is the clear preference across most data science roles at Adyen, based on publicly available job descriptions. Pandas, scikit-learn, and SQL form the core day-to-day stack. Candidates report that the ability to write clean, production-ready Python, not just notebook-style exploratory code, is something interviewers look for during technical rounds.

How should I prepare specifically for the fraud detection questions?

Understand the fundamentals of class imbalance, precision-recall trade-offs, and why accuracy is a misleading metric for rare-event problems. Study how fraud patterns shift over time (concept drift) and how real-time scoring differs from batch scoring in terms of constraints. Adyen's own engineering blog has publicly available material that gives you a realistic sense of the problems their teams actually work on day to day.

What salary can I expect for a data scientist role at Adyen in India?

Based on current market data, mid-level data scientists with 3-5 years of experience typically fall in the 18-30 LPA band, and senior roles at the 6-9 year level in the 30-48 LPA range. Adyen-specific figures for India are not publicly reported at scale, so verify current numbers on Glassdoor or levels.fyi and use those as your benchmark before entering salary discussions.
