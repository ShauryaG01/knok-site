01 Overview

Overview

Timesinternet is one of India's largest digital products companies, operating brands like Times of India, Economic Times, Cricbuzz, and MX Player. With 20 open roles at the company as of mid-2026, and 89 Android Engineer positions across India (Bangalore leading with 16, Delhi with 12), this is a solid time to target the role.

Candidates typically report a multi-stage interview process: a phone screen covering Android fundamentals, one or two technical rounds with coding and system design, and a final culture-fit or hiring-manager conversation. The focus leans heavily on hands-on Android development, real-world problem solving, and scalability, given the massive user base their apps serve.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you reduced crash rates in a production Android app.

*Situation:* Our content app was seeing frequent crashes on certain low-end devices after a major release. User reviews on the Play Store were getting worse by the day.

*Task:* I was responsible for identifying the root causes and shipping a stable hotfix within the sprint.

*Action:* I pulled crash logs from Firebase Crashlytics, grouped them by device type and OS version, and found that the primary culprit was an unhandled null response from a legacy API endpoint. I added null-safety checks, wrote regression tests, and coordinated with the backend team to fix the API contract. I also set up Crashlytics alerts so we would catch similar spikes early.

*Result:* Crashlytics showed the crash rate drop to near zero within days of the hotfix release. Play Store ratings stabilized, and the alerting system helped us catch two smaller issues before they affected users.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved app performance significantly.

*Situation:* The home feed of our app was lagging noticeably during scrolling, especially when loading image-heavy articles. Users on mid-range phones were the most affected.

*Task:* I needed to make the feed scroll smoothly without reducing the richness of the content displayed.

*Action:* I profiled the app using Android Studio's CPU and memory profilers. The bottleneck was redundant image decoding on the main thread. I switched to Coil for image loading with proper caching, moved JSON parsing off the main thread using coroutines, and implemented RecyclerView view-holder optimizations with DiffUtil.

*Result:* Scroll jank disappeared on test devices. The QA team confirmed smooth performance across a range of phones, and user complaints about lag dropped sharply in the following release cycle.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you collaborated across teams to ship a feature.

*Situation:* Our product team wanted to add live cricket score widgets to the main app, pulling data from a sister product's backend.

*Task:* As the Android developer on the project, I had to integrate with an API I had never used, coordinate with the backend and design teams, and deliver on a tight timeline.

*Action:* I set up a shared channel with the backend team, agreed on an API contract early, and built the feature using MVVM with a clean repository layer so the data source could be swapped if needed. I paired with the designer to match the widget's look to the existing app theme and wrote integration tests against a mock server.

*Result:* The feature shipped on schedule and was picked up by the editorial team for prominent placement during a major cricket series. The modular architecture made it easy to reuse the widget in other sections of the app later.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions

Keep each part tight: one or two sentences for Situation and Task, slightly more for Action (this is where you show your skill), and a concrete Result. Timesinternet's products serve millions, so results that mention user impact, stability, or scale resonate well.

Component-Lifecycle-Tradeoff for technical questions

When asked about an Android concept, structure your answer as: (a) what the component does, (b) where it sits in the Android lifecycle or architecture, and (c) the tradeoffs of using it versus alternatives. For example, when comparing LiveData and StateFlow, explain what each does, how each behaves during configuration changes, and when you would pick one over the other.

Situation-Approach-Outcome for system design

For design questions (like 'architect a news feed'), state the constraints first (offline support, scale, low-end devices), then walk through your approach layer by layer (network, cache, UI), and finally explain what tradeoffs you made and why.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep Android fundamentals, not just library knowledge. Timesinternet apps handle high traffic and diverse devices. Interviewers typically want to see that you understand lifecycles, threading, and memory management at a level deeper than just calling a library function.

Real-world problem solving. Given the scale of products like Cricbuzz and Times of India, candidates who can talk about debugging production issues, handling edge cases on low-end devices, and optimizing for real users stand out.

Ownership and collaboration. Timesinternet operates multiple products under one roof. Interviewers reportedly value candidates who can work across teams, take ownership of a feature end to end, and communicate clearly with non-technical stakeholders.

Modern Android skills. Familiarity with Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, coroutines, Hilt or Dagger, and modular architecture is typically expected. Showing that you stay current with the Android ecosystem signals long-term value.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Fundamentals and coding

Revise Activity and Fragment lifecycles, ViewModel scoping, coroutine structured concurrency, and Jetpack Compose basics. Solve two or three coding problems daily on arrays, strings, and trees, since these are common in Android interviews.

Week 2: System design and architecture

Practice designing screens like a news feed, a live scores widget, or a video player. Focus on offline caching, pagination, and clean architecture (MVVM or MVI). Study how to modularize a large Android codebase.

Week 3: Timesinternet-specific prep

Download Timesinternet apps (Times of India, Cricbuzz, MX Player) and explore them as a developer. Note how they handle push notifications, deep links, content loading, and offline mode. Think about what you would improve and be ready to discuss it.

Week 4: Mock interviews and polish

Do at least two mock interviews focusing on STAR-format answers for behavioral rounds and whiteboard-style system design. Record yourself to check clarity and pacing. Review your resume to ensure every project listed has a clear STAR story ready.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Talking only about libraries, not concepts. Saying 'I used Retrofit' without explaining how HTTP caching, interceptors, or error handling work underneath will not impress.
  1. Ignoring low-end device scenarios. Timesinternet products run on a huge range of Indian smartphones. Candidates who only optimize for flagship devices miss the point.
  1. Skipping the 'why' in architecture answers. Choosing MVVM is not enough. Explain why you picked it over MVI for a given scenario, and what tradeoffs you accepted.
  1. Not knowing the company's products. Walking into a Timesinternet interview without having used Cricbuzz or Times of India is a missed opportunity. Interviewers notice when you reference their own apps.
  1. Giving vague STAR answers. 'I fixed a bug and things improved' is weak. Specify what you did, which tools you used, and what changed as a result, even if you describe the outcome qualitatively.
  1. Neglecting testing questions. Android interviews increasingly cover unit testing, UI testing, and CI pipelines. Brushing these off signals that you ship without quality checks.
