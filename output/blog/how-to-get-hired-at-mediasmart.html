01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Mediasmart is a programmatic advertising technology company that builds mobile DSP (Demand-Side Platform) products, helping brands reach audiences through location-based targeting and mobile campaigns. The company operates as a lean, international team with a strong engineering and data culture.

As of July 2026, mediasmart has 7 open roles listed across functions like engineering, data science, product, and sales. The team is relatively small, which means each hire carries weight. Hiring tends to be deliberate: expect multiple conversations rather than a quick offer.

Most roles are technical or closely tied to ad tech. If you are coming from a digital advertising, data engineering, or mobile product background, you are likely in the right zone. Business development and account management roles do appear from time to time, especially for candidates with experience in the programmatic or DSP space.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Mediasmart's interview process is not publicly documented in detail, but based on patterns common in ad tech companies of similar size, here is what candidates typically experience.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen
Your resume is reviewed by the hiring team or a recruiter. Because mediasmart is a niche ad tech company, they tend to look for signals of domain familiarity: prior work in programmatic advertising, mobile marketing, or data pipelines. Tailor your application to highlight any DSP, RTB (real-time bidding), or audience targeting experience you have.

Stage 2: Initial HR or recruiter call
A short screening call to check your background, current situation, notice period, and salary expectations. Be honest about your notice period upfront. They may also ask why you are interested in ad tech specifically, so have a genuine answer ready.

Stage 3: Technical or functional interview
For engineering roles, this is typically a coding round or a take-home assignment focused on data processing, backend systems, or algorithm problems relevant to ad serving. For data roles, expect SQL, Python, and a case study involving campaign analytics or audience segmentation. For business roles, this stage often involves a scenario-based discussion about client handling or campaign strategy.

Stage 4: Hiring manager interview
A deeper conversation with the team lead or hiring manager. They will probe your thinking on product or technical decisions, how you work in cross-functional teams, and your understanding of the programmatic advertising ecosystem. This is also your chance to ask sharp questions about the team's roadmap.

Stage 5: Final round or culture fit conversation
Some candidates report a final conversation with a senior leader or founder-level person. This is less about hard skills and more about alignment with how the company thinks and operates. They want to know you are genuinely curious about ad tech and can work in a fast, autonomous environment.

Offer and onboarding
If selected, expect a written offer covering compensation, equity (if applicable), and role scope. Mediasmart's team is international, so onboarding may be partly remote.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Mediasmart is an ad tech company at its core, so domain knowledge matters more here than at a generic software company. Here is what they commonly value across roles.

Ad tech and programmatic understanding
Candidates who already know how a DSP works, what RTB means, how bid requests flow, or how audience segments are built will stand out immediately. You do not need to have worked at a DSP before, but you should be able to talk intelligently about mobile advertising and campaign measurement.

Strong data and engineering fundamentals
For technical roles, mediasmart handles high-volume, real-time data: large location datasets, streaming bid requests, and complex pipelines. Comfort with distributed systems, SQL at scale, and Python or Scala is a common expectation. Showing you have worked with large datasets in a production setting is a strong signal.

Ownership mindset
As a lean team, mediasmart does not have layers of management. They want people who can pick up a problem, define the solution, and ship it without being micromanaged. In interviews, give examples of projects where you drove an outcome end to end.

Communication across functions
Ad tech sits at the intersection of engineering, data, media, and sales. The ability to explain a technical concept to a non-technical client, or to translate a business need into a technical requirement, is valued across all roles.

Curiosity about the industry
This is a niche space. Candidates who follow changes in mobile identity (like IDFA deprecation), understand the evolving privacy landscape, or have opinions on where programmatic is heading come across as genuinely motivated rather than just job-hunting.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the basics of programmatic advertising before your first call
You do not need to be an expert, but you should know what a DSP is, how RTB auctions work, and what terms like CPM, CTR, and viewability mean. There are free resources and industry blogs that cover this well. Mediasmart will expect you to speak this language comfortably from the very first conversation.

Tailor your resume to highlight data scale and ad tech exposure
If you have worked with large datasets, real-time systems, or mobile analytics, make that explicit. Mention the scale: rows processed, latency targets, team size. Vague resume bullet points get filtered out quickly in a team that values precision.

Prepare a strong answer for why ad tech
Small ad tech companies hear a lot of candidates who are 'open to anything.' Show genuine interest. Talk about what draws you to programmatic advertising, location data, or mobile targeting. A specific reason beats a generic one every time.

Practice SQL and data case studies
For data and analytics roles, SQL is non-negotiable. Practice writing queries involving joins, window functions, and aggregations on large tables. Be ready to walk through how you would approach a campaign performance analysis from raw log data.

Prepare questions that show you understand their business
Ask about their DSP's differentiator in a crowded market, how they handle signal loss from privacy changes, or what their data infrastructure looks like. Good questions signal that you are already thinking like a team member.

Sort out logistics early
Mediasmart is an international company and some roles may be remote or hybrid. Clarify the work location, time zone expectations, and visa situation (if relevant) in the recruiter call so there are no surprises later.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Mediasmart operates as a compact, international team where individual contributors carry significant responsibility. The culture is generally described as autonomous and results-driven, which suits people who prefer doing over reporting.

Small team, high ownership
Because the team is lean, there is limited bureaucracy. Engineers and data professionals often own features or systems end to end. This can be energising if you like autonomy, but it also means you need to be self-directed and comfortable with ambiguity.

International and remote-friendly
The company has roots in Spain but works with clients and team members across geographies. English is the working language for most technical and cross-functional communication. If you are based in India, some roles may involve working across time zones, so check the expected overlap hours during your interview.

Data and performance culture
In ad tech, everything is measurable. Mediasmart values people who think in metrics, question assumptions with data, and are comfortable being held to clear performance indicators. Decisions are expected to be evidence-based.

Pragmatic and direct communication
The team tends to prefer direct feedback and concise communication over long processes and consensus meetings. If you come from a larger corporate environment, the pace and directness may feel different at first.
