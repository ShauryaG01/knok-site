01 Overview

Overview

Freshworks is one of India's best-known SaaS success stories, building products like Freshdesk, Freshsales, Freshservice, and Freshchat that serve businesses worldwide. Founded in Chennai, the company has grown into a global player while keeping strong roots in the Indian market. Business Analyst roles here sit at the crossroads of product, data, and customer success, making interviews rigorous on several fronts.

Candidates report that the process typically spans multiple rounds covering SQL and data analysis, product thinking, stakeholder communication, and SaaS business fundamentals. Freshworks serves SMBs and mid-market clients globally, so interviewers often test whether you understand customer-facing metrics, onboarding funnels, and churn. As of mid-2026, Freshworks lists 145 open roles, reflecting strong hiring activity.

For context, the broader Business Analyst market in India is active: there are 398 open BA roles tracked across major cities, including 53 in Bangalore, 48 in Delhi, 24 in Mumbai, 21 in Hyderabad, 21 in Pune, and 16 in Chennai.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you used data to influence a product or business decision.

*Situation:* At my previous company, new users were dropping off heavily during the product onboarding flow, and different team members had conflicting theories about why.

*Task:* I was asked to analyse the data, identify the actual cause, and recommend changes the product team could act on.

*Action:* I pulled usage event data and mapped each step of the onboarding journey. I found two steps where users were spending far longer than expected before abandoning. I ran a short survey with a small group of affected users and found they were confused by the integration setup wizard. I built a summary showing the funnel breakdown at each step alongside the survey themes, then presented it to the PM and design lead with a specific recommendation.

*Result:* The team simplified those two steps in the next sprint. The following quarter, the product team reported a clear improvement in onboarding completion and credited it as a key driver for better early engagement.

---

Q: Describe a time when you had to handle conflicting requirements from different internal teams.

*Situation:* At a previous role, the sales team wanted a highly configurable reporting module to close a large enterprise deal, but the product team was focused on keeping the core interface simple for their SMB user base.

*Task:* I needed to find a path that addressed both groups without stalling the roadmap or compromising the product experience for existing customers.

*Action:* I documented the specific use cases each side needed, then pulled available CRM data to understand the revenue contribution of each customer segment. I set up a joint session with both teams to walk through the tradeoffs on a shared screen. I proposed a tiered approach: keep the core module simple for SMB users, but offer advanced configuration as an optional add-on that enterprise customers could enable separately.

*Result:* Both teams aligned on the proposal. It was added to the planning roadmap, and the sales team used the roadmap commitment to secure the enterprise deal.

---

Q: How would you investigate a sudden drop in a key engagement metric?

*Situation:* While working on a customer success dashboard, my team noticed that a key weekly engagement metric had dropped noticeably compared to the previous week.

*Task:* I was responsible for diagnosing the root cause and sharing a clear, actionable finding within a couple of days so the team could respond quickly.

*Action:* I broke the metric down by user segment, geography, and product area to isolate where the drop was concentrated. I found it was limited to users of one specific product module that had received an update the prior week. I cross-referenced with support ticket data and found a cluster of complaints about the redesigned navigation in that module. I compiled the findings into a short brief with annotated screenshots and a rollback recommendation.

*Result:* The product team rolled back the interface change and the metric recovered within a few days. The PM later used the analysis as a template for monitoring future releases.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions: Structure every story as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Freshworks interviewers typically probe for specifics, so be ready to explain what data you used, what tools you worked with, and what happened after your recommendation was implemented.

Issue tree for analytical questions: When asked to diagnose a metric drop or a business problem, start by breaking it into mutually exclusive branches. For example, a drop in active users could come from an acquisition problem (fewer new users coming in), an activation problem (new users not completing setup), or a retention problem (existing users leaving). Walk the interviewer through your branching logic before jumping to a conclusion.

RICE for prioritisation questions: Use Reach (how many customers does this affect), Impact (how much does it move a key metric), Confidence (how certain are you), and Effort (how long to build). Freshworks serves multiple customer tiers, so always clarify which segment you are prioritising for before applying any framework.

Hypothesis-first for case questions: Lead with a clear hypothesis, then describe what data you would look for to confirm or disprove it. This shows structured thinking even before you have access to the numbers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Freshworks interviewers typically look for a combination of analytical rigour and practical product sense. Based on candidate reports, a few qualities come up repeatedly.

SaaS metric fluency: You should be comfortable with concepts like activation rate, churn, net revenue retention, and product-qualified leads. Interviewers want to see that you understand how a SaaS business makes money, keeps customers, and grows, not just how to build a report.

Customer empathy: Freshworks is known for building products that feel easy to use. Interviewers often test whether you would advocate for the end user when internal stakeholders push for complexity.

Clear, jargon-free communication: BA roles sit between technical and non-technical teams. Candidates who can explain data findings in plain language tend to stand out, especially when presenting to sales or support leadership.

End-to-end ownership: Freshworks has a strong bias toward people who follow through. In your answers, make clear that you tracked whether your recommendations actually worked, not just that you made them.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to solutions without structuring the problem first. Freshworks interviewers typically want to see your thinking process, not just your conclusion. Take a moment to break the problem into branches before giving an answer.

Using generic SaaS metrics without tying them to Freshworks. Saying 'I would track DAUs and churn' is less impressive than saying 'For Freshdesk, I would track ticket deflection rate because it directly shows whether customers are getting value from self-service.' Show that you have done your homework on the actual products.

Underestimating the SQL component. Many candidates are surprised by how hands-on the SQL questions can be. Practise writing queries from scratch, not just reading them. Focus on business-relevant scenarios like identifying inactive users or calculating retention by cohort.

Overclaiming results in STAR stories. Candidates sometimes invent large improvement figures to make stories sound better. Interviewers at product-driven companies often ask exactly how you measured a result. If you cannot explain your methodology, a made-up number hurts more than it helps. Stick to results you can genuinely defend.

Not asking questions at the end. Freshworks interviews are a two-way conversation. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare two or three specific, thoughtful questions before you walk in.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Freshworks Business Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves multiple rounds, starting with a recruiter screening call, followed by one or two analytical or technical rounds, and then a round with a senior stakeholder or hiring manager. Some candidates also report a written case study or take-home assignment as part of the process. The exact format varies by team and role level, so it is worth asking the recruiter to outline the structure early on.

Is SQL tested in the Freshworks BA interview?

Yes, candidates consistently report SQL being tested, either as a live coding exercise or as part of a case study. Common question types include filtering and aggregation, joining multiple tables, and writing queries that answer a business question such as 'find users who have not completed onboarding.' Practising on platforms like LeetCode or HackerRank is widely recommended. Focus on business-context problems rather than purely algorithmic puzzles.

Which Freshworks products should I know before the interview?

You should be familiar with at least Freshdesk (customer support ticketing), Freshsales (CRM), and Freshservice (IT service management), as these are Freshworks' flagship products. Signing up for a free trial gives you a much better feel for the user experience than just reading about them. Interviewers often root their questions in specific product scenarios, so hands-on familiarity matters. Understanding how each product serves a different buyer (support teams, sales teams, IT teams) will help you give sharper, more contextual answers.

Does Freshworks expect prior SaaS experience for BA roles?

It depends on the role level. For senior positions, candidates report that prior B2B SaaS or product analytics experience is often preferred. For mid-level roles, a strong grasp of SaaS metrics and customer-facing analysis can substitute for direct industry background. Demonstrating in your answers that you understand concepts like churn, activation, and product-led growth, with real examples from your own work, goes a long way even if your past experience was in a different industry.

How should I prepare for the product case study round?

Practise structured problem-solving by breaking every case into smaller parts before jumping to an answer. Common case types at Freshworks include diagnosing a metric drop, prioritising a feature backlog, and evaluating a new market opportunity. Write out your framework for each type and practise saying it aloud so it sounds natural, not rehearsed. Reviewing publicly available SaaS product teardowns and case studies will help you build intuition for the kinds of tradeoffs Freshworks faces.

What salary can I expect for a Business Analyst role at Freshworks in India?

Freshworks does not publish fixed salary bands publicly, but Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry community-reported figures from current and former employees that can give you a reference range by experience level. It is worth checking both platforms for recent, India-specific data points. Bangalore, with 53 BA openings tracked across the market, and Delhi, with 48, tend to attract competitive compensation because of high demand for analytics talent in those cities.
