02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly, based on what candidates report from Zluri Data Engineer interviews. The focus leans heavily into the company's core domain: SaaS integrations, multi-tenant pipelines, and real-time analytics reliability.

  1. Walk me through how you would design a pipeline to ingest data from hundreds of SaaS APIs that each have different rate limits and schemas.
  2. How would you detect that a SaaS integration has silently started returning incomplete or stale data?
  3. Describe a time you improved the performance of a slow data pipeline. What was the bottleneck and how did you resolve it?
  4. How do you handle schema evolution when upstream SaaS vendors change their API response structure without warning?
  5. Zluri surfaces spend and usage metrics to customers in near real time. How would you architect a system to keep latency low under high write volumes?
  6. What strategies do you use to ensure idempotency in your ETL jobs?
  7. How would you design a data model to track per-user SaaS licence usage across multiple apps for a single enterprise customer?
  8. Explain your approach to data quality checks. Where do you place them in a pipeline and what do you alert on?
  9. You have a daily pipeline that processes a very large volume of rows and it fails halfway through. How do you recover without reprocessing everything from scratch?
  10. How do you approach cost optimisation for cloud data warehouses like BigQuery or Snowflake?
  11. What is your experience with orchestration tools such as Airflow or Prefect? How have you handled dependency failures between DAG tasks?
  12. How would you design access controls so that one customer cannot accidentally see another customer's SaaS usage data in a multi-tenant warehouse?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How have you handled a situation where an upstream API changed its schema and broke a downstream pipeline?

*Situation:* At my previous company we had a production pipeline pulling data from a third-party HR SaaS tool. One morning the vendor silently renamed a key field and added several new optional fields with no notice.

*Task:* I needed to restore the pipeline quickly and prevent the same breakage from happening again without major rework.

*Action:* First I added schema validation at the ingestion layer using a data quality library so any deviation from the expected structure would raise an alert before data moved downstream. I also introduced a raw landing zone where we stored the unmodified API response as JSON, giving us a full replay option. Finally I wrote a lightweight contract test that ran nightly against the vendor's sandbox endpoint to catch drift early.

*Result:* The pipeline recovered within a couple of hours. Over the following months the contract test caught two more undocumented vendor changes before they reached production.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you improved the performance of a slow pipeline.

*Situation:* We had a daily Spark job that was taking several hours to join customer activity data with a product catalogue. The delay was pushing reports past the agreed SLA window.

*Task:* Bring the runtime down significantly without changing the output or requiring new infrastructure.

*Action:* I profiled the job and found two problems. One key had extreme skew, causing a single partition to hold a disproportionate share of data. The product catalogue was also being scanned repeatedly inside a loop. I salted the skewed key to spread it across partitions and cached the catalogue as a broadcast variable. I also rewrote the join order so the smaller table was always broadcast.

*Result:* The runtime dropped by more than half. The fix required no infrastructure changes and the improvement showed up immediately on the next scheduled run.

---

Q: How do you ensure data quality across a multi-tenant pipeline?

*Situation:* Our platform served analytics to multiple enterprise clients from a shared pipeline. One client raised a ticket saying their usage numbers looked wrong, and we had no quick way to trace which pipeline stage introduced the error.

*Task:* Implement data quality checks that could isolate issues to a specific tenant without slowing down the full pipeline.

*Action:* I introduced row-level checksums at each stage and added a tenant tag to every quality-check alert. I set up monitoring dashboards showing per-tenant row counts, null rates, and late-arrival metrics. For critical fields I added threshold alerts that fired when a tenant's record count dropped significantly compared to the prior day.

*Result:* The next data quality issue was caught at the ingestion layer, attributed to the correct tenant within minutes, and resolved before the client noticed. Support tickets related to data quality dropped noticeably over the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioural question. Keep each part tight: one or two sentences on context, one sentence on your specific responsibility, the bulk of your time on what you actually did and why, and a concrete observable result.

For technical and system design questions, a three-part structure works well. Start with Context (what the system looked like and what constraints applied). Move to Approach (what you designed or built and the reasoning behind each choice). Close with Trade-offs (what you gave up and why that was acceptable given the constraints).

For system design questions at Zluri specifically, always clarify scale and SLAs before drawing any architecture. The company handles multi-tenant data and real-time spend signals, so interviewers will probe whether you think about tenant isolation, data freshness, and cloud cost from the start rather than adding them later.

For coding questions, talk through your approach before writing. Mention edge cases: null values, schema mismatches, partial failures, and retry behaviour. Clear, readable code with a brief explanation of your choices typically scores better than clever one-liners with no commentary.

For domain questions about SaaS integrations, frame answers around reliability first. What happens when the third-party API is down, slow, or returns bad data? Zluri's value to customers depends on accurate, up-to-date integration data, so showing that instinct early signals strong product fit.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity. Zluri's product is built on understanding SaaS ecosystems. Interviewers want engineers who have genuinely thought about the challenges of integrating with hundreds of third-party APIs, not just someone who can write a generic ETL job. Reading up on SaaS management concepts before your interview makes a noticeable difference.

Pipeline reliability instincts. Expect questions that probe how you handle failure. Candidates who default to 'it won't fail' or 'we will just re-run it' report being pushed hard. Show that you design for failure from the start, not as an afterthought.

Data ownership mindset. Because Zluri is multi-tenant, data isolation is a core concern. Interviewers typically look for candidates who treat tenant boundaries as a hard constraint built into the schema and pipeline design, not a feature to add later.

Clear communication. Especially in system design rounds, the ability to explain trade-offs in plain language matters. Interviewers want to see that you can work with product and business stakeholders, not just with other engineers.

Practical cost awareness. Cloud infrastructure costs are central to Zluri's product (spend analytics is a core feature). Engineers who think about query cost, storage tiering, and compute efficiency stand out from those who treat cloud resources as unlimited.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core data engineering skills
Review SQL fundamentals: window functions, query optimisation, and reading execution plans. Brush up on distributed processing concepts in Spark or a comparable framework. Practice writing pipeline code with proper error handling, retries, and idempotency built in from the start.

Week 2: Zluri domain prep
Read about SaaS management platforms and the problems IT teams face with shadow IT and licence waste. Think through how you would ingest, normalise, and version data from APIs that have different authentication schemes, pagination styles, and rate limits. Practice designing a multi-tenant data model from scratch without looking anything up.

Week 3: System design and behavioural prep
Work through system design problems focused on real-time data pipelines and multi-tenant architectures. For behavioural rounds, prepare three to five STAR stories covering pipeline failures, performance improvements, and cross-team collaboration. Record yourself answering so you can hear where you ramble or skip the result entirely.

Week 4: Mock interviews and final review
Do at least two full mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Review your answers on schema evolution and data quality scenarios. The day before, read Zluri's product blog and recent announcements so you can tie your answers to their actual use cases and current roadmap.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating every pipeline like a single-tenant system. At Zluri, multi-tenancy is the default. Candidates who design schemas or pipelines without thinking about data isolation between customers are typically flagged in the system design round.

Skipping failure modes in technical answers. A common pattern among candidates who did not progress is that they described the happy path clearly but stumbled when asked 'what if the API returns an error' or 'what if the job crashes halfway.' Always address failure before the interviewer has to prompt you.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved pipeline performance' without explaining your specific technical choice or the observable before-and-after reads as shallow. Even if you cannot share exact proprietary metrics, describe the change in concrete, observable terms.

Not asking clarifying questions in system design. Jumping straight into an architecture without asking about scale, SLAs, or existing infrastructure is a common mistake. Interviewers at product companies like Zluri want to see that you think about constraints before proposing solutions.

Underestimating the domain knowledge component. Zluri is a SaaS tool for SaaS teams. Candidates who come in knowing nothing about SaaS management, OAuth integrations, or licence analytics tend to struggle with product-specific questions even if their core engineering skills are strong.
