01 Overview

Overview

Visa is a global payments technology company, and its India engineering centres are serious product shops building the platforms behind billions of card transactions. 03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would build a reusable, accessible form component in React for a payment flow.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had six payment forms across three products, each built differently, causing inconsistent validation behaviour and repeated accessibility bugs.

*Task:* I was asked to design a single reusable PaymentForm component that all teams could adopt.

*Action:* I started by auditing the existing forms to find common patterns, then built a controlled React component with composable field slots. I added ARIA labels, live-region announcements for validation errors, and keyboard trap management for modal-based flows. I wrote Storybook stories for every variant and a test suite using React Testing Library focused on user interactions rather than implementation details.

*Result:* All six forms adopted the component within one quarter. Accessibility audit findings dropped, and the design team could ship new payment flows without needing engineering support for the boilerplate layer.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product or design decision. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* Our product manager proposed auto-submitting a payment form after the last field was filled, to reduce the number of clicks.

*Task:* I believed this was a usability and risk issue for a payments context, but I needed to raise it constructively rather than simply blocking it.

*Action:* I prepared a short document citing publicly reported incidents from other fintechs where auto-submission caused user complaints, and I proposed a 'Review and Pay' confirmation step as an alternative. I asked for a brief slot to walk the team through it before the decision was finalised.

*Result:* The team agreed to the confirmation step. After launch, support tickets related to accidental payments dropped, and the UX research team flagged the review screen as a positively rated feature in the next user study.

---

Q: A transaction dashboard is loading slowly for users on poor connections. How do you diagnose and fix it?

*Situation:* Our QA team flagged that the dashboard took a long time to become interactive on mobile networks during a testing cycle.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause and ship a fix within the sprint.

*Action:* I ran Lighthouse and Chrome DevTools Network audits. The main culprits were an unoptimised JavaScript bundle and several render-blocking third-party scripts. I set up route-based code splitting using React.lazy, deferred non-critical analytics scripts, and replaced a heavy charting library with a lighter alternative. I also added skeleton loading states so users saw progressive content rather than a blank screen.

*Result:* Performance scores on throttled network profiles moved from failing to passing in Lighthouse audits, and mobile user complaints about loading times dropped in the following support cycle.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical questions, lead with your mental model before writing code. Interviewers want to see how you think, not just whether you know the syntax. A useful pattern: state the constraints, name the trade-offs, then explain your solution and what you would change at larger scale.

For frontend system design questions, use a structured approach: clarify requirements (scale, users, devices, languages), sketch the component tree and data flow, discuss state management, then address performance, accessibility, and security. Visa interviewers specifically notice when candidates think about security implications in UI decisions rather than treating them as a backend concern.

For behavioural questions, use the STAR format: Situation (brief context), Task (what you were responsible for), Action (what you specifically did), Result (observable or measurable outcome). Keep each answer to two to three minutes. Show how you worked with product, design, and backend teams, not just what you built in isolation.

For live coding rounds, think aloud. Narrate your reasoning as you type. If you spot an edge case, call it out before the interviewer does. This signals the systematic thinking Visa values in engineers who touch financial-grade UI.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Visa's engineering culture sits at the intersection of financial rigour and product velocity. Interviewers typically look for three qualities beyond raw technical skill.

Security-first instincts. In a payments company, every UI decision carries a security angle. Candidates who naturally consider XSS vectors, CSRF protection, safe token storage, and input sanitisation stand out from those who treat these as an afterthought or a backend problem.

Scale and reliability thinking. Visa handles enormous transaction volumes globally. Frontend engineers are expected to reason about performance budgets, graceful degradation when APIs are slow, and what users experience during partial outages.

Clear, collaborative communication. Visa teams are cross-functional and often cross-geography. Candidates who can explain technical trade-offs to non-engineers, and who demonstrate they can push back constructively on decisions, score well in behavioural rounds.

Candidates report that showing genuine curiosity about the payments domain, rather than treating this as just another React role, is noticed positively by interviewers.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: JavaScript and React fundamentals.
Revise closures, the event loop, promises, and async/await. Build two or three small React projects from scratch, focusing on hooks, controlled components, and performance patterns like useMemo, useCallback, and React.memo.

Week 2: Security and accessibility.
Study the OWASP top-10 from a frontend perspective: XSS, CSRF, clickjacking, and insecure API data exposure. Practice making a complex component (a modal, a multi-select dropdown, a data table) fully keyboard-navigable with correct ARIA attributes.

Week 3: System design and STAR stories.
Prepare two or three STAR stories from your experience: one covering a technical challenge, one covering a disagreement or conflict, and one covering a process or quality improvement. Practice explaining a past project's architecture in five minutes, including the trade-offs you made.

Week 4: Mock interviews and Visa-specific prep.
Do live mock interviews with a peer or via a practice platform. Read any publicly available information about Visa's developer APIs and engineering work. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask your interviewer about the team's current challenges and priorities.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Saying what without saying why. Stating 'I would use Redux' without explaining the trade-off against lighter alternatives signals a memorised answer. Always explain why you made a specific technical choice in that context.

Ignoring security in UI questions. Candidates who treat frontend as purely visual miss a core Visa expectation. Mention input sanitisation, CSP headers, or safe token storage even when the question does not explicitly ask for it.

Underestimating the behavioural rounds. Many strong technical candidates lose offers at the values or behavioural stage. Prepare STAR stories with the same seriousness you apply to coding prep.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'The team was happy' is weak. Use observable outcomes: fewer support tickets, adoption numbers, improvement in audit scores, or positive findings from a user study.

Not asking questions at the end. Interviewers typically note whether a candidate shows curiosity about the product and the team. Prepare at least two genuine questions rather than saying 'I think you covered everything.'

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Visa Frontend Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves four to five rounds: a recruiter screen, an online coding assessment, two technical interviews covering JavaScript and React (and sometimes a frontend system design question for senior roles), and a final round with behavioural or values-based questions. The exact structure varies by team and level, so confirm with your recruiter after the first call.

What salary can I expect as a Frontend Engineer at Visa in India?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, salary bands for Frontend Engineers in India are 5-11 LPA at entry level, 12-22 LPA at mid level, 24-40 LPA at senior level, and 38-58+ LPA for Lead or Staff roles. These are base salary ranges. Total compensation including performance bonuses and equity is commonly cited as higher; check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for candidate-reported breakdowns with stated sample sizes.

Does Visa use React or Angular, and does it matter which one I know?

Candidates report that both React and Angular are used at Visa depending on the product and team. Most job descriptions for product-facing roles list React as the primary requirement, while some internal tooling teams use Angular. Vue is less commonly seen in Visa postings. Check the specific job description for the role you are applying to, and be honest with your recruiter about which framework you are strongest in.

Does Visa ask frontend system design questions?

Candidates at mid and senior level report that at least one round includes a frontend system design question, such as designing a payment dashboard, a micro-frontend architecture, or an internationalised component library. Entry-level candidates are less likely to face a full system design round, but may be asked to design a component or explain an architectural trade-off. Even a basic framework for approaching component design will help at any level.

How important is accessibility knowledge for the Visa interview?

Accessibility comes up regularly in Visa Frontend Engineer interviews, particularly for senior and lead roles. Interviewers commonly ask candidates to walk through making a component keyboard-navigable or to explain WCAG principles. Visa products are used across diverse populations globally, so accessibility is treated as a core engineering skill rather than a nice-to-have. Having at least one concrete example of implementing ARIA correctly and testing with a screen reader is strongly recommended.

Which cities in India have the most Frontend Engineer openings?

Based on knok jobradar data for Frontend Engineer roles across all companies in India, Bangalore leads with 102 of 405 total openings, followed by Delhi with 36. Visa's India engineering presence is widely reported to be primarily in Bangalore, though the city breakdown for Visa's specific openings is not separately tracked. Confirm the location of your target role with your recruiter, and ask about hybrid or remote arrangements as policies vary by team.
