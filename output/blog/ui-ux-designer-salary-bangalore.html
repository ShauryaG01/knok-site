01 Salary Overview

Salary Overview

Most UI/UX Designer job listings in India do not publish pay. The 6 Bangalore openings tracked by knok's jobradar as of July 2026 are a clear example: not one listing disclosed compensation. This is standard practice on Indian job boards. Pay details are shared only at the offer stage, if at all.

Instead of presenting invented numbers, this guide labels every figure clearly. Ranges from Glassdoor, levels.fyi, and industry surveys are cited as such. The dataset itself is used to show how many roles are active and where.

Bangalore still stands out as one of the top cities for design hiring. With 6 active listings in this snapshot, it sits ahead of Hyderabad (1) and Pune (1), and just behind Delhi (8) for raw job volume. The quality of companies makes the stronger case for Bangalore: funded product startups, design-forward unicorns, and global MNCs with India design studios are all concentrated here. The city consistently offers some of the widest salary bands in design, even if individual listings rarely show them.

02 By Experience Level

By Experience Level

The 6 Bangalore listings in this dataset disclosed no salary bands, so the figures below come from Glassdoor, levels.fyi, and industry surveys. Treat these as reference points, not guarantees. Your actual offer will depend on the company type (agency vs. product startup vs. MNC), the strength of your portfolio, and how well you negotiate.

Fresher (0-2 years). Glassdoor commonly cites UI/UX Designers starting out in Bangalore at 4-8 LPA. Agencies and early-stage startups tend to sit at the lower end. Funded product companies and MNCs typically start higher.

Mid-level (2-5 years). Industry surveys commonly cite 8-18 LPA for designers with solid product experience. A Figma-fluent designer who can run end-to-end research and own a feature from wireframe to final handoff tends to command the upper half of this band.

Senior (5-9 years). Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly place senior UI/UX Designers in Bangalore at 18-35 LPA. At this level, the gap between a good offer and a great offer is almost entirely about portfolio proof and negotiation.

Lead or Principal (9+ years). levels.fyi and industry surveys commonly cite 35-70 LPA for staff designers, design leads, and heads of design at large product companies. The range is wide because titles and responsibilities vary significantly across company types.

04 Negotiation Tips

Negotiation Tips

Since salary data is hidden in most Indian listings, you go into most interviews without knowing the company's budget. Here is how to handle that.

Research before you walk in. Look up the company on Glassdoor and check levels.fyi if it is a product company. Industry surveys for UI/UX roles commonly show wide bands by seniority, so knowing even the rough range helps you anchor correctly from the start.

Let them name a number first. When asked about your expectation, it is perfectly acceptable in Indian hiring culture to ask what the role's budget is. Many hiring managers will tell you, especially at startups. If they push you for a figure, give a range with your floor set at what you actually want.

Your portfolio is your leverage. Unlike many tech roles, a senior designer's offer is more negotiable because the value of the work is visible. If you can point to shipped products, measurable outcomes, or a recognisable brand in your portfolio, use it explicitly when asking for the higher end of the range.

Counter at least once. Publicly reported data consistently shows that candidates who counter their first offer get a better outcome more often than those who accept immediately. A simple 'I was hoping for X based on my experience and the scope of this role' is enough to open the conversation.

Negotiate the full package. If base salary has a ceiling, ask about joining bonus, ESOPs (for startups), variable pay, and WFH flexibility. These are all part of the real value of an offer.

05 Total Comp Breakdown

Total Comp Breakdown

Most Bangalore-based UI/UX Designer offers have several components beyond base salary. Understanding each one helps you compare offers that look similar on paper.

Base salary. This is the fixed annual amount, and the number most listings (when they do show one) call 'CTC.' It is the primary figure to compare across companies.

Variable pay. Some product companies include a performance bonus, commonly cited at 10-20% of base for mid-senior designers. Startups sometimes skip variable pay and increase ESOPs instead.

ESOPs (Employee Stock Ownership Plan). Funded startups frequently include ESOPs in the package. Industry surveys commonly cite a 4-year vesting period with a 1-year cliff as the standard structure. Whether ESOPs translate to real money depends entirely on whether the company exits.

Joining bonus. Occasionally offered to cover unvested stock or a notice period buy-out at a previous company. It is one-time and non-recurring, so factor that into your second-year take-home expectations.

Benefits and perks. Health insurance, meal allowance, internet reimbursement, and learning budgets are all worth asking about. For designers specifically, a licensed Figma seat and access to user research tools are practical asks that good employers will honour.

In-hand vs. CTC. The number on your offer letter is CTC, not in-hand pay. PF contributions, professional tax, and income tax will reduce your monthly take-home noticeably. Use a salary calculator to verify what you will actually receive each month.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Why do most UI/UX Designer job listings in India not show the salary?

Indian companies almost universally leave salary off public listings to keep candidates from self-selecting out and to preserve negotiation leverage. The practice is far more common here than in markets like the US or UK, where pay transparency laws are becoming standard. It means you will need to research ranges yourself using Glassdoor, speak to people inside the company on LinkedIn, or ask the recruiter directly on the first call.

Is Bangalore or Delhi better for UI/UX Designer jobs right now?

Delhi has more raw openings in this snapshot (8 vs. 6 in Bangalore), but Bangalore's listings tend to come from funded product startups and tech companies that invest in design as a core function rather than a support role. If you are building a career in product-led design culture, Bangalore is generally the stronger market. If you are open to enterprise design work or government-linked digital projects, Delhi opens more doors.

What skills push a UI/UX Designer's salary higher in Bangalore?

Proficiency in Figma, experience running end-to-end user research, and a portfolio showing shipped products rather than just concepts are the three things that most consistently push salaries higher, based on what hiring managers describe in job descriptions and industry surveys. Familiarity with design systems and the ability to hand off to developers without back-and-forth is increasingly listed as a hard requirement at mid-senior levels.

Do agencies pay less than product companies for UI/UX Designers?

Yes, agencies commonly pay less than product companies for equivalent experience, based on publicly reported data and industry surveys. The trade-off is breadth: agencies often give early-career designers access to a wider variety of industries and client problems in a shorter time. Transitioning from an agency to an in-house product role after a few years, with a meaningful salary jump, is a well-trodden path in Bangalore's design community.

How do I find out a company's salary range before the interview?

Glassdoor is the most accessible starting point, and levels.fyi covers product companies particularly well. Speaking to former employees via LinkedIn tends to yield more current and specific numbers than either platform. Some designers also ask the recruiter directly: 'Can you share the budgeted range for this role?' Many will answer, especially at mid-sized startups that want to move quickly.

Should I negotiate even if the offer is from a small startup?

Yes, always negotiate. Even small startups have flexibility, particularly on ESOPs, joining bonuses, and remote work terms if not on base salary. A polite counter rarely costs you the offer. Publicly reported hiring data suggests most candidates who do not counter leave real value on the table, so treat the first offer as the opening position, not the final answer.
