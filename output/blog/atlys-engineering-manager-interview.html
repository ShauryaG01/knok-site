01 Overview

Overview

Atlys is a travel-tech company that automates visa applications for Indian travellers, removing the paperwork nightmare millions of Indians face every time they plan an international trip. It is a funded startup moving at speed, and Engineering Manager is one of its most consequential hires. The company currently has 29 open roles, making this a live opportunity worth targeting seriously.

Candidates report a multi-stage process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical leadership conversation, a values and culture interview, and a final round with a senior leader. The exact structure can vary, so confirm the stages with your recruiter early. Atlys wants EMs who are as comfortable reviewing an API reliability design as they are giving hard feedback to an underperforming engineer. The role is not for pure people-managers who have stepped away from technical thinking.

For context on the broader market, knok jobradar showed 975 open Engineering Manager roles across India as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 182 openings, Delhi at 53, and Pune and Chennai at 20 each. Salary bands from the same data put Manager-level EM roles at 35-60 LPA and Senior Manager roles at 55-90 LPA.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Atlys interviewers probe two things above all: your ability to run a lean, high-trust engineering team, and your technical credibility on distributed and API-heavy systems. Candidates report these questions come up most often.

  1. Walk me through how you have built and grown an engineering team from scratch.
  2. Describe a time you had to make a hard technical trade-off under deadline pressure. How did you decide?
  3. How do you measure the health and productivity of your team?
  4. Tell me about a time you managed a sustained underperformer. What was the outcome?
  5. How do you balance engineering excellence with shipping speed in a startup?
  6. Atlys depends on third-party government APIs it cannot control. How would you manage reliability in that environment?
  7. How do you run 1:1s, and what do you track over time?
  8. Tell me about a cross-functional conflict between engineering and product that you had to resolve.
  9. Walk me through your hiring process for a senior engineer role.
  10. What does your incident response process look like, and how do you run post-mortems?
  11. How do you keep technical skills sharp on your team while maintaining delivery velocity?
  12. How have you handled technical debt in a high-growth environment?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioural question. Here are three worked examples.

Q: Tell me about a time you managed a sustained underperformer.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a mid-level engineer consistently missed sprint commitments and his code reviews were creating delays for the rest of the team.

*Task:* As EM, it was my responsibility to address this before it damaged team morale and our delivery record.

*Action:* I set up a structured improvement plan with fortnightly check-ins. I paired him with a senior engineer for one sprint to surface specific skill gaps, gave direct written feedback after every review cycle, and made expectations explicit in writing so there was no ambiguity.

*Result:* Within two quarters his delivery improved and he started contributing to architecture discussions. The team's sprint completion rate rose noticeably. Had the plan not worked, I was prepared to escalate, but that step was not needed.

---

Q: Describe a time you resolved a conflict between engineering and product.

*Situation:* Product wanted to launch a new visa-tracking feature in three weeks. Engineering estimated the work at six weeks because of integration complexity with a third-party document verification API.

*Task:* I needed a path that kept the product roadmap moving without creating a reliability risk for end users.

*Action:* I ran a joint scoping session with the product manager and two senior engineers. We agreed to ship a smaller version in three weeks with a manual fallback flow, then complete the full integration in the next cycle. I wrote up the trade-offs in a shared doc so leadership had visibility into what we were deferring and why.

*Result:* We launched on schedule with zero critical bugs. The full integration shipped two sprints later. The product manager later cited this session as a model for how engineering and product should scope together.

---

Q: How have you handled technical debt in a high-growth environment?

*Situation:* My team had inherited a monolith that was slowing down every new feature. The business was simultaneously demanding a new product line.

*Task:* I needed a debt reduction plan that did not stop feature delivery.

*Action:* I introduced a policy where every sprint reserved a fixed capacity slice for debt reduction. I created a debt register and worked with product to translate each item into a business risk, which made prioritisation straightforward. I also ran monthly architecture reviews so the team could flag new debt before it compounded.

*Result:* Over three quarters, deployment frequency improved and incidents tied to legacy code dropped. Product became an active advocate for the debt reduction process once they saw faster feature delivery as a result.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for every behavioural question. Keep each component tight: one sentence for Situation, one for Task, two or three for Action (this is where interviewers listen hardest), and one for Result. At Atlys, the Action section should show that you made a conscious decision, not just that things happened around you.

The 'Why Behind the Decision' layer matters here. For technical trade-off questions, briefly explain the constraints you were operating under: timeline, team size, system dependencies. This signals that you think like a product-aware engineer, not just a process manager.

The Manager-IC spectrum is a frame Atlys interviewers use consciously. When asked about hands-on technical involvement, be specific about when you reviewed architecture, stepped into a design discussion, or deliberately stepped back and trusted your team. Vague answers like 'I stayed close to the work' do not land.

Numbers anchor your stories. Team size, hiring volume, scale of the system you owned, or delivery outcomes all make your answers credible. Use real numbers from your own career freely. Just make sure they are accurate, because follow-up questions will probe them.

The Atlys context hook. Where you can, connect your answer to Atlys's world: consumer trust, government API reliability, visa processing accuracy. Candidates who tie their past experience to the company's specific challenges consistently stand out.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Atlys is a startup, so interviewers are not looking for a process-heavy enterprise manager. They want someone who can do three things at once: lead people well, maintain technical credibility, and move fast without breaking user trust.

People leadership signals they look for: Have you grown engineers through coaching and promotion? Can you give hard feedback without losing the relationship? Do you absorb chaos from above and protect your team, or do you simply pass it down?

Technical credibility signals: Can you hold your own in a system design conversation? Do you understand trade-offs between reliability, speed, and cost in an API-heavy product? Have you made architectural decisions you can defend with reasoning, not just outcomes?

Startup mindset signals: Atlys is not a large enterprise. Interviewers want to see comfort with ambiguity, the ability to decide with incomplete information, and a view of process as a tool rather than a comfort blanket. Candidates who over-engineer their governance answers often raise flags.

Values fit: Atlys publicly emphasises trust, speed, and customer obsession. Candidates report that cultural fit questions are taken seriously. Answers that show you have engaged with the company's actual mission, making international travel easier for Indians, land better than generic leadership talking points.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

One week before your first round, do three things. First, read Atlys's product and understand the core user problem: Indian travellers navigating visa applications. Second, prepare five strong STAR stories from your own career covering team building, conflict resolution, a technical trade-off, a hiring decision, and an incident response. Third, practise talking through a system design for a high-availability integration with a third-party API, since this maps directly to Atlys's engineering challenges.

Three days before, review your own metrics. Be ready to cite team sizes, hiring numbers, and delivery outcomes from your own experience. Vague answers are the most common reason candidates do not advance past the second round.

The day before, do a mock interview out loud with a peer or solo. Time your STAR answers. Most should land between two and three minutes. Anything longer loses the interviewer's thread.

After each round, send a short, specific thank-you note to your recruiter. Mention one concrete topic from the conversation. This is not about formality; it keeps you memorable in a pipeline that may have multiple strong candidates.

For the salary conversation, knok jobradar data puts Manager-level EM roles in the 35-60 LPA range. Know your number before the call, anchor at the higher end of the band, and be ready to explain your reasoning based on your current package and the scope of the role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Atlys like a large tech company. Candidates who talk about managing very large enterprise teams, multi-year transformation roadmaps, or heavyweight governance frameworks often signal a mismatch. Atlys wants lean, decisive managers who can execute.

Vague impact claims. Saying 'I improved team performance' without specifics is unconvincing. Use real numbers from your own career: team sizes, engineers promoted, incidents resolved, delivery improvements. The interviewer cannot verify your claims but they can tell when you are being precise versus padding.

Skipping technical depth. Some EM candidates assume the role is purely managerial and skip system design prep. At Atlys, you will likely be asked about reliability, API design, or architectural trade-offs. Being underprepared here is a common reason for rejection.

Underplaying people challenges. Atlys interviewers ask about underperformers, difficult feedback, and exits. Candidates who give sanitised, conflict-free answers are not believed. Be direct about the hard moments and what you learned from them.

Not researching the product. Generic answers that could apply to any company lose points. Candidates who can speak to visa processing reliability, government API dependencies, or consumer trust consistently score higher.

Over-explaining at the expense of clarity. Long, circuitous STAR answers frustrate interviewers. Practise being crisp. If an interviewer wants more detail, they will ask a follow-up.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Atlys Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates report a process of three to five rounds, though the exact structure varies. A recruiter screen is typically followed by a technical leadership conversation, a values and culture round, and a final interview with a senior leader. Atlys moves relatively quickly for a startup, and the full process often wraps up within two to three weeks. Confirm the exact stages with your recruiter at the start of the process.

Is there a coding round for Engineering Manager roles at Atlys?

Candidates report that pure coding tests are uncommon for EM roles at Atlys, but technical depth is still tested through system design and architecture discussions. You may be asked to walk through how you would design a reliable integration with a third-party API or how you would approach a reliability problem in a distributed system. Being underprepared on system design is a more common failure point than being asked to write code.

What salary can I expect for an Engineering Manager role at Atlys?

Based on knok jobradar data, Manager-level EM roles in India fall in the 35-60 LPA range, and Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range. Atlys is a funded startup, so total compensation typically includes equity alongside base pay. The final number depends on your current package, the scope of the role, and how the negotiation goes. Anchor your ask toward the higher end of the band and be ready to explain your reasoning.

How much does Atlys weigh cultural fit versus technical skills for this role?

Both matter, and candidates report that cultural fit questions are taken seriously. The company's stated values around speed, trust, and customer obsession come up directly in interviews. Technical credibility is also a hard requirement, so it is not an either-or tradeoff. The strongest candidates connect their technical decisions to business and user outcomes, rather than treating people leadership and engineering as separate tracks.

How should I prepare for a system design question specific to Atlys?

Focus on systems that depend on external APIs you do not control, since that is Atlys's core engineering challenge. Think through retry logic, circuit breakers, fallback flows, and how you communicate reliability risks to a product or business audience. Practise explaining a design to both a technical peer and a non-engineer, since Atlys EMs are expected to bridge both audiences comfortably.
