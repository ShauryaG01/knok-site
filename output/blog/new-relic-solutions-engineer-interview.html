01 Overview

Overview

New Relic is a cloud observability company whose platform brings together application performance monitoring, infrastructure metrics, distributed tracing, and log management in one unified place. As a Solutions Engineer at New Relic, you are the technical face of the sales cycle: you run product demos, lead proof-of-concept evaluations, field deep technical questions, and help engineering teams see clear value in the platform before they sign.

The Solutions Engineer market across India is active. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings nationwide. Demand is concentrated in Bangalore (55 roles), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20), with additional openings in Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). New Relic itself had 72 open roles at that time, making it one of the more active hirers in the observability space.

On compensation, New Relic does not publish salary bands publicly. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for Solutions Engineers in India vary considerably by city and seniority band, so it is worth researching those sources and confirming the specific level with your recruiter before negotiations begin.

The interview process typically spans several stages: a recruiter call, a hiring manager conversation, a technical screen, a live demo or presentation exercise, and a final round with senior leadership. Candidates report the full process taking two to four weeks.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

New Relic's Solutions Engineer interviews test both technical depth and the ability to communicate complex ideas clearly to different audiences. Here are the questions candidates commonly report across rounds:

  1. Walk me through how you would demo New Relic's APM capabilities to a developer team that has never used an observability platform.
  2. How does New Relic differ from Datadog or Dynatrace? What would you lead with in a competitive evaluation?
  3. A customer tells you their application is slow and users are complaining. Using New Relic, walk me through how you would help them find the root cause.
  4. You have a prospect already using open-source tools like Prometheus and Grafana. How do you make the case for New Relic's value?
  5. How comfortable are you learning a proprietary query language, and how would you teach NRQL to a customer's engineering team?
  6. Describe a proof-of-concept you ran where the customer was initially skeptical. What was your approach and what was the outcome?
  7. How would you handle a technical objection during a live demo that you cannot answer on the spot?
  8. Tell me about a time you worked closely with an Account Executive to move a deal forward. What was your specific technical contribution?
  9. How would you structure a plan to help a new customer get their first set of services fully instrumented on New Relic within their first month?
  10. A customer wants to monitor a microservices application built on Go and Kubernetes. Walk me through the instrumentation approach you would recommend.
  11. How do you manage your time when several customer proof-of-concepts are running in parallel and all of them need your attention at once?
  12. Describe a situation where a product limitation cost you a deal. How did you handle it?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a proof-of-concept you ran where the customer was initially skeptical.

*Situation:* I was supporting a mid-size fintech firm evaluating New Relic alongside a long-standing competitor. Their CTO had used the competitor's tool for years and was openly doubtful that anything else could match it.

*Task:* My goal was to design a proof-of-concept that would give their SRE team a genuine hands-on experience and produce a fair comparison, not just a polished slide deck.

*Action:* I spent the first two days pairing with their engineers to instrument their three most critical services using New Relic's APM agents. I built custom dashboards around the KPIs they actually cared about rather than generic templates. Midway through, I set up an alert that caught a latency spike in their payments service before their own monitoring had fired. I documented the finding clearly and walked the CTO through the trace in a short call.

*Result:* That moment shifted the entire conversation. The team chose New Relic at the end of the proof-of-concept, citing the ease of distributed tracing and the quality of the alert. The CTO later told the Account Executive it was the live demo moment, not the slide deck, that made the difference.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked closely with an Account Executive to move a deal forward.

*Situation:* We had a large e-commerce prospect stalled at the technical evaluation stage. The Account Executive had built a strong relationship with the commercial team, but the engineering side kept raising objections about data residency and agent overhead.

*Task:* I needed to address those technical blockers without derailing the commercial timeline the AE had already set with the business stakeholders.

*Action:* I arranged a focused call with the prospect's platform architects and walked them through New Relic's data ingest controls, regional hosting options, and publicly available agent overhead benchmarks. I also prepared a one-page technical FAQ the AE could share in follow-up emails so engineering questions would not pile up between calls.

*Result:* The technical objections cleared within a week. The AE moved straight to commercial negotiation and the deal closed that quarter.

---

Q: How would you handle a technical objection during a live demo that you cannot answer on the spot?

*Situation:* During a demo to a logistics company, a senior engineer asked a specific question about how New Relic handles high-cardinality metrics at scale, citing numbers from their own environment that I had not seen in advance.

*Task:* I needed to keep the demo and the room's confidence intact without giving an inaccurate answer under pressure.

*Action:* I said clearly: 'That is a great specific question and I want to give you an accurate answer rather than guess. Let me note it and come back to you with the right data by tomorrow morning.' I kept the demo moving, covered the remaining features, and that evening I worked with our product team to pull precise documentation and a relevant reference case. I sent a detailed written response the next morning.

*Result:* The engineer replied that he appreciated the honesty. It built more trust than a confident but vague answer would have. The evaluation continued and eventually converted to a paid account.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for experience questions: Structure your answer as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (one or two sentences each) so you spend more time on the Action, which is where interviewers judge your thinking. Always close with a concrete Result that shows real impact.

Problem-Solution-Impact for technical walkthroughs: When asked how you would solve a customer's technical problem, start by restating the problem in your own words to show you listened. Then describe the specific steps or New Relic features you would use. Close by describing the impact the customer would experience. This structure works well for questions like 'A customer's app is slow, walk me through how you diagnose it.'

Competitive positioning: When comparing New Relic to a competitor, avoid talking down the other product. A clean approach: acknowledge the competitor's genuine strengths honestly, then pivot to a specific customer scenario where New Relic's approach is a better fit. Interviewers at product companies notice when you are fair and nuanced rather than dismissive.

Objection handling: Use a three-step pattern. First, validate the concern so the customer feels heard. Second, ask a clarifying question to make sure you understand exactly what is behind the objection. Third, address the specific concern with evidence, a product reference, or a concrete answer. Practise this pattern until it feels natural in a live setting with a real audience.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

New Relic Solutions Engineer interviewers look for a combination of technical credibility and communication skill. Neither alone is sufficient.

Technical depth: You should be able to speak fluently about observability concepts: metrics, logs, traces, distributed tracing, instrumentation agents, alerting, and dashboards. Familiarity with modern stacks such as Kubernetes, microservices, and cloud-native architectures is a strong signal. You do not need to be a software developer, but you must be comfortable in a terminal and able to read code.

Customer empathy: Candidates who succeed approach every technical question through the lens of customer value. Instead of describing a feature, they describe the problem the feature solves and what the customer actually experiences as a result. This is a pre-sales role, so empathy and storytelling carry as much weight as technical knowledge.

Competitive awareness: New Relic operates in a crowded market. Interviewers want to see that you understand the landscape and can position clearly without being dismissive of alternatives. Knowing where Datadog, Dynatrace, and open-source options fit, and where New Relic has a genuine edge, is a real advantage going into any round.

Ownership and follow-through: Pre-sales cycles involve many moving parts. Interviewers look for evidence that you take ownership of the technical relationship, follow up on commitments, and keep both the customer and the internal team aligned. Your STAR stories should demonstrate this quality clearly, not just claim it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Platform and product knowledge

Sign up for New Relic's free tier and instrument a small personal project or a public sample application. Explore APM, distributed tracing, logs, and dashboards firsthand. Write several NRQL queries so you are not seeing the language for the first time during an interview. Read New Relic's public documentation on key integrations such as Kubernetes, AWS, and popular language agents for Java and Python.

Week 2: Competitive and market context

Research how New Relic positions against Datadog and Dynatrace. Read publicly available analyst commentary and the G2 or Gartner Peer Insights review pages to understand what real customers say about each platform. Prepare a short and honest comparison you could deliver confidently if asked in any round.

Week 3: Story preparation and mock demos

Write out your best STAR stories covering: a successful proof-of-concept, a competitive win, an objection you handled well, and a time you collaborated closely with a sales colleague. Practise delivering each story clearly and concisely. Record yourself doing a mock product demo, watch it back, and cut anything that is unclear or too slow.

Before each round

Research the interviewer on LinkedIn if you know their name in advance. Review recent news about New Relic's product direction. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for each round that show you have done your homework on the platform and the company.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going too deep too fast in a demo: Many candidates dive straight into product features before understanding what the audience cares about. Always open with a question: what does success look like for you in this evaluation? Then tailor everything that follows to that answer.

Treating the role as purely technical: This is a pre-sales position. Candidates who focus only on technical architecture and skip the commercial and relationship side of the job consistently score lower. Show that you understand how your technical work maps to revenue and customer retention.

Bluffing on technical questions: Interviewers at observability companies know their product deeply. If you do not know an answer, say so and describe how you would find it. Guessing and getting it wrong costs more credibility than honestly admitting a gap.

Weak competitive knowledge: Many candidates say 'New Relic is better than Datadog' without being able to say why in a specific customer scenario. Generic claims are unconvincing. Prepare concrete examples tied to real customer problems and real product differences.

Under-preparing the demo: Candidates who have never actually logged into New Relic stand out immediately, and not in a good way. Hands-on practice with the free tier is the single highest-return preparation activity for this role.

Not proposing the next step: In pre-sales, always end an interaction by proposing what happens next. Show this instinct in your interview answers. It signals that you think like a seller and a partner, not just a technician.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What does a Solutions Engineer at New Relic actually do day to day?

You work alongside Account Executives to help prospects evaluate New Relic's platform before they buy. On a typical day you might run a technical discovery call with a customer's SRE team, build a custom dashboard for an active proof of concept, respond to a security questionnaire, and join a deal review with the sales leader. The role is roughly half customer-facing and half internal coordination with sales, product, and support teams.

Do I need to know how to code to get this role?

You do not need to be a software developer, but you need to be comfortable at the command line and able to read and lightly modify code. New Relic's instrumentation often involves dropping an agent into an application or writing a small script to push custom metrics. Candidates who can do this confidently have a clear edge over those who are purely business-focused.

How many interview rounds does New Relic typically have for this role?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a technical interview, a demo or presentation exercise, and a final round with senior leadership or cross-functional stakeholders. The exact structure can vary by location and team, so ask your recruiter to map out the rounds clearly at the start of your process.

What salary can I expect for this role in India?

New Relic does not publish fixed salary bands publicly, and the knok data for this role did not include verified compensation ranges. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for Solutions Engineers in India vary considerably by city and seniority level. Research those sources before your first recruiter call so you can anchor your expectations and negotiate from an informed position.

How important is knowledge of New Relic's competitors in the interview?

Very important. New Relic competes directly with Datadog, Dynatrace, and open-source stacks like Prometheus paired with Grafana. Interviewers want to see that you can articulate a clear and honest competitive position without being dismissive of alternatives. Candidates who have done their homework on the landscape consistently report competitive questions coming up in at least one round.

Is there a live product demo as part of the interview process?

Many candidates report being asked to deliver a product demo or a short presentation as part of the evaluation. The format varies: sometimes it is a structured demo to a mock customer panel, sometimes it is a more conversational walkthrough. Practising a live demo with New Relic's free tier before your interview is one of the most impactful ways you can prepare, and candidates who skip this step tend to show it.
