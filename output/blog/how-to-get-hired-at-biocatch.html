01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

BioCatch is a behavioral biometrics company that helps banks and financial institutions detect online fraud in real time. Instead of relying only on passwords or OTPs, it studies *how* a person types, moves a mouse, or scrolls on a touchscreen, then flags sessions that feel 'off'. That makes it a high-stakes, technically demanding product, and the hiring bar reflects that.

As of July 2026, BioCatch has 24 open roles spanning data science, backend engineering, product management, and security research. The team is lean, hiring is selective, and each role carries real ownership. If you are targeting a company where your work directly protects people's money from fraud, BioCatch is worth pursuing seriously.

India-based candidates typically apply for roles in R&D, data engineering, and software development. The process is run centrally but often involves interviews with colleagues spread across multiple countries.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at BioCatch commonly runs across five to six stages, though the exact flow depends on the role and seniority level. Timelines based on publicly reported candidate experiences tend to run four to six weeks from application to offer.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. Recruiters look for relevant experience in fraud detection, fintech, ML, or large-scale backend systems. Tailor your resume to highlight any work involving anomaly detection, real-time data pipelines, or behavioural data. With 24 open roles listed as of July 2026, it is worth checking whether more than one position fits your background.

Stage 2: HR call. A recruiter checks your background, motivation for joining BioCatch, and basic logistics like your notice period and location preference. Be ready to explain, in your own words, what BioCatch does and why you want to work there specifically.

Stage 3: Technical screen. For engineering roles this is usually a live coding round covering data structures, algorithms, or SQL. For data science roles expect statistics, ML fundamentals, and sometimes a short take-home problem. The difficulty is publicly reported as moderate to hard, not just 'easy LeetCode'.

Stage 4: Deep technical rounds (one or two interviews). These go into your domain: system design for backend candidates, model design and feature engineering for data scientists, or security architecture for research roles. Interviewers want to see how you reason through a problem, not just whether you land on the right answer.

Stage 5: Behavioural interview. A senior team member or hiring manager asks about past projects, how you handled ambiguous situations, and how you collaborate across time zones. BioCatch teams span multiple countries, so cross-functional communication is a real focus here.

Stage 6: Offer. Compensation discussions happen with the recruiter. Based on Glassdoor and industry surveys for fraud-tech companies of a similar profile, packages are generally considered competitive. Exact figures vary by level and role.

Junior roles sometimes skip the system-design round. Senior roles may add a presentation or case study before the offer stage.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

BioCatch hires for a mix of deep technical skill and genuine curiosity about the fraud-detection problem. Across roles, a few themes come up consistently.

Domain awareness. You do not need a research background in behavioral biometrics to apply, but you should understand the basics: why passive behavioural signals are harder to spoof than static credentials, and what false-positive and false-negative costs look like in a fraud context. Candidates who come in already thinking about these trade-offs stand out from the crowd.

Strong ML or engineering fundamentals. For data science roles, expect questions on classification, anomaly detection, imbalanced datasets, and model evaluation metrics. For backend roles, the focus is on high-throughput, low-latency systems, since BioCatch processes signals in near-real-time at scale.

Precision mindset. In fraud detection, a false positive means a real customer gets blocked. A false negative means a fraudster slips through. Interviewers look for people who think carefully about those trade-offs rather than people who simply optimise for shipping speed.

Cross-functional communication. BioCatch works with bank clients, internal product teams, and engineering. Being able to explain a complex model or system to a non-technical stakeholder is a real differentiator at the interview stage.

Ownership and self-direction. Teams are distributed and relatively flat. You will be expected to pick up context quickly and drive your work without heavy hand-holding from managers.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product before your first call. Read publicly available material on behavioral biometrics: how it works, why banks use it, and what makes it different from traditional fraud tools. Being able to describe BioCatch's value proposition in your own words signals genuine interest, not just a scatter-shot job search.

Sharpen your ML and statistics foundations. Focus on classification metrics (precision, recall, F1, AUC), handling class imbalance, feature engineering from time-series data, and real-time model inference. These come up repeatedly in technical screens for data science roles.

Practice system design for data-intensive systems. If you are going for a backend or data engineering role, study how to design streaming pipelines, low-latency APIs, and fault-tolerant architectures. Think about how you would process a very large volume of user sessions per day without dropping events or introducing latency spikes.

Prepare concrete project stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for the behavioural round. Pick examples where you dealt with messy data, a hard technical constraint, or a stakeholder disagreement that you resolved using evidence.

Read their published research and blog posts. BioCatch researchers publish on fraud trends, behavioural signals, and detection methods. Reading even a couple of posts gives you talking points and shows you have done your homework before the interview begins.

Apply to the right role among the open listings. With 24 roles currently open, there may be more than one that fits your background. Apply to the one whose requirements align most closely with your strongest skills, not the one with the fanciest title.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

BioCatch frames its mission around protecting people's money from fraud, and that gives teams a shared sense of purpose that goes beyond shipping features. Employees across publicly available reviews commonly note that the problem space itself is motivating day to day.

The culture leans technical and analytical. Opinions are expected to be backed by data. Debate is welcomed but needs to be constructive and move toward a decision quickly. If you prefer environments where instinct or seniority alone drives choices, BioCatch may feel like a mismatch.

The team is globally distributed, with colleagues spread across multiple time zones and offices. Async communication matters: writing clearly, documenting decisions, and being reliable across time-zone gaps are all valued habits. People who can keep projects moving without constant meetings tend to do well here.

Growth within the company tends to come from taking ownership of hard problems rather than waiting for a formal promotion track. Performance is visible in a team of this size, and people who step up on difficult projects tend to find their scope expanding naturally.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How hard is it to get a job at BioCatch compared to a typical product company?

BioCatch sits in a niche where deep technical skill and domain knowledge both matter, which raises the bar compared to a generalist product company. The team is relatively lean, so each hire is scrutinised carefully. That said, if your background is in ML, fraud analytics, or high-throughput backend systems, you are already in the right lane. Preparation on the domain side (behavioral biometrics and fraud detection trade-offs) can meaningfully improve your odds.

Does BioCatch hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

Most of the 24 open roles as of July 2026 appear to target candidates with some industry experience, but BioCatch does occasionally hire strong final-year students or recent graduates for research or junior engineering positions. If you are a fresher, focus your resume on any relevant project work involving anomaly detection, time-series data, or security, and look for roles explicitly labelled 'junior' or 'associate'. A strong internship in fintech or cybersecurity makes a meaningful difference at this stage.

What salary can I expect at BioCatch in India?

BioCatch does not publish salary bands publicly. Based on Glassdoor and industry surveys for fraud-tech companies of a similar profile, compensation is generally considered competitive for the segment. The actual package varies by role, level, and negotiation. It is worth researching comparable roles on Glassdoor and levels.fyi to arrive at a well-informed ask before the offer conversation.

Is BioCatch open to remote or hybrid work for India-based roles?

Policies can change, so confirm directly during the HR call rather than assuming. Publicly reported information suggests BioCatch offers flexible or hybrid arrangements for many technical roles, given their globally distributed team structure. If your preference is fully remote, raise it early in the process rather than at the offer stage, so there are no surprises on either side.

What are common reasons candidates get rejected at BioCatch?

Commonly cited reasons from candidate feedback include weak fundamentals in ML or system design, and an inability to explain trade-offs clearly (for example, why you would favour precision over recall in a fraud-detection context). Generic behavioural answers that lack specific examples also hurt, as does arriving without any knowledge of what BioCatch actually does. Preparation on both the technical and domain fronts reduces all of these risks significantly.

How long does the BioCatch hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, the end-to-end process commonly takes four to six weeks, though it can run shorter for urgent roles or longer if scheduling across time zones becomes tricky. Following up politely with the recruiter after each stage is normal and acceptable. If you have a competing offer with a deadline, mention it early so the team can try to align their timeline with yours.
