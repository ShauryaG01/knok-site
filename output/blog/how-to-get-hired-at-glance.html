01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Glance is a Bengaluru-based AI and content platform best known for its lock screen content discovery product. Backed by investors including Google and Mithril Capital, it serves users across India and Southeast Asia on hundreds of millions of Android devices. As of mid-2026, Glance has 39 open roles spanning engineering, product, design, data science, and business functions, making it one of the more active hirers in the Indian consumer-tech space.

Glance hires across experience levels, from fresh graduates entering junior engineering or analyst positions to senior engineers and product leaders. Most roles are based in Bengaluru, with a smaller number of hybrid or remote-friendly positions depending on the team. The company generally moves at a steady pace through its hiring cycle, completing the process within a few weeks for shortlisted candidates.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Glance's interview process typically runs through four to five stages. The exact number and format can vary by role and team, so treat this as a representative pattern based on publicly shared candidate experiences.

Application and initial screening. You apply through Glance's careers page or a job portal. A recruiter reviews your profile for basic fit, and if shortlisted, you receive a call from the talent team to confirm your background, current compensation, and interest in the role.

Online assessment (for technical roles). Engineers and data candidates often receive a coding or analytical test at this stage. Expect standard data structures and algorithms problems for engineering, or SQL and statistics questions for data roles. This is typically a take-home or proctored platform test.

Technical round 1. A live coding or problem-solving interview with an engineer from the team. The focus is on core fundamentals: algorithms, code quality, and how you think through a problem out loud, not just whether you reach the right answer.

Technical round 2 or system design. Senior candidates usually face a system design interview here. You may be asked to design a scalable content recommendation engine, a notification delivery pipeline, or a similar system relevant to Glance's product. Junior candidates typically receive a second coding round instead.

Hiring manager round. This conversation focuses on your past projects, how you made decisions, and how you handle ambiguity. Expect depth-of-experience questions such as: 'Walk me through a time you built something from scratch' or 'How did you handle a conflict with a stakeholder?'

Culture or leadership round (for senior roles). Some senior hires report an additional conversation with a cross-functional leader or executive. It tends to be more open-ended, covering your career goals, your thinking on product impact, and your working style.

Offer. If all rounds go well, the recruiter shares the offer verbally and follows up with documentation. This is the right moment to negotiate if needed, as Glance, like most funded startups, has some flexibility on compensation components.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Glance builds for scale. Its products run on low-to-mid-range Android devices across varied network conditions, so the company values engineers who think about performance, efficiency, and real-world constraints, not just clean code in ideal settings.

For engineers, strong fundamentals in data structures and algorithms are a baseline. Beyond that, Glance values experience with distributed systems, Android development, or machine learning for personalisation. If you have worked on recommendation systems, content delivery, or on-device AI, highlight that prominently.

For product managers, the bar is around product thinking grounded in data. They want PMs who can read metrics, identify what is actually moving the needle, and make prioritisation calls without needing consensus from a committee. Experience with consumer apps and growth loops is a strong positive signal.

For data roles, SQL fluency and the ability to turn raw data into a clear story for a non-technical audience are essential. Knowledge of experimentation, specifically A/B testing frameworks and statistical significance, comes up frequently in interviews.

Across all roles, Glance looks for people comfortable with ambiguity who can move fast without losing quality. They favour candidates who show genuine ownership: people who did not just complete a task but drove it through to a meaningful outcome.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product first. Install Glance on an Android device if you have not already. Spend time using the lock screen, notice what content surfaces, think about how recommendations might work, and come to your interview with a genuine point of view. Interviewers consistently notice when a candidate has actually used the product.

Brush up on your core domain. For engineers, revise data structures, algorithms, and system design with a focus on content ranking, caching strategies, and mobile performance, as these appear frequently in Glance's interview context. For data roles, revise SQL window functions, cohort analysis, and experiment design.

Prepare project deep-dives. Pick a small number of projects from your experience that show scale, ownership, and a measurable outcome. Be ready to go several levels deep: what was the problem, what did you specifically do, and what changed as a result. Vague or passive answers hurt your chances.

Practise system design out loud. Even if you know the answer, Glance interviewers want to hear your reasoning process. Practise narrating your design choices: 'I chose a push model here because the read-to-write ratio is high' signals stronger thinking than jumping straight to a diagram.

Prepare salary expectations. Glance is a funded startup and salaries are competitive for the Bengaluru market. Research compensation benchmarks on Glassdoor and levels.fyi before your recruiter call so you have a confident, informed number ready rather than an on-the-spot guess.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Glance operates with a startup energy inside a company that has grown to significant scale. Internal culture rewards initiative and speed. Teams are relatively flat, and individual contributors are expected to hold strong opinions and defend them with data.

Ownership is the dominant value. Employees are expected to treat their area of responsibility as their own problem to solve, not a ticket to close. This shows up directly in hiring: interviewers specifically probe for moments when you went beyond the scope of your role.

Data drives decisions. The company runs a large number of experiments and decisions are expected to be grounded in numbers. If you rely mainly on gut instinct, the culture will feel like friction. If you enjoy A/B tests, dashboards, and metric reviews, you will feel at home.

The pace is fast. People who have worked at Glance often describe a high-velocity environment where priorities shift. This suits candidates who thrive on variety and fast learning, but it can be challenging if you prefer slow, deeply planned work with stable scope.

Background is not the only signal. Glance's teams include people from top engineering colleges as well as strong performers from tier-2 institutions. What matters more than pedigree is the quality of your thinking and your track record of delivering real outcomes.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does Glance's hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on candidate accounts, the process typically wraps up within a few weeks for shortlisted candidates. Engineering roles can take a little longer if there are multiple technical rounds to coordinate. If you have not heard back within a week of your last round, a polite follow-up email to your recruiter is perfectly reasonable and usually appreciated.

Does Glance hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

Glance does hire freshers and recent graduates, primarily into junior engineering, analyst, and associate product roles. Competition for these spots is high, so a strong internship record or a notable personal project helps your application stand out. Check the careers page for roles explicitly marked as entry-level or associate, as these have different interview tracks.

Is the interview process different for product management versus engineering roles?

Yes, the structure differs by function. Engineering interviews lean heavily on coding rounds and system design. Product management interviews focus on product thinking exercises (such as 'how would you improve Glance's user retention?'), past project walkthroughs, and metric-based decision-making. Both tracks include a hiring manager conversation that assesses ownership and judgment.

What should I expect in a Glance system design interview?

Expect to design systems that reflect Glance's real-world challenges: content recommendation at scale, real-time personalisation, push notification delivery, or mobile-first architecture for constrained devices. Start by clarifying requirements and scale assumptions before jumping into a solution. Interviewers care as much about your reasoning process as the final design you arrive at.

Can I negotiate my salary offer at Glance?

Yes, negotiation is generally possible, especially for mid and senior roles. Come prepared with data from Glassdoor and levels.fyi to support your ask, so the conversation is grounded in market reality. Glance, like most funded startups, has some flexibility on both base and stock components. Make your case clearly and professionally as recruiters are accustomed to this conversation.

Are there remote roles at Glance?

Most Glance roles are based in Bengaluru, where the company is headquartered. Some teams do have hybrid or remote-friendly arrangements, but these are role-specific and not the default across the company. Check each job listing carefully for the work-mode details, and confirm the arrangement during the recruiter call if it matters to your decision.
