01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Epic Software (officially Epic Systems) builds the electronic health record software used by thousands of hospitals and clinics worldwide. It is one of the largest healthcare IT companies in the US, and its hiring is known for being structured, thorough, and mission-driven.

As of July 2026, Epic has 12 open roles across functions including software engineering, implementation consulting, technical writing, and project management. Roles span entry-level to senior, and Epic regularly hires fresh graduates from engineering programmes, making it a realistic target for candidates at multiple career stages.

For Indian professionals, Epic is primarily a destination through US work authorisation (OPT, CPT, or H-1B sponsorship). Virtually all roles are based at or near the company's campus in Verona, Wisconsin, so relocation is part of the equation. The hiring process typically takes several weeks from application to offer, and candidates who prepare methodically have a strong chance of clearing each stage.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Epic's process commonly involves four to five stages. The exact structure varies by role, but this is what most candidates report.

Application and Resume Screen
You apply through Epic's careers portal. Recruiters look for relevant educational background, technical skills, and work authorisation eligibility. Candidates commonly report hearing back within one to three weeks if their profile matches.

Recruiter Phone Screen
A call with a recruiter, typically around twenty to thirty minutes, covering your background, interest in Epic, location preferences, and availability. This is a fit check, not a technical evaluation.

Technical or Skills Assessment
For software engineering roles, this is usually a coding assessment on an online platform covering data structures, algorithms, and problem-solving. For implementation and project management roles, the assessment may be a written scenario or a case-based exercise.

Interview Day or Virtual Loop
This is the core evaluation. Candidates typically go through three to five back-to-back interviews covering technical problem-solving, behavioural questions using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result), and role-specific topics. For implementation roles, expect questions about client communication and managing challenging stakeholders.

Offer and Background Check
Candidates who clear the loop receive a verbal offer, followed by a written offer letter. A background verification check is standard before the offer is finalised.

Epic's process is methodical. Preparation matters more than luck here.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Epic looks for a specific mix of skills and mindset. Here is what consistently appears in publicly reported candidate experiences.

Strong fundamentals over framework familiarity. For engineering roles, Epic's interviewers focus on core computer science: data structures, algorithms, object-oriented design, and readable code. Knowing a popular framework helps, but it will not save you if your fundamentals are weak.

Thinking out loud. Epic interviewers want to see your reasoning process, not just your final answer. Candidates who talk through their approach as they solve a problem tend to do better than those who code in silence and reveal the answer only at the end.

Genuine interest in healthcare. Epic's mission is improving patient care through better software. Candidates who show real curiosity about how hospitals use technology, or who have any background in health-adjacent work, stand out from those who treat it as just another tech job.

Communication and client skills (for non-engineering roles). Implementation consultants work directly with hospital staff. Strong verbal communication, patience, and the ability to explain technical concepts clearly to non-technical audiences are heavily weighted in these interviews.

Ownership and follow-through. Epic teams are lean relative to the product's scale. They want people who take initiative, deliver on commitments, and surface problems early rather than going quiet when something goes wrong.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Brush up on core CS concepts. For software roles, focus on arrays, trees, graphs, sorting, and dynamic programming. Medium-difficulty problems on LeetCode are a practical benchmark. Write clean, readable code rather than clever one-liners.

Prepare STAR stories for behavioural rounds. Epic interviewers commonly ask questions like 'Tell me about a time you handled a difficult team situation' or 'Describe a project where you had to learn something new under pressure.' Prepare five to six strong stories from your past that you can adapt to different questions. Be specific, not vague.

Learn Epic's product landscape. You do not need to be a healthcare expert, but knowing what an EHR is, how Epic's modules work at a high level, and why hospitals choose Epic, will help you in both technical and behavioural rounds. Epic's own public materials and commonly cited analyst reports are good starting points.

Prepare thoughtful questions. Epic interviewers note whether candidates ask curious, informed questions. Ask about team structure, how priorities are set, or what success looks like in the first six months. Avoid asking about salary or benefits in early rounds.

Simulate the Interview Day format. The back-to-back structure is tiring. Practice doing multiple interviews in a row with a peer so the fatigue does not surprise you on the actual day.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Epic's culture is distinctive, and being clear-eyed about it upfront will help you decide if it is the right environment for you.

Mission comes first. Nearly everything at Epic connects back to improving patient care. Employees commonly describe the work as meaningful, and this purpose is a big reason people stay long-term. If you are motivated primarily by chasing the highest salary in the market, the culture may feel misaligned.

An actual campus, not a metaphor. Epic's Verona campus is famously elaborate, with themed buildings and on-site amenities. The expectation is that you are present. Remote work exists but is limited and role-dependent. For candidates relocating from India, this is worth factoring in from day one.

High responsibility, strong support. New hires at Epic are given real ownership early. The internal onboarding (commonly referred to as Epic University by employees) is thorough, and managers are generally accessible. The learning curve is steep but well-supported.

Collaborative over competitive. Epic's internal culture is cooperative. Teams work toward shared outcomes, and there is no publicly reported stack ranking or forced distribution system. If you are used to environments where individual performance scores drive everything, this will feel noticeably different.

Stability as a feature. Epic is privately held and has historically avoided the cycles of mass hiring and layoffs common at public tech companies. For Indian professionals managing work authorisation timelines, this stability is often cited as a meaningful advantage.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Epic Software sponsor H-1B visas for Indian candidates?

Epic has historically sponsored H-1B visas and has hired many Indian professionals on OPT or CPT pathways. That said, sponsorship decisions depend on the role, team, and timing, so ask the recruiter directly early in the process. Do not assume sponsorship is guaranteed for every role. Confirm it before investing significant time in the interview rounds.

How long does the full hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates commonly report the process taking several weeks end to end. The recruiter screen often happens within one to two weeks of applying if your profile matches, and the final interview loop is usually scheduled shortly after the assessment clears. Offer timelines after the loop are typically a few weeks. Ask your recruiter for a rough schedule early so you can plan accordingly.

What programming language should I use for the Epic coding assessment?

Epic's core product is built in a proprietary language called MUMPS, but candidates are assessed in common languages like Java, C++, Python, or C#. You do not need to know MUMPS before joining. Choose the language you are most comfortable with, and focus on demonstrating clear problem-solving and readable code rather than language-specific tricks.

Are Epic's salaries competitive compared to other US tech companies?

According to Glassdoor and levels.fyi, Epic's compensation is generally considered solid for the Verona, Wisconsin area but below the top-of-market packages at large tech companies in major metro areas. The tradeoff includes strong job stability, a clear mission, and benefits that are commonly cited as generous, including relocation assistance. Review publicly reported figures on those platforms for the specific role you are targeting before drawing conclusions.

What does an implementation consultant at Epic actually do, and do I need an engineering background?

Implementation consultants help hospitals and clinics configure and launch Epic's software after purchase. The role combines project management, technical training, and client relationship skills. You do not need to be a software engineer, but comfort with technical systems and strong communication skills are essential. The role commonly involves significant travel in the early years, which is worth factoring into your decision before you apply.

How should I follow up after my Epic interview if I have not heard back?

A polite follow-up email to your recruiter after five to seven business days is appropriate and unlikely to hurt your chances. Keep it brief: thank them for the process, confirm your continued interest, and ask if there is any update or additional information they need from you. Avoid following up more than twice, as Epic receives a high volume of applications and repeated messages can work against you.
