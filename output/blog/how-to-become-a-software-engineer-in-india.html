01 Role Overview

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Programming language basics are the foundation. Most Indian freshers start with Python or Java. JavaScript is essential if you lean toward web development. Pick one and get genuinely comfortable before jumping to a second.

Data structures and algorithms (DSA) are non-negotiable if you want to get into product companies. LeetCode-style problems are a standard part of hiring at most mid-to-large tech firms in India.

Core computer science concepts matter more than people expect. Operating systems, databases, networking, and object-oriented design come up in interviews at all levels.

Version control with Git is used by every team. Learn branching, pull requests, and basic conflict resolution before you join your first job.

SQL appears in almost every role, even frontend ones. Know how to write and read a query.

System design becomes critical from the senior level onward. Start learning it once you have a few years of experience under your belt.

Soft skills that actually matter in Indian workplaces: clear written English for Slack and email, the ability to ask precise questions, and comfort explaining your code to a non-technical colleague.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

  1. Learn the basics of one programming language. Python is the easiest entry point for most people. Spend a couple of months on syntax, functions, loops, and small projects. Free resources like freeCodeCamp and Harvard's introductory computer science course online are well-regarded globally.
  1. Build your computer science fundamentals. Study DSA seriously: arrays, linked lists, trees, sorting, and recursion. Use platforms like GeeksforGeeks, which is tailored for Indian interviews, or Striver's DSA sheet, which many freshers in India swear by.
  1. Create a project from scratch. A to-do app or a weather app is fine to start. What matters is that you build it yourself, deploy it (even on a free platform), and can explain every line. Put it on GitHub.
  1. Learn web or backend basics. Choose a direction: frontend (HTML, CSS, React), backend (Node.js, Django, Spring Boot), or full-stack. Most fresher jobs in India ask for at least basic web development knowledge.
  1. Practice interview problems consistently. Once your fundamentals are solid, solve DSA problems daily. Track your progress. This step runs in parallel with everything else.
  1. Apply for internships or entry-level roles. Do not wait until you feel 'ready.' Internships at startups are often easier to land and give you real experience fast. Many of India's top product companies hire heavily through campus or off-campus drives.
  1. Build your online presence. Keep your LinkedIn profile updated with projects, skills, and a clear headline. A clean GitHub profile with active contributions signals seriousness to recruiters.
  1. Crack the interview. A typical product company interview has a coding round, a technical round (DSA plus concepts), and an HR round. Service companies like TCS, Infosys, and Wipro have their own online tests. Practice both formats.
04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

Month 1-3: Foundation
Learn one programming language, get comfortable with Git, and complete an online course covering the basics. By the end of this phase you should be able to write small programs independently.

Month 4-6: Core CS and first project
Start DSA practice, study operating system and database basics, and build your first complete project. Deploy it and share the link on LinkedIn.

Month 7-9: Interview preparation
Solve problems on LeetCode or GeeksforGeeks daily. Start applying for internships. Aim for at least one referral from a college senior or LinkedIn contact.

Month 10-12: First role
Land an internship or a fresher role. Even a startup counts. Real work experience accelerates learning faster than any course.

Year 1-2: Entry level (6-12 LPA)
Focus on learning from your team, shipping features, and understanding production code. Pick up one cloud platform. Start contributing to code reviews.

Year 3-5: Mid level (15-25 LPA)
Take ownership of modules, mentor juniors, and begin learning system design. This is when most engineers consider switching companies for a significant salary jump.

Year 6-9: Senior level (28-45 LPA)
Lead technical decisions, design systems end-to-end, and influence the roadmap. By this stage your network and reputation matter as much as your coding skills.

Year 10+: Lead or Staff level (40-65+ LPA)
At this level you are setting technical direction, hiring, and possibly moving into engineering management or a principal IC track.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

College matters, but not as much as skills. IITs, NITs, and BITS graduates do have an advantage in campus placements at top product companies. But thousands of engineers from tier-2 and tier-3 colleges land great jobs every year by building strong projects and clearing off-campus drives. Do not let your college name stop you from applying.

Naukri and LinkedIn are not optional. Naukri is where most Indian recruiters, especially at service companies and mid-size firms, actively search for candidates. Keep your profile updated with your skills, projects, and current status. LinkedIn is essential for product companies, startups, and any role with international teams.

The service company vs. product company split is real. Companies like TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, and Cognizant hire in large volumes and are a valid starting point. Product companies (think Flipkart, Swiggy, Zepto, Razorpay, and Indian arms of global firms) typically pay more but have more competitive hiring. Many engineers start at a service company, build experience, and move to a product company after two to three years.

Communities accelerate your growth. Join communities like HasGeek, local developer meetups, and Discord servers for Python India or ReactJS India. These are where jobs get shared, referrals happen, and you meet engineers who have already solved the problems you are facing.

Referrals work. A referral from someone inside a company dramatically increases your chances of getting an interview call. Do not hesitate to message ex-classmates, LinkedIn contacts, or community members to ask if they can refer you. Most people are willing to help if you ask politely and have a decent profile.

Off-campus hiring is very active. Many product startups post roles directly on Wellfound, Cutshort, and LinkedIn rather than going through campus drives. Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a computer science degree to become a software engineer in India?

A CS or IT degree helps, especially for campus placements at top companies, but it is not strictly required. Many working engineers in India come from electronics, mechanical, or even non-engineering backgrounds. What matters most to recruiters is that you can code, clear the interview rounds, and show real projects. Several bootcamps and self-study paths have produced engineers who landed solid roles at startups and product companies.

How long does it realistically take to get a job as a fresher?

For someone starting from scratch, around a year of focused preparation before landing a first role is commonly cited by engineers who document their journey online. If you already have some coding exposure from college, six to nine months of structured preparation is often enough. The timeline depends heavily on how consistently you practice DSA, how many applications you send out, and how quickly you get referrals.

Which programming language should I learn first?

Python is the most beginner-friendly and is widely used in India for both web development and data roles. Java is preferred in many enterprise and service company environments, while JavaScript is essential for web development. Pick one based on the kind of role you want, learn it well, and add more languages once you are working. Switching languages is easy once you have solid fundamentals.

Is DSA really necessary if I want to build apps or work on web development?

Yes, if you are targeting product companies or mid-to-large startups in India. Most of them use LeetCode-style DSA rounds as a filter, regardless of the actual job. Service companies tend to have different test formats with less focus on complex algorithms. If your target is a startup that skips DSA rounds and cares more about what you have built, then a strong portfolio matters more than DSA prep.

What salary can I expect as a fresher software engineer in India?

Based on knok's job radar data as of July 2026, the typical range for entry-level Software Engineer roles (0-2 years of experience) is 6-12 LPA. The actual offer depends on the company, city, and your interview performance. Product companies and well-funded startups often offer at the higher end of that range, while service companies tend to start lower.

How important is it to have a GitHub profile or personal projects?

A GitHub profile with at least a couple of real projects is strongly recommended. It shows recruiters you can write and ship code, not just solve textbook problems. Open-source contributions are a bonus but not required at the fresher level. What matters is that your projects are complete, have a readable README, and you can explain them clearly in an interview.
