01 Overview

Overview

Infilect is a Bangalore-based retail AI company building computer vision solutions for FMCG brands and modern trade retailers. Their core product lets field reps photograph store shelves with an iOS app, and Infilect's AI engine checks planogram compliance, share-of-shelf, and stock availability in near real time.

The iOS Engineer role at Infilect blends mobile development with applied AI work. You will likely own camera capture pipelines, on-device inference using CoreML, and offline-first architectures that let reps work in stores with patchy connectivity. The work is hands-on and close to the product's core value.

As of mid-2026, Infilect has 8 open roles across the company. Across the broader iOS Engineer market in India, the knok jobradar tracked 70 active openings around the same period, with Delhi (21 openings) and Bangalore (6 openings) being the most active cities for this skill set.

Candidates typically go through an online or take-home coding assessment, one or two technical conversations, and a final discussion with a senior engineer or product lead. Infilect does not publicly describe its interview rounds, so treat this as a general guide based on what candidates report.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for story-based and technical questions. Three worked examples are below.

---

Q: How have you built offline-first sync in an iOS app?

*Situation:* I worked on a field sales app used by reps in rural areas where connectivity dropped frequently during store audits.

*Task:* My task was to ensure that all audit data, including photos and form responses, was saved locally and synced reliably when connectivity returned, without any data loss.

*Action:* I used Core Data as the local store and added a sync manager that tracked 'pending', 'in-flight', and 'synced' states for each record. For images, I stored them on disk and kept only file paths in Core Data to avoid bloating the database. I used URLSession background tasks for uploads so the OS could continue them even if the user switched apps. I added retry logic with exponential backoff and idempotency tokens so duplicate uploads would be safely ignored by the backend.

*Result:* The app went from frequent data-loss complaints in low-connectivity zones to zero reported incidents over the following quarter. Field reps could complete a full audit without worrying about connectivity at all.

---

Q: Walk us through integrating a CoreML model into an iOS app.

*Situation:* My team needed to add on-device product recognition to a retail app so reps could get instant feedback without waiting for a server response.

*Task:* I was responsible for the full integration: model conversion, inference pipeline, and surfacing results in the UI.

*Action:* I worked with the ML team to export the model in ONNX format and convert it to CoreML using coremltools in Python. I wrapped the model in a service class that ran inference on a background serial queue using Vision's VNCoreMLRequest, batching frames from the camera preview layer. I added a confidence threshold filter so only high-confidence predictions reached the UI, reducing false positives. I also set up model versioning so we could ship updated models via a remote config flag without a full app release.

*Result:* On-device inference latency was well within the product team's target on a mid-range device. The feedback loop for reps became nearly instant, and the feature worked with no connectivity at all.

---

Q: Tell us about a performance regression you debugged in an image-heavy iOS feature.

*Situation:* After a release, users reported that the shelf-photography screen became sluggish and the app sometimes crashed after capturing multiple images in one session.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause and fix it without breaking the existing camera flow.

*Action:* I used Instruments' Allocations and Leaks templates to profile the session. I found that UIImage objects created from raw camera data were being retained by a dispatch closure that captured self strongly, preventing deallocation. Every captured image was staying in memory for the entire session. I also noticed we were decoding full-resolution images on the main thread before displaying thumbnails. I fixed the retain cycle with a weak capture, moved decoding to a background queue, and switched to loading thumbnails using ImageIO with a reduced-size hint.

*Result:* Peak memory during extended capture sessions dropped significantly according to the Allocations instrument, and crash reports for that screen disappeared in the following release.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical design questions (architecture, offline sync, ML integration): open by naming the constraints you would clarify first (bandwidth, data volume, device support range), then walk through your chosen approach layer by layer. Interviewers at product-focused companies like Infilect care about trade-offs, not just the 'correct answer'. Explain what you considered and why you ruled out alternatives.

For debugging and performance questions: name the specific tool you reached for first (Instruments, Xcode Memory Graph, Console logs) and explain what signal you were looking for. Specifics impress more than general statements. 'I used the Allocations instrument and found a retain cycle in a dispatch closure' is far stronger than 'I profiled the app and fixed a memory issue'.

For behavioural questions: use STAR tightly. Situation in two sentences, Task in one, Action in the bulk of the answer with specific steps rather than vague verbs, Result with a concrete outcome. Avoid saying 'we' for your individual actions, as the interviewer needs to assess your specific contribution.

For system or API design questions: start with the user journey (field rep walks into a store, opens the app), then move to the data flow (capture, local store, sync, backend), then highlight the hard parts (conflict resolution, battery and bandwidth constraints). Grounding your design in the retail field context signals that you understand Infilect's actual product.

Closing every answer: leave space for 'does that match what you had in mind?' It signals confidence without arrogance and opens a technical dialogue that Infilect interviewers typically appreciate.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Infilect is a product-led AI company, and their iOS interviews reflect that. Based on what candidates report and the nature of the product, interviewers typically look for a few things beyond raw Swift knowledge.

Comfort at the hardware-software boundary. The iOS app is the input layer for an AI system. Interviewers want engineers who understand camera APIs, image buffers, CoreML, and the Vision framework, not just UIKit layout and navigation.

Offline-first thinking. Field reps work in stores, markets, and warehouses where connectivity is unreliable. If you have never thought about local persistence, background sync, or conflict resolution, that gap will surface quickly in the technical round.

Performance instincts. Processing images is expensive on mobile hardware. Interviewers look for candidates who naturally think about memory, threading, and battery impact without being prompted.

Product curiosity. Infilect is small enough that engineers interact directly with product and business stakeholders. Showing genuine interest in the retail AI problem, asking how planogram data is structured or how reps use the app in the field, signals cultural fit as much as technical depth.

Clear technical communication. Candidates who can explain a complex architectural decision in plain terms tend to do well. Practice talking through your thinking out loud, not just arriving at answers silently and then stating a conclusion.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused preparation path for the Infilect iOS Engineer interview.

Week 1: Core iOS and Swift fundamentals
Revise Swift concurrency (async/await, actors, DispatchQueue), memory management (ARC, weak/unowned, retain cycles), and the UIKit/SwiftUI lifecycle. Read Apple's documentation on URLSession background tasks and understand how they differ from foreground uploads.

Week 2: On-device AI and Vision
Build a small demo app that uses a CoreML model (Apple's public model gallery has good starting options) via the Vision framework. Walk through the VNCoreMLRequest pipeline end to end. Understand how to convert a model using coremltools and what quantisation means for model size and accuracy.

Week 3: Offline-first and data persistence
Study Core Data (or SQLite via GRDB) for local storage. Implement a simple sync manager that handles pending, in-flight, and failed states. Understand idempotency and why it matters for upload retry logic.

Week 4: Performance and debugging
Spend time in Instruments with the Allocations, Time Profiler, and Network templates. Profile an existing project of yours and find at least one real issue to fix. This gives you a specific, concrete story to tell during the debugging discussion.

Company research: Read Infilect's public blog posts and case studies to understand how their product is used by FMCG brands in practice. Knowing the product context helps you frame your answers around real constraints, not generic mobile scenarios.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a generic iOS interview. Infilect is not a consumer app company. Candidates who only prepare for social-feed or e-commerce app patterns, without thinking about image-heavy, offline-first, AI-integrated mobile apps, often find themselves unprepared for the first technical question.

Vague answers on concurrency. Saying 'I use background threads' is not enough. Be ready to explain specifically why you chose DispatchQueue over async/await for a particular use case, and what happens if you capture self strongly in a closure passed to a background queue.

Ignoring the ML integration layer. Even if you have not shipped a CoreML feature in production, candidates who have not explored the Vision framework documentation will struggle with image-processing questions. A quick demo project before the interview is worth more than theoretical knowledge alone.

Skipping the 'why'. Infilect engineers are expected to own architectural decisions. Answers that describe what you did without explaining why you chose that approach over alternatives signal a lack of depth to senior interviewers.

Not asking questions. Infilect is a startup. Asking about team size, the current state of the iOS codebase, or a specific edge case you thought about during your prep shows initiative. Candidates who say 'no questions' at the end typically leave a weaker impression.

Overstating ML experience. If you have used CoreML at a basic level, say so honestly. Infilect has strong ML engineers who will probe claims quickly. Honesty about your current depth, paired with clear evidence of active learning, is a stronger signal than inflated claims.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Infilect iOS Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report between two and four conversations in total. There is typically an initial screening or take-home coding task, followed by one or two technical rounds, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or team lead. Infilect does not publish an official process, so this outline is based on candidate accounts and may vary by seniority level.

Does Infilect expect deep ML experience for the iOS Engineer role?

You do not need to be an ML researcher, but comfort with CoreML and the Vision framework is important. Infilect's iOS app feeds images into their AI pipeline, so you will be expected to understand on-device inference, image preprocessing, and how to pass data between the mobile app and backend AI services. A working demo project using CoreML is a practical way to demonstrate this capability before the interview.

What salary can I expect for an iOS Engineer at Infilect?

Infilect does not publish salary bands publicly. For current compensation benchmarks, Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry community-reported figures for similar-stage AI startups in India. Having a clear sense of publicly reported ranges for your experience level before you negotiate will help you set expectations confidently.

Is SwiftUI knowledge required, or is UIKit enough?

Candidates report that Infilect's codebase leans heavily on UIKit, given the complexity of the camera and image-handling flows. That said, interviewers typically ask about your SwiftUI exposure and your view on incremental migration. Being honest about your UIKit depth while showing awareness of SwiftUI is a reasonable position to take.

How important is testing knowledge for this role?

Interviewers commonly ask how you test camera-dependent code and CoreML inference flows, which are difficult to cover with standard unit tests. You should be prepared to discuss approaches like mocking AVCaptureSession, using XCTest expectations for async callbacks, and separating business logic from hardware-dependent code so it can be tested in isolation. Candidates who have a clear philosophy for testing hardware-adjacent code tend to stand out.
