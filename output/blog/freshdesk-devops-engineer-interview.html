02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Freshdesk DevOps interviews, based on patterns candidates report.

  1. Walk me through how you set up a CI/CD pipeline from scratch. What tools did you pick and why?
  2. How do you manage secrets and credentials in a Kubernetes environment? What is your approach in practice?
  3. Describe a production incident you owned end-to-end. How did you detect it, resolve it, and prevent recurrence?
  4. Freshdesk serves millions of support tickets. How would you design a zero-downtime deployment strategy for a critical microservice?
  5. We use Terraform heavily. How do you handle state, manage drift, and structure modules for a large team?
  6. Walk me through your Kubernetes experience. Have you worked with resource limits, horizontal pod autoscaling, or cluster autoscaling in production?
  7. How do you build observability for a new service? Describe your approach to monitoring, logging, and alerting.
  8. Tell me about a time you reduced infrastructure costs. What changes did you make?
  9. How do you handle security and compliance in your pipelines? Give a concrete example.
  10. What is your approach to on-call and incident response? How do you write a useful post-mortem?
  11. Freshdesk runs heavily on AWS. How have you improved performance or reliability in a multi-region setup?
  12. How do you collaborate with developers to shift infrastructure concerns left in the development cycle?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these as templates, not scripts. Adapt each answer to your actual experience.

Q: Describe a production incident you owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* Our payment service started returning errors for a subset of users during peak hours. Alerts fired quickly but the root cause was not immediately clear.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer, I needed to triage fast, stabilise the service without a full release rollback, and document the event clearly.

*Action:* I checked recent deployment history and found a config change had gone out shortly before the errors began. I toggled the relevant feature flag off, which restored normal behaviour within minutes. I then worked with the developer who had made the change to understand what had been missed in staging.

*Result:* The service recovered without reverting the entire release. We updated staging to better mirror production config and added an automated config-validation step to the pipeline. Candidates report this kind of structured, ownership-driven approach lands well in Freshdesk panels.

---

Q: Walk me through a CI/CD pipeline you built from scratch.

*Situation:* My team was deploying manually to staging and production, which caused inconsistencies and slowed down releases.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement a fully automated pipeline for a Node.js microservice.

*Action:* I set up GitHub Actions to run unit and integration tests on every pull request. On merge to main, the pipeline built a Docker image, pushed it to ECR, and triggered a Helm chart update to our Kubernetes cluster via ArgoCD. I added a health check gate so a failed rollout would automatically revert.

*Result:* Deployments became consistent and repeatable. The team could ship multiple times a day without manual steps, and the automated rollback meant engineers felt far less anxious about releasing.

---

Q: How do you manage Terraform state for a large team?

*Situation:* Multiple engineers were working on the same Terraform codebase and we were hitting state conflicts regularly.

*Task:* I needed to introduce a state management approach that would scale with team size.

*Action:* I migrated state to S3 with DynamoDB locking so only one operation could run at a time. I split the monolithic root module into smaller, bounded stacks by service domain so teams could work in parallel without touching each other's state. I also added a mandatory pull request review step before any apply ran in production.

*Result:* State conflicts dropped to near zero. New engineers could contribute to infrastructure work without the risk of corrupting shared state.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Two frameworks cover most Freshdesk DevOps interview questions well.

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard for behavioural and incident questions. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences each). Spend most of your time on Action, because that is where technical depth shows. Close with a concrete Result and, where relevant, what you changed afterwards to prevent recurrence.

Design-first for system or tooling questions: When asked 'how would you...' or 'how do you approach...', resist jumping straight to tool names. Start with the requirement (reliability, security, speed), then the tradeoffs you considered, then the tools you chose and why. Interviewers at product companies like Freshdesk want to see that you reason about problems, not just name tools.

For incident or on-call questions, a clean structure is: detect, contain, resolve, post-mortem. Walking through each step shows operational maturity regardless of the specific technical details.

Where you can honestly quantify your impact, do so. Where you cannot give a precise figure, use directional language such as 'deployment frequency roughly doubled' or 'on-call pages dropped noticeably over the following quarter'. Freshdesk engineers typically appreciate honesty over inflated claims.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on patterns candidates report, Freshdesk DevOps panels consistently look for a few specific qualities.

Ownership mindset. Freshdesk is a product company where engineers are expected to own services end-to-end. Interviewers want to see that you went beyond running pipelines and proactively improved reliability, cost, or developer experience. The question 'and what did you change after that?' comes up often.

Cloud-native depth. Freshdesk runs significant workloads on AWS and Kubernetes. Surface-level answers about containers or infrastructure-as-code will not stand out. Be ready to discuss specifics: IAM structure, pod scheduling behaviour, Helm chart organisation, and how you approached rollout strategies.

Collaboration with developers. DevOps at a SaaS company means working closely with product engineers. Interviewers look for candidates who communicate infrastructure concerns clearly and help developers build more reliable software, rather than just managing pipelines in isolation.

Calm under pressure. Incident and on-call questions test more than technical knowledge. Interviewers want to see how you communicate during an outage, how you prioritise competing concerns, and how you extract learning from failures. A clear post-mortem process typically scores well.

Curiosity about the product. Freshdesk is a customer support platform. Candidates who understand why uptime matters in that context (customers are already frustrated when they reach out for support) tend to connect more naturally with interviewers.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two to three week plan based on what candidates report matters most.

Week 1: Sharpen your core skills. Review Kubernetes internals including pod lifecycle, resource limits, and autoscaling. Revisit your Terraform work and be ready to explain state management and module structure. Set up a small AWS project if you have not worked hands-on with cloud infrastructure recently.

Week 2: Build your story bank. Write out three to five incidents or projects you have owned. Use the STAR structure for each. Make sure at least one covers a production incident, one covers a pipeline or automation improvement, and one covers cost or reliability work. Practise saying these out loud, not just reviewing them silently.

Week 3: Company context and mock interviews. Read Freshdesk engineering blog posts, which are publicly available, to understand their scale and technology choices. Do at least two mock technical interviews with a peer or a platform that offers DevOps-specific practice. Candidates consistently report that spoken practice makes a bigger difference than silent review.

Throughout: Keep your resume honest and specific. Freshdesk interviewers typically go line by line on your resume, so every tool or project you list should be something you can discuss in depth. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

These are the mistakes that most often cost candidates the Freshdesk DevOps offer, based on what candidates report.

  1. Listing tools instead of solving problems. Saying 'I know Kubernetes, Terraform, Jenkins, and Prometheus' without connecting each tool to a specific outcome tells the interviewer very little. Always anchor tool choices to a problem and a result.
  1. Vague incident stories. 'We had an outage and I fixed it' is not enough. Interviewers want to hear how you detected the issue, your first hypothesis, what you checked, how you communicated during it, and what changed after.
  1. Ignoring the product context. Generic infrastructure answers that could apply to any company miss the mark. Connect your answers to the realities of a customer-facing SaaS platform where downtime directly affects paying customers.
  1. Overstating automation. Candidates sometimes claim fully automated pipelines that, on closer questioning, still had manual steps. Be honest. Interviewers respect candidates who say 'we automated deployment but rollbacks were still manual because of a compliance requirement.'
  1. Not asking good questions. Freshdesk interviewers typically expect you to ask about their stack, on-call culture, or how deployments work in practice. Candidates who ask no questions often come across as disengaged.
  1. Freezing on system design. When asked to design a deployment or observability system, start by clarifying requirements before proposing an architecture. Jumping straight to a solution without asking about scale, team size, or constraints is a common error that signals inexperience.

How many interview rounds does the Freshdesk DevOps process typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: a recruiter screening call, one or two technical interviews covering hands-on DevOps skills, and a final round that may include system design or a discussion with a hiring manager. The exact structure varies by team and seniority level, so it is worth asking the recruiter upfront what to expect.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer role at Freshdesk?

Based on knok data, DevOps Engineers in India typically earn 6-12 LPA at entry level, 15-28 LPA at mid level, and 30-50 LPA at senior level. Freshworks is publicly listed and Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry publicly reported compensation figures specific to Freshworks that can give you a more precise benchmark for negotiation.

Is Kubernetes knowledge mandatory for Freshdesk DevOps interviews?

Based on candidate reports, Kubernetes is a core focus of the technical interview at Freshdesk. You should be comfortable with pod scheduling, resource limits, autoscaling, and troubleshooting common cluster issues. If your Kubernetes exposure has been mostly theoretical or lab-based, spend time working with a real cluster before your interview.

Does Freshdesk ask coding questions in DevOps interviews?

Candidates report that Freshdesk DevOps interviews are primarily infrastructure and system-focused rather than algorithm-heavy. You may be asked to write a shell script, a small Python utility, or a Dockerfile during a technical round. Brushing up on scripting basics is worthwhile, but heavy preparation for competitive coding problems is typically not necessary.

How long does the Freshdesk hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report the full process takes two to four weeks, though this varies based on team availability and scheduling. Following up politely with your recruiter after each round is a reasonable way to stay informed on your status and signal continued interest.

Are Freshdesk DevOps roles available outside Bangalore?

According to knok data from July 2026, Freshdesk's DevOps openings are concentrated in Bangalore, with a smaller presence in other cities.
