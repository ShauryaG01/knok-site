02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Data Analyst interviews at finance and capital-markets companies like outsidecapital. Candidates report that technical SQL questions appear in almost every round, while business case and behavioural questions tend to come later.

  1. Walk me through how you would build a dashboard to track portfolio performance for a fund manager. What metrics would you include?
  2. Explain the difference between INNER JOIN and LEFT JOIN. Give an example using investor or transaction data.
  3. Write a SQL query to find investors who made a deposit in the last quarter but have not made any withdrawal in the same period.
  4. You notice a sudden drop in a key metric overnight. How do you investigate it, and who do you involve?
  5. How would you segment investors by behaviour or risk appetite using only their transaction history?
  6. Describe how you have dealt with missing or inconsistent data in a financial dataset. What was your process?
  7. A fund manager wants to prove that a new investment strategy is outperforming the benchmark. How do you set up that analysis?
  8. What is a window function in SQL? Give a practical example from a finance context.
  9. How would you design a data model to track deal pipeline stages from lead to close?
  10. How do you validate that numbers in a report are accurate before it goes to a senior stakeholder?
  11. Describe a time you had to simplify a complex data finding for a non-technical audience.
  12. What experience do you have with regulatory or compliance reporting, for example SEBI or RBI data requirements?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural questions. Below are three examples shaped around the kind of work outsidecapital cares about.

Q: Describe a time you caught a data error that would have affected a key business decision.

*Situation:* I was preparing a monthly performance report for a portfolio team. Data was being pulled from two separate systems and reconciled manually each month.
*Task:* My responsibility was to validate the final figures before the report went to the investment committee.
*Action:* During a routine cross-check, I found that one source was using trade-date accounting while the other used settlement-date accounting, creating a mismatch for recent transactions. I flagged this to the data engineering team, documented the discrepancy with specific examples, and proposed a standardisation rule for future pulls.
*Result:* The report was corrected before it reached the committee. The team adopted a data reconciliation checklist that has been used in every monthly cycle since.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you turned a large dataset into a clear recommendation.

*Situation:* A senior stakeholder needed a market-segment analysis to decide where to focus acquisition efforts for the coming quarter.
*Task:* I was asked to analyse investor onboarding and activity data to identify which segments had the highest engagement after joining.
*Action:* I pulled cohort data from our CRM and transaction database, cleaned and joined it in Python, and built a segmentation grouping investors by product type, asset size, and activity frequency. I then created a one-page visual summary showing which cohorts had the strongest retention and which dropped off early.
*Result:* The stakeholder used the analysis to prioritise two specific investor segments for the next campaign. The same approach was reused by another team for a similar exercise the following quarter.

---

Q: Give an example of a time you worked under pressure to deliver analysis on a tight deadline.

*Situation:* A regulatory submission was due by end of day, but the usual data pipeline failed that morning.
*Task:* I needed to reconstruct the required data extract manually and ensure it matched the format specified by the compliance team.
*Action:* I identified the affected tables, wrote ad-hoc SQL queries to pull and validate the data directly, cross-referenced the output against a previous submission for consistency, and added a brief data-quality note explaining the pipeline issue and the manual process used.
*Result:* The submission went out on time. The compliance team accepted it without issues, and the pipeline failure was escalated for a permanent fix.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical SQL and Python questions: Start by restating the problem in your own words to confirm you understood it. Describe your approach before writing any code: what tables you would join, what filters you would apply, what the output should look like. At outsidecapital, show comfort with aggregations, window functions, and date-based filtering, since these come up constantly in financial reporting.

For 'investigate a metric drop' questions: Use a structured diagnostic approach. State what you would check first (data pipeline, upstream sources, recent product changes), then move to external factors. Show that you can distinguish between a data problem and a real business problem before escalating to stakeholders.

For behavioural questions: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep the Situation brief (one to two sentences). Spend most of your time on the Action: what specifically did you do, not what the team did. End with a concrete Result, even if it is qualitative (for example, 'the stakeholder approved the approach' or 'the process was adopted by the wider team').

For stakeholder communication questions: Show that you can translate a data finding into a decision. Lead with the conclusion, then support it with evidence. Avoid jargon when talking to non-technical stakeholders. This matters especially at a company where analysts work closely with fund managers and investors.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Financial domain fluency. You do not need a CFA, but you should be comfortable talking about portfolios, returns, investor behaviour, and basic financial metrics. If you have worked with finance data before, highlight it explicitly.

SQL confidence. Most rounds will include at least one SQL question. Candidates report that window functions, CTEs (Common Table Expressions), and date-range queries come up regularly. Practice writing clean, readable queries, not just ones that run.

Attention to data quality. In financial services, a wrong number in a report can have serious consequences. Interviewers at companies like outsidecapital tend to probe how you validate your work. Have a clear answer for how you check your own output.

Clear communication. The ability to explain a data finding to a fund manager or a compliance officer in plain language is valued as much as technical skill. Practice presenting your analysis out loud, not just building it.

Ownership mindset. Interviewers want to see that you follow through. Saying 'I flagged the issue to my manager' is weaker than 'I identified the issue, documented it, proposed a fix, and confirmed it was resolved.'

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your SQL base. Focus on joins, aggregations, window functions (ROW_NUMBER, RANK, LAG, LEAD), and date functions. Practice with finance-themed datasets if possible. Write queries from scratch without autocomplete.

Week 2: Review finance fundamentals. Understand portfolio performance metrics, how returns are calculated, and concepts like AUM, NAV, and redemptions. You do not need deep expertise, but you should be able to discuss these without stumbling.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories. Write out three to five experiences using the STAR format. Cover at least one example of catching a data error, one of communicating a complex finding, and one of working under pressure. Practice saying them out loud in three minutes or less.

Week 4: Case study and mock rounds. Find a public financial dataset (NSE or BSE market data is freely available) and build a short analysis. Practice the 'metric drop investigation' framework. Do at least one mock interview with a peer or record yourself answering questions.

Before the interview: Research outsidecapital's products and the clients they serve. Tailor your examples to their context. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about the team's data stack and the problems they are currently focused on.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to code without understanding the question. In SQL rounds, candidates often start writing immediately. Slow down, restate the question, confirm your understanding, and then write. This shows structured thinking and reduces errors.

Giving team answers to individual questions. When asked what 'you' did, do not say 'we cleaned the data' or 'our team built the dashboard.' Describe your specific contribution clearly.

Skipping data quality in case studies. Candidates often go straight to analysis without mentioning how they would validate the input data. At a finance company, this is a red flag. Always mention your QA step.

Using jargon without translation. Saying 'I used a star schema with a slowly changing dimension type 2' is fine if the interviewer is technical. But if you are talking to a business stakeholder, translate it: 'I designed the database so we could track how investor details changed over time.'

Vague results in STAR answers. 'The project went well' is not a result. Aim for something concrete: a decision that was made, a process that changed, a stakeholder who approved the output, or a problem that did not recur.

Not preparing questions for the interviewer. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team, the data infrastructure, or the problems they are focused on right now.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the outsidecapital Data Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report two to three rounds in most cases. The first is typically a recruiter or HR screening call, followed by a technical round, and then a final round with a business stakeholder or senior team member. The exact structure can vary by team, so pay close attention to the scheduling emails you receive for the most accurate picture of your specific process.

What SQL topics should I focus on for a finance company like outsidecapital?

Prioritise window functions (ROW_NUMBER, LAG, LEAD, SUM OVER), CTEs, date-range filtering, and multi-table joins. Finance workflows often involve running totals, period-over-period comparisons, and cohort analysis, so practise these patterns specifically. Writing clean, readable queries matters as much as getting the correct result.

What salary can I expect for a Data Analyst role at outsidecapital?

Based on knok jobradar data, Data Analyst salaries in India broadly fall into bands of 5-10 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 10-18 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 18-30 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 28-45+ LPA for lead roles. For company-specific figures at outsidecapital, Glassdoor and levels.fyi are the best sources for employee-reported numbers, as individual companies can differ from market averages.

Do I need finance domain knowledge to get this role?

You do not need a finance degree or certification, but familiarity with basic concepts such as AUM, NAV, portfolio returns, and investor onboarding will help you stand out. Interviewers at capital-markets companies tend to test whether you can contextualise your analysis in financial terms. Spending a few hours reading about how fund operations or investment platforms work is worth the time before your interview.

Is there a take-home assignment in the outsidecapital interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home case study, typically involving a dataset that needs cleaning, analysis, and a short presentation of findings. Not every candidate gets one, and the format varies by team. If you do receive one, focus on data validation and clear storytelling in your output, not just the analysis itself.

How should I prepare if I have no prior finance industry experience?

Start by learning key terms used in investment and wealth management: portfolios, returns, AUM, NAV, redemptions, and investor segmentation. Find public financial datasets (NSE or BSE market data is freely available) and build a small project showing you can analyse and present financial data. Highlight any experience with large, structured datasets from other industries and connect it clearly to how you would handle financial data at outsidecapital.
