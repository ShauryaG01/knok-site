01 Overview

Overview

Bolna is an AI voice agent company that helps businesses build and deploy conversational voice bots at scale. The Solutions Engineer role sits at the intersection of sales and engineering: you help prospects understand how Bolna fits their existing stack, guide technical evaluations, assist with integrations, and act as a trusted technical advisor post-sale.

With 11 open roles at Bolna as of mid-2026, the company is in active hiring mode, which typically means interviews move at a faster pace and the bar is weighted toward practical skill over theoretical depth. Across all companies, there are 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings listed on knok jobradar as of that date, with the highest concentration in Bangalore (55), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20).

Candidates report the process at Bolna typically includes an initial recruiter or HR screen, a technical round or take-home assessment focused on API knowledge and voice AI concepts, and one or two rounds with the hiring manager or a senior team member. Some candidates also report a short conversation with a founder. Expect questions that blend product knowledge, hands-on technical ability, and clear communication with both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would onboard a new enterprise customer onto Bolna's voice agent platform.

*Situation:* At my previous company, I managed onboarding for a large BFSI client who wanted to automate first-level support calls using a voice agent for the first time.

*Task:* My goal was to take them from signed contract to live production within six weeks, while also leaving their ops team capable of managing the agent independently afterward.

*Action:* I started with a two-hour discovery workshop to map their top call intents and identify which ones had clear, repeatable resolution paths. I built a working prototype using their actual IVR scripts, ran it through two rounds of joint testing with their QA team, and documented every integration touchpoint with their telephony provider. I also wrote a plain-language runbook so their support leads could configure new flows without raising a ticket.

*Result:* The deployment went live on schedule. Their internal report showed a measurable drop in calls routed to live agents for the covered intents, and their team was self-sufficient within the first month.

---

Q: How do you handle a customer request for a feature Bolna does not currently support?

*Situation:* A fintech prospect at my previous company needed a specific compliance logging format that our platform did not natively produce.

*Task:* I needed to keep the evaluation moving without overpromising on roadmap items or eroding the customer's trust.

*Action:* I confirmed the exact requirement in writing first so I was not guessing at the scope. I then checked whether a workaround was feasible using webhooks feeding into their own data pipeline, and looped in our product team for an honest delivery timeline. I came back to the client with two clear options: a short-term workaround they could implement quickly, and a transparent note that native support was on the roadmap for the following quarter.

*Result:* The client chose the workaround, went live with it, and later migrated to the native feature when it shipped. More importantly, they cited our transparency as one reason they renewed rather than moving to a competitor.

---

Q: Describe a time you built a proof of concept to help close a technical evaluation.

*Situation:* We had a prospect in healthcare diagnostics who doubted that a voice agent could handle their appointment booking flow, which had several conditional branches depending on the patient's history.

*Task:* I had two days before their internal review meeting to produce something concrete enough to shift their perspective from 'this will not work' to 'show us more.'

*Action:* I built a stripped-down demo in the platform sandbox, hardcoded the three scenarios they had flagged as 'impossible to automate', and recorded a walkthrough video alongside a live dial-in number they could test themselves. I also put together a one-page flow map connecting each branch in their script to a specific platform capability.

*Result:* They came to their review meeting with detailed follow-up questions rather than objections, which typically signals a demo has landed well. They progressed to a paid pilot within three weeks.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical diagnostic questions, use a layered approach: start with the user-facing symptom, move to the system component most likely to cause it (network, model inference time, audio codec, integration endpoint), and describe a specific test that rules it in or out. Bolna interviewers want to see that you think in systems, not just in checklists.

For customer scenario questions, anchor your answer in three things: what you heard from the customer, what you decided to do and why, and what you communicated back to them. Solutions Engineers are bridges between product and customer, so your answer should show empathy and technical rigour together, not separately.

For 'explain the product' questions, use a value-before-jargon rule: lead with the business outcome (fewer calls reaching live agents, faster resolution for routine queries), then introduce the technical layer (LLM-powered voice agent, telephony integration, webhook callbacks) only after the listener understands why it matters to their situation.

For behavioural questions, the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) keeps your answer focused. Keep Situation and Task to two or three sentences combined. Spend the majority of your time on Action, where you show your specific choices and reasoning. Results should name what changed, even if you can only say 'measurably improved' rather than cite a precise figure.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Bolna is an early-stage company, which means the Solutions Engineer is often the first or second deeply technical person a customer speaks with. Based on what candidates report, interviewers at this stage typically look for three qualities.

Technical fluency without engineering snobbery. You do not need to write production code, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, making test calls, and explaining concepts like streaming latency, webhook retries, and fallback handling in plain terms. Candidates who can demonstrate a working integration tend to be remembered.

Customer-first instinct. Candidates who talk only about product features and not about the customer's workflow tend not to progress past the first technical round. Show that your first instinct is to ask 'what problem are they trying to solve' before jumping to 'here is how our product works.'

Comfort with ambiguity. At a startup, the playbook evolves fast. Interviewers want evidence that you can find a path when documentation is sparse, or when a customer's use case is genuinely novel. Share examples of times you figured something out that was not in any standard script.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Understand the product yourself.
If Bolna offers a free trial or sandbox, use it. Build a simple voice agent flow end to end. Read their public documentation and any GitHub repositories or blog posts they have published. Note how the platform handles edge cases: interruptions, no-input timeouts, fallbacks, and third-party API callbacks. You will need this detail to answer scenario questions with specifics, not generalities.

Step 2: Practise your scenarios out loud.
Write answers to all twelve questions in the most-asked list. Then record yourself answering three or four of them. Solutions Engineer interviews are heavily scenario-based, and fluency matters as much as accuracy. Hearing yourself answer out loud reveals the gaps that written notes hide.

Step 3: Brush up on voice AI fundamentals.
Revisit the basics of how speech-to-text, LLM inference, and text-to-speech chain together in a real-time call, and where latency enters each stage. You do not need to implement any of it, but you should explain the pipeline confidently when asked. Public technical blog posts from voice AI companies and conference talks are solid sources here.

Step 4: Research Bolna specifically.
Check their recent product announcements, any press coverage, and their public positioning relative to competitors. Prepare two or three specific questions for your interviewer about the customer base, the current integration ecosystem, or where the product is heading. This signals genuine interest rather than generic job-seeking.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure engineering interview. Solutions Engineers are evaluated on business impact and communication, not algorithmic problem-solving. If a question is really about how you would handle a nervous decision-maker, an answer that focuses entirely on technical architecture misses the point.

Being vague about past results. 'It went well' and 'the customer was happy' are not outcomes. State what changed: the deployment went live on time, the escalation was resolved within a day, call deflection improved measurably. Qualitative specifics are better than no specifics at all.

Not knowing the product. Bolna has a specific approach to voice AI. Candidates who arrive with only generic chatbot knowledge and no familiarity with how Bolna specifically works tend to struggle in scenario rounds. Spend real time with the product before the interview.

Overpromising on the roadmap. If asked how you would respond to a customer requesting a missing feature, the wrong answer is 'I would tell them it is coming soon.' Solutions Engineers who oversell roadmap items damage customer trust. Show that you know how to hold the line honestly while keeping the relationship intact.

Skipping the business context. A customer's technical problem is almost always a business problem in disguise. Answers that address only the technical layer, without connecting back to why the issue matters to the customer's operations, miss what Bolna interviewers are typically looking for.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Bolna typically have for the Solutions Engineer role?

Candidates report a process that typically includes three stages: an initial recruiter or HR screen, a technical or skills round (sometimes a take-home assignment around API or voice AI concepts), and one or two rounds with the hiring manager or a senior team member. Some candidates also report a short founder conversation at the end. The structure can vary, so confirm the exact format with your recruiter at the start of the process.

Do I need to know how to code for this role?

You do not need to be a software engineer, but comfort with APIs, JSON payloads, and basic scripting is expected. Solutions Engineers at AI platforms like Bolna typically handle integration troubleshooting, API testing, and technical demos. Being able to read documentation, make a test API call, and explain the response confidently is more important than writing production-grade code.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at Bolna?

Bolna has not published official salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and industry surveys for Solutions Engineer roles at early-stage AI companies in India commonly cite a wide range depending on seniority and the specific city. Check Glassdoor for 'Solutions Engineer AI startup India' to get the most current data, and anchor your expectation to your years of experience and the actual scope of responsibilities before negotiating.

Is Bolna a stable company to join right now?

Bolna had 11 open roles as of mid-2026, which points to active growth rather than contraction. Early-stage AI companies offer fast learning curves and direct product impact, but they also carry typical startup risks around process maturity and longer-term stability. Whether it is the right fit depends on your appetite for ambiguity and your career priorities at this stage.

How should I prepare a demo if the technical round asks me to present one?

Lead with the customer's problem before showing any product feature. Keep the demo flow to three to five steps, and anticipate the one or two objections your interviewer is most likely to raise. Candidates who practise the demo out loud before the round tend to handle unexpected questions far more confidently than those who only prepare slides. If you have access to Bolna's sandbox, use real platform capabilities rather than mock-ups.

According to knok jobradar data from mid-2026, Solutions Engineer openings across all companies are most concentrated in Bangalore (55 jobs), followed by Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20). Pune has 12 openings, Hyderabad 6, and Chennai 5. Remote and hybrid arrangements are common in the AI sector, so it is worth asking your Bolna recruiter directly about location flexibility.
