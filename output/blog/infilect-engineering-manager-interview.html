01 Overview

Overview

Infilect is a Bangalore-based retail AI company building computer vision and shelf intelligence products for FMCG, grocery, and consumer goods brands. An Engineering Manager here leads teams that ship ML inference pipelines, image processing APIs, and retail analytics products, so the bar is set for both deep technical credibility and strong people management.

As of July 2026, Infilect has 8 open Engineering Manager roles. Across India, 975 Engineering Manager openings are live, with Bangalore leading at 182 roles. Market salary bands run 35-60 LPA for Manager, 55-90 LPA for Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at Director level.

The interview process typically spans four to five conversations. Candidates report a mix of technical system design discussions, behavioral questions on team management, and cross-functional problem-solving exercises. No fixed round structure is publicly documented, so prepare for multiple themes in every conversation.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time when an AI pipeline degraded in production and how you fixed it.

*Situation:* At a previous company, our shelf-detection model started producing false positives for out-of-stock items after a major retail client updated their store planograms.

*Task:* I had to stabilize accuracy without taking the service offline, while managing a team of three ML engineers and two backend engineers.

*Action:* I set up a lightweight model monitoring dashboard to track prediction confidence over time, identified the distribution shift as the root cause, and coordinated a focused sprint to collect and label fresh images from the updated store layouts. I also communicated a revised timeline to the client with a clear rollback plan in case accuracy did not recover.

*Result:* Accuracy returned to acceptable levels within three weeks, the client renewed their contract, and the monitoring system became standard practice across all our retail deployments.

---

Q: How have you handled a disagreement between your ML team and product stakeholders on a release timeline?

*Situation:* Product management wanted a new shelf-analysis feature shipped in two weeks. My ML team said they needed six weeks to validate it properly on real store data.

*Task:* I had to find a path that respected both the client commitment and the team's quality bar.

*Action:* I facilitated a working session where the engineers demoed a working prototype with clearly stated confidence thresholds. We agreed to ship a limited version with documented limitations to the client, with a fully validated release following on the engineering team's original timeline.

*Result:* The client accepted the phased approach, the team felt their concerns were heard, and the full version shipped on the revised timeline with no accuracy complaints from the client.

---

Q: Tell us about recruiting and retaining ML talent in a competitive market.

*Situation:* My team had two open ML engineer positions that had been unfilled for several months, with the existing team stretched thin on delivery.

*Task:* I needed to close both roles within a quarter while keeping the team motivated.

*Action:* I rewrote the job descriptions to lead with the technical problems we were solving rather than a generic tools list. I personally engaged candidates through ML community forums and LinkedIn, and introduced a learning budget for conference attendance and online courses to make the team a more compelling destination.

*Result:* Both roles were filled within six weeks. Team retention improved noticeably over the following year, and two of those hires became strong contributors within their first few months.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions: Structure every story as Situation, Task, Action, Result. At a product-focused AI company like Infilect, interviewers want to hear how you navigated technical uncertainty, not just how you executed a plan. Make your Action section specific: what exactly did you decide, and why.

System design for ML pipelines: When asked to design a computer vision or inference system, think in four layers: data ingestion and labelling, model training and versioning, inference serving and latency, and monitoring for drift. Infilect's products run at retail scale, so address reliability and cost trade-offs in your design.

People management questions: Use a two-part structure. First, describe the situation and what was at stake for the team or company. Second, explain the specific conversation or process change you introduced and what changed as a result. Avoid generic answers like 'I gave feedback.' Name the approach you used, for example weekly one-on-ones, a structured performance conversation, or a team-level OKR review.

Cross-functional prioritization: When asked how you partner with product or business stakeholders, show that you translate between technical complexity and business value. A strong answer names the trade-off you surfaced, the stakeholder you aligned with, and the business outcome that followed.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Infilect interviewers are looking for a leader who can operate at the boundary of AI research and production engineering. Several themes come up repeatedly in candidate-reported feedback.

Technical credibility: You do not need to code in the interview, but you should be able to discuss ML system design, model lifecycle management, and inference architecture without hand-waving. Shallow answers on computer vision or ML topics tend to flag candidates out early.

Product-oriented thinking: Infilect ships software for enterprise retail clients. Interviewers want to see that you think about customer impact, not just engineering quality. Frame your answers in terms of what the product outcome was, not just what the technical fix was.

Team-building depth: Hiring, growing, and retaining ML engineers is genuinely hard in the current market. Candidates who have concrete stories about building ML teams, closing tough roles, or developing junior engineers tend to score well.

Comfort with ambiguity: AI product companies move fast and deal with probabilistic outputs. Interviewers want evidence that you can make decisions with incomplete information and course-correct quickly, rather than waiting for perfect data before acting.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one, company and domain research. Read Infilect's published case studies and blog posts to understand their shelf intelligence product, their retail clients, and the scale at which they operate. Map your own experience to their domain, especially any exposure to computer vision, image processing, or retail analytics.

Week one, system design practice. Prepare at least two ML system design walkthroughs: one for image recognition at scale and one for a real-time inference pipeline. Practice explaining trade-offs between accuracy, latency, and cost out loud, not just on paper.

Week two, behavioral story bank. Write out six to eight stories from your career using the STAR format. Cover these themes: managing underperformance, navigating a technical failure in production, recruiting ML talent, disagreeing with a stakeholder, and managing technical debt under time pressure.

Week two, people management depth. Be ready to discuss your specific process for one-on-ones, performance reviews, and career development conversations. Vague answers here are common and tend to score poorly with interviewers who manage engineering orgs themselves.

Before every conversation. Review the Infilect job description for the specific role you applied for, note any keywords around their tech stack, and prepare one thoughtful question per interviewer about the team's current challenges and roadmap.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too generic on ML topics. Saying 'I have managed ML teams' without specifics on model deployment, data pipelines, or accuracy monitoring signals a lack of depth. Infilect is a technical AI company and interviewers can tell when answers are surface-level.

Skipping the business impact. Candidates who focus only on the technical fix and never mention the client or business outcome tend to score lower. Always close your story with what changed for the product or the customer.

Underestimating the people management bar. Some candidates with strong technical backgrounds spend too little time preparing management stories. Infilect is scaling its engineering org, so hiring and developing talent is a real priority for this role.

Vague answers on team structure. 'I ran a team of engineers' is not enough. Be specific about team size, how you structured work, and how you handled growth or attrition.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask no questions or ask only about compensation tend to leave a weaker impression. Prepare questions about the team's current technical challenges, the product roadmap, and how success is measured in the first six months.

Over-preparing for coding. Engineering Manager interviews at this level typically do not include live coding. Spending most of your prep time on algorithmic problems and neglecting system design or behavioral rounds is a common and costly misstep.

How many rounds does the Infilect Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates typically report four to five conversations covering technical leadership, people management, system design, and cross-functional alignment. Round names and structure are not publicly standardized, so treat every conversation as potentially covering multiple themes. Interviewers at Infilect commonly blend technical and behavioral questions in the same discussion rather than keeping them separate.

Does Infilect expect Engineering Managers to write code in the interview?

Candidates typically report that live coding is not required for Engineering Manager roles at Infilect. The technical bar is assessed through system design discussions and your ability to reason about ML pipelines, model architecture, and engineering trade-offs. That said, interviewers may ask you to sketch out a data flow or component diagram on a shared document, so be ready to think visually.

What salary can I expect for an Engineering Manager role at Infilect?

Market data shows Engineering Manager roles in India running 35-60 LPA, Senior Manager roles at 55-90 LPA, and Director-level roles at 90-150+ LPA. Infilect-specific compensation is not publicly reported in detail, so treat these as reference points rather than guarantees. Should I have a computer vision or ML background to apply for EM roles at Infilect?

A strong ML or CV background helps, but candidates report that Infilect also values engineering managers who can learn the domain quickly and lead technically strong teams effectively. What matters most is your ability to ask sharp questions about ML systems and translate technical output into product value. If your background is in backend or distributed systems engineering, be prepared to show how you would get up to speed on computer vision concepts and the retail domain.

How should I prepare for system design questions at Infilect?

Focus on ML system design rather than traditional web architecture. Infilect's products run at retail scale, so be ready to discuss latency, throughput, and cost trade-offs in concrete terms. Candidates report that showing awareness of model drift detection and monitoring systems tends to stand out.
