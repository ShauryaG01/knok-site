01 Overview

Overview

Increff is a Bangalore-based supply chain and retail technology company. Their core products include IRIS (inventory management and reconciliation), a merchandising tool for demand planning, and a Warehouse Management System (WMS). Their clients are typically mid-to-large brands and retailers running omnichannel or e-commerce operations.

As of July 2026, Increff has 15 open Product Manager roles, which signals active growth in their product team. PM roles here are B2B-focused: you work closely with enterprise clients, operations teams, and engineers solving real supply chain problems.

The broader market has 2009 open PM roles across India right now. Bangalore leads with 271 openings, followed by Delhi at 177. Salary bands from knok jobradar show Associate PMs earning 12-20 LPA, PMs with 3-6 years at 24-40 LPA, Senior PMs at 40-60 LPA, and Group or Principal PMs at 55-90+ LPA.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report a mix of product design, execution, and domain questions. Typically, expect 3-4 rounds covering product thinking, analytical ability, and a cultural or leadership discussion.

  1. Walk us through a product you shipped that reduced an operational problem. What was the outcome?
  2. How would you prioritize features for Increff's IRIS platform when engineering bandwidth is limited?
  3. Design a dashboard for a warehouse manager who needs to track daily inventory accuracy. What metrics would you show?
  4. A large retail client says your WMS is too complex for their floor staff. How do you handle this?
  5. How would you measure the success of a new reconciliation feature in an order management system?
  6. Define the roadmap for a new SKU-level demand forecasting tool. Where do you start?
  7. Increff serves both D2C brands and large marketplace sellers. How do you handle conflicting feature requests from these two segments?
  8. How would you approach onboarding a new enterprise client onto the Increff platform end-to-end?
  9. A competitor launches a free inventory management tool targeting your mid-market clients. What is your response?
  10. How do you validate a new product idea with B2B enterprise clients before committing engineering resources?
  11. Tell me about a time you used data to change a product decision you had already made.
  12. How would you improve the returns management workflow in a supply chain platform like Increff's?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a product you built that reduced an operational problem.

*Situation:* Our operations team was manually reconciling inventory counts across three warehouses every morning, a process that took a long time and often surfaced errors only after orders had already been picked.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the design of an automated daily reconciliation report that would flag discrepancies before the picking cycle began.

*Action:* I started by sitting with the ops team for a week to map every step of their manual process. I then wrote the PRD, worked with data engineering to define the reconciliation logic, and ran two rounds of usability testing with warehouse supervisors before launch. I also added an alert so managers received a summary on their mobile early each morning.

*Result:* After rollout, the morning reconciliation process became dramatically faster based on the team's own time logs. The operations lead specifically called out the discrepancy catch rate improvement in the quarterly business review.

---

Q: How do you handle conflicting feature requests from two different client segments?

*Situation:* At my previous role, a D2C brand wanted single-click bulk order processing, while a large marketplace seller wanted configurable approval workflows before any bulk action could go through.

*Task:* Both clients were strategic accounts and both requests were justified given their very different operational models.

*Action:* I mapped the underlying job-to-be-done for each client. The D2C brand needed speed; the marketplace seller needed control and an audit trail. I designed a single bulk-action module with an optional approval step that each client could toggle on or off, so the workflow matched their operations. I involved both clients in feedback sessions before finalizing the specs.

*Result:* Both clients adopted the feature within the first quarter after release. Neither required custom engineering work, which kept the codebase clean and reduced our maintenance load going forward.

---

Q: Tell me about a time data changed a product decision you had already made.

*Situation:* I had committed to building a forecasting feature based on historical sales data alone, assuming that was what clients needed most.

*Task:* I was responsible for the feature spec and had already aligned the engineering team on the scope.

*Action:* Mid-sprint, I pulled early usage data from a pilot client and noticed they were exporting our forecasts into Excel to manually add adjustments for sale seasons and promotional periods. I paused, interviewed three client ops leads, and found that all three needed an event calendar built directly into the forecasting tool. I revised the spec to include an events layer before we built the core engine.

*Result:* The updated feature received strong positive feedback from all three pilot clients in the review session, and post-launch support requests were noticeably lower compared to similar past launches.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

These frameworks come up often in Increff-style PM interviews and are worth practising before your rounds.

RICE for prioritization: Score each feature by Reach (how many clients or users benefit), Impact (how much it moves the needle), Confidence (how sure you are about your estimates), and Effort (engineering weeks). This is especially useful when Increff asks you to prioritize a roadmap with limited bandwidth.

Jobs-to-be-Done: Focus on what the client is actually trying to accomplish, not what they are asking for. A warehouse manager asking for 'more reports' is really trying to catch errors before they cause order failures. This lens helps you design sharper, more targeted solutions.

Opportunity Solution Tree: Map a top-level outcome (for example, reduce client churn) to the opportunities (pain points clients face) and then to possible solutions. Useful when interviewers ask 'where would you start on a new problem area.'

STAR for behavioural rounds: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep each element concise. Interviewers at B2B companies want to see that your Action involved real stakeholders (clients, ops teams, engineers), not just solo analysis.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Increff builds software for operations and supply chain teams. Their interviewers typically look for things that differ from what consumer PM interviews prioritize.

Domain curiosity: You do not need to be a supply chain expert, but you should understand concepts like inventory reconciliation, SKU management, order management cycles, and warehouse operations at a working level. Candidates who have done even basic research on how a WMS works tend to stand out noticeably.

Client empathy in a B2B context: Consumer PMs think about end users. At Increff, your 'user' is often a warehouse supervisor or a brand's operations head. Interviewers want to see you reason from that person's daily workflow, not from a generic user persona.

Structured thinking under ambiguity: Increff's domain has many edge cases (returns, cancellations, multi-warehouse inventory splits). Interviewers test whether you can lay out a clear approach when a problem is messy and constraints are unclear.

Data-driven decision making: Expect questions about how you define and track success. Be ready to talk about leading indicators alongside outcome metrics, and explain why you chose each one.

Stakeholder management: In a B2B SaaS company, enterprise clients often push for custom features. Interviewers look for how you balance client requests against platform scalability and long-term product vision.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Give yourself at least two weeks of focused prep. Here is a practical plan.

Week 1: Company and domain research
Read through Increff's website and any publicly available case studies or blog posts. Understand what IRIS does, how their WMS differs from competitors, and what problems their clients face. Spend time on basic supply chain concepts: what is inventory reconciliation, how does demand forecasting work, and what does a returns workflow look like in an omnichannel context.

Week 2: Practice rounds
Pick 4-5 questions from the list in this guide and write out full STAR answers for each. Record yourself answering at least two of them out loud to check for clarity and pacing. Do one timed product design exercise where you design a feature for a B2B inventory tool from scratch, define success metrics, and outline a prioritized roadmap.

Before each round: Review the job description again. Candidates report that interviewers often refer directly to the JD when framing questions. Prepare two or three sharp questions to ask your interviewer about Increff's current product challenges and team structure.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

These are patterns that typically hurt candidates in Increff PM interviews.

Treating it like a consumer PM interview: Answers built around growth loops, user acquisition funnels, and retention hacks do not land well here. Frame every answer around operations efficiency, client ROI, and platform reliability.

Not knowing the product: Walking in without knowing what IRIS or Increff's WMS does signals low interest. Spend at least an hour on their website and any demos or walkthroughs available publicly before your first round.

Vague metrics: Saying 'engagement went up' is not enough. Interviewers want to know what you measured, how you measured it, and what the result actually meant for the client or the business.

Skipping the 'why' in prioritization: When asked to prioritize, candidates often list features without explaining the reasoning. Always tie your priority order back to client impact, revenue risk, or strategic fit.

Ignoring the client perspective in design questions: If you design a feature and your answer does not mention how a warehouse manager or ops lead would actually use it day-to-day, you have missed the core expectation for a B2B PM role.

Rushing to solutions: Increff interviewers typically value structured problem-framing. Take a moment to state your assumptions and clarify the problem before jumping into your answer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Increff PM interview typically have?

Candidates report typically 3-4 rounds. These usually cover a product thinking or design round, an analytical or metrics round, a behavioural round, and sometimes a final discussion with a senior leader or founder. Round names and sequences vary, so ask your recruiter for the exact format when you receive your first call.

Does Increff ask case studies or coding questions for PM roles?

Candidates report that PM interviews at Increff focus on product design, prioritization, and behavioural questions rather than coding. You may get a light analytical question around data interpretation or SQL-style thinking, but typically no coding is expected. Case study-style questions about improving a supply chain product are common.

What salary can a PM expect at Increff?

Knok jobradar data shows PM roles with 3-6 years of experience in India broadly ranging from 24-40 LPA. Increff-specific compensation figures are not publicly confirmed, so check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for company-specific data points before you negotiate your offer.

Is domain knowledge in supply chain required to get a PM role at Increff?

You do not need to be a supply chain expert, but basic familiarity with inventory management, order management cycles, and warehouse operations will clearly help. Candidates who show genuine curiosity about the domain and have done their homework on Increff's products tend to perform better than those with strong PM credentials but no domain preparation.

How should I prepare for the product design round at Increff?

Pick a supply chain or inventory management problem (such as reducing stockouts or improving the returns processing flow) and practise designing a solution end-to-end: define the user, map the workflow, propose features, and define success metrics. Use the Jobs-to-be-Done framework to anchor your design in what operations teams are actually trying to accomplish, not just what features sound impressive.

How do I stand out as a candidate for a PM role at Increff?

Three things tend to differentiate candidates: knowing Increff's actual products before the interview, showing structured thinking when problems are ambiguous, and framing every answer through the lens of client operations and business impact. Asking sharp questions about Increff's product challenges during your interview also signals genuine interest and preparation.
