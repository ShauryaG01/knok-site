01 Overview

Overview

Elastic, the company behind Elasticsearch, Kibana, Logstash, and Beats, runs a DevOps team that directly supports how its platform scales and ships globally. Preparing for a DevOps Engineer role here means showing strong hands-on experience with the Elastic Stack alongside core DevOps fundamentals like CI/CD, containerisation, and cloud infrastructure.

As of mid-2026, Elastic has 233 DevOps-related openings tracked on knok jobradar. The broader Indian market shows 811 active DevOps Engineer jobs right now, with Bangalore leading at 187 openings, followed by Delhi (40), Pune (37), Hyderabad (28), Chennai (13), and Mumbai (11).

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions appear frequently in Elastic DevOps interviews, based on candidate reports and the company's known technical focus areas.

  1. Walk me through how you have used the Elastic Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana, Beats) in a real production environment.
  2. How would you design and maintain a CI/CD pipeline for a large microservices application?
  3. Describe your experience with Kubernetes. How do you handle cluster upgrades and node failures?
  4. How do you approach log aggregation and centralised observability for a distributed system?
  5. A Kibana dashboard is showing high query latency in Elasticsearch. How do you diagnose and resolve it?
  6. What is your approach to infrastructure-as-code? Which tools have you used, and why did you choose them?
  7. How do you manage secrets and credentials securely across dev, staging, and production environments?
  8. Describe a production incident you owned from detection to resolution and prevention.
  9. How do you configure Elasticsearch index lifecycle management (ILM) policies for cost and performance?
  10. Which cloud platforms have you worked on, and how have you deployed or managed Elastic products there?
  11. How do you design for high availability and disaster recovery for services your team is responsible for?
  12. Elastic has strong open-source roots. Have you contributed to or customised open-source tooling in your DevOps work?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you have used the Elastic Stack in a production environment.

*Situation:* Our e-commerce platform was generating logs from services across multiple teams with no centralised visibility. On-call engineers spent hours SSH-ing into individual servers during incidents.

*Task:* I was asked to build a centralised logging and alerting system before the upcoming peak sale season.

*Action:* I deployed Filebeat agents on each service host, set up a Logstash pipeline to parse and enrich logs, and pushed them into Elasticsearch. I then built Kibana dashboards tracking error rate, latency, and order-failure metrics, and configured Watcher alerts to notify the team on Slack.

*Result:* Incident detection became significantly faster. The on-call team went from manually grepping logs across servers to triaging through a single Kibana view within one sprint of the rollout.

---

Q: Describe a production incident you owned from detection to resolution and prevention.

*Situation:* Our Elasticsearch cluster ran out of disk space during a high-traffic period. Writes started failing silently and the application degraded without showing a visible error page to users.

*Task:* As the on-call DevOps engineer, I needed to restore write availability and prevent data loss, then make sure this could not happen again.

*Action:* I identified the disk saturation through Kibana's monitoring view, deleted old indices using ILM policies I pushed manually, freed up space, and cleared the write block. I then set up automated ILM rollover and a Kibana disk-usage alert to fire well before saturation.

*Result:* We had no repeat disk-related write failures in the months that followed. The alert now triggers a runbook automatically, removing the need for manual intervention on routine index rotation.

---

Q: How do you manage secrets and credentials across different environments?

*Situation:* A team I joined had database passwords hardcoded in environment files that were committed to Git. A security audit flagged this as a critical risk.

*Task:* I needed to migrate all secrets out of source control into a secure, auditable system without breaking any running deployments.

*Action:* I introduced HashiCorp Vault for secrets management, updated our CI/CD pipelines to fetch credentials at runtime using short-lived tokens, rotated all exposed secrets, and walked the team through the new process with a written runbook.

*Result:* The next security audit marked secrets management as resolved. No engineer sees plaintext credentials in any config file today, and audit logs track every secrets access event.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for behavioural and incident questions. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Elastic interviewers ask many 'tell me about a time' questions because they care about how you work, not just what you know. Keep Situation and Task brief (one to two sentences each) and spend most of your answer on Action and Result.

Use a structured diagnosis flow for troubleshooting questions. When asked 'how would you debug X', a strong answer follows this pattern: (1) gather symptoms and scope the impact, (2) form a hypothesis, (3) isolate the component, (4) apply a fix, (5) verify and monitor. This maps to how Elastic SRE and DevOps teams work in practice.

For system design questions, anchor on trade-offs. Elastic engineers care about why you made a choice, not just what you chose. For every design decision, briefly name the alternative you rejected and why. For example: 'I chose Kafka over a direct Logstash push because we needed replay capability and backpressure handling.'

For open-source and tooling questions, lead with real context. Elastic has deep open-source roots. If you have contributed to any project, raised issues, or customised open-source tools, mention it. Even small contributions signal genuine alignment with the company's culture.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep familiarity with the Elastic Stack. This is not optional at Elastic. You do not need to have used every product, but you should speak fluently about Elasticsearch indexing, Kibana dashboards, and at least one ingest method (Beats or Logstash). Surface-level knowledge gets spotted quickly.

Ownership and reliability instincts. Elastic's engineering culture emphasises accountability. Interviewers listen for whether you treat incidents as learning events, whether you set up monitoring before things break, and whether you write runbooks or postmortems without being asked.

Cloud-native thinking. Most Elastic infrastructure work happens on AWS, GCP, or Azure. They want engineers who think in terms of managed services, autoscaling, and cost optimisation rather than 'it runs on a VM.'

Collaboration across teams. DevOps at Elastic spans engineering, SRE, and product teams. Interviewers look for candidates who can explain a technical decision clearly to a non-DevOps colleague and who have a track record of working well across functions.

Curiosity about scale. Elastic runs some of the largest Elasticsearch deployments in the world. Candidates who have thought about shard sizing, cluster topology, index lifecycle, or query performance at scale make a far stronger impression than those who treat Elasticsearch as a black box.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before the interview:

Set up a local Elastic Stack (Elasticsearch, Kibana, Logstash, Filebeat) using Docker Compose. Ingest a sample dataset, build a dashboard, and configure a Watcher alert. This hands-on practice is more useful than reading documentation alone.

Review Kubernetes fundamentals: deployments, services, configmaps, horizontal pod autoscaling, and rolling updates. Practise writing a Kubernetes manifest or basic Helm chart from memory.

Read Elastic's engineering blog (search for it by name). Posts on cluster performance, index design, and observability patterns reflect the topics that come up in technical rounds.

One week before:

Prepare three to five STAR stories covering: a major incident you resolved end to end, a system you designed or significantly improved, a time you introduced a new tool or practice to your team, and a situation where you navigated a disagreement or miscommunication.

Revise infrastructure-as-code basics. Be ready to discuss how you have used Terraform, Ansible, or a similar tool in real projects, not just tutorials.

Practise explaining your most complex system design out loud. Recording yourself helps catch unclear reasoning before the interview.

Day before:

Re-read the job description. Map each requirement to a prepared story or example. If there is a gap, plan to acknowledge it honestly rather than bluffing.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating the Elastic Stack as optional background knowledge. Candidates who say 'I have used ELK a bit' without specifics lose ground fast. Elastic interviewers expect operational depth: ILM policies, mapping types, shard allocation, and at minimum a working knowledge of query DSL. Brush up before the call.

Over-generalising DevOps answers. Saying 'I use Terraform for everything' without explaining a real problem it solved tells the interviewer very little. Always connect tools to specific outcomes and trade-offs.

Ignoring the open-source angle. Elastic grew from open-source roots. Candidates who have never engaged with open-source tooling, filed issues, or read a changelog can come across as passive consumers. This is a soft signal but interviewers notice it.

Skipping the 'why' in system design. Elastic engineers make architectural trade-offs constantly. Describing a design without mentioning what alternatives you considered means interviewers cannot tell if you made a deliberate decision or just stumbled into a working setup.

Giving vague incident answers. 'We had an outage and fixed it' is not enough. Interviewers want to know: how did you detect it, what was your first hypothesis, how did you isolate the cause, and what changed afterwards. Prepare a specific story with real steps and a clear result.

Not asking questions. Elastic is a distributed-first company. Not asking about team structure, on-call rotation, or how the DevOps team interfaces with product engineering signals low curiosity, which is a red flag at a company that values a growth mindset.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Elastic DevOps interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from 3 to 5 rounds, though Elastic does not publicly confirm a fixed structure. The pattern that appears most often includes a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds, a practical or system design exercise, and a final round with the hiring manager. The total process typically spans two to four weeks, based on timelines shared publicly by candidates.

Does Elastic ask Elasticsearch-specific questions even for a DevOps role?

Yes, more than most companies. Because Elastic sells and operates the Elastic Stack, DevOps Engineers are expected to run, monitor, and troubleshoot Elasticsearch in production. Candidates report questions on ILM policies, shard sizing, cluster health, and Kibana alerting. You do not need to be a developer, but operational depth on the stack is expected.

What is the salary range for a DevOps Engineer role at Elastic in India?

Elastic does not publicly publish India-specific salary bands. For context, the broader Indian DevOps market shows 6-12 LPA for entry level (0-2 years), 15-28 LPA for mid-level (3-5 years), 30-50 LPA for senior (6-9 years), and 45-70+ LPA for Lead/Staff roles, based on knok jobradar data. Elastic's compensation is publicly reported to be competitive for a product-led tech company at its scale.

Is prior Elastic Stack experience required to apply?

It is not listed as a hard requirement in most job descriptions, but it is strongly preferred. Candidates who can speak to real production experience with the Elastic Stack, even at a small scale, consistently perform better than those with only generic DevOps backgrounds. If you have not used the stack before, building a local setup before the interview is the fastest way to close that gap.

Does Elastic give a take-home assignment for DevOps roles?

Some candidates report a practical exercise, which may be a system design question, a short infrastructure task, or a scenario-based troubleshooting problem. Others report that the technical interview rounds covered everything without a separate take-home component. Elastic typically communicates the interview format in advance, so ask your recruiter what to expect after the screening call.

How important is cloud platform experience for this role?

Very important. Elastic Cloud runs on AWS, GCP, and Azure, and DevOps Engineers work with these platforms regularly. Candidates report questions on IAM, managed Kubernetes services, autoscaling, and cost controls. In-depth knowledge of at least one major cloud provider is the baseline expectation, and experience with Elastic Cloud deployments specifically is a strong bonus.
