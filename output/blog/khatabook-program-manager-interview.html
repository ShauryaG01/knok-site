01 Overview

Overview

Khatabook has grown into one of India's leading fintech platforms for small businesses, and its engineering org currently lists 61 open roles. For the Technical Program Manager (TPM) position, candidates typically report a multi-stage process: an initial recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds focused on program execution and systems thinking, and a final conversation with a senior leader or hiring manager.

TPMs at Khatabook sit at the intersection of product, engineering, and business teams. Expect interviewers to probe how you coordinate cross-functional launches, manage risk in fast-moving sprints, and use data to keep stakeholders aligned. The bar leans toward candidates who can show real ownership of delivery outcomes, not just process facilitation.

Across India, there are 313 open TPM roles right now, with Bangalore (41), Delhi (14), Pune (13), and Hyderabad (12) leading the count. Khatabook's focus on SMB fintech means your answers should reflect an understanding of scale, reliability, and the unique challenges of building for India's small-business segment.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on what candidates commonly share, here are the types of questions Khatabook interviewers typically ask for TPM roles:

  1. Tell us about a program you managed end-to-end where requirements changed midway. How did you handle it?
  2. How would you coordinate a product launch across engineering, design, QA, and business teams at Khatabook?
  3. Describe a time you identified a critical risk in a project and what you did to mitigate it.
  4. How do you prioritize competing requests from multiple stakeholders?
  5. Walk us through how you would set up a program roadmap for a new payments feature.
  6. Tell us about a time a project you managed missed its deadline. What happened and what did you learn?
  7. How do you measure the success of a program after it ships?
  8. Describe your approach to running a weekly sync with engineering leads who sit across different squads.
  9. How would you handle a situation where an engineering team pushes back on a committed timeline?
  10. What tools and frameworks do you use to track program health and dependencies?
  11. Khatabook serves millions of small businesses. How would you factor reliability and uptime into your program plan?
  12. Give an example of how you used data to influence a decision during a program review.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a program you managed where requirements changed midway.

*Situation:* I was leading the rollout of a new invoicing module at a fintech company. Two sprints in, the product team received merchant feedback that changed the core workflow.

*Task:* I needed to re-scope the release without losing the original launch window or demoralising the engineering team.

*Action:* I called a re-planning session within two days, mapping the new requirements against what was already built. I worked with engineering leads to identify reusable components and cut three low-priority features to keep the timeline intact. I updated the stakeholder tracker the same week so leadership had full visibility.

*Result:* We shipped the revised module only one sprint behind the original date. Merchant adoption in the first month matched our target, and the features we deferred were picked up in the next quarter.

---

Q: Describe a time you identified a critical risk in a project.

*Situation:* During a payment gateway migration, I noticed that the new provider's sandbox had intermittent timeout issues during peak-hour testing.

*Task:* I had to determine whether this was a sandbox-only problem or something that could affect production, and flag it before the go-live date.

*Action:* I partnered with the backend lead to run a focused load test simulating peak traffic. The results confirmed latency spikes under high concurrency. I escalated to the vendor, documented the findings, and proposed a phased rollout with automatic fallback to the old gateway.

*Result:* Leadership approved the phased approach. We caught two production-grade issues during the controlled rollout that would have caused payment failures at scale.

---

Q: How do you handle pushback from engineering on a committed timeline?

*Situation:* An engineering squad informed me, three weeks before a committed date, that a dependency on an internal service was more complex than estimated.

*Task:* I needed to either find a path to meet the deadline or negotiate a revised date with the business team transparently.

*Action:* I sat down with the tech lead to break the dependency into smaller tasks and identify which parts were truly blocking. We found that one integration could use a simpler interim approach, saving about a week of work. I presented the revised plan to the business team with clear trade-offs.

*Result:* We delivered the core functionality on time. The remaining integration shipped in a follow-up release, and the business team appreciated the transparency.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable format for behavioural questions. Khatabook interviewers, like most fintech hiring panels, want to hear specifics, not theory. Keep each section to two or three sentences.

For system design or process questions (like 'how would you set up a program roadmap'), try a simple structure:

  • Context: restate the problem in your own words to show understanding.
  • Approach: walk through your steps, naming the tools, meetings, or artifacts you would use.
  • Trade-offs: mention what you would watch out for or what could go wrong.

A useful tip for Khatabook specifically: tie your answers to the SMB fintech domain. Mentioning reliability, merchant impact, or scale shows you understand the environment you would be operating in.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Khatabook TPM interviewers typically evaluate candidates on a few clear dimensions:

Ownership of delivery. They want to see that you drive outcomes, not just track Jira tickets. Show examples where you made decisions, escalated risks early, or unblocked teams.

Cross-functional communication. TPMs at Khatabook work across product, engineering, data, and business teams. Interviewers look for your ability to translate between technical and non-technical audiences.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Fintech programs often shift scope. Demonstrating that you can re-plan without losing momentum is highly valued.

Domain awareness. You do not need deep fintech expertise, but showing that you understand the stakes of building reliable systems for small businesses in India will set you apart.

Data-driven decision making. Expect follow-up questions like 'how did you measure that?' or 'what metrics did you track?' Have concrete examples ready.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: research and foundations.
Study Khatabook's product suite, its SMB user base, and recent company news. Review the job description carefully and map your past experience to each listed responsibility. Practise articulating your top five career stories using the STAR format.

Week two: mock interviews and depth.
Run at least two mock interviews with a friend or mentor, focusing on the question types listed above. Record yourself and check for clarity, conciseness, and whether you quantify results. Brush up on program management fundamentals: dependency mapping, risk registers, stakeholder communication plans.

Week three: refine and rehearse.
Revisit your weakest answers from mocks. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about Khatabook's engineering culture, team structure, or upcoming priorities. Do a final dry run the day before.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too generic. Saying 'I coordinated with stakeholders' without naming who, what, or how. Always anchor answers in a real scenario with specific details.

Ignoring the domain. Khatabook builds for India's small-business segment. Candidates who do not reference reliability, scale, or merchant impact miss an easy way to stand out.

Over-indexing on tools. Listing every project management tool you have used is less compelling than explaining how you used one tool to solve a real problem.

Skipping the result. Many candidates describe the situation and action but forget to state the outcome. Interviewers need to know the impact of your work.

Not asking questions. Ending the interview without questions signals low interest. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team or product.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Khatabook typically have for TPM roles?

Candidates commonly report three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two technical or behavioural interviews, and a final round with a senior leader. The exact structure can vary by team and level.

Does Khatabook ask system design questions for TPMs?

TPM interviews at Khatabook typically focus more on program execution and stakeholder management than deep system design. That said, candidates report being asked to walk through how they would plan a technical rollout, so a working knowledge of system concepts is helpful.

What level of technical depth is expected?

You are not expected to write code, but you should be comfortable discussing APIs, databases, and service dependencies at a conceptual level. Being able to ask the right technical questions matters more than having all the answers.

How should I prepare if I have no fintech experience?

Focus on transferable skills: cross-functional coordination, risk management, and data-driven decision making. Research Khatabook's product and user base so you can speak to the domain with confidence, even if your past work is in a different industry.

Are there TPM openings at other companies right now?

What soft skills matter most for this role?

Clear communication, the ability to push back respectfully, and strong organisational habits. Khatabook operates in a fast-paced environment, so showing that you can stay calm and structured under pressure goes a long way.
