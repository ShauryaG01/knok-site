01 Overview

Overview

Univest is an Indian fintech company building tools for retail investors, covering stock advisory, portfolio management, and personal finance guidance. The company is actively growing its product team and currently has 3 open Product Manager roles, making this a relevant time to understand what the interview process looks like.

Candidates report that the PM interview process at Univest typically runs across two to four rounds, covering a mix of product sense, analytical thinking, and behavioural questions. There is typically no separate take-home assignment, but you should be ready for live product design and case questions focused on Univest's core user needs.

Univest PMs work closely with engineering, design, and business teams to ship features that simplify investing for users who may not have deep financial knowledge. Interviewers tend to value domain curiosity alongside standard PM skills like prioritisation, metrics thinking, and stakeholder management.

For wider market context, knok jobradar tracked 2,009 active PM openings across India as of 8 July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 271 roles and Delhi at 177. Salary bands for PM roles range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate level up to 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PMs.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would improve the onboarding experience for a first-time investor on Univest.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a significant portion of newly registered users on our financial app dropped off before completing profile setup.

*Task:* My goal was to redesign the onboarding flow to improve activation without reducing the quality of information we collected from users.

*Action:* I ran user interviews with people who had abandoned onboarding and found that the KYC document upload step felt too early and trust-breaking. I worked with design and engineering to restructure the flow: show the product's value first through a personalised portfolio suggestion, then request documents. I also added a progress bar and broke the multi-field form into shorter screens.

*Result:* The next cohort showed a clear improvement in activation based on our internal dashboards. I would apply the same 'value before ask' principle to Univest, since new investors need to feel the product is genuinely useful before sharing sensitive details.

---

Q: A key metric drops noticeably week over week. How do you diagnose the root cause?

*Situation:* At a previous role, our weekly active user count fell across two consecutive weeks with no obvious product release to explain it.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly and bring a clear hypothesis with supporting evidence to the leadership team.

*Action:* I decomposed the metric into components: new users, returning users, and reactivated users. The drop was entirely in returning users. I then sliced by platform and found the decline was isolated to Android. Checking release timelines, a recent build had introduced a performance issue causing the home feed to load slowly on older devices. I confirmed this with session length and crash log data.

*Result:* Engineering shipped a fix within days and the metric recovered the following week. The key lesson: always segment before forming a hypothesis, or you end up chasing the wrong cause.

---

Q: A senior stakeholder wants to build a feature you believe will not move the needle for users. How do you handle this?

*Situation:* A senior sales leader at my company wanted us to build a PDF export of portfolio reports, believing it would speed up enterprise deal closures.

*Task:* I had to evaluate the request honestly and either align on building it or make a well-reasoned case for deprioritisation.

*Action:* Rather than saying no outright, I pulled usage data on similar export features we had, then interviewed several enterprise clients directly. The real blocker to closing deals was not the PDF but the absence of multi-user access. I shared the findings using the stakeholder's own success criteria: 'will this close deals faster?' The data did not support the PDF feature, and he agreed to reprioritise toward multi-user access.

*Result:* Two enterprise accounts converted after the multi-user feature shipped. The stakeholder later said the conversation helped him trust the product team more with prioritisation calls.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions (such as 'improve onboarding' or 'design a new feature'), start with the user and their specific goal, identify key pain points, explore a few possible solutions, select one and explain the trade-off, then define how you would measure success. Avoid jumping to a solution before establishing the user context.

For metric-drop or analytical questions, use a metric tree. Break the top-line number into components (acquisition, activation, retention, revenue), isolate which component is falling, then segment by platform, geography, or cohort before proposing a root cause. For Univest specifically, be ready to reason about fintech metrics like 'advisory viewed', 'investment made', or 'portfolio linked'.

For prioritisation questions, a simple impact-versus-effort framing works well in a live conversation. You can also use a user-value lens: how painful is the problem, how many users face it, and what does it cost to solve? Be ready to defend your ranking when the interviewer pushes back.

For behavioural questions, the STAR structure keeps your answer focused: Situation (one to two sentences), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, not 'we'), Result (a concrete outcome with a metric if possible). Candidates report that Univest interviewers often follow up with 'what would you do differently?', so prepare a reflection for each story.

For competitive positioning questions, anchor on Univest's specific user segment (retail investors who may be new to markets) rather than listing generic differentiators. Trust, simplicity, and financial education are angles that tend to resonate in this space.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity. Univest builds for retail investors, many of whom are navigating markets for the first time. Interviewers want to see that you understand this user's anxiety and hesitation, not just their demographic profile. Showing familiarity with fintech products and being able to articulate what makes them confusing or delightful signals that you will ramp up quickly.

Structured thinking under pressure. PM roles at fintech companies involve decisions that directly affect users' money. Interviewers typically look for candidates who can slow down, frame the problem clearly, and resist the urge to jump to a solution before defining the goal. Candidates report that interviewers will sometimes let you talk yourself into a corner to see if you self-correct.

Ownership over coordination. Candidates report that Univest interviewers respond well to stories where you personally drove a decision or unblocked a situation, not just facilitated a meeting. Use 'I' more than 'we' in your STAR answers, and attribute credit to the team in your closing rather than throughout.

Comfort with data. You do not need to be a data analyst, but you should be able to name the specific metric you would track, describe how you would segment it, and explain what a healthy versus unhealthy trend looks like. Vague answers like 'I would look at engagement' tend to get probed until you arrive at something specific.

User empathy grounded in evidence. Saying 'I think users want X' is weaker than 'In user research, we found X.' If you have direct user research experience, bring it. If not, show that you know the difference between an assumption and a validated insight.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product. Download the Univest app and use it as a first-time investor would. Note where you feel uncertain, where the experience is clear, and what one feature you would change and why. Prepare a short product teardown you can deliver in two minutes if asked.

Week 1: Build domain basics. Review how retail investing and stock advisory works in India. Understand terms like SIP, mutual funds, equity advisory, and the role of SEBI well enough to hold a sensible conversation. You do not need deep expertise, but you should not appear lost when these topics come up.

Week 2: Build your story bank. Prepare five to seven STAR stories covering: a feature you shipped, a metric you improved, a conflict with a stakeholder, a decision made with incomplete data, and a time you had to deprioritise something. Each story should take roughly ninety seconds to two minutes to tell.

Week 2: Practise frameworks out loud. Do mock answers to at least three of the questions listed in this guide. Record yourself or practise with a friend. The aim is to sound structured but natural, not like you are reading from a checklist.

Day before the interview. Look for any recent news, product updates, or blog posts from Univest. Referencing something specific and current signals genuine interest and preparation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic answers. Answering a Univest interview question the same way you would answer it for any PM role is a missed opportunity. Ground your answers in fintech, retail investors, or Univest's product context wherever you can.

Skipping the reasoning behind your prioritisation. Saying 'I would build feature A first' without explaining the logic leaves interviewers unsatisfied. Always state the user problem you are solving and the trade-off you are accepting.

Over-using frameworks. Walking through a six-step framework for a simple question signals that you have studied PM content without internalising it. Fit the structure to the question, and drop steps that add no value in that context.

Weak success metrics. Finishing a product design answer with 'I would track engagement' is too vague. Name the specific metric, describe what moving it means for the user and the business, and mention the time window over which you would evaluate change.

Jumping in without clarifying. For open-ended design or strategy questions, candidates who start answering immediately often go in the wrong direction. A brief clarifying question, such as 'Are we designing for first-time investors or experienced traders?', signals product maturity.

Saying 'we' throughout behavioural answers. In behavioural rounds, interviewers are evaluating your individual contribution. Make sure your specific actions and decisions are clearly visible, even if you credit the broader team at the close.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Univest PM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves two to four rounds. These commonly include an introductory call, one or two product and case rounds, and a final round with a senior leader. The exact number can vary based on the seniority of the role, so ask your recruiter what to expect after the first call.

Does Univest give a take-home case study for PM roles?

Most candidates report that Univest does not use a separate take-home assignment, though this can vary by role or team. Product design and analytical questions are typically covered in live interview rounds. Confirm the format with your recruiter contact so you know exactly how to prepare.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Univest?

Univest does not publicly publish its compensation bands. As a reference point, salary bands commonly cited for PM roles in India range from 24-40 LPA for PMs with three to six years of experience, and 40-60 LPA at the Senior PM level. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific role, and negotiation, so treat these figures as a starting reference and verify during the offer stage.

How important is fintech or investing domain knowledge for this interview?

It matters, but candidates without deep fintech backgrounds have cleared the process by showing strong user empathy and genuine curiosity about the domain. At a minimum, understand Univest's product, know who its users are, and be able to speak to the problems retail investors commonly face. Arriving with no domain prep at all is likely to be a clear negative signal to interviewers.

How should I answer the 'improve our product' question at Univest?

Use the app yourself before the interview and identify one real friction point from the user's perspective. Frame your answer around a specific user segment, state the problem clearly, propose one focused solution, and define a metric to track success. A single well-reasoned improvement lands better than a long list of feature ideas.

Is Univest suitable for a first PM role, or does the company prefer experienced candidates?

Univest currently has 3 open PM roles based on knok jobradar data, spanning different experience levels. The company has hired at both Associate PM and more senior levels depending on the team's needs. If you are earlier in your career, lead with any product-adjacent work, structured thinking, and evidence that you can turn user problems into clear product decisions.
