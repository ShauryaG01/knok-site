01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

The Convergence Foundation is one of India's most ambitious policy research and action organisations, working at the intersection of government, civil society, and technology to drive large-scale systemic reform. Based in Bengaluru, it focuses on areas like public digital infrastructure, economic inclusion, and governance innovation.

As of July 2026, the foundation has 29 open roles across functions including research, programme management, policy analysis, communications, and operations. This breadth of openings means the organisation is actively building capacity, and there are genuine entry points for both early-career researchers and experienced professionals.

Hiring here is selective. The foundation tends to attract candidates who are deeply motivated by public good outcomes rather than purely corporate career tracks. If your background is in economics, public policy, development studies, technology, or social impact, this is a place where that expertise is directly valued.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Expect a multi-stage process that tests both your domain knowledge and your alignment with the foundation's mission. Timelines and exact stages vary by role and team, but here is what candidates commonly report.

Stage 1: Application and screening.
You apply through the foundation's careers page or a job portal. The team reviews your resume and cover letter carefully. A strong cover letter that shows you understand the foundation's specific focus areas goes a long way at this stage.

Stage 2: Initial HR or recruiter conversation.
If shortlisted, you will get a call from someone in the talent or operations team. This is mostly a fit and logistics check, covering your background, availability, and why you want to work in the policy or social impact space.

Stage 3: Written or analytical task.
Many roles, especially on the research and programme side, include a written assignment. This could be a policy brief, a data analysis exercise, or a short note summarising a complex issue. Quality of thinking and clarity of writing matter more here than length.

Stage 4: Domain interview.
This is usually a conversation with a senior member of the team you would be joining. Expect questions about your subject matter expertise, past projects, and how you approach problems in the policy or development space.

Stage 5: Panel or leadership interview.
Final-round candidates typically meet with a panel or a senior leader. This round often goes deeper into strategic thinking, your views on specific policy issues, and how you handle ambiguity or stakeholder complexity.

Reference checks are common before an offer is made. The full process can run across several weeks, so follow up politely if you have not heard back after the timeline they gave you.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Mission alignment comes first. The Convergence Foundation is not a consulting firm or a corporate, and it screens hard for candidates who genuinely care about the problems it works on. You should be able to speak concretely about why systemic change in India matters to you, not just recite the foundation's website back to them.

Strong analytical and writing skills. Almost every role here involves synthesising complex information and communicating it clearly to policymakers, funders, or the public. If you can write a crisp two-page note on a thorny policy question, you have a core skill this organisation prizes.

Relevant domain knowledge. Depending on the role, this might mean economics, public health, digital governance, urban planning, education policy, or financial inclusion. Deep knowledge in one area is valued; the ability to work across areas is a bonus.

Collaboration and humility. The foundation works closely with government partners, academics, civil society groups, and technology organisations. People who are comfortable working across very different institutional cultures, and who listen as much as they talk, tend to fit well here.

Comfort with ambiguity. Policy work rarely has clean answers. Candidates who can frame uncertain situations, design small experiments to test ideas, and adapt when the ground shifts are valued more than those who need rigid processes to function.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Read the foundation's published work before any interview. Go through their reports, policy notes, and any public commentary by its leaders. Pick two or three pieces that genuinely interest you and be ready to discuss them, including what you found compelling and what you might push back on.

Know your own work deeply. You will be asked to walk through past projects in detail. Prepare clear narratives around two or three pieces of work that show analytical rigour, impact, or both. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure these stories.

Prepare a point of view on a current policy challenge. The foundation works on live issues. Come with a considered opinion on something relevant, whether that is the state of India's digital public infrastructure, a gap in social protection coverage, or a governance challenge at the state level. You do not need to have all the answers, but you should be able to frame the problem well.

Practise your writing before a written task. If you are applying for a research or programme role, dust off your policy writing. A clean, well-structured one-page note is a strong signal. Avoid jargon and get to the point early.

Prepare thoughtful questions. At the end of interviews, ask about the team's current priorities, how success is measured, and how the foundation collaborates with government. Shallow questions (like 'what is the work culture like?') will not impress here.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Intellectual rigour is the baseline. The Convergence Foundation attracts people who have often come from top universities, government fellowships, or respected research organisations. The bar for the quality of analysis and argument is high, and that is true at every level of seniority.

The work is collaborative but serious. Teams work closely together and across the organisation, but there is a focused quality to the culture. People are here because they care deeply about outcomes, not because it is a comfortable nine-to-six role.

Government partnership is central. Much of the foundation's work is designed to eventually influence or be adopted by state and central government. This shapes the culture in important ways: patience, relationship-building, and the ability to translate complex ideas into actionable policy recommendations are all valued traits.

The organisation is relatively lean. This means individuals often carry significant responsibility early in their tenure. If you are someone who wants to own work and see it through from idea to implementation, that opportunity exists here.

Diversity of background is welcome. The foundation brings together economists, technologists, lawyers, civil servants, and social scientists. Comfort working with people whose training and worldview differ from yours is a genuine part of the culture, not just a stated value.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a postgraduate degree to get hired at The Convergence Foundation?

A postgraduate degree in economics, public policy, law, or a related field is common among the foundation's researchers and programme leads. That said, strong demonstrated experience in policy, government, or social impact work can carry significant weight alongside or instead of formal credentials. For more operational or technical roles, the degree requirement may be more flexible. Check the specific job description carefully, as requirements vary by team and seniority level.

Does the foundation hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

The foundation does hire at junior levels, particularly for research analyst or associate roles, and occasionally takes on fellows or interns. Most roles, however, assume at least some relevant prior experience. If you are a fresher, a strong academic record in a relevant field, published work, or a government or civil society internship will strengthen your profile considerably.

How long does the hiring process typically take?

Candidates commonly report a process that runs across several weeks, and senior or specialised roles can take longer depending on the number of stages involved. If you have not heard back after the timeline the recruiter gave you, a polite follow-up email is appropriate and generally well-received. Avoid sending multiple follow-ups in quick succession, as the team tends to be small and busy.

Is the written assignment graded formally or is it just a screening tool?

The written task is taken seriously and is a genuine part of the evaluation, not just a checkbox exercise. Interviewers typically review what you submitted and may ask you to walk through your reasoning in a subsequent conversation. Focus on clarity of argument and honest engagement with the complexity of the issue rather than trying to guess the 'right' answer, as the foundation values original thinking over safe, conventional positions.

What kind of salaries does the foundation offer?

The Convergence Foundation is a non-profit, and compensation is generally described in industry surveys as competitive within the social and policy sector. Glassdoor has limited data specific to this organisation, so treat any figures you find there as indicative rather than reliable. The foundation's offer tends to appeal most to candidates who weigh mission, learning, and real-world impact heavily alongside salary, rather than those benchmarking purely against corporate or consulting-firm packages.

Can I apply for multiple roles at once?

Generally yes, you can apply to more than one open role if you genuinely meet the requirements for each. Be thoughtful about it: applying to many unrelated positions at the same organisation can signal a lack of focus or genuine interest. Aim for roles that are clearly adjacent to your skills and experience, and tailor your cover letter to each specific team and function rather than sending the same note across all of them.
