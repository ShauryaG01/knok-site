01 Overview

Overview

CashKaro is one of India's largest cashback and coupons platforms, connecting shoppers with a large network of brand partners through affiliate links and crediting real cashback to bank accounts. The company also operates EarnKaro, an affiliate marketing app for creators and micro-influencers who earn commissions by sharing deals. PM roles at CashKaro typically span consumer growth, monetisation, B2B partner products, and the creator ecosystem.

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening, a product case or take-home assignment, and two to three rounds covering product thinking, metrics, and cross-functional collaboration. Knok's jobradar tracked 2,009 Product Manager openings across India and 46 open roles at CashKaro specifically as of July 2026, with Bangalore and Delhi concentrating the most PM hiring nationally. Salary bands for Product Managers in India run 12-20 LPA at the Associate level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PMs, based on knok data.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions appear frequently in CashKaro PM interviews, based on what candidates typically report.

  1. How would you improve the cashback discovery experience on CashKaro for a first-time user?
  2. CashKaro's core habit relies on users clicking through before they shop. How would you build this behaviour into the product?
  3. Walk us through how you would prioritise features for EarnKaro's affiliate creators versus CashKaro's end consumers when resources are limited.
  4. How would you measure the success of a push notification strategy aimed at reducing missed cashback occasions?
  5. A key brand partner wants more clicks and higher conversion from CashKaro. How would you design a product solution without hurting the user experience?
  6. CashKaro competes with credit card cashback programmes. How would you position CashKaro for a user who already earns rewards on every swipe?
  7. How would you design a referral programme that grows both the EarnKaro creator base and the CashKaro end-user base simultaneously?
  8. Tell us about a time you worked with a business or revenue team to balance user experience against a commercial goal.
  9. How would you build a personalisation engine for cashback offers when you have very little data on a new user?
  10. CashKaro earns through affiliate commissions. How does this model shape the product trade-offs you would make day to day?
  11. How would you define the 'aha moment' for a new CashKaro user and what would you do to help more users reach it faster?
  12. Walk us through a product metric that moved in the wrong direction and how you found the root cause.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for every behavioural question. Three examples follow, framed around problems relevant to CashKaro.

Q: Tell us about a time you improved a user habit or repeat-use behaviour in a product.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a shopping discovery app, most registered users did not return to the app within the first seven days of sign-up.
*Task:* I was asked to lead a sprint to improve early retention without increasing marketing spend.
*Action:* I ran user interviews and found the biggest drop-off reason was that users forgot the app existed after their first session. I designed a 'saved deal reminder' feature: users who bookmarked a deal received a single, well-timed push notification when that deal was about to expire. I worked with engineering to ship a lightweight version in two weeks and ran an A/B test with a control group.
*Result:* The treatment group showed a clear lift in day-7 retention compared to the control. The feature rolled out fully and became a template for our re-engagement playbook.

Q: Describe a time you had to balance the needs of two different user groups with conflicting goals.

*Situation:* Our platform served both brand advertisers and end consumers. Advertisers wanted prominent placements while consumers wanted unbiased recommendations.
*Task:* I needed to redesign the homepage feed to satisfy both groups without the product feeling like a pure advertising channel.
*Action:* I introduced a 'sponsored' label with a consistent visual treatment so users always knew when a placement was paid, and capped the number of sponsored slots per screen. For advertisers, I created a performance dashboard so they could see ROI clearly. I ran usability tests with consumers before launch to confirm trust was maintained.
*Result:* Post-launch surveys showed user trust scores stayed flat, and advertiser renewal rates improved because they could see clear attribution data.

Q: Walk us through a product decision where data pointed one way but you made a different call.

*Situation:* Analytics showed that our highest cashback category drove the most clicks, so leadership wanted to feature it prominently across all surfaces.
*Task:* I was responsible for the home-screen roadmap and had to decide whether to follow the data recommendation.
*Action:* I dug deeper and found that the high-click category had very low payout rates because most purchases in it were returned. Featuring it everywhere would inflate click volume but hurt real user earnings and erode trust. I presented this finding with a user-lifetime-value lens and proposed a mixed approach featuring both high-click and high-payout categories.
*Result:* The mixed approach was approved. Over the next quarter, user-reported satisfaction with cashback payouts improved and support complaints about missing cashback dropped.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product improvement questions (Q1, Q2, Q11): Start by stating the user's job-to-be-done, identify where in the funnel they drop off, list two or three possible solutions with trade-offs, and pick one with a clear success metric. Avoid jumping straight to a feature without diagnosing the problem first.

For prioritisation questions (Q3, Q7): Use a simple impact-vs-effort grid out loud. Name the business goal first (growth, retention, or revenue), explain how each option maps to it, then rank. RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) works well if you can fill in rough estimates.

For metrics questions (Q4, Q12): State your North Star metric, break it into component metrics using a simple tree, identify which component moved, and explain what you would investigate next. A reasonable North Star for CashKaro is something like 'cashback earned per active user per month.' Build your answer around that logic.

For business model questions (Q6, Q10): Show you understand that CashKaro's revenue comes from affiliate commissions paid by brands, not from users. Every feature decision must pass the test: does this help users earn more, or does it help brands convert more? The best features do both.

For stakeholder and cross-functional questions (Q5, Q8): Use a brief situation setup, name the stakeholders and their incentives, explain how you found a shared goal, and describe the outcome. Avoid framing the other party as the obstacle.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep business model literacy. CashKaro's model is affiliate-led. Interviewers want to see that you understand how the company earns money, because a PM who does not grasp the affiliate commission structure will propose features that feel user-friendly but destroy margin.

Familiarity with the Indian online shopper. CashKaro's core user shops on large Indian e-commerce platforms, is value-sensitive, and may not be on the platform every day. Proposals that assume a Western 'daily-active' app model will stand out negatively.

Data fluency without data dependency. You should be able to define metrics, set up A/B tests, and interpret results. CashKaro interviewers typically value candidates who can make a clear recommendation with incomplete data rather than asking for more time to gather it.

Dual-sided product thinking. CashKaro serves consumers and brand partners simultaneously. Candidates who only think from one side (usually the consumer) miss half the problem. Show that you can represent both groups in a room.

Ownership and speed. Growth-stage companies like CashKaro move fast. Interviewers look for candidates who can scope down a problem, ship a small version, learn, and iterate. Candidates who describe perfect multi-quarter roadmaps can come across as slow to move.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product cold. Install both the CashKaro app and EarnKaro. Make at least one purchase through a cashback link and track the full experience from click to credit. Note where the flow feels smooth and where it breaks. Read any publicly available coverage of CashKaro's growth story in Indian startup media.

Week 2: Practise the core case types. CashKaro interviews typically include at least one product improvement case and one metric question. Practise answering each type out loud, not just in writing. Aim for a structured answer in under eight minutes before you open the discussion.

Week 3: Sharpen the behavioural bank. Prepare five to six STAR stories covering: a metric you improved, a stakeholder conflict you resolved, a trade-off you made between user experience and revenue, and a product that failed or underperformed. Tailor at least two stories to consumer or e-commerce contexts.

Ongoing: Stay current. Read about the Indian cashback and deals market, affiliate marketing trends, and how platforms like CashKaro are responding to UPI-based rewards from banks. Interviewers often open with a market question to test whether you follow the space.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating CashKaro like a pure consumer app. It is a two-sided platform with affiliate revenue at its core. Candidates who propose features purely from a consumer lens without addressing merchant or commission impact tend to lose points in the business model round.

Proposing features that require large data volumes from day one. Personalisation and AI recommendations are popular answers, but interviewers will probe how you handle the cold-start problem. Have a plan for the first weeks of a new user's journey before you reach the 'with enough data' part of your answer.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Name the specific metric, for example 'cashback link click-through rate per session', explain why it matters, and say how you would move it.

Not knowing the competitive set. CashKaro competes with other cashback apps, bank loyalty programmes, and credit card reward schemes. Candidates who cannot name at least two competitors come across as unprepared.

Overcomplicating the roadmap. Interviewers want to see that you can ship something small, learn, and iterate. Presenting a six-month feature roadmap with five parallel workstreams signals poor judgement about startup pace.

Generic behavioural answers. Stories that could apply to any company are less convincing than answers tied to consumer fintech or e-commerce trade-offs. Connect your experiences to CashKaro's specific challenges wherever you can.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the CashKaro PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that usually runs three to four rounds: a recruiter or HR screening, a product case or take-home assignment, and one or two rounds with a senior PM or cross-functional leader. Some candidates also report a final culture or founder round. The exact structure varies by role level, so confirm with your recruiter after the first call.

Is there a take-home assignment and how long does it typically take?

Many candidates report receiving a product case or strategy document as a take-home, typically with a window of one to three days. The brief usually involves improving a CashKaro feature or assessing a new market opportunity. Keep your document focused at four to six pages, and lead with a clear recommendation rather than a long situation analysis.

What background do CashKaro PMs typically come from?

Publicly visible CashKaro PM profiles tend to show backgrounds in e-commerce, consumer internet, fintech, or growth marketing. Engineering and MBA backgrounds both appear on the team. The company seems to value candidates who have shipped consumer products at scale more than any single educational credential, based on what is publicly reported about their hiring.

How important are SQL or data skills for this role?

At a growth-stage company like CashKaro, being able to pull and interpret your own data is a strong advantage. Candidates report that interviewers ask metric-definition and root-cause questions that assume basic data literacy. You do not need to be a data engineer, but being comfortable with SQL queries and funnel data interpretation will help you stand out.

Should I prepare questions to ask the interviewer?

Yes, and make them specific to CashKaro. Generic questions like 'what does a day in the life look like' are less memorable than questions about the team's current growth bets, how PM success is measured in the first 90 days, or how product and business teams collaborate on merchant relationships. Targeted questions signal that you have done your homework.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at CashKaro?

CashKaro does not publicly disclose salary ranges for individual PM roles. Across the Indian PM market, knok data shows bands of 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, and 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs. Your actual offer will depend on your experience, the specific role, and your negotiation, so use these ranges as a starting benchmark.
