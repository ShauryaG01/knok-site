01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

The Semios Group builds precision agriculture technology, using IoT sensors and machine learning to help fruit and nut growers protect their crops from pests and disease. Based in Canada, the company operates across North America and is expanding its platform to serve more growers globally.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 7 open roles at The Semios Group, spanning software engineering, data, and operations. If you are an Indian professional with a background in software development, data engineering, or agri-tech, this is a company worth targeting seriously.

Getting hired here typically involves a multi-stage process with strong emphasis on technical depth and collaborative problem-solving. The team is relatively lean, so each hire matters, and the bar for both skills and cultural alignment is high. The company's mission (using technology to help farmers grow food more sustainably) tends to attract engineers who want their work to have tangible, real-world impact.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Semios Group's hiring process is commonly reported to run across four to five stages, though the exact sequence can vary by role.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume for relevant experience. For technical roles, your background in software, data, or IoT is the first filter they apply.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR call. A short call, commonly cited as around 30 minutes, to confirm your interest, check basic fit, and walk you through the role. This is a good moment to ask about team structure and what specific problem the role is solving.

Stage 3: Technical assessment. Depending on the role, this may be a take-home coding challenge or a live technical screen. For software engineers, expect questions around data structures, algorithms, or system design. For data roles, SQL and Python are commonly tested.

Stage 4: Technical interview with the team. A deeper conversation with engineers or data scientists on the team. Expect real-world problem-solving questions tied to the company's domain, which includes sensor data, time-series analysis, and agricultural monitoring.

Stage 5: Final interview. A conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder. This round typically covers your motivation for joining, how you handle ambiguity, and how you collaborate with non-technical teams like field agronomists.

The full process from application to offer can span a few weeks. Response times vary, so follow up politely if you have not heard back after a week from each stage.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

The Semios Group looks for people who combine strong technical skills with genuine curiosity about agriculture and sustainability. Because the company works at the intersection of hardware, software, and field operations, they value engineers who can think beyond isolated code and understand how their work connects to real growers in the field.

Technical skills that matter. For software roles: proficiency in Python, experience with cloud platforms (commonly AWS or GCP, based on publicly reported job listings), and comfort with large datasets. For data roles: SQL, machine learning basics, and ideally experience with time-series or sensor data. For any technical role, clean code habits and the ability to explain trade-offs clearly are valued.

Non-technical qualities. The team values people who communicate well with non-engineers, since agronomists and field teams are key stakeholders. Adaptability matters too: the company is still growing, which means priorities shift and job scopes evolve.

Domain interest. You do not need a farming background, but candidates who show genuine curiosity about how technology can improve sustainable agriculture tend to stand out. Read up on precision agriculture before your interview.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the company deeply. Understand what The Semios Group actually does: IoT-based crop monitoring, mating disruption for pest control, and decision-support tools for growers. Being able to speak to a specific product or feature in your interview signals real interest, not a generic application.

Brush up on your core technical skills. For engineering roles, practice data structures, algorithms, and system design. For data roles, focus on SQL, Python, and working with sensor or time-series data. Use platforms like LeetCode or HackerRank to get back in shape if you have been away from interview prep.

Prepare domain-relevant examples. Think about times you worked with large volumes of sensor data, built data pipelines, or collaborated with non-technical stakeholders. Even if your examples come from a different industry, map them clearly to the Semios context.

Ask good questions. The interview is also your chance to evaluate the company. Ask about team size, how product decisions get made, how they handle the gap between field reality and software assumptions, and what success looks like in the first few months of the role.

Tailor your resume. Highlight experience with IoT, cloud infrastructure, agriculture (if any), data pipelines, or cross-functional work. Keep it concise and achievement-focused, leading with impact over job duties.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

The Semios Group has the feel of a company that takes its mission seriously. Precision agriculture is not a trend for them; it is the core reason the company exists. Employees commonly describe the culture as collaborative, mission-driven, and practical. The team includes both software engineers and people with deep agricultural domain knowledge, which creates a working environment where you learn from people very different from yourself.

Pace and ownership. As a growing company, Semios tends to give employees real ownership over their work. This is motivating for people who want to see the direct impact of their contributions, but it also means you need to be comfortable with some ambiguity and with shifting priorities.

Collaboration across disciplines. Engineers regularly interact with agronomists, field teams, and grower customers. If you enjoy seeing your work used by real people in real environments, that is a strong selling point of working here.

Remote and flexible work. Based on publicly reported job listings, The Semios Group has offered remote and hybrid arrangements for some roles, particularly for software and data positions. Confirm the specific arrangement for your role during the recruiter call, since policies can differ across teams.

The overall culture rewards people who are curious, practical, and genuinely motivated by the idea that software can help feed the world more sustainably.
