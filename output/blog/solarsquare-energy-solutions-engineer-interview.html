01 Overview

Overview

SolarSquare Energy is one of India's fastest-growing rooftop solar companies, focused on helping homeowners and businesses cut electricity bills by switching to solar. A Solutions Engineer here sits at the crossroads of technical consulting and customer sales: you visit sites, assess rooftop feasibility, design proposals, and guide customers from first inquiry to installation.

Candidates typically report a process that includes a phone screening, a technical or case-study discussion, and a final round with a sales or operations leader. Interviewers want to see that you can explain solar technology in plain language and handle a hesitant or price-sensitive customer with real confidence.

As of mid-2026, knok jobradar shows 8 open roles at SolarSquare. Across India, 1270 Solutions Engineer openings are live, with Bangalore (55 openings), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20) concentrating the most demand.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

  1. Walk us through how you would assess a customer's rooftop for solar suitability.
  2. A customer says solar is 'too expensive' for them right now. How do you respond?
  3. How do you explain solar system components (panels, inverter, battery, net metering) to a homeowner with no technical background?
  4. Describe a time you guided a customer from initial inquiry all the way to closing the sale.
  5. How would you differentiate SolarSquare from a competitor offering a lower-cost installation?
  6. How do you calculate or present the payback period and ROI to a customer?
  7. Tell us about a time you coordinated with an installation or technical team to solve a customer problem mid-project.
  8. What do you know about net metering policies and the DISCOM approval process in India?
  9. A customer is comparing buying a rooftop system outright versus a no-cost solar-as-a-service model. How do you guide that conversation?
  10. How do you manage a large pipeline of active leads at different stages simultaneously?
  11. Describe your experience with proposal or quotation tools. How do you ensure accuracy in a solar design proposal?
  12. What is the biggest challenge for rooftop solar adoption in India today, and how does SolarSquare address it?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A customer says solar is 'too expensive.' How do you respond?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a homeowner was interested in solar but told me the upfront cost felt out of reach compared to simply paying the monthly electricity bill.

*Task:* My job was to reframe the conversation from 'upfront cost' to 'total cost of ownership' without being pushy or dismissive.

*Action:* I pulled up their last few electricity bills, noted the average monthly spend, and showed a simple year-by-year comparison. I explained the payback period using estimates I clearly labeled as approximate, walked them through the government subsidy they qualified for, and offered a written proposal they could review with their family before deciding.

*Result:* The customer asked to schedule a site visit within the week and proceeded with installation the following month. I learned that 'too expensive' usually means 'help me understand the numbers,' not a firm refusal.

---

Q: How do you assess a rooftop for solar suitability?

*Situation:* Early in my role as a field sales engineer, I was sent to assess a customer's bungalow before a technical team visit.

*Task:* I needed to give an accurate feasibility recommendation so the technical team could commit to a detailed design with confidence.

*Action:* I checked the usable roof area and orientation (south-facing is optimal in India), noted shading from trees or adjacent structures, asked about monthly electricity consumption from the bill, confirmed the DISCOM-sanctioned load, and assessed roof material and age.

*Result:* I flagged that one section was heavily shaded and recommended we use only the west-facing slope. This reduced the system size but improved actual generation. The customer appreciated the honest scoping and the project went ahead with no surprises at installation.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you coordinated between a technical team and a customer during installation.

*Situation:* A customer called me in a panic on installation day because the DISCOM had not yet approved net metering, and the team had already arrived on site.

*Task:* I had to manage the customer's frustration while keeping the installation on track and ensuring nothing was done out of sequence.

*Action:* I asked the installation supervisor to pause work on the grid-tie connection, explained the exact approval stage to the customer in plain terms, committed to a specific follow-up date, and escalated internally to the approvals coordinator to expedite the paperwork.

*Result:* Net metering approval came through within a few days and installation completed without further issues. The customer mentioned in their review that they felt kept in the loop throughout, which was exactly the outcome I had aimed for.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The ROI bridge. When facing price or value questions, move the conversation from 'cost today' to 'savings over time.' State the monthly bill, the estimated generation, any applicable subsidy, and the payback period. Be upfront that these are estimates based on typical assumptions.

Plain-language translation. Lead with the benefit before the spec. 'A 5 kWp system' becomes 'a system that should cover most of your monthly consumption based on your current bill.' Save the technical label for the written proposal.

The objection ladder. Hear the objection fully, acknowledge it genuinely, answer with data, and then ask a question to re-engage. For example: 'I understand the upfront cost feels high. May I show you what the monthly equivalent looks like spread over a few years?'

STAR for experience questions. Keep *Situation* to one or two sentences. Invest most of your answer in *Action*, since that is where your skill shows. Always close with a concrete *Result* and, where relevant, what you learned from the situation.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

SolarSquare interviewers typically look for several things beyond general sales ability.

Technical grounding without jargon. You do not need to be a design engineer, but you should understand how a rooftop solar system works end to end: panels, inverter, metering, DISCOM connection, and the subsidy process. Candidates who cannot explain basic terminology tend not to progress past early rounds.

Consultative selling instinct. This is not a high-pressure pitch role. Interviewers want to see that you ask good questions, listen to the customer's real situation, and shape the proposal around their specific needs rather than a standard script.

Process discipline. With multiple active leads at any time, consistent tracking of follow-ups, documentation, and approvals is something interviewers probe through situational questions. Vague answers here raise a flag.

Mission alignment. SolarSquare's pitch to customers is both environmental and financial. Candidates who speak naturally about India's energy transition, rather than treating it as a rehearsed talking point, tend to stand out in final rounds.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product and market knowledge. Study how a rooftop solar system works end to end: panel types, inverter options, net metering, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy process. Read publicly available news about SolarSquare's growth and customer focus. Review how competitors position themselves on price and service.

Week 2: Customer conversation practice. Role-play a full customer consultation with a friend or record yourself. Cover the first call, a site assessment walkthrough, a proposal explanation, handling a price objection, and managing a DISCOM approval delay. Aim to use plain language every time, with no acronyms until the customer asks.

Week 3: Sharpen your stories. Map your past experience to the most likely questions listed above. Prepare at least four STAR stories covering: a complex customer sale, a problem you resolved mid-project, a time you worked closely with a technical team, and a time you used data to change a customer's mind.

Before the interview. Review SolarSquare's website. Know the cities they currently serve, the system sizes they typically install, and the warranty or after-service commitment they advertise. Prepare two or three genuine questions for the interviewer, such as how leads are sourced or how the Solutions Engineer and design team collaborate on proposals.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Talking in specs, not benefits. Saying '5 kWp, MPPT inverter' without translating it into what the customer actually gets is a quick way to lose the interviewer's confidence.
  1. Weak policy knowledge. Candidates who cannot explain the net metering process or the PM Surya Ghar subsidy at a basic level signal that they will struggle when a real customer asks. Interviewers probe this directly.
  1. Generic objection handling. 'I listen and then explain the value' is not an answer. Walk through a specific technique or a real example from your experience.
  1. Missing the coordination angle. Solutions Engineers work alongside surveyors, design teams, and DISCOM liaisons. Candidates who only discuss their one-on-one customer skills address only half of what the role demands.
  1. No questions for the interviewer. Arriving with nothing to ask signals low engagement. Prepare genuine questions about the sales cycle, how inbound leads compare to outbound, or how performance is measured in the role.
  1. Inventing numbers. Do not make up payback periods or generation estimates in your answers. Interviewers notice, and it mirrors the kind of behaviour that creates customer complaints and cancellations.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the SolarSquare Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds. This often includes a phone or video screen, a technical or case-study discussion, and a final conversation with a senior sales or operations leader. Round structure varies, so ask your recruiter for the exact process when you receive your schedule.

Do I need an engineering degree to apply for this role?

Not necessarily. Solutions Engineers at solar companies come from varied backgrounds including science graduates, MBA holders, and B.Tech candidates. What matters more is enough technical understanding to explain the product clearly, combined with a track record in consultative selling or customer-facing work.

Will there be a case study or mock customer call in the interview?

Candidates report that some rounds include a scenario-based exercise, such as walking through a solar proposal for a hypothetical homeowner or handling a price objection live. Practicing a short, structured customer conversation before your interview is strongly advisable.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at SolarSquare?

SolarSquare has not publicly disclosed its compensation bands. Glassdoor and industry surveys suggest Solutions Engineer roles in India's clean energy sector vary widely based on experience, city, and the variable (incentive) component of the package. Research current listings and compare with similar roles at other solar companies to build a realistic expectation before negotiating.

How important is knowledge of government solar subsidies for this role?

Very important. A large part of customer conversations involves explaining the PM Surya Ghar subsidy: eligibility, the application process, and how long approval typically takes. Interviewers are likely to probe this directly, so read the latest official guidelines before your interview so you can speak to it with confidence.

Is the Solutions Engineer role mostly field-based or office-based?

Typically a mix of both. Job descriptions and candidates indicate that Solutions Engineers spend significant time on site visits and customer meetings, particularly in cities where SolarSquare is actively growing. Comfort with regular travel within your assigned city is generally expected for this role.
