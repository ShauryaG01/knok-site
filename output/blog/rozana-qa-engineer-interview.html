02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from rozana QA Engineer interviews. Prepare a concrete answer for each one before your call.

  1. Walk me through how you would design a test plan for a new feature in the app.
  2. How do you decide which bugs to escalate immediately and which to log and track later?
  3. Which automation frameworks have you used, and what made you choose them for your projects?
  4. A developer tells you the behaviour you raised is 'working as designed.' How do you handle that?
  5. How do you approach regression testing when the release cycle is very short?
  6. Walk us through an API test you have written, from the request to the assertions you checked.
  7. How do you ensure good test coverage without testing every theoretical edge case?
  8. Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle. What did you do?
  9. How do you keep test cases and documentation updated when the product is changing fast?
  10. What metrics do you track to judge whether your QA process is actually working?
  11. How would you test a payment or checkout flow end-to-end, including edge cases?
  12. Describe a time you introduced an improvement to a QA process or convinced the team to adopt something new.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would design a test plan for a new feature.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were launching a new onboarding flow for first-time users.

*Task:* I was responsible for writing the full test plan before development was complete.

*Action:* I started by reviewing the product requirement document and listing every user path, happy and unhappy. I split tests into three layers: functional (does each step work?), edge cases (what if the user drops off mid-flow?), and integration (does it connect correctly to the backend and email service?). I shared the draft with the developer and PM before writing a single test case so we could catch gaps early.

*Result:* We caught two missing validations during review, which saved a full regression cycle after launch. The feature shipped with zero critical bugs.

---

Q: A developer says the bug you raised is 'working as designed.' How do you respond?

*Situation:* I had logged a bug where a form accepted a negative quantity in a shopping cart.

*Task:* The developer closed it as 'by design,' but I believed it was a real problem.

*Action:* I went back to the original requirements, confirmed there was no spec for negative values, and put together screenshots showing the broken cart total that resulted. I shared this calmly in a comment, tagging the PM for a decision rather than escalating emotionally.

*Result:* The PM confirmed it was a bug, the fix went into the same sprint, and we added a rule to our checklist so the same class of issue would always be caught going forward.

---

Q: Tell me about a critical bug you found late in the release cycle.

*Situation:* Two days before a major app update, I discovered that the OTP verification screen was silently failing for a subset of phone number formats.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to block the release or push for a targeted fix quickly.

*Action:* I documented the exact conditions that triggered the failure, estimated the affected user segment from our data, and called an urgent sync with the dev lead. We agreed on a targeted patch rather than a full rollback.

*Result:* The patch was tested and merged within a few hours. The release shipped on schedule and no users reported OTP failures post-launch. The incident led us to add phone-format variation tests to our standard suite.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioural question. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Each part has a job: Situation sets the scene in one or two sentences, Task says what you personally were responsible for, Action is the bulk of your answer (what you did and why), and Result shows the outcome with a concrete detail if possible. Keep each story focused and crisp rather than rambling through every background detail.

For technical questions, use the 'think out loud' approach. State your assumption, explain your reasoning step by step, and invite pushback at the end. Saying something like 'does that match what you have in mind?' shows structured thinking and keeps the conversation collaborative rather than one-sided.

For 'how would you test X' questions, use a three-layer structure:

LayerWhat to cover
FunctionalDoes the feature do what the spec says?
Edge casesWhat breaks at boundaries, empty inputs, or unexpected sequences?
IntegrationDoes it work correctly with other systems or services?

This three-layer structure signals that you think beyond happy-path testing, which is one of the clearest ways to stand out from other candidates.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Rozana interviewers, like most product-company QA panels, are typically looking for four things.

Product empathy. Can you think like a user, not just like a tester? Answers that reference user impact land better than purely technical ones. Saying 'this would confuse a first-time user' shows you care about the product experience, not just the checklist.

Communication with developers. QA works closely with engineering teams. Interviewers want to see that you can raise issues clearly, hold your ground on a real bug without creating friction, and write bug reports that a developer can act on immediately.

Automation mindset. Even if the role includes manual testing, candidates who can write or review automation scripts, understand CI/CD pipelines, and talk about test stability are rated more highly in 2025 and 2026 hiring cycles.

Ownership over quality. The strongest answers show that you treat quality as a shared responsibility, not just a sign-off step at the end of a sprint. Examples of you catching something before it reached users, or improving a process proactively, are what interviewers tend to remember.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Refresh your fundamentals. Revisit the basics of test case design, boundary value analysis, and equivalence partitioning. Write out a sample test plan for an app you use every day. A food delivery or payments app works well as practice material because the flows are familiar and the edge cases are real.

Week 2: Sharpen your automation skills. Pick one framework you know well (Selenium, Cypress, Appium, or Postman for APIs) and write or review a small suite of tests. Be ready to walk through your code and explain your design choices out loud.

Week 3: Prepare your stories. For each of the 12 questions listed above, write a one-paragraph STAR answer. Focus on outcomes: what broke, what you caught, what shipped cleanly because of your work.

Before the interview. Research rozana's product from the user side. Use the app or website, note anything that feels unclear or could break, and bring one concrete observation into the conversation. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually used the product. Also confirm the format in advance, because candidates typically report a screening call followed by one or two technical rounds, but round counts can vary by team.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Talking only about what you tested, not why. Interviewers want to understand your thinking, not just a list of test cases. Always explain your prioritisation logic.
  1. Giving vague answers to behavioural questions. 'I usually do X' is not a story. Use a real example with a specific outcome.
  1. Skipping the unhappy path. When asked to design tests for a feature, many candidates describe only what should work. Always mention what you would test when things go wrong: invalid inputs, timeouts, partial failures.
  1. Treating bugs as wins only if they were accepted. A bug you raised that was correctly rejected (because you misread the spec) is still a good story if you focus on what you learned from it.
  1. Not asking clarifying questions. In 'how would you test X' questions, asking one or two clarifying questions before diving in shows senior thinking. What platform? What user segment? What is already covered?
  1. Underselling automation experience. Even basic scripting or tool configuration is worth mentioning. Many candidates in manual-heavy roles assume automation questions do not apply to them, and miss a chance to stand out.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the rozana QA Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds: an initial HR or recruiter screening, a technical round focused on testing concepts and automation, and a final conversation with a hiring manager or team lead. The exact number can vary by team and seniority level. It is worth confirming the format when you receive your interview invite.

What automation tools should I know for a rozana QA role?

Based on what candidates report, familiarity with at least one of Selenium, Cypress, or Appium is useful, along with API testing using Postman or RestAssured. You do not need to know every tool. It is better to know one or two deeply and be able to explain your test design choices than to name-drop tools without depth.

What salary can I expect as a QA Engineer at rozana?

Specific compensation details for rozana are not publicly available at this time. As a general benchmark, QA Engineer salaries across India run from 4-9 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 9-17 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 17-30 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), based on knok jobradar data. Actual offers will vary by your skills, your negotiation, and the band rozana has set for the role.

Is the interview more manual testing or automation focused?

Candidates report that both areas come up, but the balance depends on the specific team and role level. Entry-level roles lean more on test case design, bug reporting, and manual testing concepts. Mid and senior roles tend to have more automation and process-related questions. Reviewing the job description closely usually signals where the emphasis will be.

How should I prepare if I have only manual testing experience?

Focus on showing strong fundamentals: test case design, risk-based prioritisation, clear bug reports, and product thinking. For automation, learning the basics of one tool before the interview and being honest about where you are in your learning journey works better than overstating experience. Many interviewers value honesty and a clear learning orientation over a polished but thin skill set.

Does rozana ask puzzles or brain-teasers in QA interviews?

Most product companies have moved away from puzzles in favour of practical and situational questions. Candidates at rozana typically report scenario-based questions ('how would you test this feature?') and behavioural questions rather than abstract brain-teasers. Be prepared for 'think out loud' technical scenarios where your reasoning process matters as much as your final answer.
