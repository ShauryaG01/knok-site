01 Overview

Overview

Adyen is a Dutch payment technology company that processes transactions for some of the world's largest merchants, from global retail giants to fast-growing startups. The Solutions Engineer role sits at the intersection of technical implementation and client partnership: you help merchants integrate Adyen's payment infrastructure, troubleshoot live issues, and act as a trusted advisor during the sales and onboarding process.

Candidates report that the interview process typically includes a recruiter screen, one or more technical rounds focused on payments knowledge and API concepts, and a business-case or client-simulation round. Final-stage conversations typically involve senior stakeholders who want to see how you think under pressure. As of mid-2026, Adyen has 253 open roles globally, reflecting the company's continued expansion. In the Indian market, Bangalore accounts for 55 of the broader Solutions Engineer opportunities, with Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20) also active.

Public salary data is limited, but Glassdoor commonly cites competitive packages for this role at global payment companies. The process rewards both technical precision and the ability to speak plainly to non-technical clients, so prepare equally on both fronts.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these questions coming up most often across Adyen Solutions Engineer interviews:

  1. Walk us through how you would explain Adyen's payment processing flow to a merchant who has no technical background.
  2. A high-volume e-commerce client is seeing a sudden spike in payment declines. How do you diagnose and resolve this?
  3. How would you scope and deliver a proof of concept for a prospective enterprise client within a tight timeline?
  4. Tell me about a time you translated a complex technical requirement into a clear action plan for a non-technical stakeholder.
  5. A client's development team is reluctant to adopt a new API version. How do you handle the pushback?
  6. How do you stay current with local payment methods and regulatory requirements across different markets, including India-specific options like UPI?
  7. Describe a situation where you had to manage competing priorities from a client and your internal team at the same time.
  8. A merchant's checkout conversion rate has dropped after going live on Adyen. Walk us through your troubleshooting steps.
  9. How would you position Adyen's unified commerce platform against a competitor during an active sales cycle?
  10. Describe how you would onboard a new developer team from sandbox setup through to their first successful live transaction.
  11. Tell me about a time you influenced a product or process change based on feedback you gathered directly from clients.
  12. How do you balance deep technical expertise with the need to be a credible advisor to a CFO or a Head of Digital Commerce?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A high-volume e-commerce client is seeing a spike in payment declines. How do you diagnose and resolve this?

*Situation:* At a previous role, a major retail client reported that their decline rate had jumped sharply over a holiday weekend, directly impacting revenue.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly, communicate clearly with a non-technical client team, and coordinate a fix without adding to the panic.

*Action:* I pulled transaction logs and segmented declines by card type, issuing bank, and geography. The spike turned out to be concentrated in one issuing bank for a specific card scheme. I looped in the payments operations team internally, drafted a plain-language update for the client's commerce head, and recommended a short-term retry logic adjustment while we worked on a longer-term resolution.

*Result:* Decline rates returned to baseline by end of day. The client later cited the clarity of our communication during the incident as a key reason they expanded their contract.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you translated a complex technical requirement into a workable solution for a client.

*Situation:* A fintech client wanted real-time reconciliation data piped directly into their internal ERP system, which used a non-standard data schema.

*Task:* My job was to bridge the gap between the available webhook output and the client's finance team's expectations, without requiring a full custom build on either side.

*Action:* I mapped the client's data fields against the webhook payload, identified a lightweight middleware approach their team could own, and ran two working sessions with their developers and finance leads together so both sides could ask questions in the same room.

*Result:* The integration went live ahead of the original timeline. The finance team could reconcile in near real-time for the first time, and the client's CTO shared the case internally as a best-practice example.

---

Q: How do you handle a client's development team that is resistant to adopting a new API version?

*Situation:* During a major platform upgrade, a long-standing client pushed back hard on migrating to a new API version, citing development resource constraints and fear of regression.

*Task:* I needed to maintain the relationship, address legitimate technical concerns, and still move the migration forward before a deprecation deadline.

*Action:* I set up a technical workshop with their lead developer and walked through a side-by-side comparison of the old and new endpoints, highlighting which parts of their existing code would carry over unchanged. I also prepared a migration checklist and offered to join their first test run on a call.

*Result:* The client completed the migration ahead of the deprecation date. Their developer later told me it was 'the least painful upgrade we have done in years.'

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical troubleshooting questions: Start by describing how you isolate the problem. Segment by variable (region, card type, device), check logs before forming a hypothesis, and explain how you communicate findings at different levels of technical detail to different audiences. Interviewers want to see structured thinking, not just a list of actions.

For behavioural and client-story questions (STAR): Open with a one-sentence context so the interviewer knows the stakes. Keep the Situation and Task brief, two to three sentences combined. Spend most of your time on Action, specifically what *you* did rather than what the team did. Close the Result with a concrete outcome, even if it is qualitative ('client renewed', 'timeline improved').

For product positioning questions: Acknowledge the competitor briefly, then pivot to the client's specific needs. Frame Adyen's strengths in terms of what that particular client values most. Avoid generic claims. Interviewers watch for candidates who can read a room and adjust their pitch accordingly.

For 'how would you' scenario questions: Think out loud. Lay out your assumptions first, then your steps. If a question is ambiguous, say so and ask one clarifying question before diving in. This mirrors exactly what clients will expect from a Solutions Engineer on the job.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Adyen Solutions Engineer interviews typically test four things at once.

Technical depth. You need a working understanding of payment flows, card scheme rules, API authentication, webhooks, and common integration patterns. You do not need to be a software developer, but you should be able to read a code snippet, spot a configuration error, and speak confidently with developer clients about what is happening under the hood.

Client empathy. Interviewers look for candidates who default to 'what does the client actually need' rather than 'what does our platform do.' Expect scenario questions designed to reveal whether you will over-engineer a solution or push back helpfully when a client's request does not actually serve their own goals.

Communication across audiences. Adyen serves both technical and non-technical stakeholders within the same merchant organization. Candidates who can shift registers, speaking API specs with a developer in the morning and ROI with a CFO in the afternoon, stand out clearly from those who can only do one or the other.

Ownership and follow-through. Candidates report that interviewers probe for examples of problems you owned end-to-end. Stories where you stayed involved through resolution are valued over stories where you escalated and moved on.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the platform. Read Adyen's public developer documentation, particularly the checkout API, payment lifecycle, and webhook guides. Understand the difference between authorization, capture, and settlement. Study how Adyen's unified commerce positioning differs from a traditional payment gateway. Familiarize yourself with India-specific payment methods such as UPI and local card schemes, since regional knowledge comes up in interviews.

Week 2: Build your story bank. Map your past experience to the question list in this guide. Write down three to five client stories in STAR format. For each story, identify the technical element, the stakeholder challenge, and the outcome. Practise saying each story out loud in under two minutes.

Week 3: Mock interviews and market awareness. Run two or three mock interviews with someone who will push back on your answers. Read recent news about Adyen, including earnings commentary, product launches, and partnership announcements. Know their key markets and any recent India-specific developments.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating it like a pure sales interview. Solutions Engineers at Adyen are expected to go deep on technical details. Candidates who rely only on relationship-building stories without demonstrating technical fluency typically do not advance past the early rounds.
  1. Generic client stories. Answers like 'I always make sure to listen to the client' land flat. Interviewers want specifics: what was the client's actual problem, what did you personally do, and what changed as a result.
  1. Not knowing Adyen's product. Candidates who cannot explain how Adyen's unified commerce approach differs from a traditional payment service provider are at a significant disadvantage. Spend real time on the public documentation before any round.
  1. Overselling your technical depth. If you cannot back up a claim in a follow-up question, it damages trust quickly. Be honest about what you know deeply versus what you know at a working level.
  1. Ignoring the business context in technical answers. When asked a troubleshooting question, always connect the technical steps back to business impact. Every payment decline is a missed transaction. Every integration delay is a revenue delay.
  1. Asking no questions. Solutions Engineers are expected to be curious and to push back thoughtfully. Arriving with no questions signals a lack of genuine interest in either the role or the company.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Adyen Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two technical or case-based rounds, and a final conversation with a hiring manager or senior stakeholder. The exact structure can vary by region and team, so confirm the format with your recruiter early. Some candidates also report a client-simulation exercise at the final stage.

Is coding required for the Solutions Engineer role at Adyen?

Candidates generally report that you are not expected to write production code from scratch, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding JSON payloads, and spotting configuration errors. Being able to hold a credible technical conversation with a developer client is important. Preparing to discuss authentication flows, webhooks, and common error codes in plain terms is a solid baseline.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at Adyen in India?

Public salary data for this specific role is limited. Glassdoor and similar platforms commonly cite competitive packages for Solutions Engineers at global payment companies in India, but sample sizes are small and figures vary by level and location. Your best source of current numbers is a direct recruiter conversation or the broader Solutions Engineer salary guides covering the Indian market.

How should I prepare if I come from a non-payments background?

Focus first on the fundamentals: how a card payment moves from a customer's tap to a merchant's bank account, and where Adyen sits in that chain. Adyen's public developer documentation is clear and free to read. Map your existing client-facing or technical experience to the skills the role needs, and be transparent with your interviewer about your learning curve while demonstrating how quickly you pick up new domains.

Does Adyen hire Solutions Engineers in cities other than Bangalore?

Adyen's Solutions Engineer openings in India are most active in Bangalore, which leads in relevant market opportunities. Mumbai and Delhi also see openings periodically. The role tends to follow Adyen's commercial priorities in each city, so hiring activity shifts with where merchant relationships are growing. Check Adyen's careers page directly for the current picture.

How important is prior payment industry experience for this role?

Candidates with direct payments experience have a natural head start, but interviewers typically care more about how you think through technical problems and communicate with clients than about years spent in fintech specifically. Demonstrating that you have done serious pre-interview research on Adyen's platform and the broader payments ecosystem can close a meaningful part of the experience gap.
