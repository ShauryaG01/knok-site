01 Overview

Overview

DevRev at a glance

DevRev is a CRM and customer operations platform built for tech companies that want to bring their support, product, and engineering workflows onto one system. It competes in overlapping ways with tools like Zendesk, Salesforce, and Jira, but its core pitch is unification: one place where a customer complaint, a product bug, and an engineering sprint are all connected. DevRev has grown rapidly and is known for a high hiring bar, especially for people who combine technical credibility with genuine customer empathy.

The Solutions Engineer (SE) role at DevRev is a technical pre-sales position. You partner with Account Executives to run product demos, lead proofs-of-concept, handle deep integration questions, and bridge the gap between what a prospect needs and what the product delivers today. The role requires comfort with APIs, webhooks, and SaaS integrations, alongside the ability to build a business case for a non-technical executive.

Candidates report that the process typically includes a recruiter or HR screen, a product demo or case study round, and one or more conversations with sales leaders and cross-functional stakeholders. Some candidates also report a technical exercise around integrations. The full process typically spans 2-4 weeks depending on the team and level. As of July 2026, DevRev has 40 open roles active on the knok job radar, reflecting a period of sustained hiring.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were selling a data integration platform to a large retail client. The technical champion understood our API workflow well, but the final decision-maker was a Chief Marketing Officer with no engineering background.

*Task:* I needed her to understand why our real-time data sync approach was better than the competitor's batch processing model, without losing her in technical detail.

*Action:* I dropped the API flow diagrams entirely. Instead, I used an analogy she immediately connected with: batch processing is like getting a summary of all your WhatsApp messages at the end of the day, while real-time sync is like getting each message the moment it arrives. I then tied this directly to her pain: campaign triggers firing on stale customer data. I followed up with a one-page visual summary built around her team's own numbers.

*Result:* The CMO became a stronger internal advocate than the technical champion. She personally pushed the deal through procurement and we closed within two weeks of that conversation.

---

Q: A customer escalates post-onboarding because the product behaves differently than expected from your demo. How do you handle it?

*Situation:* After a mid-market SaaS company went live on our platform, their operations lead raised a serious complaint. A workflow automation they had seen in my demo was not working the same way in production because of a configuration dependency I had not clearly called out during the sales cycle.

*Task:* I was the SE who ran the original demo, so I owned this problem.

*Action:* I set up a call quickly and went in without being defensive. I acknowledged the gap directly, walked through exactly what had been miscommunicated, and laid out a clear path to the outcome they expected. I looped in our implementation team and committed to a specific resolution timeline. I also flagged this internally so the demo script would include that dependency upfront for future prospects.

*Result:* The customer moved from 'at-risk' to 'satisfied' within a few weeks and later expanded their contract. The internal fix also meant two future prospects were properly set up during their proof-of-concept, reducing similar post-sale surprises.

---

Q: Tell me about a deal you lost. What did you learn?

*Situation:* I was the SE on a competitive deal where our product was technically stronger in several areas, but we lost at the final stage to a well-known incumbent.

*Task:* I needed to understand exactly why we lost and build that learning into my process going forward.

*Action:* I called our champion after the loss and asked directly. He told me the competitor had a pre-existing enterprise contract with the company that made their pricing far easier for the CFO to approve. I had focused almost entirely on the technical evaluation and had never built a financial business case for the economic buyer.

*Result:* I did not recover that deal. But from that point, I added a step to every sales cycle: a simple ROI summary for the economic buyer alongside the technical evaluation. In the next comparable competitive situation, this framing made a clear difference in how the final decision was presented internally.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For demo and 'how would you approach' questions: discover before you show

Start with 3-4 targeted discovery questions: what tool are they replacing, what is the biggest pain with it today, who else in the company is affected, and what does success look like in the first quarter. Build your demo entirely around those answers rather than running a standard feature tour. End with a clear confirmation of what they saw and a specific next step.

For competitive positioning questions: pain, difference, proof

First name the prospect's current pain with the incumbent (slow handoffs, no engineering context in the support queue, fragmented data across tools). Then explain what DevRev does differently, focused on the outcome rather than the feature name. Finally, anchor to a proof point: a customer story, a case study result, or a live product moment that demonstrates the claim rather than just asserting it.

For behavioral questions: STAR with weight on Action

Keep Situation and Task brief (2-3 sentences combined). Spend most of your time on Action: what you specifically did, why you chose that approach, and how you handled any resistance or complexity. Close Result with a concrete outcome and, where possible, what you learned or changed afterward. Interviewers notice when a result has been inflated, so be specific and honest.

For objection handling questions: acknowledge, normalize, resolve

Do not argue with the objection. Acknowledge that the concern is valid. Normalize it by noting that other customers have raised the same question. Then resolve it with a direct, specific answer: a workaround, a roadmap update, or a redirect to what matters most to that particular prospect.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

DevRev interviews for a specific combination of traits in SE candidates. Understanding what they are looking for helps you frame every answer more precisely.

Technical credibility that opens doors, not closes them. You should be comfortable discussing APIs, webhooks, SSO, and SaaS integrations. But the goal is to help customers move forward, not to make them feel they are being evaluated. Interviewers want to see that you translate complexity into clarity, quickly and without condescension.

Customer obsession backed by specific stories. DevRev's culture puts the customer experience at the center of the product. They want SEs who can recall real moments where they went beyond the standard script to get a customer to a successful outcome, not just to close a deal.

Commercial instinct. Knowing the product inside out is not enough. Interviewers look for candidates who understand deal dynamics: who the economic buyer is, what it takes to build internal consensus at a prospect company, and when to push versus when to wait and listen.

Comfort with ambiguity and change. DevRev moves fast. Processes, features, and team structures evolve. Candidates who show adaptability and the ability to operate without a complete playbook tend to stand out.

Genuine product curiosity. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually explored the product, thought critically about how it compares to alternatives, and can speak to specific workflows. This signals that the candidate will be a strong internal voice for product improvement, not just a demo machine.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the product before anything else

Sign up for a DevRev free trial or request a sandbox. Go through every core workflow: ticketing, PLuG (the in-app support widget), the CRM module, and the engineering integration. Understand how DevRev connects the customer-facing side of a tech company to the product and engineering side. Read the public documentation and any available case studies before your first round.

Build your competitive understanding

Map out how DevRev compares to Zendesk, Freshdesk, Salesforce Service Cloud, and Jira Service Management. Focus on where DevRev's approach is genuinely different, not just marketing language. Know where the product has gaps today and how you would handle those honestly in a live prospect conversation.

Prepare your story bank

Prepare at least 5 STAR stories covering: a complex technical explanation to a non-technical audience, a deal you lost and what you learned, a post-sale escalation you managed well, a cross-functional collaboration under pressure, and a competitive displacement. Practice each story out loud so it flows naturally in under 3 minutes.

Research your interviewer before each round

Look up your interviewer on LinkedIn and understand their background. If they come from engineering, lean into technical depth. If they come from sales leadership, emphasize commercial outcomes and deal dynamics. Tailoring your emphasis to the interviewer's frame of reference is a small detail that makes a real difference.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Running a feature demo instead of a solution demo

The most common mistake in the demo round is clicking through product features in order without connecting them to the prospect's specific pain. Interviewers want to see that you would start with discovery, build a narrative around the customer's problem, and show only what is relevant. A generic feature tour loses deals and interview rounds alike.

Not knowing the DevRev product before the interview

DevRev interviewers expect you to have explored the product in depth, not just read the homepage. If you cannot describe how DevRev handles a support ticket differently from Zendesk, or what PLuG is, you are not adequately prepared. A free trial or sandbox walk-through takes a few hours and signals genuine interest.

Ignoring the economic buyer in your answers

Many SE candidates focus entirely on the technical champion when telling deal stories. DevRev interviewers want to see that you understand how to build a business case for the person signing the contract, who often has no interest in the technical details at all.

Getting defensive when product gaps come up

Every product has gaps. If an interviewer asks how you handle a missing feature, the wrong approach is to deflect or minimize. The right answer shows that you acknowledge the gap honestly, bridge to what the product does well today, and know when to loop in the product team rather than over-promising.

Giving generic behavioral answers without specific outcomes

Answers like 'I always put the customer first' without a real story behind them waste the interviewer's time. Every behavioral answer needs a specific situation, a concrete action, and a clearly stated result. If you are constructing these stories on the spot in the interview room, they will not land.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What does a typical DevRev Solutions Engineer interview process look like?

Candidates typically report a process that includes a recruiter or HR screen, followed by a product demo or case study round, and then one or more conversations with sales leaders or cross-functional partners. Some candidates also report a technical exercise around integrations or APIs. The full process typically spans 2-4 weeks, though timelines vary by team and level.

Does DevRev ask coding questions in SE interviews?

Candidates generally report that DevRev SE interviews do not require you to write code from scratch. However, you should be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding webhook payloads, and discussing SaaS integration concepts in plain terms. If you are applying for a more technical SE variant, confirm the scope of the technical assessment with your recruiter upfront.

How important is it to know the DevRev product before interviewing?

Very important. DevRev interviewers expect candidates to have explored the product before arriving, not just skimmed the marketing website. Sign up for a free trial or request a sandbox and go through the core workflows: ticketing, PLuG, the CRM module, and the engineering integration. Speaking concretely about the product signals both preparation and genuine enthusiasm for the role.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at DevRev?

DevRev does not publicly disclose fixed salary bands for this role. Publicly reported figures on platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest that SE compensation at growth-stage SaaS companies in India varies significantly based on experience, location, and whether the role carries a variable component. It is worth clarifying the full package, including fixed pay, variable, and any equity, with the recruiter early in the process.

Is prior SaaS sales experience required to get an SE role at DevRev?

Not strictly required. Candidates report that DevRev values technical depth and customer empathy over a specific sales background. Engineers who have moved into pre-sales, consultants with SaaS exposure, and candidates from implementation or customer success roles have all progressed through the process. What matters most is that you can run a credible demo, handle technical questions confidently, and show commercial awareness.
