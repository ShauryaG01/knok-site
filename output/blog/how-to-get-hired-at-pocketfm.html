01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

PocketFM is one of India's most recognized audio series platforms, built around short-form, story-driven content in Hindi and regional languages. The company has grown a large listener base by focusing on bite-sized serialized content that resonates with audiences across metro and non-metro India. As of our latest data, PocketFM has 19 open roles spanning engineering, product, content, marketing, and operations.

Hiring here tends to attract people who are genuinely excited about audio entertainment and the creator economy, not just those looking for a stable tech job. The company operates at a startup pace even as it scales, so they prioritize people who can handle ambiguity and move quickly. Most roles are based in Bengaluru, with a few offering flexibility depending on the function and seniority level.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process typically runs across 3-4 rounds, though this varies by role and team. Here is a stage-by-stage sense of what to expect, based on what candidates commonly report.

Screening call: A recruiter or HR associate connects for an initial conversation covering your background, why you are interested in PocketFM, your notice period, and salary expectations. This round is primarily a fit check before the process goes deeper.

Technical or skills round: For engineering roles, expect a coding assessment on an online platform or during a live interview session. For product roles, expect a case study or product thinking question. Content and marketing candidates may be asked to review an existing feature or pitch a campaign concept. This stage tests your core, role-specific skills.

Manager or panel interview: A deeper conversation with the hiring manager or a small panel. Expect questions on past projects, how you handled specific challenges, and how you think through problems. 'Tell me about a time when' style questions are common here alongside role-specific scenarios.

Culture or leadership round: Some roles include a final round with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder. This is less about skill assessment and more about whether you fit how the team operates. Interviewers typically explore your motivations and long-term goals.

The full process can take 2-4 weeks depending on the role and how quickly the relevant stakeholders can align their schedules.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

PocketFM values people who understand their product as actual users, not just professionals analyzing it from the outside. If you genuinely follow audio content or the creator economy, that shows up naturally in interviews. Beyond that, here are the qualities they tend to prioritize.

Ownership mindset: They want people who treat problems as their own and follow through without constant direction. At a startup scale, fewer people share the load, so individual ownership matters more than it would at a large company.

Comfort with data: Whether you are in product, marketing, or engineering, the ability to pull numbers, spot patterns, and make decisions from them matters across most functions here.

Speed and adaptability: PocketFM moves fast and priorities can shift. They value people who can context-switch and still deliver quality work under changing conditions.

Clear communication: Because PocketFM serves audiences across multiple languages and regions, sharp written and verbal communication is expected across all roles, not just client-facing ones.

Relevant domain expertise: Engineering candidates should have strong fundamentals in the relevant stack. Content candidates benefit from familiarity with regional storytelling formats. Growth and marketing candidates do well with experience in user acquisition or retention for consumer apps.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product before your interview. Download PocketFM, listen to a few series, and form genuine opinions about what works and what could be better. Interviewers at product-first companies notice when candidates have real product perspectives rather than generic praise.

Understand the audio content space. Know how PocketFM is different from podcast apps or music streaming. Know who their core audience is and what specific problem the product solves for that audience. Searching for public interviews with their founders or leadership gives you useful context on where the company is headed.

Prepare a specific 'why PocketFM' answer. Generic answers about 'exciting space' or 'great growth trajectory' do not stand out. Tie your interest to something concrete: a content format you find clever, a regional language market you care about, or a gap in the audio space you think they are positioned to address.

For engineering candidates: Practice data structures and algorithms, and be ready to walk through your approach step by step, not just arrive at a final answer.

For product candidates: Practice structuring a product problem clearly: user, goal, constraints, options, tradeoffs. Be ready to disagree with a hypothetical feature decision and defend your reasoning calmly.

For content and marketing candidates: Come prepared to discuss short-form storytelling formats, retention hooks in serialized content, and regional audience behavior. Showing genuine thought about the non-metro Indian listener is a real differentiator.

On compensation: Salary data for PocketFM roles is not widely published. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have some data points, but sample sizes are small, so treat them as rough signals rather than firm benchmarks. Come prepared with a specific number and a clear reason for it.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

PocketFM operates with a lean, fast-moving culture typical of a growth-stage consumer startup. Teams are small relative to their output, which means individuals tend to own large chunks of work end to end. People who thrive here generally enjoy building things from scratch rather than maintaining large existing systems with many layers of process.

The company has a strong regional focus. A significant part of their content and audience comes from non-metro India, and this shapes how teams approach product decisions, language choices, and content strategy. Genuine appreciation for India's linguistic and cultural diversity is valued in day-to-day work, not just listed on job descriptions as a checkbox.

Work style leans towards close collaboration across functions. Product, content, engineering, and growth teams work alongside each other, so people who communicate well across disciplines and avoid working in silos tend to do better here.

PocketFM is at a stage where decisions individual contributors make can show up in the product fairly quickly. That kind of visibility and direct impact is a real draw for people who want to see their work matter early in their career rather than waiting for it to move through layers of approvals.
