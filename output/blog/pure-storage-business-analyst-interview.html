01 Overview

Overview

Pure Storage is a US-headquartered flash storage and cloud data services company with a growing India presence. As of July 2026, knok's job radar showed 366 open roles at Pure Storage, making it one of the more active tech companies hiring right now. The Business Analyst role here typically sits at the intersection of sales operations, product strategy, and data analytics, supporting teams across engineering, finance, and go-to-market functions.

Candidates report the interview process runs across multiple rounds, usually starting with a recruiter phone screen, followed by one or two analytical and case-based rounds, and ending with a hiring manager or panel discussion. The full process typically takes two to four weeks. Interviewers are known to test both hard skills (SQL, Excel, dashboards) and soft skills (stakeholder communication, business judgment, presenting findings clearly).

Pure Storage's core products include all-flash arrays and its subscription service, Evergreen//One. Familiarity with subscription metrics like ARR, churn, and net retention will help you in the technical rounds. You do not need deep storage engineering knowledge, but showing you understand how Pure Storage makes money will set you apart.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you used data to change a business decision.

*Situation:* Our sales team was pushing to expand into a new vertical, convinced it had high potential based on a few big deals.

*Task:* I was asked to validate this before the company committed budget to hiring additional headcount.

*Action:* I pulled CRM data covering multiple quarters, segmented deals by vertical, and calculated average deal size, sales cycle length, and win rate for each segment. I built a comparison table in Excel and reviewed it with the sales ops head before presenting to leadership.

*Result:* The data showed the target vertical had a notably lower win rate than our core verticals and a sales cycle nearly twice as long. Leadership paused the expansion and redirected the headcount budget to a vertical where we already had strong traction. The team appreciated having the numbers before making the call.

---

Q: How would you define and track customer retention metrics for a subscription business like Pure Storage?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we ran a SaaS product with annual contracts and had no single agreed definition of 'retention' across teams.

*Task:* I was asked to build a retention reporting framework the whole business could align on.

*Action:* I mapped out three core metrics: logo retention rate (did the customer renew?), net revenue retention (did their spend grow or shrink?), and time-to-renewal (were deals closing before or after the contract end date?). I documented the calculation logic, built a recurring SQL pull, and set up a monthly dashboard in Tableau.

*Result:* For the first time, sales, finance, and customer success were all looking at the same numbers. The dashboard flagged a segment of customers renewing late, which led to a proactive outreach programme that improved on-time renewals over the following two quarters.

---

Q: Describe a time your analysis revealed something uncomfortable for the business.

*Situation:* I was asked to investigate why one of our top-performing product lines was showing slower revenue growth despite strong marketing investment.

*Task:* I needed to present findings to the VP of Sales and the CMO, both of whom had championed the marketing spend.

*Action:* I structured the analysis around three possible explanations: market saturation, pricing pressure, and channel mix. The data pointed clearly to pricing. Specifically, discounting in one region was eroding margins without driving meaningful volume. I prepared a one-page summary with the key finding upfront (Pyramid Principle style), followed by supporting charts. I shared a draft with my manager first so there were no surprises in the room.

*Result:* The CMO was initially resistant, but the data held up under questioning. We introduced a discount approval process over the next quarter. Leadership later said they valued being given clear information even when it was uncomfortable.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions

Most Pure Storage BA interviews lean heavily on behavioural questions. STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) keeps your answers structured. Aim for around two minutes per answer. Candidates report that interviewers at Pure Storage often probe the Result step, asking 'how did you measure that?' so always quantify outcomes where you can.

Pyramid Principle for presenting analysis

When asked to walk through an analysis or present findings, lead with the conclusion, then supporting data, then detail. This mirrors how Pure Storage's internal stakeholders (often time-pressed sales or finance leaders) prefer to receive information.

MECE for structuring problem breakdowns

For case-style questions (such as the win/loss analysis or territory health question), use a Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive structure. Break the problem into non-overlapping buckets that together cover the full picture. For a territory health question, you might split into pipeline health, customer retention, and market coverage.

Root Cause Analysis for data anomalies

For questions about data errors or unexpected results, show a disciplined approach: confirm the anomaly is real (not a reporting bug), trace it to source systems, check for recent process changes, and escalate with context rather than just flagging the problem.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have gone through Pure Storage Business Analyst interviews typically describe interviewers as looking for a specific combination of traits.

Analytical rigour without over-engineering. Pure Storage interviewers want to see that you ask the right question before jumping into analysis. They value focus and clarity over complex methods used for their own sake.

Business context awareness. This is an enterprise B2B company. Demonstrating that you understand subscription economics, sales cycles, and how finance and sales teams use data will earn you clear credit.

Communication that simplifies, not impresses. Several candidates report being asked to explain technical concepts in plain terms. The bar is: can a non-technical sales manager understand your output and act on it?

Ownership and follow-through. Pure Storage has a stated cultural emphasis on accountability. Interviewers probe for examples where you drove something to completion, not just contributed to it.

Cross-functional collaboration. BA roles here sit between multiple teams. Showing you can navigate conflicting priorities across stakeholders without losing the thread of your own work is important.

Comfort with ambiguity. Expect at least one question where the brief is intentionally vague. Show that you know how to scope a problem before solving it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the company

Read Pure Storage's investor materials and recent earnings commentary, which are publicly available. Understand the Evergreen//One subscription model, their competitive positioning in enterprise storage, and how they talk about customer success. Note the language they use, as interviewers respond well when you reflect it back accurately.

Week 2: Sharpen your SQL and tools

Pure Storage BA interviews typically include at least one SQL question or a take-home analytical task. Practise writing queries for aggregations, window functions, and multi-table joins. If you use Tableau, Power BI, or Looker in your current role, prepare one clear example of a dashboard you built and can explain end to end.

Week 3: Prepare your story bank

Map at least one strong STAR story to each of these themes: using data to influence a decision, managing competing stakeholder priorities, presenting findings to senior leadership, finding and resolving a data quality issue, and improving a process. Four to six well-rehearsed stories can answer almost any behavioural question by adjusting the framing.

Before the interview

Research your interviewer on LinkedIn if you have their name. Review the job description and note which responsibilities appear first. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions about the team's current data infrastructure or the biggest analytical challenges they face. Candidates report that asking specific, informed questions stands out strongly.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'the team appreciated it' is not a result. Quantify outcomes where possible. If exact numbers are confidential, use directional language like 'reduced reporting time by roughly half' or note that the improvement was validated by the team lead.

Treating the role as purely technical. Pure Storage BA interviews test business judgment as much as SQL. Candidates who focus entirely on tools and skip the strategic and communication angle often struggle in the final rounds.

Not knowing Pure Storage's business model. Several candidates report being caught off guard by questions about subscription metrics or enterprise sales cycles. Spending even one hour reading about Evergreen//One before your interview gives you a clear edge.

Overcomplicating case answers. When asked to structure an analysis, interviewers want clarity and logic, not an exhaustive list of every possible variable. Two or three well-chosen metrics with clear rationale beat a sprawling framework every time.

Skipping the 'so what'. Candidates often describe what they did in great detail but rush past the business impact. Slow down at the Result step. That is what the interviewer remembers.

Asking no questions at the end. Candidates who say 'I think you have covered everything' signal low curiosity. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team, the data stack, or what success looks like in the first six months.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Pure Storage Business Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two analytical or case-based interviews, and a hiring manager or panel discussion. Some candidates also report a short take-home task involving data analysis. The full process typically takes two to four weeks from application to offer.

Do I need deep knowledge of storage technology to clear the BA interview?

No, deep storage engineering knowledge is not expected for the BA role. You should understand how Pure Storage makes money, what its core products are, and how its subscription model works. Being able to explain concepts like deduplication in plain business terms is valued, but you are not expected to have a hardware or infrastructure background.

What SQL skills should I prepare for?

Candidates report questions covering aggregations (GROUP BY, COUNT, SUM), joins across multiple tables, and window functions (RANK, ROW_NUMBER, running totals). You should also be comfortable explaining your query logic out loud as you write. Some rounds include a take-home task where you work on a sample dataset and present findings, so practise the full flow from query to insight.

How important is cultural fit in the Pure Storage interview?
