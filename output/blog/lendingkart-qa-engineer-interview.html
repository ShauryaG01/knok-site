01 Overview

Overview

Lendingkart is a Bangalore-based fintech company that gives working capital loans to small and medium businesses across India. Their platform handles loan applications, credit assessments, disbursements, and repayments, so QA Engineers here work on financially sensitive systems where a missed bug can directly affect a borrower's money or credit record.

Candidates report the interview process typically runs two to three rounds: a screening call to check your QA fundamentals, a technical round with hands-on questions on automation and API testing, and a final discussion covering system thinking or team fit. Lendingkart does not officially publish round details, so treat these stages as approximate.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 72 open roles at Lendingkart, a strong signal of active hiring. Across India, QA Engineer openings stood at 459 at the same point in time, with Bangalore leading at 87 postings and Delhi close behind at 67.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a critical bug you found and the impact it could have had if it reached production.

*Situation:* I was testing a loan repayment module at my previous company ahead of a scheduled production release.

*Task:* My goal was to verify that EMI calculations were accurate across different loan amounts, tenures, and interest rates.

*Action:* I ran boundary value tests on edge cases, including the maximum loan amount at the shortest available tenure. I noticed the calculated EMI was slightly lower than the expected value. I traced the issue to a rounding function that was truncating instead of rounding up on the final decimal place. I documented the defect with reproducible steps, tagged it as critical, and flagged it directly to the lead developer before the release window closed.

*Result:* The fix went in before release. The finance team confirmed the error would have created a recurring shortfall across a large number of borrower accounts had it gone live.

---

Q: How would you test a loan disbursement API from end to end?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were releasing a new personal loan disbursement feature that integrated with a banking partner's API.

*Task:* I was responsible for designing and executing the full API test plan before the integration went live.

*Action:* I started by mapping the complete happy path: application approved, credit check passed, disbursement triggered, confirmation received. Then I covered negative scenarios such as invalid account numbers, insufficient funds, timeout from the banking partner, and duplicate disbursement requests. I used Postman for manual exploration and RestAssured for automated regression. I also verified that every response payload matched the contract spec and that all transaction IDs were unique and correctly logged in the database.

*Result:* We caught a duplicate request handling bug before release. The fix enforced idempotency on all disbursement calls, which the backend team then extended to other payment flows as well.

---

Q: How do you handle regression testing when a new lending product launches on a short timeline?

*Situation:* My team had one week to test a new loan product that reused a large portion of the existing loan origination codebase.

*Task:* I needed to protect existing functionality from breakage while also validating all the new product-specific flows.

*Action:* I applied risk-based testing to prioritise. I listed all the shared code paths that the new product touched, ranked them by financial impact and usage frequency, and ran the automated regression suite on those first. For new flows, I wrote targeted test cases covering the unique eligibility rules and documentation checks. I held a short daily sync with the developer to get ahead of last-minute changes.

*Result:* Testing completed within the timeline. We found and resolved two medium-severity defects, and the product launched without post-release incidents.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Every 'tell me about a time' question at Lendingkart expects a concrete story. Structure it as Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you did, in detail), and Result (outcome or learning). Keep Situation and Task short; spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Boundary Value Analysis and Equivalence Partitioning for numeric input questions. Fintech systems process loan amounts, tenures, interest rates, and credit scores with strict numeric rules. Interviewers expect you to test at the boundary, for example the minimum and maximum loan amount, and just inside and outside it, not just a random middle value.

Risk-based prioritisation for coverage and timeline questions. When asked how you handle limited time, explain that you rank test cases by the financial or user impact of a failure. A bug in disbursement is higher risk than a bug in a notification email. Name the impact, then name the test priority.

Contract testing for API and integration questions. Lendingkart integrates with banks, credit bureaus, and payment gateways. Explaining that you validate API responses against a published contract spec (schema, status codes, field types) shows the interviewer you think about multi-system testing, not just happy-path checks.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hands-on automation skill. Lendingkart's backend is API-first. Interviewers want to see that you have written automation, not just run it. Be ready to explain your framework structure and describe a real test you built from scratch.

Fintech domain awareness. You do not need to be a finance expert, but knowing what an EMI is, how a credit bureau query works, why idempotency matters in payment APIs, and what NACH means for loan repayment shows you can ask the right test questions without needing to be tutored by the product team.

Data precision mindset. Financial systems depend on exact numbers. Interviewers look for candidates who validate amounts precisely, check database records directly, and treat rounding and decimal handling as explicit test cases rather than afterthoughts.

Early defect detection habits. Candidates who describe shift-left practices, such as reviewing requirements before sprint start or pairing with developers during coding, stand out. Lendingkart teams work in agile sprints and value QA engineers who reduce rework.

Clear, business-aware communication. You will explain bugs to product managers and developers alike. Interviewers pay attention to whether you describe defects in terms of business impact, not just technical symptoms.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core QA foundations. Revisit test design techniques (boundary value analysis, equivalence partitioning, decision tables), the defect life cycle, and STLC. Practice writing detailed test cases for a loan application form covering happy path, negative inputs, and edge cases. Make sure you can explain your test case format clearly and defend your coverage choices.

Week 2: Fintech domain and Lendingkart research. Study how EMI is calculated using principal, rate, and tenure. Understand what a credit bureau check involves, how NACH mandates work for loan repayments, and what LTV means in secured lending. Visit Lendingkart's website, read about their products, and think through the test scenarios their systems would require.

Week 3: Automation practice and mock interview. Write or review API tests in Postman or RestAssured against a sample public API. If you use Selenium or Appium, walk through one test script you have written and be ready to explain every part of it. Do at least one timed mock interview using the questions in this guide with the STAR format. Record yourself if possible to catch vague answers before the real interview.

Day before. Review your most impactful bug story, your automation framework description, and two questions to ask the interviewer about the team's QA processes or current testing challenges.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Treating QA as only manual clicking. Lendingkart interviewers expect automation depth. If you have not written automation before, be honest and show that you understand frameworks and have a clear plan to build this skill quickly.

2. No fintech domain preparation. Walking in without knowing what EMI, NACH, or a credit bureau query means signals you have not done basic research. Even one hour of reading about lending terminology changes how confidently you answer domain questions.

3. Vague answers without outcomes. Saying 'I improved test coverage' is weak. Say what the situation was, what you did, and what the result was. If you do not have exact figures, describe the observable outcome, for example zero post-release defects in that module or a release unblocked on time.

4. Ignoring performance and security testing. For a platform handling sensitive borrower data and real money, interviewers expect at least a working knowledge of load testing concepts and common security risks like SQL injection or data exposure.

5. Not asking the interviewer any questions. Asking nothing suggests low curiosity or low interest. Prepare one question about the team's current testing challenges and one about what success looks like in the first few months.

How many interview rounds does Lendingkart typically have for QA Engineer roles?

Candidates report the process typically runs two to three rounds. Is automation mandatory, or can I clear the interview with manual testing experience only?

Candidates report that automation is a firm expectation for most QA Engineer roles at Lendingkart, not an optional skill. Interviewers typically ask about your framework, the tools you use, and may ask you to walk through or write a sample test. If your background is primarily manual, prepare to explain what automation exposure you have had and show a credible plan to build that skill further.
