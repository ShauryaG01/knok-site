01 Overview

Overview

Narmi builds digital banking software for credit unions and community banks across the US. Their platform covers account opening, digital banking experiences, and payments infrastructure. As of mid-2026, Narmi has 12 open Software Engineer roles, pointing to real team growth across their engineering org.

The interview process typically spans 3-4 stages. Candidates report a recruiter or HR screen first, then a technical assessment (coding challenge or take-home project), followed by a technical panel with engineers, and a final conversation focused on culture and mission alignment. The full process typically takes 2-4 weeks end to end.

Because Narmi works in fintech, the bar for code quality, reliability, and security awareness is higher than at a typical product startup. Engineers who have touched payments, user authentication, or regulated data domains tend to feel at home here. Interviewers want to see that you understand why accuracy and uptime matter in financial systems, not just that you can write working code.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a production incident you helped resolve.

*Situation:* Our payments service started failing intermittently for a subset of users on a Saturday morning. Transactions were timing out without returning clear errors, so users could not tell whether their payment had gone through.

*Task:* I was on call and needed to identify the root cause quickly, limit user impact, and prevent any duplicate transactions from being created.

*Action:* I pulled logs immediately and noticed all failures were hitting one specific instance. I checked recent deploys and found a config change that had gone out that morning with an incorrect connection pool size for that instance. I rolled back the config, confirmed the error rate dropped to zero, then wrote a post-mortem with a new pre-deploy checklist item to validate connection pool settings before any release.

*Result:* The incident was contained within the hour. The post-mortem checklist was adopted across all services. Affected users received an in-app message confirming their payment status, which reduced support ticket volume noticeably.

---

Q: How do you manage technical debt when your team is moving fast?

*Situation:* At my previous company, our user onboarding flow had grown messy over two years. Each new feature had layered in conditional logic, making the codebase hard to reason about and test reliably.

*Task:* I wanted to reduce the debt without blocking the product roadmap or taking a multi-week detour away from feature work.

*Action:* I proposed a 'boy scout rule' approach: any engineer touching the onboarding module would refactor one nearby piece of messy code before shipping their feature. I documented the worst areas in a shared doc so contributors knew where to focus first. I also proposed setting aside one engineering day per sprint for debt reduction, backing the request with data showing the module had contributed to three recent bugs.

*Result:* Over one quarter, the onboarding module's bug rate dropped noticeably and new feature development in that area became faster. The team adopted the same approach for other high-debt modules across the product.

---

Q: Describe a significant architectural decision you made.

*Situation:* We were building a notifications system that needed to send emails, SMS, and in-app alerts. The first version called notification logic directly inside each service, creating tight coupling and making retries unreliable.

*Task:* As volume grew and silent failures became more common, I was asked to propose a more robust architecture before we expanded the feature further.

*Action:* I evaluated three options: keeping direct calls with better error handling, building a dedicated notifications microservice, or introducing a message queue with a consumer. I mapped out failure modes for each. Direct calls were simple but could not handle retries cleanly. A standalone microservice added operational overhead our small team could not sustain. A message queue gave us retry logic, decoupling, and a delivery audit trail, which mattered for our compliance requirements. I built a proof of concept over one week, presented the tradeoffs to team leads, and got buy-in to proceed.

*Result:* The queue-based system handled volume spikes without dropping notifications and gave us a delivery log we could share with compliance reviewers. It became the standard pattern for other async operations across the platform.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use STAR: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you did and why), Result (observable outcome). Keep Situation and Task short. Spend most of your time on Action and Result, since that is where interviewers form their judgment.

For system design in a fintech context, structure your answer around: clarifying requirements (read vs write load, consistency needs, regulatory constraints), data model, component design, failure modes and recovery, and monitoring. Always ask 'what happens when this fails?' before asking 'how do I make it faster?' Narmi's users are bank customers, so correctness beats speed every time.

For debugging and incident questions, walk through your mental model: what signals you looked at first, how you narrowed down the problem space, what you tried, and what you learned. Interviewers want to see methodical thinking, not just the lucky answer.

For 'why Narmi?' questions, connect the mission (supporting community banks and credit unions) to something genuine in your background or values. Generic 'I love fintech' answers fall flat. If you have worked with small businesses, local institutions, or underserved users, say so directly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Reliability mindset. Narmi's customers run live banking operations. Interviewers look for candidates who instinctively think about failure modes, data consistency, and user impact, not just whether the happy path works.

Fintech domain awareness. You do not need prior fintech experience, but showing you understand why uptime, security, and audit trails matter in a regulated environment signals cultural fit. Candidates who treat banking software like a typical SaaS product often struggle here.

Clean, testable code. Narmi is a product company, not a consulting shop. They want engineers who care about long-term code quality, write meaningful tests, and can refactor safely without breaking things.

Communication with non-engineers. Because Narmi works with bank product managers and operations staff, engineers need to explain technical decisions in plain language. Interviewers notice candidates who can simplify without dumbing down.

Genuine mission interest. Community banks and credit unions serve customers that large national banks often overlook. Interviewers can tell when a 'why Narmi?' answer is rehearsed. Prepare a real, personal one before you walk in.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and domain. Read Narmi's public blog and product pages to understand what they build and who their customers are. Study how digital account opening, ACH transfers, and core banking integrations work at a high level. You do not need to be a fintech expert, but you should be able to hold an informed conversation about the domain without needing prompts.

Week 2: Coding and system design practice. Practice medium-to-hard problems covering arrays, trees, and string manipulation. For system design, work through designing payment processing systems, notification pipelines, and authentication flows with a focus on reliability and data consistency. Prioritize correctness and fault tolerance over raw throughput in every design you practice.

Week 3: Behavioral prep. Write out 6-8 STAR stories covering: a production incident, a technical decision you owned, a disagreement with a teammate, managing technical debt, and a time you delivered under pressure. Practice saying them out loud. Timed run-throughs catch filler words and vague answers before the interview does.

Before your interview. Review Narmi's open roles (currently 12 positions) to understand which teams are growing and what stack they mention. Prepare two or three genuine questions about engineering culture, on-call practices, and how the team balances feature velocity with reliability investments. Asking thoughtful questions signals you are evaluating them as much as they are evaluating you.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating it like a generic coding interview. Narmi cares about domain judgment, not just algorithm speed. Candidates who optimize for hard competitive-programming problems but cannot discuss reliability or security often underperform here.
  1. Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved system performance' without specifics leaves interviewers with nothing to evaluate. Anchor every answer in concrete actions and observable results.
  1. Skipping the mission fit question. Candidates often prep technical answers thoroughly but give a generic 'I love fintech' answer for 'why Narmi?' This reads as unserious. Prepare a real, personal answer.
  1. Not asking about failure modes. Fintech companies respect engineers who ask 'what happens when this breaks?' Candidates who only think about the happy path signal shallow thinking to interviewers.
  1. Over-engineering system design answers. Narmi is a growing startup, not a hyperscaler. Proposing a massively distributed architecture for a problem a well-designed monolith could handle shows poor judgment. Right-size your designs to the actual requirements.
  1. Skipping test discussion. If you describe a solution without mentioning how you would test it, especially in a financial context, interviewers will notice. Bring up testing naturally as part of every technical answer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Narmi hire engineers without fintech experience?

Yes, candidates typically report that Narmi does not require prior fintech experience. What matters more is showing you understand the stakes of working in a regulated domain: reliability, data accuracy, and security. If you can demonstrate that mindset through examples from other industries, you can compete effectively. Prepare specific stories that show you think about failure modes and user impact as a habit, not just when prompted.

How technical is the Narmi Software Engineer interview?

Candidates report a technical screen with coding problems and at least one system design discussion. The coding portion tends to be practical rather than competitive-programming style. System design questions focus on building reliable, maintainable systems rather than optimizing for massive scale. Expect to discuss testing strategies, security considerations, and architectural tradeoffs as part of your answer.

What tech stack does Narmi use?

Based on publicly available job descriptions, Narmi's engineering teams work with Python and JavaScript/TypeScript, among other tools. The specific stack varies by team, so ask your recruiter early which technologies the team you are interviewing for uses. Focus your preparation on the fundamentals of reliability, testing, and system design rather than memorizing a particular stack.

How long does the Narmi interview process take?

Candidates typically report the process taking 2-4 weeks from first screen to offer. The number of rounds and specific format can vary by role and team, so ask your recruiter for a timeline and format breakdown early on. Having your STAR stories and system design frameworks ready before the first screen puts you in a strong position throughout.

What salary can I expect as a Software Engineer at Narmi?

Narmi is a US-based company, so compensation is denominated in USD rather than LPA. For publicly reported salary data specific to Narmi, Glassdoor and levels.fyi are the most reliable sources. If you are an Indian engineer exploring remote or India-based roles, industry surveys suggest compensation varies significantly by experience band, so ask your recruiter directly about the range attached to the specific role you are interviewing for.

Is there a take-home assignment in the Narmi interview process?

Some candidates report a take-home coding project, while others describe a live coding screen. The format can differ by team and role, so ask your recruiter what to expect so you can prepare accordingly. For take-home projects, focus on clean code, meaningful tests, and a clear README that walks through your decisions, since Narmi values code quality and communication over raw cleverness.
