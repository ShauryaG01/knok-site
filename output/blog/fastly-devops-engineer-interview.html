01 Overview

Overview

Fastly is an edge cloud platform provider known for its CDN, edge computing, and network security products. DevOps Engineers here build and operate the infrastructure that keeps global content delivery fast and reliable for enterprise customers. With 54 open DevOps Engineer roles at Fastly as of July 2026, the company is actively hiring.

Candidates typically go through three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering infrastructure and networking, a system design discussion, and a final cultural or leadership round. Candidates report that Fastly interviewers focus more on real-world distributed systems experience and automation thinking than on algorithm puzzles. Expect detailed questions on CDN concepts, Varnish Configuration Language (VCL), CI/CD for edge infrastructure, and incident ownership.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Fastly DevOps interviews, based on candidate reports and Fastly's known tech stack (Varnish/VCL, edge computing, Terraform, Kubernetes, observability tooling).

  1. Fastly uses Varnish Configuration Language (VCL) to control cache behavior at the edge. Have you worked with VCL or a similar domain-specific language? How would you approach getting up to speed on it quickly?
  2. Walk us through how you would design a CI/CD pipeline for deploying configuration changes to a globally distributed edge network.
  3. A cache invalidation bug is causing stale content to be served to users worldwide. How do you investigate and resolve it under time pressure?
  4. How do you set up monitoring and alerting for edge nodes so you catch degradation before customers report it?
  5. Describe a time when you had to scale infrastructure to handle a sudden, large traffic spike with little or no advance warning.
  6. How do you manage infrastructure-as-code for a system that spans many regions and a large number of nodes simultaneously?
  7. Fastly's network uses BGP and anycast routing. How comfortable are you with network-layer debugging, and what tools do you reach for first?
  8. If you need to roll out a configuration change to all Fastly edge nodes at once, how do you structure the rollout to reduce the risk of a widespread outage?
  9. How have you used distributed tracing, metrics, and structured logs together to find a performance bottleneck in a live production system?
  10. Describe the most complex piece of automation you have built. What problem did it solve, and what would you change if you built it today?
  11. Fastly customers escalate urgently when their sites go down. How do you stay methodical and communicate clearly during a high-pressure incident?
  12. What is your experience running Kubernetes in production? How have you handled stateful workloads or persistent storage challenges in a high-availability environment?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would design a CI/CD pipeline for deploying configuration changes to a globally distributed edge network.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we operated a CDN-like setup across multiple points of presence for a media streaming client. Engineers applied Varnish configuration changes manually by logging into each node one by one.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement a deployment pipeline that could push VCL changes safely without causing downtime for the client.

*Action:* I set up a GitOps workflow using GitLab CI. Every merge to main triggered a pipeline that validated VCL syntax in a staging environment, ran smoke tests against a shadow traffic replica, and then deployed to production in a canary ring of two nodes first. The pipeline waited for error-rate metrics to stay within agreed thresholds before rolling out to the remaining nodes in batches. I used Terraform to manage the node fleet and Ansible to apply VCL configs atomically.

*Result:* Deployment time dropped from several hours of manual work to well under half an hour. We caught two configuration errors in canary before they reached all nodes. The client reported no customer-facing downtime in the following quarter.

---

Q: A cache invalidation bug is causing stale content to be served to users worldwide. How do you investigate and fix it?

*Situation:* At a previous role, a content platform reported that a product page update was not reflecting for users even half an hour after publishing.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer responsible for diagnosing and fixing the issue within our SLA window.

*Action:* I first confirmed the symptom by fetching the affected URL from multiple edge nodes using curl with cache-bypass headers, comparing what the origin returned versus what the cache was serving. I then checked the cache TTL headers and found that a recent VCL change had set 'beresp.ttl' to a very high value for a URL pattern matching the affected pages. I issued a targeted purge via the Fastly API for those URLs, confirmed fresh content was being served, and then reviewed the change that introduced the bug. I also added a pipeline check to flag unusually high TTL values on dynamic content patterns.

*Result:* Stale content was cleared within minutes of diagnosis. The root-cause fix was merged the same day. The new pipeline check caught a similar misconfiguration three weeks later before it reached production.

---

Q: Describe the most complex automation you have built.

*Situation:* At a SaaS company, the on-call team spent a large part of each shift manually triaging alerts, checking dashboards, and deciding whether to escalate to a senior engineer. This caused alert fatigue and slow response times on genuine incidents.

*Task:* I proposed and built an automated alert triage service to reduce noise and speed up escalation for real issues.

*Action:* I built a Python service that consumed alerts from PagerDuty, queried Prometheus and Elasticsearch for correlated signals, and classified each alert into one of three buckets: 'likely noise', 'known issue with a runbook', or 'novel incident requiring human review'. For known issues, it acknowledged the alert automatically and triggered a remediation script. For novel incidents, it enriched the page with relevant dashboard links, recent deploy history, and on-call context before notifying the engineer on duty.

*Result:* Alert volume requiring human action dropped significantly according to our internal metrics, tracked over a full quarter. Mean time to acknowledge genuine incidents improved noticeably. The system is still running at that company today.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions (CI/CD pipelines, monitoring setups, rollout strategies): Start by clarifying constraints: scale, latency requirements, team size, and acceptable downtime. Then describe your solution in layers: how you build it, how you test it, how you operate it, and how you would improve it over time. Fastly interviewers want to see operational thinking, not just architecture sketches.

For incident and debugging questions: Use a structured approach. First, confirm the symptom with actual data. Then isolate the blast radius. Then narrow the root cause using layered investigation, starting at the application layer, then infrastructure, then network. Always mention how you communicate with stakeholders during the investigation itself, not just in the post-mortem.

For 'tell me about a time' questions: Use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep Situation and Task brief, two to three sentences each. Spend most of your answer on Action: what you specifically did, the tools you used, and the tradeoffs you made. End with a concrete Result and, if possible, what you learned or would do differently.

For automation questions: Lead with the problem you were solving, not the technology you chose. Fastly values engineers who automate because it makes systems safer and more reliable, not just because automation looks impressive on a resume.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fastly DevOps interviews typically probe a handful of core qualities.

Deep networking and CDN knowledge. Fastly's product is the network. Candidates who can speak confidently to BGP, anycast, TLS termination at the edge, and cache behavior stand out. You do not need prior Fastly experience, but you do need to understand how CDNs work at a technical level.

Automation-first thinking. Fastly operates at scale and moves fast. Interviewers want to see that your instinct, when faced with a repetitive or risky manual process, is to automate it. More importantly, they want evidence that you have actually done this, not just proposed it in a meeting.

Incident ownership. Candidates report that Fastly places high value on engineers who take ownership during outages: stay calm, communicate clearly, investigate methodically, and write honest post-mortems. Be ready to walk through a real incident you owned from detection to resolution.

Comfort with distributed systems complexity. Questions about partial failures, observability in multi-region setups, and safe rollout strategies come up often. Show that you have operated these systems in production, not just drawn them on a whiteboard.

Clear communication and collaboration. Fastly's engineering teams are globally distributed. Interviewers look for people who write clearly, document their decisions well, and can explain complex technical choices to non-technical stakeholders without losing accuracy.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand Fastly's product and tech stack. Read Fastly's engineering blog (search 'Fastly engineering blog' to find it). Focus on posts about VCL, edge computing, and reliability engineering. Set up a free Fastly trial account if possible and experiment with writing basic VCL cache rules. Review how CDNs work: cache hierarchies, purge strategies, TTL management, and the difference between edge and origin behavior.

Week 2: Refresh core DevOps skills. Review Terraform for multi-region infrastructure management. Brush up on Kubernetes, particularly pod scheduling, rolling deployments, and persistent storage. Practice explaining CI/CD pipeline design out loud, from code commit to production deployment, including how you would handle a bad rollout.

Week 3: Practice structured answers. Pick three to four questions from the list above and write out full STAR answers. Time yourself: aim for three to four minutes per answer. Do at least one mock system design session focused on edge infrastructure or CDN design. Practice walking through a real incident you have handled, using a clear structure.

Week 4: Company-specific research. Study the Fastly job description carefully and map your experience to each listed requirement. Review Fastly's published network map and any recent product announcements. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for your interviewers about on-call culture, current infrastructure challenges, and how the team measures DevOps success.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Focusing on algorithms instead of systems. Fastly DevOps interviews are not heavy on coding puzzles. Candidates who spend all their prep time on data structures and skip system design, networking, and operational scenarios often find themselves underprepared when the actual questions start.

Using 'we' instead of 'I'. When describing past work, be specific about your personal contribution. Interviewers are assessing you individually, not your team's collective output.

Skipping the tradeoffs. A strong candidate does not just describe a solution. They explain why they chose it over alternatives, what its weaknesses are, and how they would improve it given more time or resources. This matters especially in system design rounds where there is rarely one correct answer.

Not knowing Fastly's product. Candidates who cannot connect their experience to CDN or edge computing concepts tend to be screened out early. Even a basic familiarity with VCL and edge caching logic is a meaningful differentiator in the recruiter screen.

Underestimating the networking questions. Many DevOps candidates are strong on cloud infrastructure but weaker on networking fundamentals. If BGP, anycast, DNS resolution, or TCP/IP debugging are gaps for you, invest time there before your first technical round.

Not asking questions at the end. Fastly interviewers typically leave time for your questions. Asking nothing, or asking only about salary and perks, signals low interest. Ask about on-call load, recent technical challenges, or how the team approaches post-mortems after a major incident.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Fastly DevOps Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process of three to four rounds, typically starting with a recruiter screen, followed by one or two technical rounds and a final round covering system design or cultural fit. The exact structure varies by team and level. The full process typically takes three to five weeks from first contact to offer, according to candidate reports.

Do I need prior experience with Fastly products like VCL or Compute@Edge?

Prior Fastly experience is helpful but not required. Candidates report that interviewers care more about whether you understand CDN concepts, caching logic, and edge computing principles than whether you have used Fastly's specific products before. Spending a few hours reading Fastly's documentation and writing basic VCL rules before your interview will give you a real advantage over candidates who skip this step.

What salary can I expect as a DevOps Engineer at Fastly India?

Fastly does not publicly publish India-specific salary data. How important are networking skills like BGP and anycast for this role?

Very important at Fastly specifically, because the product is built on network infrastructure. Candidates report networking questions coming up in technical rounds, covering CDN traffic routing, anycast behavior, and network-layer debugging techniques. This is a stronger focus here than in typical cloud-focused DevOps roles, so do not skip networking fundamentals in your preparation.

Is there a coding test in the Fastly DevOps interview process?

Candidates report that Fastly DevOps interviews lean toward system design and operational questions rather than algorithmic coding tests. You may be asked to write or review a script in Python or Bash, or work through a Terraform or infrastructure-as-code problem. Brushing up on scripting and infrastructure tooling will serve you better here than competitive programming practice.
