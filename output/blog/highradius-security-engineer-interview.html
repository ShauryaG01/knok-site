01 Overview

Overview

HighRadius is a Hyderabad-founded, US-headquartered fintech SaaS company that automates Order-to-Cash, treasury, and accounts receivable workflows for large enterprises. Because it processes sensitive financial data for global clients, the security function carries real weight here.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracks 71 open roles at HighRadius and 628 Security Engineer jobs across India, with Bangalore leading at 69 openings.

What the role covers. Security Engineers at HighRadius typically work across cloud security (AWS and Azure), application security, compliance programmes (PCI-DSS and SOC 2), and securing the AI/ML pipelines that power their intelligent automation products. Expect a mix of hands-on technical work and cross-functional collaboration with product and engineering teams.

Interview format. Candidates report a process that typically includes an online assessment or take-home challenge, followed by two to four technical rounds, and a hiring manager or culture-fit conversation. Rounds cover secure coding, threat modelling, cloud security, incident response, and compliance awareness.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in HighRadius Security Engineer interviews, based on candidate reports and the nature of their fintech SaaS product:

  1. How would you approach securing a multi-tenant SaaS application that processes financial data for large enterprises?
  2. Walk us through how you perform threat modelling for a new feature in an Order-to-Cash workflow.
  3. What is your experience with PCI-DSS or SOC 2 compliance, and how have you supported a compliance audit?
  4. Describe how you would conduct a vulnerability assessment on a cloud-hosted application running on AWS or Azure.
  5. How do you secure REST APIs that handle sensitive financial transactions?
  6. What is your approach to integrating security into a CI/CD pipeline (DevSecOps)?
  7. How would you detect and respond to a security incident where customer financial data may have been exposed?
  8. Explain how you would implement Identity and Access Management (IAM) controls in a multi-cloud environment.
  9. How do you balance security requirements with the need for rapid product delivery in an agile team?
  10. Describe your experience with SIEM tools and how you use them to detect and triage threats.
  11. How would you design a zero-trust architecture for a distributed SaaS platform?
  12. Tell me about a critical vulnerability you discovered. How did you manage disclosure and remediation?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you approach securing a multi-tenant SaaS application that processes financial data?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we ran a cloud-based B2B platform where dozens of enterprise clients stored billing and payment data in a shared infrastructure.

*Task:* I was asked to lead a security review after a competitor suffered a data breach, and leadership wanted to validate our tenant isolation controls.

*Action:* I started with a data-flow mapping exercise to identify every point where one tenant's data could theoretically touch another tenant's context. I then ran automated scanning with a cloud security posture management tool and manually reviewed IAM policies, database row-level security, and API gateway authorisation logic. I also worked with the engineering team to add tenant-context validation middleware across all internal services.

*Result:* The review surfaced three medium-severity misconfiguration issues, all remediated within a two-week sprint. We also produced a reusable security checklist that now gates every new feature deployment.

---

Q: Tell me about a critical vulnerability you discovered and how you managed it.

*Situation:* While doing a routine code review on a payment integration module, I noticed that a third-party callback endpoint was not validating the source signature.

*Task:* I needed to assess the actual exploitability, brief stakeholders without causing panic, and drive a fast fix without breaking the live payment flow.

*Action:* I reproduced the issue in a staging environment to confirm it allowed replay attacks on payment confirmations. I then wrote a confidential incident brief for the engineering lead and CISO, proposed a patch using HMAC signature verification with a short replay window, and coordinated a hotfix release outside the normal sprint cycle. I also notified the third-party vendor.

*Result:* The patch went live within two days. No customer data was affected. The incident led to a new policy requiring all inbound webhook endpoints to pass a signature-validation gate during code review.

---

Q: How do you integrate security into a CI/CD pipeline?

*Situation:* My team shipped features fast but security reviews were a bottleneck, happening only at the end of each release cycle.

*Task:* I was asked to shift security left so that issues were caught during development, not at release time.

*Action:* I introduced SAST tooling (Semgrep) into the pull-request pipeline so every merge triggered an automated scan. I added dependency scanning (OWASP Dependency-Check) and container image scanning (Trivy) to the build stage. I also wrote a short internal guide so developers could understand alerts and self-serve on common fixes.

*Result:* Critical findings at release time dropped noticeably, and developer confidence in shipping secure code improved, based on retrospective feedback from the team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Use this for all behavioural and scenario questions. Keep the Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined) and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Threat modelling with STRIDE. When asked how you would secure a feature or system, walk through Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, and Elevation of Privilege. It shows structured thinking, which HighRadius interviewers value because their product teams ship fast and need security engineers who can keep up.

Detect, Contain, Remediate, Learn. For incident response questions, frame your answer in these four stages. It signals maturity and process discipline, both of which matter in a company that holds enterprise financial data.

Compliance framing. When PCI-DSS or SOC 2 comes up, anchor your answer to specific controls (for example, PCI-DSS Requirement 6 for secure development, or SOC 2 Trust Service Criteria CC6 for logical access). Vague answers about 'following best practices' do not score well here.

Trade-off articulation. For questions about balancing security and velocity, always name the trade-off explicitly, explain how you quantified or communicated the risk, and describe the outcome. Interviewers want to see that you can collaborate with product managers, not just enforce rules.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fintech context awareness. HighRadius serves large enterprise clients and handles financial transactions at scale. Interviewers want to see that you understand why PCI-DSS, data residency, and audit logging matter in this context, not just in abstract terms.

Cloud security depth. AWS and Azure come up heavily in candidate reports. Be ready to discuss IAM policies, VPC security, secrets management, and cloud-native security tooling at a practical level.

Product security mindset. HighRadius ships AI-powered automation products. Interviewers respond well to candidates who think about security as a product quality concern, not a compliance checkbox.

Clear communication. You will work with engineers, product managers, and sometimes client-facing teams. Interviewers assess whether you can explain a vulnerability or a risk decision to a non-security audience without losing accuracy.

Ownership and follow-through. Stories that show you found a problem AND saw it through to resolution score higher than stories that end with 'I flagged it to the team.'

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product. Read HighRadius product pages for Order-to-Cash, treasury management, and their AI agent products. Understand what data they process and why that makes security critical. This context improves every answer you give.

Week 1: Compliance basics. If you are not already familiar, spend time on PCI-DSS v4.0 requirements (especially Requirements 6, 7, and 8) and the SOC 2 Trust Service Criteria. You do not need to be an auditor, but you should be able to discuss specific controls.

Week 2: Cloud security practice. Review IAM least-privilege patterns, storage misconfiguration scenarios, secrets management (AWS Secrets Manager, Azure Key Vault), and network segmentation. If you have AWS or Azure certifications, refresh the security-focused content.

Week 2: Threat modelling drill. Pick one HighRadius product feature (for example, an automated invoice matching workflow) and write a quick STRIDE threat model for it. Practice explaining it aloud in under five minutes.

Week 3: STAR stories. Prepare at least five STAR answers covering: a vulnerability you found and fixed, a compliance or audit experience, a DevSecOps improvement, a cross-team security conflict, and an incident response situation.

Week 3: Mock interviews and job tracking. Practice with a peer or record yourself. Keep Situation and Task brief, and make your Action steps specific and technical. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too generic. Saying 'I follow OWASP best practices' without tying it to a specific scenario signals shallow experience. Always anchor to a real example.

Ignoring fintech context. Candidates who treat HighRadius like a generic software company miss the point. Financial data, enterprise clients, and audit requirements are central to the role.

Weak compliance knowledge. Many candidates mention PCI-DSS by name but cannot discuss a specific requirement. Interviewers at a fintech SaaS company test this concretely.

Stopping at 'I flagged it'. If your incident or vulnerability story ends with handing off to someone else, it signals limited ownership. Show that you drove the fix.

Overcomplicating technical explanations. When asked to explain a security concept to a non-technical stakeholder (a common HighRadius scenario), candidates who cannot simplify are flagged as communication risks.

Not preparing questions. HighRadius is growing fast and has multiple security sub-teams. Asking specific questions about team structure, current threat landscape, or compliance roadmap signals genuine interest and preparation.
