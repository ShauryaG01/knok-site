01 Overview

Overview

TELUS International is a global digital customer experience and managed IT services company headquartered in Canada, with delivery centers across India. They work with enterprise clients on cloud operations, automation, and IT service delivery, making DevOps engineers central to their delivery teams. As of July 2026, knok's jobradar shows 3 open DevOps Engineer positions at TELUS International, part of 811 total DevOps openings tracked across India.

Candidates report the interview process typically spans two to three technical discussions plus an HR round, covering CI/CD pipelines, cloud platforms, Kubernetes, scripting, and incident management. Because TELUS serves enterprise clients with strict uptime and security requirements, interviewers tend to weigh reliability thinking and client-impact awareness alongside hands-on tool knowledge.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates report most frequently in TELUS International DevOps interviews, reflecting the kind of work their teams handle daily.

  1. Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you built end-to-end. Which tools did you use and why?
  2. How do you manage infrastructure as code? Describe a real project using Terraform, Pulumi, or a similar tool.
  3. Explain your hands-on experience with Kubernetes. How have you handled pod scaling and resource limits in production?
  4. TELUS serves enterprise clients with strict SLAs. How do you approach incident response and root cause analysis?
  5. How have you handled compliance and security requirements (such as SOC 2 audits) within your DevOps workflow?
  6. Which observability tools do you use for monitoring application and infrastructure health? How do you configure meaningful alerts?
  7. How do you collaborate with development teams to increase deployment frequency while keeping rollbacks safe?
  8. Have you worked in a multi-client or managed services environment? How did you maintain environment separation?
  9. How do you approach cloud cost optimisation on AWS, Azure, or GCP?
  10. Describe a time you automated a repetitive operational task. What was the measurable impact?
  11. How do you manage secrets and access control in your deployment pipelines?
  12. TELUS operates across time zones. How have you handled on-call rotations and cross-region knowledge handoffs?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these as templates and replace the tools and context with your own experience.

---

Q: Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you built from scratch.

*Situation:* My team was deploying a microservices application through a manual, step-by-step process that caused frequent environment mismatches and delayed releases by several days each cycle.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement a fully automated pipeline before the next release.

*Action:* I set up Jenkins with multibranch pipelines, integrated SonarQube for static code analysis, containerised builds using Docker, and pushed images to a private registry. I then configured ArgoCD for GitOps-based deployments to our Kubernetes cluster, with automated rollback on health-check failure.

*Result:* Releases became consistent and reproducible. The team reported a clear drop in production incidents tied to manual deployment errors, and we could ship features more confidently each sprint.

---

Q: Tell me about a major production incident you handled.

*Situation:* A client-facing application started throwing gateway errors during peak hours, causing widespread user impact and triggering an SLA breach alert.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer I had to triage the issue quickly, communicate status to stakeholders, and restore service.

*Action:* I pulled logs from our centralised logging platform and identified that a misconfigured load balancer health check was removing healthy pods from rotation. I patched the health check configuration and triggered a controlled rolling restart.

*Result:* The service recovered within minutes. I then led a structured post-mortem, wrote a detailed RCA document, and added an automated alert to catch similar misconfigurations before they reach production again.

---

Q: How have you reduced cloud infrastructure costs for a project?

*Situation:* Our staging environment ran continuously on full-size instances, generating a significant monthly cloud bill with very low utilisation during nights and weekends.

*Task:* I was asked to cut costs without reducing developer productivity or environment stability.

*Action:* I implemented auto-scaling policies, migrated non-critical workloads to spot instances, set up scheduled shutdowns for environments outside business hours, and right-sized over-provisioned services using monitoring data.

*Result:* The team reported a meaningful reduction in monthly cloud spend. Developer feedback was positive because environment startup time remained acceptable and no workflows were disrupted.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Every 'tell me about a time' question needs a clear Situation (one or two sentences), Task (what you specifically owned), Action (what you did and why), and Result (a measurable or observable outcome). Keep it under two minutes when speaking.

Tool-comparison questions. For questions like 'Ansible vs Terraform' or 'Prometheus vs Datadog', answer in three beats: what each tool does best, a real project where you chose one, and a brief note showing you are comfortable with both. Avoid positioning one tool as always superior.

Scenario or design questions. When asked 'how would you' questions, state your approach first, walk through the steps, then close with the trade-offs you considered. Candidates report TELUS interviewers appreciate when you tie design choices back to client SLA or security requirements.

Compliance and security questions. Acknowledge the requirement, describe the specific controls you put in place (access policies, audit logging, secrets management), and explain how you verified compliance. Vague answers like 'we followed best practices' without specifics tend to lose points.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

TELUS International manages IT operations for enterprise clients, so interviewers typically look for engineers who combine technical depth with reliability thinking and clear communication.

Technical depth. Hands-on cloud experience (AWS, Azure, or GCP) beyond basic deployments. Strong scripting ability in Python, Bash, or similar. Production-grade Kubernetes experience, not just local or sandbox clusters.

Reliability mindset. They want engineers who think about uptime, rollback strategies, and graceful degradation. Be ready to discuss your approach to SLAs and post-mortems with specific examples.

Security awareness. In a managed services environment, client data isolation and access control are non-negotiable. Candidates who can speak concretely to secrets management, role-based access, and audit logging stand out.

Client and team communication. Because TELUS engineers often work in client-facing roles, interviewers assess whether you can explain a production issue clearly to a non-technical stakeholder and coordinate calmly under pressure.

Ownership. They look for candidates who follow through: not just fixing the immediate problem but preventing recurrence and documenting the resolution for the team.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

One to two weeks before.

Review your CI/CD and infrastructure-as-code fundamentals. Practice explaining your pipeline architecture out loud as if to a colleague who was not there when you built it. Brush up on Kubernetes core concepts: pods, services, ingress controllers, horizontal pod autoscaler, and resource limits.

Prepare four to five STAR stories covering: a major incident you resolved, an automation project you led, a cross-team collaboration challenge, a cost or performance optimisation, and a security or compliance situation. Having these ready means you can adapt them to different question phrasings on the day.

Three to four days before.

Research TELUS International's recent announcements, clients, and service areas. Read the job description carefully and map your experience to each listed requirement. Do one mock technical discussion with a peer or record yourself answering aloud.

The day before.

Prepare two or three genuine questions for the interviewer, such as how DevOps teams interact with client delivery teams, what the current CI/CD toolchain looks like, or what a typical on-call rotation involves. Good questions signal engagement and help you assess whether the role is the right fit.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Generic answers without enterprise context. TELUS serves large enterprise clients. If your STAR stories do not mention client impact, SLA awareness, or multi-environment complexity, they will feel thin. Frame every example around reliability and business outcome.

2. Listing tools without explaining outcomes. Saying 'I used Kubernetes and Terraform' is not enough. Interviewers want to know what changed as a result: faster releases, fewer incidents, lower cloud cost.

3. Skipping observability. Monitoring, alerting, and logging are at the core of managed services work. If you do not address how you detect and diagnose a production issue, interviewers will notice the gap.

4. Underestimating soft skills. In a client-facing environment, your ability to communicate clearly during an incident, write a readable RCA, or explain a technical trade-off to a project manager matters alongside your technical ability.

5. Not explaining your reasoning. Always say why you chose a tool or approach, not just what you chose. 'We used Ansible because our team already had Python skills and the target machines were stateless' is far more convincing than 'we used Ansible because it is popular.'
