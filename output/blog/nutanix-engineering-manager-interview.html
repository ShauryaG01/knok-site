01 Overview

Overview

Nutanix is a global cloud infrastructure company with a strong engineering presence in India, particularly in Bangalore. Engineering Manager interviews here are thorough, typically covering technical depth, people leadership, and business alignment across multiple rounds.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 122 open roles at Nutanix in India, part of a broader market with 975 Engineering Manager positions available nationally. Competition for these roles is real, and preparation makes a measurable difference.

This guide covers the questions Nutanix interviewers typically ask, how to structure strong answers, what the panel is actually evaluating, and how to spend your prep time well.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report that Nutanix Engineering Manager interviews cover a consistent set of themes, blending technical credibility, team leadership, and product thinking. Here are the questions that come up most often:

  1. Tell us about a time you had to make a hard technical trade-off. How did you decide, and what was the outcome?
  2. Nutanix operates across on-premise and cloud environments. How do you keep your team current with infrastructure and cloud-native trends?
  3. Describe how you managed a team that was consistently missing sprint commitments. What did you change, and why?
  4. Walk us through a situation where two senior engineers on your team had a strong technical disagreement. How did you resolve it without simply picking sides?
  5. How do you approach hiring? What signals do you look for in a candidate that would not appear on a resume?
  6. How do you balance reliability versus velocity for a team working on infrastructure software?
  7. Tell us about a product or feature you drove end-to-end as a manager. How did you align engineering with product and business stakeholders?
  8. How do you structure one-on-ones and performance conversations? What does 'good' look like to you?
  9. Describe a time you advocated for a technical decision that leadership initially pushed back on. What happened?
  10. How do you handle a high performer who is burning out or showing signs of disengagement?
  11. Give an example of a time your team solved a problem that had a direct, measurable customer impact.
  12. How do you measure engineering team health beyond velocity and ticket counts?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you had to make a hard technical trade-off.

*Situation:* My team was building a distributed storage feature under a tight deadline tied to a major customer commitment.

*Task:* I had to choose between a faster but less fault-tolerant implementation and a slower, more robust approach that would push delivery by three weeks.

*Action:* I brought the team together, mapped both options with clear risk profiles, and consulted with product and customer success to understand the actual failure tolerance in the customer environment. We landed on a middle path: ship the robust core on time, defer two non-critical features, and communicate the plan transparently to the stakeholder before they had to chase us.

*Result:* We met the customer deadline, had zero reliability incidents in the following quarter, and the deferred features shipped in the next cycle with full test coverage. The customer extended their contract.

---

Q: Describe how you managed a team that was consistently missing sprint commitments.

*Situation:* Six months into joining a new team, I noticed we were regularly falling short of what we committed to in sprint planning, which was creating downstream problems for QA and product.

*Task:* I needed to diagnose the root cause without demoralising a team that was already working hard.

*Action:* I ran a quiet two-sprint audit, sitting in on estimation sessions and tracking interruptions. I found three problems: over-optimistic estimation, unplanned support escalations landing on random engineers, and unclear ownership of cross-team dependencies. I introduced a planning buffer, set up a structured support rotation, and created a dependency tracker shared with partner teams.

*Result:* Completion rates improved noticeably within two sprints. By end of quarter, the team felt more in control, and product started planning more confidently against our commitments.

---

Q: How do you handle a high performer who is burning out?

*Situation:* One of my best senior engineers, the go-to person for three critical systems, started missing standups and submitting lower-quality code reviews.

*Task:* I needed to act quickly but carefully, without making them feel penalised for showing strain.

*Action:* I had a direct but private conversation, asking open questions about workload and energy rather than leading with performance feedback. It turned out they were context-switching across too many projects with no clear priority. I cut their active project load in half for six weeks, redistributed some of the on-call burden, and protected blocks of uninterrupted deep-work time on their calendar.

*Result:* Their output returned to previous levels within six weeks. They told me later it was the first time a manager had caught the problem before they had started thinking about leaving.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR is the most reliable structure for Nutanix's behavioural questions, and most interviewers here expect it even if they do not say so explicitly.

Basic STAR breakdown: Situation (one or two sentences of context). Task (what you personally owned). Action (the bulk of your answer, the majority of your speaking time, focused on your decisions and reasoning). Result (the outcome, and what you learned if things did not go perfectly).

For technical trade-off questions: Add an 'Options' layer before Action. Briefly name the two or three choices you considered and explain why you ruled out the others. This shows structured thinking, not just good outcomes.

For leadership and conflict questions: Nutanix interviewers typically probe the 'how' more than the 'what.' Do not just say you coached someone. Describe the specific conversation, the approach you used, or the process you set up.

For cross-functional questions: Always include who you consulted, where the pushback came from, and how you built alignment. Nutanix product, customer success, and engineering teams work closely together, and showing you can navigate that environment carries real weight.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Nutanix Engineering Manager interviews typically look for four things, based on what candidates report from their experience.

Technical credibility. You do not need to be coding daily, but you are expected to have strong opinions on system design, reliability, and scalability. Interviewers check whether you can have peer-level technical conversations with your team and earn their trust.

People leadership depth. This is not about reciting HR frameworks. They want to see that you have navigated real, messy situations: underperformance, conflict, attrition risk. Stories with nuance carry more weight than stories about smooth projects.

Customer and business alignment. Nutanix is an enterprise infrastructure company and its engineering culture is closely tied to customer outcomes. Expect at least one question that tests whether you think about business impact alongside technical correctness.

Comfort with ambiguity. Engineering Manager roles at Nutanix often involve working across time zones, coordinating with US leadership, and managing distributed teams. Showing that you can operate and make decisions without perfect information is a strong signal.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before the interview

Read everything publicly available about Nutanix: recent product announcements, their AOS and Prism product lines, and any engineering blog posts. Their technical direction is relatively open and interviewers notice when you have done this homework.

Write out eight to ten STAR stories from your own career covering: a technical failure, a team conflict, a hiring decision you made, a project you killed or descoped, and a time you pushed back on leadership. Having these drafted stops you from going blank mid-interview.

One week before

Do two or three mock system design sessions focused on distributed storage or infrastructure software, since that is Nutanix's core domain. You do not need to be an expert, but you should be comfortable discussing data replication, fault tolerance, and the trade-offs between on-premise and cloud deployments.

Prepare three to four questions to ask your interviewers. Strong options: 'What does the first 90 days look like for a new Engineering Manager here?', 'How is success measured for this team over the next year?', and 'What is the biggest challenge this team is working through right now?'

The day before

Review your resume line by line and prepare a two-minute narrative of your career arc. Nutanix interviewers often open with 'Walk me through your background' and use that as the anchor for follow-up questions, so a crisp version matters.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about the team instead of yourself. 'We delivered X' is not a useful answer. The interviewer is evaluating you specifically. Use 'I' deliberately and describe what you decided, said, or changed.

Skipping the result. Many candidates give detailed situation and action context but trail off before the outcome. Always close the loop, even if the project was cancelled or the result was partial. What you learned from a failure is a legitimate and often impressive close.

Being vague about technical details. Saying 'I reviewed the architecture' is weak. Say what you found, what you recommended, and what trade-off you made. Nutanix interviewers typically probe for this kind of depth.

Only preparing one type of story. Candidates who prepare only behavioural answers sometimes struggle when the interviewer pivots to 'how would you build your team's on-call process from scratch?' Prepare a mix of leadership, technical, and process-design stories.

Not knowing Nutanix's products. Coming into an Engineering Manager interview without knowing what AOS, Prism, or Nutanix Cloud Clusters do is a red flag. Spend a few hours on their public product documentation.

Treating the 'questions for us' moment as optional. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare at least three questions, and make one of them specific to the team or product area you are interviewing for.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Nutanix Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates typically report four to six rounds for Engineering Manager roles at Nutanix. This usually includes an initial HR screen, one or two technical or system design discussions, two to three leadership and behavioural rounds, and sometimes a final conversation with a senior director or VP. The exact structure varies by team and the specific role you are applying for.

What salary can I expect as an Engineering Manager at Nutanix in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Engineering Manager roles in India broadly range from 35-60 LPA at the manager level, 55-90 LPA at senior manager level, and 90-150+ LPA at director level. Nutanix is a public company and is publicly reported to position itself toward the higher end of market ranges, though exact offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and your negotiation.

Is there a coding round for Engineering Manager roles at Nutanix?

Candidates report that most Engineering Manager interviews at Nutanix do not include a live coding test. Technical depth is assessed through system design discussions and conversations about past technical decisions your team made. You should be comfortable discussing distributed systems concepts and walking through your team's architectural choices in detail.

How long does the Nutanix hiring process take from first call to offer?

Candidates typically report that the full process takes several weeks, with gaps between rounds while panels are coordinated, especially when US-based hiring managers are involved. Following up after a week of silence is generally considered reasonable. If you have a competing offer with a deadline, mentioning it early and politely is usually the right move.

What is Nutanix's work culture like for Engineering Managers?

Based on publicly reported accounts on platforms like Glassdoor, Nutanix is described as a place with strong engineering values, high ownership expectations, and a relatively direct feedback culture. Engineering Managers are given meaningful autonomy and are expected to be technically credible, not just people managers. Accountability to delivery is taken seriously, and the role involves close coordination with product and customer success teams.
