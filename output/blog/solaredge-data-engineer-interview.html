01 Overview

Overview

SolarEdge is an energy technology company known for smart inverters, power optimizers, and solar energy management platforms. Their India engineering teams handle large-scale IoT telemetry, device performance analytics, and grid data pipelines. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 144 open roles at SolarEdge, making it one of the more active technology hirers in the energy-tech space this year.

Data Engineers at SolarEdge typically work on ingesting real-time data from millions of solar panels and inverters, building pipelines in cloud environments (commonly AWS or Azure), and supporting analytics and ML teams with clean, reliable data. The work is a mix of streaming pipelines for device telemetry and batch pipelines for billing and performance reporting.

Candidates report a structured interview process, typically spanning 3-4 rounds covering coding, system design, and a hiring manager conversation. The process is technical-heavy, with a strong focus on data pipeline reliability, cloud platform depth, and comfort with time-series and IoT data patterns.

  11. What is your experience with dbt, Apache Spark, or similar transformation frameworks? When would you choose one over the other?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a data pipeline you built end-to-end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, our analytics team was running reports directly off the production PostgreSQL database, causing slowdowns during business hours.

*Task:* I was asked to design and build a dedicated reporting pipeline so analytics load would no longer touch the production database.

*Action:* I set up an incremental ELT pipeline using Apache Airflow to extract delta records from the source database, load them into Amazon S3 as Parquet files, and transform them in Amazon Redshift using dbt models. I added data quality checks at each stage using Great Expectations and configured Slack alerts for pipeline failures.

*Result:* Production database CPU load dropped noticeably, the analytics team went from waiting hours for ad-hoc reports to getting results in under a minute, and the pipeline ran reliably for over a year with minimal manual intervention.

---

Q: How do you handle late-arriving events in a streaming pipeline?

*Situation:* We were building a real-time energy consumption dashboard where sensor readings occasionally arrived several minutes late due to network issues on-site.

*Task:* I needed to ensure late data was reflected accurately without invalidating already-computed aggregates shown to customers.

*Action:* I implemented watermarking in Apache Flink with a configurable allowed-lateness window. Events within the window updated existing aggregates. Events beyond the threshold were routed to a separate 'corrections' stream that triggered a lightweight recomputation job. I documented the trade-off between latency and correctness clearly for stakeholders.

*Result:* Dashboard accuracy improved significantly according to internal QA checks, and the corrections pipeline handled edge cases without affecting the real-time path. The team later adopted this pattern as a standard approach.

---

Q: Describe a time you optimized a slow ETL job.

*Situation:* A daily Spark job that processed device log data was running for several hours and consistently missing its SLA window, causing downstream reports to be delayed.

*Task:* I was given ownership of the job to diagnose and resolve the performance issue.

*Action:* I used the Spark UI to identify a massive shuffle caused by a skewed join on device IDs. I added salting to the skewed keys, switched to a broadcast join for the smaller lookup table, and repartitioned the input data to match the cluster's executor count. I also replaced row-by-row writes with bulk Parquet writes.

*Result:* The job runtime dropped dramatically, it consistently met its SLA window, and cluster compute costs decreased because the job required far fewer retries.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For pipeline design questions, follow a 'scope, design, trade-offs' structure. Start by clarifying scale: how many events per second, retention period, and downstream consumers. Then sketch the layers: ingestion, storage, transformation, and serving. Always name the specific tools you would choose and explain why. For example, say 'Kafka over SQS here because we need replay' rather than a vague reference to 'a message queue.' Close by acknowledging the trade-off you made.

For debugging and optimization questions, use an 'observe, hypothesize, fix, verify' pattern. Say what metric told you something was wrong, what root cause you suspected, what you changed, and how you confirmed the fix worked. Avoid vague answers like 'I optimized the query.' Name the specific bottleneck: skew, a missing index, a full table scan.

For system design questions, think out loud about SolarEdge's actual domain: IoT device data, time-series volumes, multi-country deployments. Interviewers appreciate when you connect the design to their business context rather than delivering a textbook answer. Show awareness of cost, not just correctness.

For behavioral questions, keep your STAR answers concise. Cover situation and task briefly so you spend more time on what you did and what changed. Quantify outcomes where you genuinely have the numbers. If you do not have an exact figure, say 'we measured a clear improvement in X' rather than inventing a percentage.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

SolarEdge data engineering interviews typically assess three things above all else.

Domain-aware pipeline thinking. Because SolarEdge's core product generates continuous device telemetry, interviewers look for candidates who naturally think in streams, time windows, and late-data scenarios. Showing familiarity with time-series patterns, even from a different industry, signals you will ramp up faster.

Cloud platform depth. Candidates report that interviewers ask follow-up questions to test whether you have actually operated data infrastructure in the cloud versus simply read about it. Be prepared to discuss specific service choices, failure modes you have personally handled, and cost trade-offs you have navigated.

Data quality ownership. SolarEdge's downstream products, including customer dashboards, billing systems, and grid analytics, depend on accurate data. Interviewers want to see that you treat data quality as a first-class concern, not an afterthought. Mention specific tools or patterns you used: schema validation, row count reconciliation, or anomaly alerting.

Collaboration across teams. Data Engineers at SolarEdge work closely with firmware teams (data producers) and data science or product teams (data consumers). Show that you communicate schema changes proactively and push back constructively when upstream data is unreliable.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical refresh

Revise the fundamentals that consistently appear in SolarEdge Data Engineer interviews: window functions and CTEs in SQL, Spark internals (shuffles, partitioning, caching), and Kafka consumer group mechanics. Practice writing these from scratch without autocomplete, which is the closest simulation to a live interview environment.

Week 2: System design for IoT and time-series

Study how to design pipelines for high-frequency device data. Focus on ingestion patterns (Kafka, Kinesis), time-series storage options (InfluxDB, TimescaleDB, Redshift or BigQuery with partitioning), and serving layers for dashboards. Sketch an architecture for this scenario: ingesting high-frequency readings from millions of devices every few seconds and serving both real-time dashboards and daily batch reports.

Week 3: Domain and company context

Read SolarEdge's publicly available engineering blog posts and investor materials that discuss data platform scale. Look for LinkedIn posts from their India engineering team. Understand what products generate data (inverters, batteries, EV chargers) and what decisions that data drives: warranty claims, yield optimization, and grid compliance.

Week 4: Mock interviews and behavioral prep

Run at least two full mock technical interviews covering system design and SQL. Prepare 4-5 STAR stories covering pipeline reliability, cross-team collaboration, performance optimization, and a time you caught or prevented a data quality issue. Practice saying your answers out loud rather than just mentally rehearsing them.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic pipeline answers. Saying 'I would use a message queue and a data warehouse' without naming tools or explaining choices is the most common reason candidates do not progress past the technical round. SolarEdge interviewers want specifics.

Ignoring the IoT and time-series context. Candidates who treat this as a generic data engineering role miss what makes SolarEdge interviews different. The data is high-frequency, device-keyed, and time-sensitive. Connect every answer to that reality.

Underplaying data quality work. Many candidates focus entirely on pipeline throughput and skip explaining how they validated the data. This is a red flag for teams whose dashboards and billing depend on accurate figures.

Overclaiming on cloud certifications without depth. Having an AWS or Azure certification is a positive signal, but interviewers report that candidates sometimes list cloud services they cannot explain in detail. If you have used only a subset of a platform's services, say so clearly and go deep on what you do know.

Not asking questions at the end. SolarEdge typically reserves time at the end of each round for candidate questions. Arriving with nothing to ask misses an opportunity to show genuine interest in the role and the product.

Weak SQL under pressure. Window functions, ranking, deduplication, and multi-table joins come up frequently. Practice writing these in a plain text editor with no autocomplete.

How many rounds does the SolarEdge Data Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically spans 3 to 4 rounds. These commonly include an initial HR or recruiter call, one or two technical rounds covering SQL, Python, and system design, and a hiring manager round focused on experience and team fit. Some candidates also report a take-home coding or design assignment, though this varies by team and seniority level.

What salary can I expect for a Data Engineer role at SolarEdge in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Data Engineer salaries across the market range from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 14-26 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). For SolarEdge specifically, publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest compensation is competitive within these bands. Your actual offer will depend on your experience, the specific team, and how you negotiate.

Does SolarEdge ask Leetcode-style coding questions for Data Engineer roles?

Candidates report that the coding focus is primarily on SQL (window functions, aggregations, joins) and Python for data manipulation rather than competitive algorithm problems. You may see a practical task like writing a pipeline script or solving a SQL problem on a realistic dataset. Brush up on pandas, PySpark basics, and SQL rather than binary trees or graph traversal.

How important is solar energy domain knowledge for this interview?

You do not need to be a solar energy expert, but showing curiosity about the domain is noticed. Interviewers appreciate candidates who understand that the data comes from physical devices (inverters, power optimizers) and drives real business decisions like warranty claims, yield reports, and customer billing. A basic grasp of why data accuracy and latency matter in that context will set you apart from candidates treating it as a generic role.

What tools and technologies should I focus on for SolarEdge Data Engineer prep?

Candidates report that SQL, Apache Spark, and cloud platforms (primarily AWS and Azure) come up most often. Familiarity with Kafka or a similar streaming platform is valuable given the IoT telemetry context. On the transformation side, dbt knowledge is increasingly mentioned. Prioritize depth over breadth: being able to debug a Spark job or explain Kafka consumer group mechanics in detail matters more than listing many tools you have barely used.
