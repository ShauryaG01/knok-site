01 Overview

Overview

eClerx Services is a KPO (Knowledge Process Outsourcing) firm that delivers analytics, automation, and operations management to global clients in financial services, cable and telecom, and digital marketing. The Technical Program Manager role sits at the intersection of client delivery and internal engineering, making it one of the more demanding positions in the organisation. eClerx currently has 48 open roles on knok, making it one of the more active employers on the platform right now.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report that eClerx TPM interviews typically focus on three areas: delivery ownership, stakeholder management, and cross-functional coordination. The questions below come up frequently across rounds.

  1. Walk us through a program you owned end-to-end, from kickoff to closure. How did you handle scope changes along the way?
  2. eClerx works with clients in regulated industries like financial services and telecom. How do you adapt your delivery approach in those environments?
  3. Describe a situation where two client deliverables clashed on priority and shared resources. How did you resolve it?
  4. How do you define and track program health? What signals tell you a program is at risk before it visibly slips?
  5. Tell us about a time you had to push back on a stakeholder's request. How did you manage it and what was the outcome?
  6. How do you maintain alignment across onshore and offshore teams working across different time zones?
  7. Give an example of a time you had to step into technical detail to unblock your team. What specifically did you do?
  8. How do you identify and mitigate risk in a multi-workstream program?
  9. Tell us about a time a project went significantly off-track. What caused it and how did you recover it?
  10. How do you measure success for a technical program beyond on-time and on-budget delivery?
  11. Describe how you would onboard yourself to a new client engagement in the first few weeks on the job.
  12. What does good stakeholder communication look like to you? Walk us through a reporting cadence you have actually used.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for every behavioral question. Here are three examples tailored to what eClerx interviewers typically look for.

Q: Walk us through a program you owned end-to-end. How did you handle scope changes?

*Situation:* I led a data migration program for a financial services client. Midway through, the client requested additional reporting modules that were not part of the original scope.

*Task:* I needed to assess the impact of the new request without derailing the agreed timeline or budget.

*Action:* I documented the new requirements formally, ran an impact analysis with the engineering leads, and presented two options to the steering committee: phased delivery with the modules added post go-live, or a revised timeline with adjusted resourcing. I also introduced a change control process so all future requests were captured and evaluated before any work began.

*Result:* The client chose the phased approach. The core migration delivered on schedule, and the additional modules shipped in the next phase, which strengthened the relationship heading into the renewal discussion.

---

Q: Describe a time you managed competing priorities across multiple client deliverables.

*Situation:* I was managing three active programs across two clients when both escalated urgent requests in the same week.

*Task:* I had to triage and reallocate team bandwidth without causing delivery failures on either engagement.

*Action:* I mapped all open tasks by business impact and contractual commitment, had honest conversations with both client leads about realistic timelines, and temporarily shifted two engineers from a lower-priority track to cover the critical path. I also increased the standup cadence for that period so blockers surfaced early.

*Result:* Both programs hit their key milestones. One client specifically mentioned the clear communication during the crunch, which contributed directly to the contract renewal discussion that followed.

---

Q: Tell us about a time a project went off-track. What did you do to recover it?

*Situation:* A platform integration project for a telecom client fell behind because of repeated API failures from a third-party vendor.

*Task:* I needed to recover the schedule while managing client expectations and pushing the vendor to resolve the issue.

*Action:* I held a focused technical call with the vendor's engineering team to isolate the root cause, which turned out to be a configuration mismatch in the sandbox environment. My team built a temporary workaround while we ran a parallel track to validate the vendor's fix. I moved from monthly reviews to weekly written status updates to keep the client informed without creating unnecessary alarm.

*Result:* The project delivered within the revised timeline agreed with the client. The structured escalation process also improved our working relationship with the vendor for future engagements.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what the team did), and make the Result concrete wherever possible.

For 'how would you approach' questions, use a simple three-step frame: clarify the problem, outline your plan with priorities, and flag the risks you would watch. eClerx interviewers value structured thinking over memorised frameworks, so explain your reasoning as you go rather than just naming a methodology.

For conflict or stakeholder questions, lead with empathy. Show that you understood the other party's concern before explaining what you did. Interviewers want to see that you can hold a relationship under pressure, not just that you 'won' the argument.

For technical depth questions, be honest about your level. If you delegated a technical decision, explain how you evaluated options with your team and what trade-offs you weighed. TPMs are not expected to be hands-on engineers, but they are expected to ask the right questions and recognise technical risk when they see it.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Delivery ownership. eClerx clients pay for outcomes, not activity. Interviewers want to hear that you own the result, not just the plan. Use 'I' when describing decisions you made, even in team settings, and replace 'we built' with 'I coordinated' or 'I decided' wherever accurate.

Client-facing maturity. A large share of the TPM role involves managing external client relationships alongside internal delivery. Stories that show you navigated a difficult client conversation, or proactively flagged bad news before it escalated, tend to land strongly in these interviews.

Cross-functional coordination. eClerx programs often span business analysts, engineers, and domain SMEs across different geographies. Show that you have held together a distributed team under pressure and that you have a repeatable method for keeping everyone aligned.

Structured communication. Interviewers pay attention to how clearly you speak. Candidates who ramble tend to score lower even when their underlying experience is strong. Practise structuring your answers before you walk in.

Comfort with ambiguity. Clients do not always know what they want. TPMs at eClerx are expected to help define the problem, not just execute against a handed-down spec. Show examples of where you brought clarity to a fuzzy situation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Understand eClerx first. Read recent news, LinkedIn posts from current employees, and Glassdoor reviews before your first round. Focus on which verticals they serve (financial services, cable and telecom, digital marketing) and what kinds of programs their TPMs run. Their public emphasis on automation and analytics tends to come up in interviews.

Build your story bank. Select five to seven experiences from your career that cover: a program you owned end-to-end, a difficult stakeholder situation, a project that went off-track, a time you influenced without direct authority, and a moment where your technical understanding made a difference. Write each in STAR format and practise telling them out loud against a timer.

Do mock interviews. Run at least two practice sessions with a peer or mentor. Record yourself if you can. The goal is to tighten your Situation and Task sections so you have more time for Action and Result, and to cut filler words that dilute your answers.

Prepare questions to ask. Questions about program scale, how success is measured, or how the team is structured signal genuine interest. Specific, informed questions help you stand out from candidates who stay silent at the end of a round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Saying 'we' throughout. Interviewers need to understand your specific contribution. Replace 'we built' with 'I coordinated' or 'I decided' wherever accurate.

Vague results. 'The project was successful' tells the interviewer nothing. Anchor your results to something observable: a client milestone, a renewed contract, a resolved escalation, or a measurable improvement. If you cannot cite a precise figure, describe the outcome in concrete terms.

Over-explaining the Situation. Most candidates spend too long setting the scene. Keep context to two or three sentences so you have enough time for the Action and Result, where the real signal lives.

Skipping the reasoning behind your decisions. eClerx interviewers want to understand how you think, not just what you did. For every major action in your answer, briefly explain the logic that led you there.

Arriving with no questions. Showing up with nothing to ask signals that you have not thought seriously about the role. Prepare two or three specific questions about the team, the client base, or how program success is measured.

Underselling technical involvement. Candidates who position themselves as 'purely process' sometimes lose out to those who show they can engage with engineering teams as peers. If you have relevant technical exposure, make it explicit.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the eClerx TPM interview process typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves three to four rounds. This commonly includes an initial HR or recruiter screen, one or two competency rounds with program management leads, and a final round with a senior stakeholder or business head. Some candidates also report a case study or situational exercise, though this varies by team and business unit, so treat any count as approximate.

Does eClerx ask technical questions in TPM interviews?

Candidates report that eClerx TPM interviews are not heavily technical in the coding sense, but interviewers do probe your ability to engage with engineering teams and understand technical risk. Expect questions about how you have handled technically complex programs, how you work with architects or developers, and how you identify when a technical decision needs escalation. Strong candidates can explain trade-offs clearly without needing to write code.

What domain knowledge does eClerx expect from a TPM candidate?

eClerx primarily serves clients in financial services, cable and telecom, and digital marketing. Prior exposure to one of these verticals is an advantage but is rarely a strict requirement for the TPM role. What interviewers typically look for is a demonstrated ability to ramp up quickly on a new domain and ask the right questions, rather than deep pre-existing expertise in a specific industry.

How should I talk about salary expectations during the process?

Wait for the recruiter to raise compensation first if possible. When asked, give a range based on publicly reported data from sources like Glassdoor or levels.fyi for TPM roles in your city and experience band, rather than anchoring to a single number. Be prepared to justify the range with your years of experience, the scope of programs you have managed, and any specialised domain knowledge you bring.

Is a PMP or formal certification required for the eClerx TPM role?

Candidates report that eClerx does not typically make formal certification a hard requirement for TPM roles, though holding PMP, PRINCE2, or an agile certification can strengthen your profile on paper. Practical experience leading large, cross-functional programs generally carries more weight in the interview than credentials alone. If you are earlier in your TPM career, a certification can help bridge the gap on a competitive shortlist.

What is the work style like for TPMs at eClerx?

Based on publicly available employee reviews and LinkedIn commentary, eClerx TPMs typically manage multiple client programs simultaneously and work closely with both onshore and offshore delivery teams. The pace is generally described as fast, with a strong emphasis on client communication and meeting contractual commitments. Work-from-office expectations vary by location and business unit, so it is worth clarifying the arrangement during the recruiter screen.
