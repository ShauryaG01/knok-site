01 Overview

Overview

Affirm is a US-based fintech company best known for its buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) payment products. It partners with merchants ranging from small e-commerce startups to large enterprise retailers, helping them offer flexible payment options at checkout. A Solutions Engineer at Affirm sits at the intersection of sales and technology. Your day-to-day work involves running technical demos, supporting merchant integrations via Affirm's APIs, troubleshooting implementation issues, and acting as a trusted technical advisor to both merchants and internal teams.

The role is not a pure coding job, but it rewards people who can communicate clearly, think on their feet in customer conversations, and move comfortably between business and technical discussions. Fintech knowledge covering payments, lending, and APIs is a strong advantage going in.

As of July 2026, Affirm listed 191 open roles globally. Across the broader Solutions Engineer market in India, knok job radar data shows 1270 openings nationwide, with Bangalore leading at 55 roles, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). Affirm's India presence is growing, so competition is real but the opportunity is genuine.

The interview process typically spans multiple rounds covering technical ability, customer-facing communication, and cultural fit. Candidates report a mix of behavioral questions, product scenario questions, and light technical assessments involving APIs or basic scripting.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on what candidates report and the nature of the SE role at a fintech like Affirm, here are the questions you should prepare for:

  1. Walk us through how you would onboard a new merchant onto Affirm's payment platform from first call to go-live.
  2. Explain REST APIs to a non-technical business stakeholder who has no engineering background.
  3. A merchant reports that the Affirm checkout widget is not loading on their site. How do you diagnose the issue?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to translate a complex technical problem into a simple recommendation for a business audience.
  5. How would you handle a situation where a merchant wants a feature that Affirm's API does not currently support?
  6. Describe a time you worked closely with a sales or account management team to close or retain a customer.
  7. How do you stay updated on changes in payment technology, BNPL regulations, or fintech trends?
  8. Walk us through a technical demo you have given. How did you tailor it to the audience?
  9. A merchant's integration breaks after an API version update. What steps do you take to help them resolve it quickly?
  10. Tell me about a time you had to push back on a customer request because it was not technically feasible or went against product direction.
  11. How do you prioritize when multiple merchants are simultaneously escalating urgent integration issues?
  12. What do you know about Affirm's business model, and how does the Solutions Engineer contribute to merchant success?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A merchant's integration breaks after an API version update. What steps do you take?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a payments platform, we rolled out a new version of our checkout API. A mid-sized retail merchant reported that their checkout flow had stopped working the morning after the update.

*Task:* I was the SE on the account and needed to diagnose the issue, keep the merchant calm, and restore their checkout with minimal revenue impact.

*Action:* I reproduced the error in a sandbox environment using their API credentials and request logs. I found that the merchant's integration was still sending a deprecated 'order_id' field format that the new API version no longer accepted. I shared a clear diff of what had changed, sent a corrected code snippet in their language (Node.js), and stayed on a call while their developer tested the fix. I also flagged internally that our release communication had missed this merchant segment.

*Result:* The merchant was back online within two hours. I used the incident to propose a clearer API changelog distribution process, which the team adopted for the next release.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you translated a complex technical problem into a clear recommendation for a business audience.

*Situation:* A large e-commerce partner's VP of Finance was worried about why their Affirm approval rates were lower than expected in a specific product category.

*Task:* I needed to explain the underlying credit decision logic without disclosing proprietary model details, and recommend steps the partner could actually act on.

*Action:* I prepared a one-page visual showing how order value, customer profile signals, and return rates typically influence approval decisions in BNPL products. I framed everything in their business metrics: conversion rate, average order value, and customer satisfaction, with no technical jargon. I recommended they pilot Affirm on lower-ticket items first before rolling it out to higher-ticket categories.

*Result:* The partner ran a controlled pilot over several weeks and saw improved approval rates in that segment. They then expanded the Affirm integration to two more product categories the following quarter.

---

Q: How do you handle a situation where a merchant wants a feature Affirm does not currently support?

*Situation:* While supporting a fast-growing D2C brand, the merchant's CTO requested real-time refund webhooks with a custom payload format that our system did not support at the time.

*Task:* I had to manage their expectations honestly while keeping the relationship intact and finding a workable interim solution.

*Action:* I acknowledged their need clearly, explained what our webhook system could and could not do, and showed them how to use our existing refund events as a workaround with a small adapter on their side. I also filed a detailed product feedback request internally, including the merchant's use case and the potential business impact. I kept the merchant updated regularly on roadmap progress.

*Result:* The merchant ran successfully on the workaround. Several months later, improved webhook flexibility shipped as part of a broader API update. I notified the merchant directly and helped them migrate, deepening their trust in Affirm as a long-term partner.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for behavioral questions in SE interviews. Keep Situation and Task tight (one or two sentences each), put more depth into Action to show how you think, and close with a concrete Result.

Structured diagnosis for technical scenarios. For troubleshooting or API debugging questions, walk through a clear process out loud: confirm the problem, isolate variables, test a hypothesis, fix and verify, then close the loop with the customer. Narrating your thinking shows interviewers you are methodical under pressure, not just reactive.

Business model first for product knowledge questions. When asked about Affirm's business or how the SE role creates value, frame your answer around what Affirm earns (merchant fees, consumer interest on longer-term plans), what merchants gain (higher conversion, larger basket sizes), and where the SE drives those outcomes (faster merchant activation, fewer integration failures). This signals commercial awareness alongside technical skill.

Acknowledge, clarify, redirect for objection questions. When a merchant pushes back or asks for something you cannot deliver, first validate the concern genuinely, then ask a clarifying question to find the root cause, then respond with an alternative path. Never dismiss an objection. Interviewers watch for empathy as closely as they watch for problem-solving.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Affirm SE interviewers are typically looking for four things.

Technical competence without being purely a coder. You should be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding HTTP request and response cycles, and working through integration issues step by step. You do not need to be a full-stack engineer, but you need to hold a credible conversation with a merchant's developer team.

Clear, empathetic communication. The SE role is built on relationships. Interviewers watch for how you simplify complex ideas, how you handle pushback, and whether you make the customer feel heard even when delivering difficult news.

Commercial awareness. Understanding why Affirm's product matters to merchants (conversion uplift, improved checkout experience) and how the SE role contributes to revenue tells interviewers you think beyond the support ticket.

Ownership and follow-through. Candidates who show they see a problem all the way through, from diagnosis to resolution to internal feedback, stand out. Affirm values people who treat merchant success as their own responsibility, not just a task to be closed.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the product and the company first.
Read Affirm's publicly available developer documentation. Understand the checkout integration flow, how the API works at a high level, and what a merchant needs to go live. Look at Affirm's recent public announcements (partnerships, product updates, earnings calls where available) so you can speak confidently to where the company is headed. Be ready to explain what BNPL means for both merchants and consumers in plain terms.

Prepare your stories next.
Map your past experience to the 12 questions listed above. Write out STAR answers for at least six of them. Prioritize the troubleshooting story, the cross-functional collaboration story, and the 'explaining tech to a non-tech audience' story. These come up most often in SE interviews at product-led fintechs.

Practice out loud in the final stretch.
Record yourself answering two questions per day. Listen back for filler words, unclear explanations, and whether your Result is specific enough. Ask a friend or peer to play the role of a skeptical merchant and practice handling objections in real time.

Before each round: Reread the job description, check Affirm's latest news, and prepare two or three genuine questions for your interviewer about the team's current priorities or the biggest integration challenges merchants face.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too technical or too vague. SE interviews reward balance. Going too deep into code alienates business-focused interviewers. Being too vague about your technical process makes engineering-leaning interviewers nervous. Read the room and calibrate to whoever is asking.

Not knowing Affirm's product. Candidates who cannot explain BNPL or who confuse Affirm's model with a generic payment gateway signal that they skipped their homework. Spend real time on the public developer docs before any round.

Weak Results in STAR answers. 'The merchant was happy' is not a result. Be specific: 'back online within two hours', 'expanded to two more product categories', 'reduced support escalations for that account'. If you do not have a precise figure, describe the business impact in concrete terms.

Ignoring the relationship angle. Some candidates focus entirely on the technical fix and forget to mention how they communicated with the merchant throughout the process. Interviewers at Affirm want to see the relationship-building mindset alongside the debugging skill.

Asking no questions at the end. This is a missed opportunity. Prepare genuine questions about the team, the merchant portfolio, or the product roadmap. It signals curiosity and initiative, both of which matter for a role where you represent the company to customers every day.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Affirm hire Solutions Engineers based in India?

Affirm has been growing its India presence across engineering and business functions. As of July 2026, Affirm listed 191 open roles globally across all functions. Check Affirm's careers page directly for India-specific openings, as the role mix and locations change frequently depending on team headcount needs.

What technical skills does an Affirm Solutions Engineer need?

You need comfort with REST APIs, including reading documentation, understanding request and response formats, and basic debugging. Familiarity with web technologies at a working level is also expected. Candidates report that practical API troubleshooting and the ability to read code matter more than deep algorithmic problem-solving. You do not need to be a senior software engineer, but you must hold a credible conversation with a merchant's developer team.

How many interview rounds does the Affirm SE process typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes an initial recruiter screen, one or two technical or scenario-based rounds, and a final round that often includes cross-functional interviewers from sales or product. The exact structure can vary by team and hiring manager. Prepare for both behavioral and technical scenario questions in every round, since the format is not always predictable.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at Affirm in India?

Affirm does not publicly disclose India-specific compensation bands for SE roles. For current estimates, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi, where candidates occasionally share compensation data. Publicly reported figures for SE roles at US-origin fintechs in India vary widely by experience level, so use multiple sources and treat any single data point with caution.

Is LeetCode-style competitive coding tested in the Affirm SE interview?

Candidates report that the Affirm SE interview is not typically a competitive coding interview. You are more likely to face API scenario questions, integration troubleshooting walkthroughs, and technical communication exercises. Basic scripting or code-reading may appear in some rounds, but the focus is on problem-solving approach and customer communication rather than algorithmic complexity.

How can I stand out among other SE candidates at Affirm?

Hands-on experience with payment or SaaS API integrations gives you a strong edge, as does a demonstrated track record of managing customer relationships through technical issues. Coming in with genuine knowledge of Affirm's product and preparing specific stories from your past work, rather than generic answers, makes a meaningful difference. Thoughtful questions for your interviewers at the end of each round also leave a positive impression.
