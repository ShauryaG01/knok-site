01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Whatfix is a Bengaluru-based digital adoption platform (DAP) company that helps enterprises train and onboard their workforce on the software they use every day. It serves large clients across the US, Europe, and Asia Pacific, which means the bar for quality and communication is enterprise-grade across all teams.

As of mid-2026, Whatfix has 36 open roles spanning engineering, product, sales, marketing, and customer success. This is a meaningful pipeline of opportunities, and the company hires across experience levels, from early-career professionals to senior leaders.

The hiring process is structured but not bureaucratic. If you understand what the company sells, why enterprises buy it, and how your role fits into that picture, you are already ahead of most applicants.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The stages below are based on publicly reported candidate experiences and may vary by role, team, and seniority.

Stage 1: Application and Resume Screening
Your application goes through an applicant tracking system before a recruiter reviews it. Tailoring your resume to the specific job description, using keywords from the posting, improves your chances of clearing this stage. Generic resumes rarely convert.

Stage 2: Recruiter Screening Call
If shortlisted, a recruiter will reach out for a brief introductory call. This is conversational: they will confirm your background, current location, notice period, and salary expectations. Be honest and concise. This call sets the tone for the rest of your process.

Stage 3: Hiring Manager Round
This is the first substantive interview. The hiring manager will explore your past experience in depth, ask situational or behavioural questions, and gauge your understanding of the role. For technical roles, there may be light technical questions here as well.

Stage 4: Technical or Skills Assessment
Engineering candidates typically face a coding round or a system design discussion. Non-technical candidates in sales, marketing, or customer success may receive a case study or a presentation task. The goal is to see how you approach real problems, not just recall facts.

Stage 5: Final Panel or Leadership Round
A final round with senior stakeholders or a cross-functional panel covers strategic thinking, culture fit, and long-term goals. Some candidates report a separate HR conversation at this stage to align on compensation and a start date.

The full process commonly takes a few weeks from first contact to offer, depending on the role and team availability.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Whatfix sells a complex SaaS product to large enterprises, which raises the bar for communication and stakeholder management across all functions, not just sales.

Engineering roles: Strong fundamentals in your core stack, experience building or maintaining SaaS products, and comfort working with distributed systems. Frontend experience with React or Angular and backend experience with Node.js or Java are commonly cited in publicly reported job descriptions.

Sales and GTM roles: A clear track record of building pipeline or closing deals, preferably in B2B SaaS. Candidates who have navigated long enterprise procurement cycles or sold into large accounts stand out. Metrics matter here, but so does the story behind them.

Customer success and implementation roles: The ability to manage multiple accounts simultaneously, a consultative approach to problem-solving, and comfort working with data and dashboards. Clients often need support through complex implementations, so patience and clarity are valued.

Across all roles: Whatfix values people who can operate with some ambiguity, learn the product fast, and collaborate across time zones. Many clients are based in the US and Europe, so cross-cultural communication is part of the job.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product first. Look up Whatfix demos, case studies, and product walkthroughs before your first interview. Candidates who can speak to specific use cases, such as how a bank might use a digital adoption platform to onboard employees onto a new CRM, make a much stronger impression than those who treat the company as a black box.

For technical roles: Revisit core data structures, algorithms, and system design concepts relevant to your stack. Practice explaining past projects in terms of business impact, not just technical decisions. Interviewers want to know what you built and why it mattered.

For GTM roles: Prepare structured stories for your key deals and initiatives using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Have your key metrics ready, but always contextualise them with the size of the team, the market, and the time period.

Know the competitive landscape. Whatfix competes with companies like WalkMe, Pendo, and UserGuiding. Being able to articulate what differentiates Whatfix in the market signals genuine research and curiosity.

Prepare smart questions. Questions about team priorities, how success is measured in your first quarter, or current product challenges show that you are thinking like someone who already wants to contribute. Avoid questions you could easily answer with a quick search.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Whatfix describes itself as customer-obsessed and data-driven, and this shows in how teams operate. Decisions are expected to be grounded in data, and customer feedback loops heavily into product and delivery priorities.

The culture rewards ownership. You are expected to take end-to-end responsibility for your work, surface blockers early, and communicate proactively rather than wait to be asked. People who prefer close direction often find this environment uncomfortable; people who enjoy autonomy tend to thrive.

The Bengaluru headquarters has a hybrid work culture, and certain roles are open to remote arrangements. Cross-functional collaboration is common, and teams work closely with counterparts in the US and Europe.

Growth opportunities are real. Publicly reported employee reviews on Glassdoor note that high performers move into expanded roles relatively quickly. The company is still in a growth phase, which means scope tends to expand as the business scales.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does Whatfix currently have?

As of the latest data from knok jobradar (updated July 2026), Whatfix has 36 open roles across functions including engineering, product, sales, and customer success. The mix changes regularly, so it is worth checking a job aggregator or the company careers page to see what is live right now.

Does Whatfix hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

Whatfix hires across experience levels, including early-career candidates for certain engineering, marketing, and customer success roles. However, most open roles tend to require some prior experience in a relevant function. If you are a fresher, look specifically for roles labelled 'associate', 'junior', or 'graduate' and tailor your application accordingly.

How long does the full hiring process take at Whatfix?

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, the process from application to offer commonly takes a few weeks, though this varies by role and team. Technical roles with multiple assessment stages may take longer than business roles. Responding quickly to each stage and staying in touch with the recruiter helps keep things moving.

What is the best way to stand out in a Whatfix interview?

Come prepared with a solid understanding of what a digital adoption platform does and why enterprises invest in one. Concrete examples from your past work, framed in terms of business impact rather than just technical or process detail, consistently stand out. Asking sharp, specific questions about the team and the role also leaves a strong impression.

Is salary negotiable at Whatfix?

Like most companies, Whatfix has pay bands for each role, but there is typically some room to negotiate, especially at mid-senior levels. Having a clear sense of your market value, based on publicly reported ranges on Glassdoor or levels.fyi for your role and experience level, gives you a stronger position. Come prepared with a specific number and a rationale, not just a vague ask for 'more'.

What notice period does Whatfix typically expect from candidates?

This varies by role and level. For senior hires, a longer transition is commonly accepted; for junior to mid-level roles, a shorter one is often preferred, though this is usually negotiable depending on team need. The recruiter will ask about your notice period early in the process, so be upfront about it from the start rather than raising it at the offer stage.
