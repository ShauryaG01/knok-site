01 Overview

Overview

TravClan is a B2B travel technology platform that connects travel agents with hotels, flights, and holiday packages through a single marketplace. A Business Analyst here typically works on problems like agent onboarding funnel analysis, booking drop investigations, revenue reporting, and product feature prioritization alongside the product and engineering teams.

As of July 2026, knok's job radar shows TravClan has 53 open roles listed on the platform. Candidates report that the interview process typically spans multiple rounds: an initial call with HR or a recruiter, a technical round testing SQL and analytical thinking, and one or two business and behavioural rounds with senior managers or the hiring team.

The role demands solid SQL skills, comfort reading business data, and the ability to communicate findings clearly to both technical and non-technical stakeholders. Given TravClan's B2B focus, interviewers typically probe your understanding of marketplace dynamics, travel agent behaviour, and core travel industry metrics.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a data-driven project where your analysis directly influenced a business decision.

*Situation:* The sales team at my previous company believed that onboarding travel agents in Tier-2 cities was not worth the effort because average booking values were lower than in metro cities.

*Task:* I was asked to validate or challenge this assumption using the past year's transaction data.

*Action:* I pulled booking data and segmented agents by city tier. Instead of looking only at average booking value, I calculated booking frequency, total annual revenue per agent, acquisition cost, and churn rate. I built a simple comparison table to show the full picture side by side.

*Result:* The data showed that Tier-2 agents had higher booking frequency and lower churn, making their lifetime value comparable to metro agents. The sales leadership revised the expansion strategy and increased Tier-2 outreach in the following quarter.

---

Q: A travel partner reports a sudden drop in bookings. How would you investigate this?

*Situation:* At a previous role in a travel platform, a hotel partner flagged a noticeable drop in bookings over a two-week window.

*Task:* I was responsible for identifying the root cause and presenting findings to the account management team within two days.

*Action:* I structured the investigation in layers. First, I checked whether the drop was platform-wide or specific to this partner. Then I examined traffic to the partner's listings, conversion rates at each step of the booking funnel, and price competitiveness against similar properties. Finally, I checked whether any product or ranking changes had gone live during that window.

*Result:* A recent ranking update had deprioritised the partner's listings because of a data quality issue in their inventory feed. The engineering team fixed the feed, and bookings recovered to normal levels shortly after.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you simplified a complex analysis for a non-technical audience.

*Situation:* I had built a multi-variable demand forecasting model to identify underserved travel regions, but the leadership team reviewing the output did not have a data background.

*Task:* I needed to present the findings in a way that led to a clear budget decision, not confusion.

*Action:* I stripped out the statistical methodology and focused on three takeaways: which regions showed the highest unmet demand, what revenue opportunity each represented, and the single recommended action. I used simple bar charts, walked through one region as a worked example first, and then showed the full regional picture.

*Result:* The leadership team approved the regional expansion budget in that meeting without requesting a follow-up session, which my manager noted was an unusually fast decision for a strategic initiative.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions: Use the STAR structure for every answer. Keep your Situation brief (one or two sentences of context). Spend most of your time on Action (the specific steps you personally took) and Result (a measurable or observable outcome). Avoid saying 'we' during the Action part; say 'I' so your contribution is clear.

For analytical and SQL questions: Start by restating what the query or analysis needs to return. Explain your approach before writing code. Walk through the logic out loud, including which tables you would join, how you would handle duplicates or nulls, and what the output should look like. A sample SQL structure for ranking agents by total booking revenue:

`sql
SELECT
agent_id,
agent_name,
SUM(booking_value) AS total_revenue,
AVG(booking_value) AS avg_booking_value
FROM bookings
GROUP BY agent_id, agent_name
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
`

For prioritization questions: Acknowledge that multiple stakeholders have valid needs, then use a simple impact-vs-effort comparison or a scoring rubric. Always tie your final recommendation back to a business goal. For TravClan, that goal is likely agent retention, GMV growth, or partner satisfaction.

For business case questions: Follow this structure: problem statement, current state and its cost, proposed solution, expected benefit (even if qualitative), risks, and a recommended next step. You do not need exact numbers to make a strong case; showing that you know what to measure is often enough.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

SQL and data fluency. TravClan runs on booking data. Interviewers want to see that you can write clean queries, interpret results correctly, and spot data quality issues without being prompted. Practice joins, aggregations, window functions, and filtering on nulls.

Structured thinking. When given an open-ended problem like 'why did bookings drop', interviewers are watching how you break it down. They want a logical, layered approach, not a single guess. Think out loud and show your framework before jumping to an answer.

Domain awareness. You do not need deep travel industry experience, but you should understand what a B2B marketplace looks like. Know the difference between a travel agent, a supplier (hotel or airline), and the platform connecting them. Understand basic marketplace metrics: GMV, take rate, conversion rate, and agent activation rate.

Communication and stakeholder comfort. BAs at TravClan typically work with product managers, engineers, and senior business leaders. Interviewers want evidence that you can adjust your communication style for different audiences and that you are comfortable pushing back on assumptions with data.

Ownership and follow-through. Candidates report that TravClan interviewers respond well to answers that show initiative beyond the immediate task: checking whether a fix actually worked, following up with stakeholders, or flagging risks before being asked.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Company and domain research.
Read everything publicly available about TravClan: their product, their B2B model, and recent news. Understand who their customers are (travel agents) and who their suppliers are (hotels, airlines, package providers). Write down five business problems a BA at TravClan might work on daily.

Week 2: SQL practice.
Practice on a dataset that resembles a marketplace: transactions, customers, and products. Focus on GROUP BY aggregations, multi-table JOINs (inner and left joins), window functions like RANK and running totals, and NULL handling. Solve several new problems each day and time yourself.

Week 3: Behavioural preparation.
Write out STAR answers for each of the twelve questions listed above. Use actual projects from your own experience. Rehearse them out loud, not just in your head. Keep each answer under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock rounds and review.
Ask a friend or mentor to run a mock interview using the questions above. Practice explaining SQL queries verbally while writing them. Review your weak areas from earlier weeks. Also prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about the team, the data stack, and how success is measured in the BA role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague answers without outcomes. Saying 'I improved the process' is weak. Tie your Result to a specific, observable change even if you cannot share exact figures.

Not knowing the travel or B2B domain. Candidates who treat TravClan like a generic tech company typically miss important context in business and product questions. Know what GMV, take rate, and agent activation mean before you walk in.

Writing SQL without explaining your logic. Interviewers care as much about how you think as the final query. Always talk through your approach before writing code.

Accepting the problem statement without questioning it. If an interviewer says 'bookings dropped, what would you do', the best first move is to ask clarifying questions: over what time period, which segment, compared to what baseline? Jumping straight to a solution signals shallow thinking.

Forgetting to close the loop in STAR answers. Many candidates describe what they did but leave out the Result. End every behavioural answer with a clear outcome.

Over-engineering the answer. Candidates report that TravClan BA interviews value clear and practical thinking over complex models. A simple, well-explained analysis beats a sophisticated one you cannot defend under follow-up questions.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the TravClan BA interview typically have?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds: an initial screening call with HR or a recruiter, a technical round focused on SQL and analytical problem-solving, and one or two business and behavioural rounds with a senior manager. The exact structure can vary by team, so confirm the process with your recruiter when you schedule the first call.

What SQL topics should I prepare for a TravClan BA interview?

Focus on GROUP BY and aggregation functions, multi-table JOINs on booking and agent tables, window functions like RANK and running totals, and filtering on NULLs. Expect at least one question that asks you to rank or segment agents by a business metric like revenue or booking frequency. Practice writing and explaining queries out loud, not just on paper.

Is prior travel industry experience required?

Deep travel industry experience is not a hard requirement, but you should understand the basics of a B2B marketplace: what a travel agent does, how the platform connects agents to suppliers, and what metrics matter such as GMV, conversion rate, and agent retention. Candidates who walk in without this context typically struggle on product and business case questions.

How important are communication skills compared to technical skills?

Both matter for this role. Interviewers consistently include questions about stakeholder situations, presenting to leadership, and handling conflicting priorities, which signals that communication is not secondary to SQL. A candidate who is strong technically but weak at storytelling is less likely to clear the full interview loop than someone who is solid on both.
