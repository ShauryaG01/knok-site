01 Role Overview

Role Overview

A Technical Program Manager (TPM) is the person responsible for driving large, cross-functional engineering projects from idea to launch. Unlike a regular Project Manager, a TPM is expected to read design documents, question architectural choices, and understand trade-offs in technical decisions. They own the roadmap, clear blockers, coordinate across multiple engineering teams, and make sure complex products ship on time and within scope.

In India, the TPM role has grown steadily at product companies, Global Capability Centres (GCCs), and large tech firms. As of July 2026, knok job radar tracked 313 open TPM roles across India. Demand is concentrated in a few cities:

If you enjoy bringing order to complexity, can read a design document and spot a gap, and communicate equally well with engineers and business leaders, TPM is a strong career to pursue.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Technical foundation is non-negotiable. You do not need to write production code every day, but you must understand how software is built. This means comfort with system design concepts, APIs, databases, cloud infrastructure, and agile delivery processes. Recruiters consistently look for engineers who have crossed over, not MBAs who have picked up some technical vocabulary.

Programme management skills are the second pillar. You need to run sprints, write project charters, manage risk, track milestones, and report status clearly to both engineers and senior leadership. Tools like Jira, Confluence, and Asana appear regularly in TPM job descriptions across India.

Communication and stakeholder management are what separate a good TPM from a great one. You will often be the only person in a meeting who understands both the technical constraints and the business pressure. Writing a crisp one-pager, running a focused meeting, and saying 'no' diplomatically are core skills.

Data fluency is increasingly expected. Being able to define success metrics, read dashboards, and work with data teams to instrument features is a common ask at product companies and GCCs.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

  1. Start from a technical role. Almost every TPM job description in India expects prior engineering experience. If you are currently an engineer, you are closer than you think. If you are in a non-technical role, building coding skills first is the practical path before attempting the switch.
  1. Take on coordination work in your current team. Volunteer to run sprint ceremonies, write the team's weekly status update, or coordinate a release. This is how most TPMs in India got started: they were engineers who gravitated naturally toward project coordination.
  1. Get certified in programme management. Study for a PMP (Project Management Professional) or a Google Project Management Certificate. Agile and Scrum certifications (CSM, PSM) are well-recognised at product companies in India. Certifications signal intent and teach a common vocabulary recruiters look for.
  1. Study system design seriously. Even if you are not coding daily, you need to understand distributed systems, scalability trade-offs, and architectural patterns. Books like 'Designing Data-Intensive Applications' and structured system design courses help build this foundation.
  1. Make an internal move first. Many engineers make the TPM switch within their own company. Your manager already knows your technical credibility, and you get to build programme management skills in a familiar environment. This is the lowest-friction path available to most people.
  1. Build a portfolio of cross-functional wins. Document 2-3 projects where you drove alignment across teams, resolved a technical blocker, or delivered a complex feature end to end. These stories become the backbone of every TPM interview.
  1. Target GCCs and product companies in the right cities. Bangalore, Delhi, Pune, and Hyderabad have the most active TPM hiring based on current data. GCCs of global tech companies often have structured TPM tracks and hire at multiple levels of seniority.
  1. Prepare for TPM interviews. Expect a mix of system design rounds, behavioural questions in STAR format, and programme management scenarios. Common questions include 'walk me through a project you led end to end' and 'how do you handle two senior engineers who disagree on architecture?'
04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

Month 1-3: Assess your starting point. If you are already a software engineer with hands-on experience, you have a strong base. Identify honest gaps: have you run a project end to end? Do you understand system design well enough to push back on an architectural decision? Write down specific answers before you start.

Month 3-6: Learn and signal intent. Enroll in a PMP or Google PM certificate course. Start volunteering for coordination tasks at work. Begin a structured system design study plan. Update your LinkedIn profile to reflect your TPM direction so recruiters can find you.

Month 6-12: Get real experience in your current role. Take on delivery manager or associate PM responsibilities, even informally. Run one full release cycle. Write a post-mortem for a project. Document your contributions clearly so you can talk about them in interviews.

Year 1-2: Make the formal switch. Apply internally first. If your company does not have a TPM track, start applying externally. Target roles with titles like 'Technical Delivery Manager', 'Engineering Programme Manager', or 'Associate TPM'. These are practical entry points into the function.

Year 2-4: Build depth as a TPM. Once you land the role, focus on progressively larger programmes. Learn to manage dependencies across multiple teams simultaneously. Start mentoring junior engineers informally and build your internal reputation as someone who gets things done.

Year 4 and beyond: Senior TPM and leadership. Senior TPM roles typically involve org-wide programme ownership, hiring input, and influencing product strategy. Compensation at this level is publicly reported on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, and varies significantly by company tier, city, and negotiation.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

The IIT and NIT advantage is real, but not a barrier. Many TPM job descriptions at top companies mention a preference for candidates from premier institutes. However, GCCs and mid-size product companies hire widely from tier-2 engineering colleges too, especially if you have strong experience and certifications to back it up.

Both Naukri and LinkedIn are necessary. In India, Naukri still drives a large share of recruiter searches, especially outside Bangalore. LinkedIn is essential for product company and GCC roles. Keep both profiles updated with keywords like 'programme management', 'cross-functional delivery', 'roadmap', and 'agile'. Recruiters search by these terms every day.

The GCC opportunity is underrated. Global Capability Centres of major tech and finance companies hire significant numbers of TPMs in India. These roles often follow global career ladders, and compensation is publicly reported on Glassdoor and levels.fyi as competitive. Bangalore and Hyderabad are the primary GCC hubs for TPM roles.

Communities worth joining. PM School and LinkedIn groups like 'Product Management India' have active TPM discussions. The PMI India chapter runs events that are useful for meeting practising programme managers. Local chapters in Bangalore and Delhi are the most active.

On the MBA question. Many professionals in India wonder if an MBA is needed to become a TPM. The honest answer: it is not required, and the majority of practising TPMs on LinkedIn India do not have MBAs. An MBA from a top institution can open doors at companies with formal leadership programmes, but a strong engineering background combined with certifications and demonstrated experience is the more common path.

Read JDs carefully. Many TPM roles on Indian job boards are actually project coordinator or delivery manager roles with a rebranded title. A real TPM role will mention system design, architectural reviews, or technical roadmap ownership. If the JD only mentions status reports and vendor calls, it is probably not a technical programme management role.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need to be a software engineer to become a TPM in India?

Almost all TPM job descriptions in India expect prior engineering experience, and the most direct path into the role is from a software engineering background. Candidates from non-technical backgrounds do make the switch, but they typically need to demonstrate technical credibility through certifications, side projects, or open-source contributions. The bar varies considerably by company tier and the seniority of the specific role.

Which certifications actually help for a TPM role?

PMP (Project Management Professional) is widely recognised across India and adds credibility with recruiters. Agile certifications like CSM (Certified Scrum Master) or PSM are valued at product companies. Google's Project Management Certificate on Coursera is a good, affordable starting point for those early in the journey. Certifications alone will not get you hired, but they signal commitment and give you a structured framework to discuss in interviews.

Which cities in India have the most TPM opportunities right now?

Based on knok job radar data from July 2026, Bangalore has the most open TPM roles (41), followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), and Hyderabad (12). Chennai has 5 openings and Mumbai has 1. Bangalore gives you the most options, but Delhi and Pune are worth considering if you are already based there or prefer those cities.

How is a TPM different from a Product Manager?

A Product Manager owns the 'what' and 'why': features, user needs, and product strategy. A TPM owns the 'how' and 'when': execution, timelines, cross-team coordination, and delivery. In practice the roles overlap at smaller companies, but at larger tech firms and GCCs they are distinct functions that work closely together. Many TPMs transition into PM roles later in their careers, making it a useful stepping stone as well.

What does a TPM interview look like at Indian companies?

Expect a mix of system design rounds, behavioural questions in STAR format, and programme management scenarios. Common questions include 'walk me through a project you drove from start to finish' and 'how would you handle a missed milestone?' Some companies include a written case study or a take-home exercise involving a mock project plan. GCCs of global firms often mirror their global interview process closely.

Is an MBA necessary to become a TPM in India?

No, an MBA is not required for most TPM roles in India. The majority of practising TPMs have engineering degrees, not MBAs. An MBA from a top institution like IIM or ISB can be an advantage at companies running formal leadership programmes, but strong engineering experience combined with PM certifications and a track record of leading cross-functional projects is the far more common path into the role.
