01 Overview

Overview

Nablon AI is an AI-focused company with 6 open positions right now, and Solutions Engineer is one of the most cross-functional roles on the team. A Solutions Engineer sits at the crossroads of sales and technical delivery: you are the person who demos the product, runs proof-of-concept (POC) projects with prospects, answers technical objections, and helps new customers start seeing value quickly. You also act as a key feedback channel between customers and the product team.

The interview process for this role typically spans multiple conversations. Candidates report a recruiter or HR screen first, followed by one or more scenario-based or technical rounds, and a final discussion with a hiring manager or senior leader. Ask your recruiter to confirm the exact stages for your pipeline, as Nablon AI may adjust the process based on current team needs.

What separates strong candidates is the ability to do three things well: simplify complex AI concepts for non-technical buyers, handle objections without dismissing them, and treat every customer interaction as a genuine partnership. Interviewers are watching for all three throughout the process.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would handle a discovery call with a prospect who is sceptical about AI-driven solutions.

*Situation:* At my previous company, I was assigned a discovery call with a logistics firm whose procurement lead had just seen a failed AI pilot at a competitor. He opened by saying, 'We have been burned before and need proof, not promises.'

*Task:* My goal was to understand their real pain points, rebuild some trust in the category, and qualify whether our product was a genuine fit.

*Action:* I started with open questions about what had gone wrong previously, rather than launching into a demo. I listened carefully and reflected back what I heard: long integration timelines, unclear success metrics, and a vendor that had overpromised. I then proposed a small, time-bound POC focused on a single workflow they cared about, with a clear success criterion agreed upfront. I also looped in our implementation team early so the prospect could speak with them directly.

*Result:* The call ended with a scheduled POC instead of a polite 'we will think about it.' The prospect later told our account executive that my not rushing the demo was the reason they agreed to move forward.

---

Q: Tell us about a proof-of-concept you ran that ran into trouble. How did you recover it?

*Situation:* I was running a POC for a fintech company when their IT team changed the data access policy midway through, which meant the test environment no longer reflected realistic conditions.

*Task:* I had to keep the POC on track without access to production-like data, while maintaining the customer's confidence.

*Action:* I flagged the issue to the customer's internal champion immediately rather than working around it silently. We agreed on a short extension and I worked with our engineering team to generate synthetic data that closely matched their schema. I also used the opportunity to document the integration requirements precisely, which proved useful when the full deployment began.

*Result:* The POC completed successfully. The customer's CTO said that my transparency during the data issue gave them more confidence in us than the demo itself would have on its own.

---

Q: A prospect asks for a capability your product does not have. How do you handle that conversation without losing the deal?

*Situation:* During a final evaluation call with a healthcare company, they asked for a specific compliance report format that our product did not generate out of the box. The room went quiet.

*Task:* I needed to be honest about the gap while keeping the evaluation alive.

*Action:* I acknowledged the gap directly: 'That specific format is not something we support today.' I then asked clarifying questions to understand whether it was a hard requirement or a preference. It turned out they had an internal reporting team who could transform our output. I logged the requirement formally, walked them through our roadmap process so they could see how customer requests get prioritised, and connected their technical lead with our product manager for a separate call.

*Result:* The deal moved forward. The customer appreciated the honesty and the direct line to the product team. The feature also made it onto the roadmap for a later release.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for behavioural questions. Keep each element proportional: spend most of your time on Action and Result, since those show what you actually did and what it achieved. A concise Situation and Task setup is enough context.

For technical explanation questions, use the 'known to unknown' approach: start from something the listener already understands, draw an analogy, then layer in the technical detail. For example, you might explain a vector database to a sales director by comparing it to 'a filing system that finds similar documents instead of exact matches.'

For objection-handling questions, a three-part structure works well: acknowledge the concern genuinely, ask a clarifying question to understand the root of the objection, then respond with evidence or a proposed next step. Resist jumping to the rebuttal before you fully understand what is worrying the prospect.

For metrics and success criteria questions, anchor your answer to a business outcome, not just a technical metric. 'Model accuracy improved' is weaker than 'the improvement meant the customer's team spent significantly less time on manual review each week.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without arrogance. Nablon AI interviewers want to see that you can hold your own in a conversation with a customer's engineering or data science team. You do not need to be a researcher, but you need to understand AI concepts well enough to answer follow-up questions honestly and confidently.

Sales awareness. Solutions Engineers are not just demo presenters. Interviewers look for candidates who understand deal stages, can qualify a prospect, and know when to loop in an account executive versus handling a conversation themselves.

Communication clarity. This role involves translating between technical teams and business buyers. Candidates who can do that fluently, adjusting depth and vocabulary to the audience, stand out clearly.

Customer empathy. The best SEs treat every customer interaction as a chance to understand a real problem. Interviewers notice when candidates talk about customers purely as revenue targets. Show that you genuinely care about the customer's outcome.

Structured thinking. When given an open-ended scenario, interviewers want to see a clear thought process, not a stream of consciousness. Pause, structure your answer, then speak.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Research Nablon AI thoroughly. Understand what their product does, which industries they serve, and what problems they solve. Look for any publicly available case studies or product announcements. Know how their offering differs from common alternatives a prospect might consider.

Prepare your STAR stories. Build at least five stories covering: a difficult customer situation, a technical explanation you gave to a non-technical audience, a POC or project that did not go perfectly, a time you said no to a customer request, and a result you are proud of. Write each out in STAR format and practise telling them aloud until the structure feels natural without sounding rehearsed.

Practise demo delivery. Even without access to Nablon AI's product, practise delivering a short, focused pitch for a product you know well, tailored to a specific customer pain point. This shows you understand how to structure a demo around value rather than features.

Prepare thoughtful questions for your interviewer that show you have done your homework. Questions about how the SE team collaborates with the product team, what a typical POC cycle looks like, or what the most common technical objections are tend to lead to strong conversations.

On the day, arrive with concrete examples and specific outcomes where you have them. Vague answers like 'I helped improve the process' are far weaker than specific ones that describe what changed and why it mattered. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping discovery before the demo. Many SE candidates describe jumping straight to features when answering a scenario question. Strong SEs ask questions first. Demonstrate that habit in your answers.

Being too technical for the room. If the interviewer is from HR or is a business-side hiring manager, a heavily technical answer can work against you. Read the room and adjust your depth and vocabulary accordingly.

Taking all the credit. Solutions Engineers work closely with account executives, product managers, and implementation teams. Interviewers notice if you make it sound like you did everything alone. Be clear about your specific contribution and acknowledge your teammates.

Not anchoring answers in outcomes. Saying 'the POC went well' is not as strong as describing what the customer decided to do next and why. Use real outcomes wherever you have them, and be honest when you do not.

Ignoring the AI context. Nablon AI is specifically in the AI space. If your answers could apply to any software company, they are not specific enough. Show that you understand the particular dynamics of selling AI products: customer scepticism, governance concerns, and the need for explainability.

Trailing off without a Result. STAR answers that end after the Action leave interviewers with an incomplete picture. Always close the loop with what actually happened.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Nablon AI Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report somewhere between two and four rounds. These commonly include a recruiter or HR screen, a scenario or role-play round, a technical assessment, and a final conversation with a hiring manager or senior leader. The exact number varies, so ask your recruiter for the current structure when you schedule your first call. Nablon AI currently has 6 open Solutions Engineer positions, so the team may be running parallel hiring tracks.

Is there a technical test or coding round for this role?

Solutions Engineer roles typically lean toward scenario-based and communication assessments rather than pure coding tests. You may be asked to explain a technical concept, walk through a system architecture, or deliver a product demo. Some companies include a light technical screen to confirm you can hold your own in engineering conversations, but candidates report it is rarely a heavy coding exercise for SE roles.

What AI knowledge do I need to interview well for this role?

You need working familiarity with AI and machine learning concepts: what models do, how training and inference differ, common use cases, and key limitations. You do not need to build models from scratch. Being able to explain concepts like vector search, fine-tuning, or LLM hallucination clearly to a business audience is far more valuable here than deep mathematical expertise.

How should I prepare if I come from a non-AI background?

Lead with your SE fundamentals: discovery, demo delivery, objection handling, and POC management. Supplement with self-study on AI basics using publicly available courses and documentation. Candidates who are honest about what they are still learning and show genuine curiosity about AI tend to progress further than those who overclaim expertise they do not have.
