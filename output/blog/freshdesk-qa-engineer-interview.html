01 Overview

Overview

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions appear repeatedly in Freshdesk QA Engineer interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. 'Walk me through how you would test a new ticketing feature end-to-end.' Interviewers want structured thinking, not just a list of test cases.
  1. 'How do you approach API testing? Which tools have you used?' Freshdesk is API-heavy, so expect a detailed discussion on REST methods, status codes, and tools like Postman or RestAssured.
  1. 'How do you handle regression when the team ships new builds every sprint?' This checks whether you understand CI/CD pipelines and can design maintainable regression suites.
  1. 'How would you test a multi-channel support feature, where a ticket comes in through email, chat, and WhatsApp at the same time?' This is specific to the Freshdesk product context and comes up frequently.
  1. 'A critical bug is found one hour before release. What do you do?' This tests your judgment, not just your process knowledge.
  1. 'How do you decide which test cases to automate first?' They want to hear ROI-based thinking, not just technical skill.
  1. 'Describe a time you disagreed with a developer about whether something was a bug or expected behavior. How did you resolve it?'
  1. 'What metrics do you use to measure release quality?' Think defect escape rate, automation coverage, and test execution time.
  1. 'How would you test a webhook integration for a third-party CRM connector?'
  1. 'You inherit a flaky and slow test suite. How do you stabilise it without blocking the team?'
  1. 'How do you write a test plan when requirements are still changing?'
  1. 'What is your experience with performance or load testing a SaaS product with many concurrent users?'

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would test a new multi-channel ticketing feature.

*Situation:* In my previous role, we launched a feature that let customers raise support tickets via WhatsApp, in addition to email and chat.

*Task:* I was responsible for writing and executing the full test plan before the beta release.

*Action:* I started by mapping all entry points: a customer sends a WhatsApp message, it should appear as a ticket in the agent dashboard with correct metadata including channel, contact number, and timestamp. I wrote test cases for happy paths first, then edge cases including duplicate messages, attachments, special characters, and network interruptions mid-send. I also tested fallback behavior when WhatsApp was unavailable. I used Postman to verify API payloads at each step and coordinated with the developer to confirm expected behavior for every edge case.

*Result:* We caught three critical bugs before beta. One where attachments were silently dropped, one where contact numbers were stored without a country code, and one race condition that created duplicate tickets. All three were fixed before launch.

---

Q: Describe a time you disagreed with a developer about a bug.

*Situation:* During a sprint review, I flagged that the 'Merge Tickets' feature did not preserve the original ticket's creation timestamp after merging.

*Task:* The developer said this was by design. I needed to either accept that or make a case for changing it.

*Action:* Instead of arguing in the moment, I checked the product spec and found no mention of this behavior. I then pulled several support tickets from our own team's backlog where customers had reported data discrepancies caused by exactly this issue. I shared that evidence at the next standup with the product manager present.

*Result:* The team agreed it was a bug. It was fixed in the same sprint, and the product manager updated the spec to explicitly cover timestamp behavior for all future merge operations.

---

Q: How do you decide which test cases to automate first?

*Situation:* When I joined my previous company, the QA team had a large backlog of manual test cases and no automation in place.

*Task:* I had to build an automation suite from scratch with a small team and limited bandwidth.

*Action:* I scored every test case on three factors: how often it ran in regression cycles, how long it took to execute manually, and how stable the underlying feature was. High-frequency, time-consuming tests on stable features got automated first. I used Selenium with Java for UI tests and RestAssured for API tests, and presented the prioritised list to the team lead before writing a single line of code.

*Result:* We automated the highest-priority test cases over a few months. Regression time dropped significantly, and the very first automated run caught two regressions that would have gone undetected in a manual sweep.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For scenario-based questions, use the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep your Situation brief, under 30 seconds. Spend most of your time on Action, because that is where your skill shows.

For testing approach questions, use a 'Scope, Strategy, Execute, Report' flow. Start by clarifying what is in scope. Then explain your testing strategy covering types of tests, tools, and environments. Describe your execution order. Finally, explain how you would communicate results to the team and stakeholders.

For bug severity and priority questions, always tie your answer to business impact first, then technical severity. At a customer support company like Freshdesk, framing your answer around 'agent productivity' or 'customer experience' will land better than a purely technical answer.

For estimation and prioritisation questions, show your reasoning out loud. Interviewers are not just checking your final answer. They are checking whether your thinking is structured, transparent, and easy for a team to follow and trust.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Product context awareness. Freshdesk QA engineers test software that support agents and customers rely on every day. Interviewers want to see that you have thought about what 'quality' means in that specific context, not in abstract or generic terms.

Structured thinking over memorised answers. When you get a scenario question, asking clarifying questions before diving in signals good judgment. Jumping straight to test cases without understanding scope is a consistent red flag that candidates report being called out on.

Automation maturity. At mid-level and above, the expectation is that you can write and maintain automation code, not just run scripts someone else wrote. Be ready to discuss your framework choices and the reasoning behind them, not just the tool names.

Collaboration signals. QA at a product company involves constant back-and-forth with developers, product managers, and customer success teams. Answers that show you can communicate bugs clearly and negotiate priorities diplomatically will stand out from candidates who describe testing in isolation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: product and fundamentals. Sign up for Freshdesk's free trial and use it as a support agent. Raise tickets, merge them, assign them, test the email and chat channels. Understanding the product will make your answers specific instead of generic, and interviewers notice the difference immediately.

Week 2: technical prep. Revise API testing concepts including REST methods, status codes, authentication headers, and JSON payloads. Practice writing test cases for core Freshdesk features like the ticket lifecycle, SLA rules, and automations. Review your automation framework, especially design patterns like Page Object Model if you work with Selenium.

Week 3: behavioural prep. Write out three to five STAR stories from your past experience. Cover: catching a critical bug, disagreeing with a developer, handling unclear requirements, and improving a broken testing process. Practice saying them out loud or record yourself to check that your answers are clear and concise.

Day before. Re-read the job description and map each listed requirement to one of your STAR stories. Prepare two or three questions to ask the interviewer about the team's current testing challenges. Candidates report that this signals genuine interest and turns the interview into a two-way conversation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Describing what QA is instead of what you did. When asked 'how do you handle regression testing,' your answer should come from personal experience, not a textbook definition. Interviewers at Freshdesk are evaluating your judgment, not your vocabulary.

Not asking clarifying questions. In scenario questions, jumping straight to an answer without understanding scope signals poor judgment. Interviewers expect you to ask things like 'is this a web app or mobile?' or 'what is the expected concurrent user load?'

Ignoring the product context. Generic answers about 'any application' fall flat at a product company. Connect your answers to customer support scenarios wherever you can, even if the question is framed broadly.

Underselling automation experience. If you have written automation code, talk about the framework, the design choices, and the business outcome it delivered. Saying 'I used Selenium' without more detail leaves the interviewer with nothing to evaluate.

Skipping the result in STAR answers. Many candidates describe the Situation and Action in detail but forget to close the loop. Always end with a measurable or observable outcome so the interviewer can assess the actual impact of your work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Freshdesk QA interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes an initial HR screening, one or two technical rounds, and a final managerial or culture-fit discussion. Technical rounds usually cover manual testing concepts, automation, and scenario-based problem solving. The exact number of rounds can vary by role seniority and the specific team hiring. Always confirm the process structure with your recruiter at the start.

Is coding or scripting tested in the Freshdesk QA interview?

For mid-level and senior roles, candidates commonly report being asked to write or review automation code, such as a Selenium script or an API test using RestAssured. For entry-level roles, the focus is more on manual testing fundamentals, but basic programming comfort is still a positive signal. Brush up on Java or Python depending on what you have listed in your resume, and be ready to walk the interviewer through your code logic step by step.

What tools or technologies should I know before applying?

Candidates report that Postman for API testing, Selenium for UI automation, and JIRA for bug tracking come up frequently in interviews. SQL knowledge is also useful because testers often need to verify data at the database level. If the job description mentions a specific tool like Appium for mobile testing or JMeter for performance testing, treat that as a strong signal to prepare it. Always tailor your tool prep to what the specific job description actually lists.

What is the salary range for a QA Engineer at Freshdesk?

Based on publicly reported data from Glassdoor and levels.fyi, QA Engineer salaries in India range from 4-9 LPA at entry level, 9-17 LPA at mid level, and 17-30 LPA at senior level. Lead roles are commonly cited in the 28-45+ LPA range. Freshdesk, as a well-funded product company, is generally considered competitive within these bands, though individual offers vary based on your experience, the specific role, and how well you negotiate.

How do I prepare for Freshdesk-specific scenario questions?

The most practical step is to use the Freshdesk product before your interview. Sign up for their free trial and explore it from an agent's perspective: raise tickets, test automations, check SLA rules, and use the multi-channel inbox. This gives you concrete examples to reference when interviewers ask product-specific questions. Candidates who can say 'I actually tried this in your product and noticed X' consistently stand out from those who give generic answers.

Does Freshdesk hire remote QA Engineers?

Most Freshdesk QA roles are based out of Chennai and Bangalore, which are Freshworks' primary engineering hubs. Some candidates report hybrid arrangements being available depending on the team, but fully remote QA positions are less common than on-site or hybrid ones. Check each individual job listing carefully for the stated work mode before applying, as policies can differ by team and change over time.
