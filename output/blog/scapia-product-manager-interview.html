01 Overview

Overview

Scapia is a travel-focused fintech startup best known for its co-branded travel credit card offering zero forex markup and reward points on travel bookings. The company has grown steadily since its launch and currently has 8 open roles listed, with product management being a central hiring priority.

The PM interview at Scapia typically spans three to five rounds: a recruiter screening, one or two product thinking rounds, a cross-functional or leadership conversation, and sometimes a session with a senior leader for more senior positions. Candidates report that the process leans heavily on product sense, comfort with metrics, and genuine knowledge of the Indian credit card and travel market.

Scapia operates in a competitive space where product decisions connect directly to revenue and user trust, so interviewers typically push you to defend your reasoning at every step. Expect questions that test your understanding of Indian fintech alongside standard PM problem-solving patterns.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A key product metric drops sharply over one week. Walk us through how you would diagnose and respond.

*Situation:* At my previous company, weekly active users on our payments app fell noticeably shortly after a routine app update.

*Task:* I was responsible for identifying the root cause and presenting a recovery plan to the product and engineering leads within two days.

*Action:* I first confirmed whether the drop was real or a tracking issue, then segmented by platform (Android vs iOS), user cohort (new vs returning), and geography. The drop was concentrated among new users on a single onboarding screen that had a UI change in the update. I coordinated with engineering to roll back that screen within hours, then ran a post-mortem with design and QA to introduce a regression checklist for any future onboarding changes.

*Result:* The metric recovered within three days. The checklist also caught a similar issue in a later release before it went live.

---

Q: How would you design a referral programme for Scapia to grow its card base?

*Situation:* At a previous role I led a referral redesign for a lending product. The existing programme had low conversion because rewards were delayed and referrers had no visibility into whether their friends had acted.

*Task:* My goal was to redesign the programme to increase referral-driven activations without inflating cost per acquisition.

*Action:* I mapped the referral journey and identified three friction points: no in-app status update for the referrer, a single delayed reward instead of a tiered one, and a sharing flow that required too many steps. I proposed a tiered reward (a smaller credit on application, a larger one on first transaction), real-time status visibility in the app, and a WhatsApp-native sharing option with a pre-filled message. I worked with marketing to set the reward values and with engineering on the tracking layer.

*Result:* Within the quarter after launch, referral-driven activations grew substantially with no increase in fraud-related chargebacks, validating the tiered model.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you shipped a product with incomplete data.

*Situation:* We were building a travel insurance add-on for our card product and had very limited data on user willingness to pay at different price points.

*Task:* I needed to recommend a launch price and decide whether to run a broad A/B test or start with a limited rollout.

*Action:* I used industry surveys and publicly reported benchmarks from comparable insurance products to form a baseline hypothesis. I proposed a single-city pilot with a clearly defined success metric agreed with the business team upfront. I documented all assumptions explicitly so stakeholders knew we were validating a hypothesis, not confirming a known fact.

*Result:* The pilot gave us real data within six weeks. The opt-in rate was lower than our hypothesis, which led us to revise the pricing before a national launch. The product launched successfully with the adjusted pricing.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions (such as 'improve the rewards experience') work best when you open with the user segment you are solving for, state the problem clearly, offer two or three solutions with honest trade-offs, recommend one, and name the metrics you would watch after launch.

Metric drop questions follow a reliable diagnostic sequence. Confirm the data is accurate and not a tracking bug, then segment by platform, user type, and time. Identify whether the problem is in acquisition (fewer users entering a flow) or conversion (users dropping inside a flow). Form a specific hypothesis before proposing a fix, not after.

Prioritisation questions respond well to a simple value-vs-effort view. State your criteria upfront (user impact, revenue potential, strategic fit, engineering cost), apply them to the options on the table, and be ready to explain why you weighted each criterion that way for a startup at Scapia's stage.

Cross-functional conflict questions are behavioural. Use the STAR format and make sure your 'Action' shows that you listened and understood the other person's constraints before pushing your view. Candidates report that interviewers respond well to stories where alignment happened without escalation.

Market and strategy questions expect India-specific knowledge. Know the Indian credit card landscape: co-branded cards, zero forex markup as a differentiator, how travel rewards programmes compare across issuers, and where digital-first fintech products typically lose users in onboarding. Generic frameworks without local context tend to land poorly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have spoken with Scapia interviewers report a consistent theme: the company wants PMs who think like owners, not executors. Interviewers typically probe your reasoning two or three levels deep, so prepare to defend every assumption you make.

Fintech fluency matters more here than in a generalist PM role. Know how credit cards work in India (credit limits, MDR, forex markup, the collections cycle), why Indian users choose one card over another, and where digital-first card products typically lose applicants in the onboarding funnel.

Data comfort is a recurring signal. Interviewers want to see that you know which metrics matter for a credit card product (activation rate, transaction frequency, spend per user, churn) and that you can reason through metric trade-offs without waiting for someone to tell you what to track.

Cross-functional communication comes up often because Scapia's teams are small. Stories that show you unblocked a team, simplified a complex spec, or made a call that saved engineering time tend to resonate strongly.

Genuine product curiosity is noticed. Interviewers can tell when a candidate has actually used the Scapia app and thought critically about it. Arrive with a specific observation ('I noticed the rewards screen works this way, and I would consider this alternative') rather than surface-level praise.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product inside out. Download and use the Scapia app. Map every screen in the onboarding, card management, and rewards flow. Note what works well and what feels incomplete. Read publicly available coverage of Scapia from 2024 to 2026 to understand how the product has evolved.

Week 1 (parallel track): Build your fintech baseline. Understand how co-branded credit cards work in India, what zero forex markup means for a user, and how Scapia earns revenue through interchange and partnerships. This context will make your answers feel grounded rather than generic.

Week 2: Practice structured answers. Run through each question in the section above. Use STAR for all behavioural questions. For product design questions, force yourself to name the user segment before jumping to solutions.

Week 2 (parallel track): Prepare your metrics story. Pick two or three metrics you would track for Scapia's core product (such as activation rate, monthly spend per user, or referral conversion) and be ready to explain why those and not others.

Before each round: Check Scapia's LinkedIn page for recent hires and any new product announcements. Walk through the app one more time so your observations are current.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the user segment. Candidates often jump straight to features. Interviewers typically expect you to define who you are solving for before proposing anything.

Generic competitive analysis. Saying 'Scapia should focus on user experience' without naming specific competitors or concrete product trade-offs signals that you have not studied the market.

Ignoring the business model. PMs who only discuss user delight without connecting features to revenue or retention tend to score lower. Show that you understand how Scapia monetises and where unit economics constrain product decisions.

Mechanical frameworks with no judgment. Citing a prioritisation framework is fine, but interviewers push back if you apply it without contextualising it for a fintech startup at Scapia's stage. Judgment matters more than the framework itself.

Weak behavioural answers. Vague stories ('I collaborated with the team') do not land. Interviewers want specific actions you took, specific decisions you made, and specific outcomes that followed.

Not asking questions. Candidates who do not ask anything at the end of a round are often seen as less curious. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team, current roadmap challenges, or a product problem Scapia is actively working through.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Scapia PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to five rounds: a recruiter screening, one or two product thinking rounds, a cross-functional or leadership conversation, and sometimes a session with a senior leader for higher-level roles. The exact structure can vary by role level, so confirm the format with the recruiter before each round. Candidates for senior positions report additional strategy or founder-level conversations.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Scapia?

Scapia is a growth-stage startup, so compensation is competitive with the broader market. Based on knok's salary data, PMs with 3-6 years of experience typically see offers in the 24-40 LPA range, while senior PMs can expect 40-60 LPA. Actual offers depend on your specific experience, the role level, and the stock or ESOP component, which is often a meaningful part of total compensation at this stage of company.

Does Scapia give a take-home assignment or case study?

Some candidates report receiving a product case or take-home exercise, typically before or after the first product thinking round. These cases usually involve improving an existing Scapia feature or designing a new one. If you receive one, treat it seriously: structure your answer clearly, state your assumptions upfront, and specify which metrics you would track to know whether the feature worked.

How important is prior fintech experience for a Scapia PM role?

Fintech or payments experience is a strong positive signal but not a hard requirement. Candidates from adjacent domains such as ecommerce, travel, or consumer apps have also cleared the process. What interviewers typically look for is whether you can demonstrate understanding of how credit card products work in India and whether you have genuinely engaged with Scapia's product. Curiosity and learning speed are assessed as much as prior titles.

How do I stand out in the Scapia PM interview?

Arrive with specific, thought-through observations about the Scapia app: a flow that could be smoother, a feature that seems underused, or a metric you think they should track more closely. Interviewers notice when a candidate has genuinely used the product versus memorised prep material. Pairing a sharp product observation with a clear 'here is how I would approach improving it' tends to leave a strong impression.

What is the best way to handle a metric drop question in the interview?

Start by questioning whether the data is accurate before assuming the drop is real. Then segment by platform, user cohort, and time period to narrow the cause. Form a specific hypothesis, propose a diagnostic action rather than an immediate fix, and describe what recovery would look like and how you would measure it. Interviewers at fintech companies like Scapia typically value candidates who separate diagnosis from solution and resist the urge to jump straight to fixing something they do not yet fully understand.
