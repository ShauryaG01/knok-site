01 Overview

Overview

InMobi is one of India's leading ad-tech companies and the creator of Glance, a lock-screen content platform reaching hundreds of millions of Android users. As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 2009 open PM roles across India, with InMobi alone carrying 91 active openings. If you are targeting a PM role here, expect questions that blend product sense, data analysis, and the ability to navigate a two-sided marketplace serving both advertisers and publishers.

InMobi's interview process typically covers three broad areas: how you think about product (design, prioritization, strategy), how you use data (metrics, experiments, diagnosis), and how you operate in cross-functional teams (stakeholder alignment, influence without authority). Candidates report the process is rigorous but conversational, with interviewers following up on every answer, so live thinking matters as much as polished delivery.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These 12 questions come up repeatedly in candidate reports for InMobi PM interviews. They skew toward mobile advertising, Glance's content platform, and cross-functional leadership.

  1. How would you improve InMobi's advertiser platform to increase revenue per campaign?
  2. Design a new feature for Glance that increases daily active users among tier-2 city users in India.
  3. Walk me through a product you launched. What was the outcome and what would you do differently?
  4. How do you prioritize features when engineering bandwidth is limited and multiple stakeholders are pushing competing roadmap items?
  5. InMobi operates in mobile advertising and content. How do you measure the success of a new ad format?
  6. A key metric drops sharply overnight. Walk me through how you investigate and respond.
  7. How would you define and segment InMobi's users across its advertiser and publisher sides?
  8. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a senior stakeholder and how you handled it.
  9. Design an experiment to test a new pricing model for InMobi's self-serve ad platform.
  10. How would you set OKRs for InMobi's Glance team for the next quarter?
  11. What is one global trend in ad-tech or content that InMobi should be watching, and how should they respond as a product team?
  12. Describe a product failure you owned. What did you learn and how did you apply that learning?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a product you launched. What was the outcome and what would you do differently?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a push notification feature sending the same message to all users regardless of activity level, leading to high opt-out rates.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the notification system to improve retention without significantly increasing engineering cost.

*Action:* I ran user interviews to understand which notifications users found valuable. I worked with data science to build a simple segmentation model, splitting users into high, medium, and low engagement tiers. We personalized notification timing and content for each tier, then ran an A/B test over four weeks before rolling out.

*Result:* Opt-out rates fell and day-7 retention improved per our internal dashboards. If I were to do it again, I would have involved the customer success team earlier since they had direct user feedback that would have shortened our discovery phase.

---

Q: A key metric drops sharply overnight. Walk me through how you investigate.

*Situation:* At a fintech startup, weekly active users dropped sharply one Monday morning.

*Task:* As the PM on growth, I needed to diagnose the cause quickly and coordinate a response.

*Action:* I started by checking whether the drop was real or a data pipeline issue. Once confirmed real, I broke it down by platform (Android vs iOS), geography, and user cohort. The drop was concentrated among new users on Android in tier-2 cities. Cross-referencing with release notes, I found a UI change had gone out the previous Friday. I escalated to engineering immediately and we prepared a hotfix.

*Result:* We rolled back the change within hours and the metric recovered. I then set up automated alerts to catch similar drops within hours rather than days.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a senior stakeholder.

*Situation:* My VP wanted to deprioritize our mobile web experience and focus entirely on the app because app users had higher lifetime value.

*Task:* I believed this would cut off a large segment of new users who discovered us via mobile web before downloading the app.

*Action:* I pulled attribution data showing that a notable portion of our best app users had their first touch on mobile web. I presented this with a simple funnel diagram in our roadmap review, framing it as 'protecting the top of our acquisition funnel' rather than opposing the VP's direction.

*Result:* The VP agreed to a reduced but maintained investment in mobile web. It reinforced for me that disagreements land better when framed around shared goals rather than personal preference.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions, use a simplified version of CIRCLES: Clarify the goal, Identify users, List use cases, Cut to priorities, Evaluate tradeoffs, Summarize. For InMobi specifically, always tie your answer back to advertiser ROI, publisher yield, or user engagement on Glance. Do not design features in a vacuum.

For metric investigation questions, use a structured diagnostic tree: check data integrity first, then segment by platform, geography, and user type, then cross-reference with recent releases or external events. Candidates who jump straight to 'it must be a bug' without checking the data pipeline tend to score lower.

For prioritization questions, use a simple impact-vs-effort grid or RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort). Show that you can say no to good ideas when bandwidth is limited. InMobi interviewers, per candidate reports, value ruthless prioritization over long feature lists.

For OKR and goal-setting questions, anchor objectives to business outcomes such as revenue, retention, or market share. Make key results measurable and time-bound. Avoid vanity metrics like total downloads unless you can connect them to a revenue or retention outcome.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on candidate reports, InMobi PM interviewers typically look for four things.

Business context awareness. Candidates who understand InMobi's two-sided business model (serving both advertisers and publishers) and how Glance differs from a typical content app score higher. Know the difference between CPM, CPC, and CPA before walking in.

Structured thinking. Interviewers want to see you break problems down step by step. Jumping to a solution without stating your assumptions is a commonly cited reason for rejection.

Data fluency. Expect to define metrics, design experiments, and interpret hypothetical results. You do not need to be a data scientist, but you should be comfortable with funnels, cohort analysis, and A/B testing concepts.

Influence without authority. InMobi has cross-functional teams where PMs do not manage engineers or designers directly. Candidates who can show how they aligned stakeholders without direct authority tend to do well in leadership rounds.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product.
Use Glance on an Android device if you have one. Read publicly available news about InMobi's ad platform and Glance's growth strategy. Understand their advertiser and publisher value propositions. Write down three things you would change about any InMobi product and be ready to defend your reasoning.

Week 2: Practice structured answers.
Pick eight of the questions in this guide and write out STAR answers for each. Time yourself, aiming for two to three minutes per answer. Record yourself if possible to catch filler words and unclear structure.

Week 3: Mock interviews and metrics.
Do at least two mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Focus one session on product design and one on analytical questions. Review core ad-tech metrics: fill rate, eCPM, DAU, retention cohorts, and LTV. You do not need to memorize specific numbers, but you should be able to explain what each metric signals and how it connects to business health.

Week 4: Final polish.
Prepare three to four questions to ask your interviewer about the team and roadmap. Review InMobi's current open roles to understand where the company is investing. Confirm your own salary expectations against publicly reported PM salary ranges in industry surveys before the offer stage.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Not knowing InMobi's business model. Many candidates treat this like a generic PM interview. InMobi serves advertisers and publishers, not just end users. If you cannot explain how InMobi makes money in two minutes, you will struggle on at least half the questions.

Jumping to solutions. Skipping the 'clarify and define' step makes answers feel shallow. Interviewers want to see your thinking process, not just your conclusion.

Vague metrics. Saying 'engagement improved' is not enough. Always state what you would measure and how you would measure it, even in a hypothetical.

Ignoring the mobile context. InMobi is mobile-first. If your product design answers assume a desktop-first user, you signal a poor fit for the team.

Over-rehearsed answers. Candidates report that InMobi interviewers follow up on every point. If your STAR stories are memorized but not genuinely lived, follow-up questions will expose the gaps quickly.

Not asking questions. Failing to engage with the interviewer at the end signals low interest in the role. Prepare genuine questions about the team's roadmap and how success is measured in the first few months.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the InMobi PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report four to six rounds, including an initial HR screen, one or two product sense rounds, an analytical or case round, and a leadership or culture-fit round. Some senior roles include a take-home assignment before the final round. The exact structure can vary by team and level, so ask your recruiter to confirm after your first call.

Is prior ad-tech experience required to get a PM role at InMobi?

It is not always required, but candidates with ad-tech or mobile platform experience tend to find the process smoother. If you are coming from a different domain, invest extra time learning InMobi's business model and the basics of mobile advertising (CPM, CPC, fill rate) before your first round. Demonstrating genuine curiosity about the space can offset a lack of direct experience.

Will I be asked to write SQL or code during the interview?

For PM roles, candidates rarely report being asked to write code. However, SQL or basic data analysis questions do appear in some analytical rounds, particularly for roles with a strong data focus. Being able to write a simple SQL query and interpret results is a safe skill to have, even if it does not come up every time.

What salary range should I target for a PM role at InMobi?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for PM roles with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PM, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PM. Glassdoor and industry surveys suggest InMobi's compensation is broadly in line with market rates for Bangalore-based product roles. Always verify current ranges with your recruiter before and during the offer stage.

How long does the full interview process typically take?

Candidates report the process typically takes three to six weeks from first round to offer, though timelines vary by role seniority and team urgency. Staying in touch with your recruiter after each round is a good practice. If you have not heard back within a week of completing a round, a polite follow-up is appropriate.

Is there a take-home case study or assignment?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home product case, usually for senior or principal PM roles. It typically involves analyzing a product problem and presenting a structured recommendation. Candidates are usually given a few days to complete it, and the submission is followed by a live Q and A session with the interview panel.
