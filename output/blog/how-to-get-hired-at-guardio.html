01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Guardio is a cybersecurity company that protects everyday internet users from phishing attempts, malware, and scam websites through a browser security extension. Headquartered in Tel Aviv, Israel, Guardio has built a product used by millions of people globally. As of July 2026, the company has 25 open roles across engineering, data science, product, and growth functions.

While most positions are based in Israel, Guardio does list remote-friendly openings from time to time, particularly in engineering and data. If you are a security-minded developer, a data professional comfortable with large-scale behavioral data, or a product thinker who cares about protecting regular users, Guardio is worth your attention.

The team is compact, which means each hire carries real weight. The interview process reflects that: Guardio moves carefully, evaluates fit seriously, and wants people who genuinely believe in the mission, not just people looking for any well-paying job.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Guardio typically runs through four to five stages. The structure below is based on publicly shared candidate experiences and general patterns for Israeli cybersecurity product companies of this size.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. The recruiter or hiring manager reviews your profile. A resume tailored to the specific role matters here. Guardio is a product-focused security company, so highlighting experience with browser technologies, security tooling, or consumer-scale products helps you stand out immediately.

Stage 2: Recruiter screening call. If your profile gets attention, expect a short call with someone from the people or talent team. This is a fit and logistics check covering your background, what you are looking for, and a brief intro to the role. Have a concise summary of your career ready and be clear about your timeline and location flexibility.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment. For engineering roles, this is usually a take-home assignment or a live coding round. Guardio's product is built around browser behavior, web security, and large-scale data analysis, so questions lean toward these areas. For non-engineering roles, expect a case study or a task tied to the function you are applying for.

Stage 4: Technical deep-dive interviews. One or two sessions with senior engineers or team leads. Expect a mix of system design, problem-solving, and domain questions. Security concepts, thinking about scale, and product awareness are common threads across roles.

Stage 5: Final conversation. A discussion with the hiring manager, a team lead, or occasionally a founder. This stage focuses on values fit, how you think about the problem space, and your approach to working in a small, fast-moving team. Questions about past decisions and trade-offs you have navigated are common here.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Guardio is protecting real users from real threats, and the team values people who take that mission seriously. A few qualities that come up consistently in what the company looks for:

Security and product instinct together. Guardio wants people who think about both dimensions. If you have worked on consumer-facing products and understand how regular users interact with software (not just developers), that is a strong signal.

Strong fundamentals. For engineering roles, core computer science skills matter. Data structures, algorithms, system design, and browser internals such as JavaScript and browser extension APIs are particularly relevant given what Guardio builds.

Curiosity about the threat landscape. You do not need to be a red-team specialist, but showing genuine interest in how phishing campaigns are structured, how malware spreads through browsers, or how attackers operate will help you stand out from equally qualified candidates.

Ownership mindset. Guardio is not a large enterprise with layers of approval. People are expected to own their work, raise issues early, and move without heavy supervision. Past examples of shipping something end-to-end, or solving a hard problem independently, resonate well in interviews.

Clear communication. The team is distributed and cross-cultural. Writing clearly, explaining your thinking as you go, and being direct without being dismissive are qualities interviewers actively watch for.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product first. Install the Guardio browser extension, use it for a week, and notice what it does well and where you see room to improve. Speaking specifically about the product rather than security in the abstract separates serious candidates from bulk applicants.

Brush up on browser and web security basics. Topics like cross-site scripting, phishing detection methods, browser extension architecture, and content security policies come up in technical rounds. You do not need to be an expert in all of them, but familiarity shows you have done your homework.

Prepare ownership stories. For every stage from the recruiter call onward, have specific examples ready: a problem you identified without being asked, a product or feature you shipped, a difficult trade-off you made under pressure. The STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) works well for structuring these.

Research the competitive landscape. Guardio operates alongside antivirus companies, browser-native security tools, and enterprise endpoint products. Knowing where Guardio fits, what makes their approach different, and who their core users are will help you ask sharp questions and give sharper answers.

Tailor your resume to the specific role. Guardio reviews applications carefully and a generic resume is easy to pass over. Highlight skills, tools, and experiences that map directly to the job description. For data roles, show scale. For engineering roles, show security or browser-related work.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Guardio operates with a clear sense of purpose: protecting regular internet users who are not technical enough to protect themselves. That mission shapes the culture in visible ways.

User obsession over technical elegance. The team is proud of its engineering, but the north star is always whether ordinary users are actually safer. Simple, effective, widely adopted features are celebrated over cleverly over-engineered solutions.

Small team, high trust. Engineers and analysts are expected to own their domains, raise concerns early, and move fast. The autonomy is real, and so is the accountability that comes with it.

Direct feedback culture. Israeli tech companies are generally known for directness, and Guardio fits that pattern. Feedback in interviews and on the job tends to be specific and frank. If you prefer heavily indirect communication styles, it is worth being aware of this before you join.

Mission before prestige. People who thrive at Guardio tend to be motivated by the problem itself. If you want to work on a product that has a measurable impact on real users every day, Guardio is a strong fit.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Guardio hire remotely for candidates based in India?

Guardio is headquartered in Israel and most roles are on-site or hybrid in Tel Aviv. That said, select engineering and data roles have been posted as remote-friendly in the past. Check each job description carefully for the location policy. If a role is listed as remote, it is worth applying and clarifying timezone expectations during the recruiter screening call.

How long does the Guardio hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on publicly shared candidate experiences, the process commonly runs a few weeks from first contact to offer, though it can stretch longer for senior or specialized roles. Guardio is a relatively small company, so decisions tend to move faster than at large enterprises. Following up politely after each stage is acceptable and generally appreciated by the team.

What kind of technical questions should I expect in a Guardio interview?

For engineering roles, expect a mix of coding challenges, system design questions, and security-relevant topics. Browser internals, JavaScript, and large-scale data handling come up frequently given what Guardio builds. For data roles, SQL, Python, and experience with behavioral or event-stream data are commonly tested. Domain-specific security knowledge is a plus but is not always required for every engineering position.

Is prior cybersecurity experience required to join Guardio?

Not always. For core security engineering roles, relevant experience clearly matters. But Guardio also hires strong generalist engineers, data professionals, and product managers who are curious about security and willing to learn the domain on the job. Demonstrating that you have used and understood the product goes a long way even without formal security credentials or certifications.

What should I ask Guardio interviewers at the end of the process?

Good questions include: how the team measures whether users are actually safer (not just whether the product is installed), what the biggest technical challenge is in detecting new phishing campaigns, and how product decisions get made between engineering and growth teams. These questions show you have thought about the mission itself, not just the job title.

How competitive is it to get a job at Guardio?

Guardio is a focused product company with a compact team, which means they hire carefully and not in large batches. With 25 open roles as of July 2026, there is active hiring across multiple functions. Competition is real, particularly for engineering roles. A tailored application and demonstrated knowledge of the product can meaningfully improve your chances compared to a generic submission.
