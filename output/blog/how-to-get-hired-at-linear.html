01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Linear builds project management software that engineering teams around the world reach for when they want speed and simplicity. The company runs on a deliberately small, senior team, and that philosophy shapes everything about how they hire. Clearing the bar is genuinely hard, but the roles that exist are meaningful work.

As of mid-2026, Linear has 25 open roles listed publicly, spanning software engineering, design, product, and go-to-market functions. For Indian candidates, Linear does hire remotely, though many senior individual contributor and leadership positions expect significant overlap with US or European time zones. Read the job description carefully for location requirements before investing time in the process.

Linear is not a company that hires in bulk. Every open role exists because the team has a specific gap, which means your application will actually be read rather than dropped by an automated filter.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Linear does not publish a detailed breakdown of its hiring stages, but candidates who have completed the process commonly describe a sequence of four to five rounds over several weeks.

Stage 1: Application screen. Your resume and any written responses go to a recruiter or hiring manager. Linear is known to care about writing quality even at this stage. A generic note will hurt you. A short, specific observation about why you want to work on this product, written in your own voice, will help.

Stage 2: Recruiter or intro call. A short introductory conversation, typically under an hour, to confirm your background, location, timezone availability, and motivation. This is also your first chance to ask real questions about the role and team structure.

Stage 3: Work sample or async assignment. Linear is known to use take-home tasks or async exercises rather than live coding under pressure. For engineers, this often means a small project or a code review exercise. For designers, a portfolio walkthrough or a design prompt. Candidates commonly report that these tasks are intentionally scoped, but quality of thinking matters more than volume of output.

Stage 4: Technical or domain interviews. Depending on the role, this stage may involve one or two structured conversations with future teammates. Engineers typically work through architecture questions, code quality discussions, or real debugging scenarios. Designers and product managers walk through past work in detail. Interviewers at Linear tend to probe for depth rather than breadth, so be ready to go very deep on a small number of things rather than covering a wide surface.

Stage 5: Final round. A conversation with a founder or senior leader is common at this stage. This is part culture-fit, part values alignment. Linear's founders are hands-on and opinionated about product quality, so expect questions about how you think about craft, user experience, and trade-offs.

Candidates commonly report a total timeline of two to four weeks from first contact to offer. The process can move faster when a team has high urgency for a specific role.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Linear hires people who care deeply about the quality of what they build. This is not a buzzword at this company. It is observable in the product itself, which is publicly praised for its speed and thoughtful design, and it is something interviewers actively screen for.

For engineers: Strong fundamentals in systems thinking and code quality. Experience shipping production software that real users depend on. The ability to make sound trade-off decisions and explain them clearly. TypeScript and React are common in their stack, but more important is how you reason about complexity and design.

For designers: A sharp visual sense combined with strong product instincts. Linear's design quality is publicly cited as a benchmark in the industry. They look for people who have a point of view and can defend it with reasoning.

For all roles: Clear written communication. Linear is a remote company and much of the work happens asynchronously. If your writing is vague or sloppy, it signals risk. Everyone on the team is expected to write well.

Linear tends to prefer candidates with several years of relevant professional experience. Entry-level hiring is rare. If you are early in your career, contributing to open source projects that demonstrate your taste and technical craft can help make your case.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product seriously before you apply. Sign up for Linear, use it for a real project or side project for a few weeks, and form genuine opinions about it. Interviewers can tell immediately whether you have actually used the product or just read about it. Specific observations about what works well and what you would change will make conversations much more interesting.

Polish your written application. Linear receives many applications. Your resume should be clean, specific, and free of filler phrases. If the role invites a cover note, treat it as a writing sample, because that is exactly how it will be read.

Go deep on a few things rather than wide on many. In technical interviews, candidates commonly report that showing genuine mastery of one area is valued more than surface-level familiarity with ten. Pick the two or three things you know best and be ready to go very deep.

Prepare for async communication. If you receive a take-home assignment, structure your response clearly. Use short paragraphs. Make your thinking visible. The assignment is partly a writing and reasoning test, not just a technical one.

Read the company's public writing. The Linear changelog, blog posts, and founder interviews are all publicly available. Reading them gives you real material to reference in conversations. Mentioning a specific product decision you found interesting is far more memorable than saying you 'love the product.'

Be upfront about your timezone situation. If you are applying from India and the role requires significant overlap with US Pacific time, be honest with yourself and with the recruiter about whether you can and want to work those hours. This question surfaces quickly, and handling it directly builds trust.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Linear has a reputation for being one of the most craft-driven software product companies operating today. The culture is small by design. The team believes that a small group of exceptional people, working with high trust and minimal process, can build a better product than a large team operating through committees and approvals.

Speed and quality together. At most companies, speed and quality are treated as a trade-off. Linear's culture holds that they reinforce each other when you have the right people and the right tools. Employees are expected to move quickly and not treat speed as an excuse for poor decisions.

Opinions and taste matter. This is not a culture where you wait to be told what to build or how to build it. People are expected to have strong opinions about the product, to voice them, and to back them up with reasoning. If you prefer a more directive environment, Linear is probably not the right fit.

Remote and async first. The team is distributed. Written communication is the default medium. Meetings are kept to a minimum. This works well for people who are disciplined, self-directed, and write clearly.

High trust, high expectations. There is very little middle management. People are trusted to own their work end to end. The flip side is that underperformance is visible quickly in a small team. The culture rewards people who take genuine ownership.

For Indian candidates specifically, this culture can be a strong fit if you are comfortable working independently, communicating asynchronously across time zones, and holding yourself to a high standard without external nudges.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Linear hire engineers from India?

Linear does hire remotely, and candidates from India have completed their process. The key constraint is timezone overlap. Many engineering roles expect significant working hours during US or European business hours, which means late evenings in India. Read each job description carefully for explicit timezone requirements, and be upfront about your availability during the recruiter call.

How long does the full interview process take?

Candidates commonly report a timeline of two to four weeks from initial application to an offer or decision. The process can move faster when a team has high urgency for a specific role. If you have not heard back after two weeks at any stage, a short, polite follow-up to your recruiter contact is reasonable.

What compensation can I expect at Linear?

Linear does not publish salary bands publicly. Levels.fyi and Glassdoor list some data points from candidates who have shared their figures, and those numbers reflect compensation typical of well-funded US product companies. If you are hired on a local India contract rather than a US-equivalent structure, the figures will differ significantly. Always ask the recruiter about compensation structure early in the process so there are no surprises at the offer stage.

Do I need to know TypeScript and React to apply as an engineer?

Linear's product is built on TypeScript and React, so familiarity with these technologies is genuinely useful. That said, candidates commonly report that interviewers care more about how you think and reason than about whether you already know their exact stack. Strong fundamentals and the ability to pick up new tools quickly matter more than a perfect match on every technology listed in the job description.

Is a referral necessary to get an interview at Linear?

A referral is not required, and direct applications through their website are reviewed. That said, a referral from a current employee does increase the likelihood that your application gets attention quickly, especially at a company that receives many applications for each role. If you know anyone who works at Linear, even loosely, a warm introduction is worth pursuing before applying cold.
