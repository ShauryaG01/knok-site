01 Overview

Overview

EXL Service is a global analytics and digital operations company with a strong presence across India. With 398 open Business Analyst roles as of July 2026, EXL is actively hiring in Bangalore (53 openings), Delhi (48), Mumbai (24), Hyderabad (21), Pune (21), and Chennai (16). The BA interview at EXL typically spans multiple rounds covering domain knowledge, analytical thinking, SQL or Excel skills, and behavioural questions. EXL serves clients in insurance, healthcare, banking, and utilities, so expect questions tied to at least one of these verticals.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you turned raw data into a business recommendation.

*Situation:* My previous company was losing customers faster than the sales team was bringing them in, but leadership did not know where the churn was concentrated.

*Task:* I was asked to analyse several months of transaction and support-ticket data to identify the pattern.

*Action:* I pulled the data into Excel, cleaned duplicates, and built a pivot table segmented by product line and region. I found that one product line had a high return rate and that customers who contacted support more than twice rarely renewed. I prepared a one-page summary with a bar chart and three actionable recommendations: a targeted retention call campaign, a product fix request for the top complaint, and an escalation process for repeat callers.

*Result:* The retention campaign reduced churn in that segment within the next quarter, and the escalation process was implemented within the month. Stakeholders said it was the clearest analysis they had received on this problem.

---

Q: How do you handle stakeholders who disagree on requirements?

*Situation:* During a process-redesign project, the operations head wanted fully automated approvals while the compliance team insisted on manual review for flagged cases.

*Task:* My role was to document a set of requirements both sides could agree on before development started.

*Action:* I scheduled a joint workshop, listed each team's non-negotiable items separately, and then mapped the overlaps. I proposed a hybrid rule: automate standard cases and route flagged ones to a compliance queue with a strict turnaround target. I documented this as an acceptance criterion in the BRD and circulated it for sign-off.

*Result:* Both teams signed off within a week. Development started on schedule and the compliance team reported fewer escalations after go-live.

---

Q: Give an example of how you identified a cost-saving or process-optimisation opportunity.

*Situation:* A client operations team was manually re-entering data from PDF reports into their core system every day, a task that consumed several hours and had a high error rate.

*Task:* I was asked to document the as-is process and suggest improvements.

*Action:* I mapped the end-to-end workflow using a swimlane diagram, timed each step, and estimated the weekly effort involved. I then wrote a requirements document for a simple automation script and reviewed it with the IT team. I also tracked the error rate before and after a pilot run.

*Result:* The automation cut the manual effort to a fraction of the original time and the error rate dropped sharply, which the team leads confirmed in the project retrospective.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard EXL interviewers expect. Keep each component tight: one to two sentences on context, one on your specific responsibility, two to three on what you did, and one clear outcome.

SCQA for analytical walk-throughs (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) works well when explaining how you framed a business problem. State what was happening, what changed or went wrong, what question you were trying to answer, and what you found.

The 'So What' test is useful before you finish any answer. Ask yourself: 'So what did this mean for the business?' If you cannot answer that, your answer is not yet complete. EXL interviewers consistently probe for business impact, not just the technical steps you followed.

MoSCoW prioritisation is worth referencing when answering backlog or stakeholder conflict questions: Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, Won't Have. It signals structured thinking without over-complicating your response.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

EXL interviewers are looking for three things above all else.

Domain awareness. EXL's largest client verticals are insurance, healthcare, banking, and utilities. Candidates who can speak even briefly about how data is used in claims processing, underwriting, or patient outcomes stand out from those with only generic BA experience.

Structured communication. Business Analysts at EXL often sit between offshore delivery teams and onshore clients. Interviewers test whether you can explain a complex finding clearly, both in writing and verbally, without losing precision.

Hands-on data skills. Candidates report being asked to walk through SQL queries, describe Excel models, or interpret a sample dataset on the spot. This is not a purely functional BA role. Comfort with data tools is expected, not optional.

Beyond these three, EXL values ownership. They want to see that you did not just document requirements but followed through to confirm the solution actually solved the problem.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the company and the domain.
Read EXL's publicly available investor materials and client case studies. Pick one vertical (insurance or healthcare) and learn five to seven core terms: loss ratio, claims adjudication, prior authorisation, HEDIS, or equivalents. You do not need deep expertise, but you need to sound familiar with the space.

Week 2: Refresh your technical skills.
Practise writing SQL queries for aggregation, joins, and filtering. Rebuild one Excel pivot table and one chart from scratch. If you have used Power BI or Tableau, revisit one dashboard you built and be ready to walk through your design decisions.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories.
Write out five stories using the STAR format: one for a data insight, one for a stakeholder conflict, one for a process improvement, one for a tight deadline, and one for a project that did not go as planned. Practise saying each one aloud in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and question review.
Ask a colleague or use a mock-interview tool to run through the twelve questions listed in this guide. Record yourself and check: are you giving clear outcomes, are you using specific examples, and are your answers under four minutes each?

On the day, candidates typically report at least one panel-style round with both a technical manager and an HR representative present. Prepare a specific answer to 'Why EXL?' that references their analytics work, not a generic company pitch.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about tools instead of impact. Saying 'I used SQL and Power BI' tells the interviewer nothing useful. Always follow up with what decision or action your analysis enabled.

Vague outcomes. 'The project was successful' is not a result. If you do not have exact numbers to cite, use relative language: 'the team reported significantly fewer errors' or 'the client approved the solution in the first review cycle'.

Ignoring the domain. Candidates who have never thought about EXL's client industries lose credibility quickly. Even one relevant example from insurance or healthcare goes a long way.

Over-explaining process, under-explaining thinking. EXL wants to understand how you reason, not just what steps you followed. When describing your analysis, include why you made the key choices you did.

Asking no questions at the end. Interviewers note whether you asked thoughtful questions. Prepare two: one about the specific team's current projects and one about how success is measured for a BA in the first few months on the job.

Underselling soft skills. BA roles at EXL involve significant client communication. If you only talk about spreadsheets and ignore your stakeholder management experience, you are leaving out half the job.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the EXL Service BA interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from two to four rounds, though EXL does not publicly list a fixed process. Typically there is an initial HR screening, at least one technical or domain round, and a final managerial round. Some candidates report a written case study or take-home exercise before the final round.

Is SQL tested in the EXL BA interview?

Candidates consistently report being asked about SQL, especially for analyst and senior analyst roles. Questions typically cover joins, GROUP BY, subqueries, and filtering logic. You are unlikely to be asked to write complex stored procedures, but you should be comfortable explaining and writing intermediate queries on a whiteboard or shared screen.

What salary can I expect for a BA role at EXL Service?

EXL does not publish official salary bands. Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly cite ranges that vary by experience, location, and the specific team. Roles in Bangalore and Delhi, which have the most current openings, tend to reflect market rates for analytics-heavy BA positions. Always negotiate based on your total compensation picture, not just base pay.

Does EXL ask domain-specific questions for insurance or healthcare?

Yes, and candidates in these verticals report that domain knowledge is a significant differentiator. EXL's core business is analytics for insurance, healthcare, banking, and utilities clients, so interviewers often check whether you understand the business context behind the data. Even basic familiarity with terms like claims processing, loss ratios, or patient data governance helps your credibility considerably.

How long does the EXL hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report that the process typically takes two to five weeks from the first HR call to a formal offer, though timelines vary by location and team. Roles in Bangalore and Delhi may move faster given the higher volume of current openings. Following up politely after each round is generally considered appropriate and signals continued interest.
