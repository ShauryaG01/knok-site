01 Overview

Overview

Five9 is a cloud contact centre platform serving enterprise clients globally. The company currently has 165 open roles, and its PM positions typically sit at the heart of its AI and customer experience product strategy. Candidates report that the interview process includes multiple stages covering product thinking, behavioural questions, and a case or product critique component.

PM roles at Five9 require you to think in terms of enterprise buyers, long sales cycles, and complex integrations with CRMs, telephony systems, and workforce management tools. Interviewers typically want to see that you can navigate multiple internal stakeholders including engineering, sales, and customer success while keeping a clear focus on customer outcomes.

If you are in the broader market, PM roles are actively hiring across India right now. Knok's job radar shows 2,009 open PM positions as of early July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 271 openings and Delhi at 177.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these questions coming up frequently in Five9 PM interviews:

  1. Five9 competes with platforms like Genesys and NICE. How would you position Five9's AI capabilities to win a new enterprise deal?
  2. How would you prioritise features for a contact centre platform when five different enterprise clients all have conflicting requests?
  3. Walk me through how you would handle a major enterprise client asking for a custom feature not on your roadmap.
  4. Tell me about a product you shipped that had a measurable impact on customer retention or churn reduction.
  5. How would you improve Five9's Intelligent Virtual Agent product for a healthcare or financial services customer?
  6. Describe a time you worked closely with enterprise customers to define a product roadmap.
  7. How do you measure success for a contact centre supervisor dashboard? What metrics matter most?
  8. Five9 has grown partly through acquisitions. How do you manage product integration and avoid feature duplication after an M&A event?
  9. How do you balance shipping new features against paying down technical debt in an enterprise platform?
  10. Tell me about a time your product decision was challenged by the sales or customer success team. How did you handle it?
  11. How would you design a self-service onboarding flow for a product that has historically required professional services to implement?
  12. You are a few months into a product that is underperforming against targets. Walk me through how you diagnose what is wrong and what you do next.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure your answers. Here are three examples tailored to Five9-style questions.

Q: Tell me about a product you shipped that had a measurable impact on customer retention.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a B2B SaaS product for small contact centres. Churn was climbing and exit interviews consistently pointed to onboarding as the main friction point.

*Task:* I owned the onboarding experience and needed to reduce time-to-value for new customers without adding headcount to the implementation team.

*Action:* I ran discovery calls with recently churned customers and with customers who had onboarded successfully. I identified three specific drop-off points in the first two weeks of use. I worked with engineering to build a guided setup wizard and with customer success to create automated check-in messages at each drop-off point. I also restructured in-app tooltips based on what power users said they wished they had known earlier.

*Result:* Over the following two quarters, early churn dropped meaningfully, which our CS team confirmed in their quarterly business reviews. The implementation team's involvement in standard onboarding also reduced significantly, freeing them up for complex enterprise accounts.

---

Q: How would you handle a major enterprise client asking for a custom feature not on your roadmap?

*Situation:* A large healthcare enterprise client, one of our top accounts by revenue, requested a custom compliance reporting module they needed due to a state-level regulation.

*Task:* I needed to respond in a way that kept the relationship intact without derailing the roadmap for the broader customer base.

*Action:* I scheduled a deep-dive with the client's product owner and our account executive to fully understand the underlying need. I checked whether the regulation was likely to spread to other states, which would make the feature broadly valuable. I also explored whether the request could be met with a configurable export template rather than a bespoke module. I brought two options back to the client: a configurable export in the next standard release, or a paid professional services engagement for a fully custom module.

*Result:* The client chose the configurable export path, which shipped in our standard release. Two other enterprise clients in regulated industries later adopted the same feature, validating that it addressed a broader market need and not a one-off request.

---

Q: Tell me about a time your product decision was challenged by the sales team.

*Situation:* I had decided to deprecate a legacy integration that only a small set of older customers used. The sales team pushed back because two of those customers were up for renewal.

*Task:* My task was to justify the deprecation with data or find a compromise that protected revenue without keeping the team on an unsustainable maintenance path.

*Action:* I pulled usage data and showed that the integration had near-zero active usage even among the customers nominally using it. I then worked with sales to set up calls with the renewal accounts to understand whether the integration was a genuine renewal risk or a surface-level concern. We found that neither account was actively relying on it. I proposed an extended support window with proactive communication rather than an immediate cutoff.

*Result:* Both accounts renewed. The integration was sunset on schedule, and the engineering team recovered capacity that went into a feature benefiting the full customer base.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioural questions. Every answer needs a concrete result, not just a description of what you did. Five9 interviewers typically probe for specifics, so be prepared to go one level deeper on any result you mention.

CIRCLES (Comprehend, Identify, Report, Cut, List, Evaluate, Summarize) is useful for product design questions like 'How would you improve our IVA product?' Start by clarifying who the user is and what success looks like before jumping to solutions.

A root-cause tree works well for diagnostic questions like 'Why is churn rising?' Break the problem into branches such as onboarding vs. ongoing usage, then hypothesise causes in each branch before choosing what to investigate first.

A prioritisation matrix is useful when the interviewer asks you to rank features or choose between roadmap items. Explicitly state your axes (for example, customer impact vs. engineering effort) and walk the interviewer through your reasoning rather than just announcing a conclusion.

For Five9 specifically, enterprise context shapes everything. Mention ideal customer profiles, deal economics, and the role of the sales cycle when framing your answers. Showing that you understand enterprise SaaS dynamics, not just consumer product thinking, sets you apart from other candidates.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Enterprise product instincts. Can you think in terms of long contracts, multi-stakeholder buying committees, and the cost of customisation? Candidates who speak naturally about SLAs, uptime requirements, and enterprise security reviews tend to leave a strong impression.

Customer empathy grounded in data. Five9 serves contact centres that handle large volumes of customer interactions daily. Interviewers want to see that you listen to customers but also validate what you hear with usage data and business metrics.

Cross-functional credibility. PM roles at Five9 require close collaboration with engineering, sales, and customer success. Candidates report being asked how they have navigated disagreements with these functions, so prepare specific stories for each one.

AI and automation fluency. Five9 has invested heavily in AI features including intelligent virtual agents and agent assist tools. You do not need an ML engineering background, but you should be comfortable discussing how AI products are evaluated, where they fail, and how you set customer expectations around them.

Structured thinking under pressure. Case questions are typically open-ended. Interviewers are watching whether you ask clarifying questions, frame the problem before solving it, and communicate your reasoning as you go rather than jumping straight to a solution.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the market.
Explore Five9's platform through a demo or trial if possible. Read their publicly available press releases and product announcements to understand current priorities. Pick two or three product areas such as IVA, agent assist, or analytics and be ready to critique and suggest improvements for each one.

Week 2: Prepare your stories.
Map your past experience to the question types listed above. For each story, identify the enterprise context, the stakeholder tension, and a concrete result. Practice telling each story out loud and aim to deliver it clearly and concisely.

Week 3: Case practice.
Practice product design and product strategy cases with a partner or by recording yourself. Use Five9-relevant scenarios such as improving the supervisor dashboard, reducing agent handle time, or designing a new integration marketplace. Focus on asking good clarifying questions before proposing solutions.

Before each round: Refresh your knowledge of Five9's latest product announcements. Candidates report that interviewers sometimes reference recent launches or company news, so staying current signals genuine interest in the role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping clarifying questions. In case interviews, jumping straight to a solution without asking about the user, the business goal, and key constraints is the most commonly reported failure mode. Interviewers want to see your thinking process, not just your conclusion.

Treating the customer as a consumer. Five9 sells to enterprise buyers. Candidates who frame answers around app-store ratings or viral growth signal a mismatch with the context. Talk about procurement cycles, IT security reviews, and customer success metrics instead.

Vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'the feature was well received' is not a result. Even when you cannot cite exact figures, reference a business outcome like contract renewal or a stakeholder confirmation like 'our CS team confirmed improvement in their quarterly review.'

Defaulting to AI for every problem. It is tempting to propose AI solutions at a company like Five9. Interviewers typically want to see that you evaluate AI as one tool among several, not a reflexive default answer.

Not preparing questions for the interviewer. Candidates report that thoughtful questions about the team, current roadmap challenges, or strategic priorities leave a strong impression. Prepare at least a few genuine questions before each round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Five9 PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two rounds with hiring managers or senior PMs covering product thinking and behavioural questions, and a case or take-home exercise. Some candidates also report a final round with a cross-functional leader such as an engineering or sales head. The structure can vary by level and team, so ask your recruiter to walk you through the expected stages early on.

Does Five9 ask a take-home case study?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home product case, while others go through a live case discussion in the interview itself. The format appears to vary by team and seniority level. If you are given a take-home assignment, candidates typically say it centres on a product problem relevant to the contact centre space. Clarify the format with your recruiter so you can prepare accordingly.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Five9 in India?

Publicly reported data specific to Five9's India PM compensation is limited, so treat any figure as an estimate. Across the broader India PM market, knok's job radar indicates mid-level PM roles (3-6 years experience) commonly fall in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PM roles in the 40-60 LPA range. For Five9-specific data points, Glassdoor and levels.fyi listings are worth checking before you enter salary discussions.

Is prior contact centre industry experience required?

Candidates report that Five9 values enterprise SaaS experience but does not always require direct contact centre background. What matters more is demonstrating that you understand B2B product dynamics, enterprise customer needs, and technical integration patterns. If you are coming from a different SaaS vertical, draw clear parallels to the contact centre context in your interview answers.

How important is AI knowledge for Five9 PM interviews?

Five9 has made AI a central part of its product strategy, so familiarity with AI product concepts including how virtual agents work, where they fail, and how to set customer expectations is increasingly relevant. You do not need an engineering background, but candidates report that being able to discuss AI product tradeoffs is a positive signal. Reading Five9's public AI product documentation before your interview is a practical way to prepare.

How should I position my experience if I come from a B2C or startup background?

Candidates from B2C or startup backgrounds can still succeed by explicitly translating their experience into enterprise terms. For each story you prepare, identify the closest equivalent to enterprise concepts: instead of 'users' say 'client administrators', and instead of 'growth metrics' talk about 'renewal rates and expansion revenue.' Interviewers typically appreciate this self-awareness and the effort to bridge the context gap rather than hoping the connection is obvious.
