01 Overview

Overview

Hevodata is a no-code data pipeline platform used by data teams across industries. As of mid-2026, knok's job radar shows Hevodata currently has 40 open roles, and Technical Program Manager (TPM) positions sit at the intersection of engineering execution and product delivery.

The interview process typically spans multiple rounds covering program management fundamentals, cross-functional collaboration, and data pipeline domain knowledge. Candidates report a mix of behavioral, situational, and technical discussions. This guide walks you through what to expect, how to prepare, and how to frame your answers.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you manage a program with dependencies across multiple engineering teams?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were building a new API gateway that required simultaneous work from the platform team, the security team, and three product squads.

*Task:* I was responsible for keeping all five teams aligned so the final release could go out together without last-minute surprises.

*Action:* I set up a weekly dependency sync, maintained a shared tracker visible to all teams, and created a clear RACI for each milestone. When the security team flagged a certificate management issue that could block the platform team, I escalated to the engineering director within a single business day and facilitated a focused resolution meeting.

*Result:* The gateway shipped on the originally committed date with all security requirements met. Post-launch, teams told me the shared tracker significantly reduced the back-and-forth on Slack.

---

Q: Tell us about a time a project was significantly delayed. What did you learn?

*Situation:* We were migrating a set of data connectors to a new authentication model. The original plan was four sprints.

*Task:* I owned delivery, but midway through, two engineers were pulled to a critical production incident, shrinking our effective capacity.

*Action:* I immediately re-baselined the plan with the remaining engineers, communicated the revised timeline to product and customer success with clear reasoning, and identified which connectors were highest priority so we could ship a partial release first.

*Result:* We launched one week later than the revised date, but with zero customer-facing issues and full coverage of the highest-priority connectors. The partial release approach meant key enterprise customers were unblocked ahead of the full rollout.

---

Q: How do you communicate program status to senior stakeholders?

*Situation:* I was running a multi-quarter program to rebuild the alerting system for our data platform, with visibility to the VP of Engineering and two product directors.

*Task:* Stakeholders needed consistent, reliable updates without being pulled into day-to-day execution noise.

*Action:* I created a weekly one-page status report using a RAG (Red-Amber-Green) format, with a three-line summary at the top for executives and detailed risk and action items below for those who wanted depth. I also held a monthly review meeting to discuss trajectory and trade-offs.

*Result:* Stakeholders consistently said they felt informed and trusted the program. The VP of Engineering used the format as a template for other programs in the org.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR is your foundation for behavioral questions. Structure every story with a clear Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did), and always close with a concrete Result.

RACI plus Risk Register works well for questions about cross-team coordination. Describe how you assigned Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed roles, and how you maintained a live risk register with owners and mitigation steps.

RAG Status Reporting is a useful framework when asked about stakeholder communication. Red means blocked and needs escalation, Amber means at risk but being managed, Green means on track. Interviewers at product-led companies like Hevodata tend to value crisp, no-surprises communication.

OKR Alignment is a strong framing for prioritization questions. Show you can map program milestones to quarterly objectives so stakeholders understand the 'why' behind trade-off decisions.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hevodata interviewers typically look for candidates who can operate with autonomy in a fast-moving environment. Based on what candidates report, the panel pays close attention to a few things.

Cross-functional influence without authority. TPMs at Hevodata work across engineering, product, and customer-facing teams. Interviewers want to see you can align people who do not report to you by using clarity, trust, and consistent communication.

Comfort with technical depth. Hevodata builds data pipeline infrastructure, so you will be expected to understand concepts like connector architecture, data latency, schema changes, and API reliability. You do not need to write code, but you should be able to speak the language of the engineers you partner with.

Bias for delivery. Stories that show you shipped something under constraint, unblocked a team, or simplified a complex plan tend to land well. Interviewers are less impressed by perfect process descriptions and more impressed by outcomes.

Structured communication. Whether it is a risk escalation, a status update, or a trade-off decision, Hevodata TPMs are expected to write and speak clearly. Practicing concise, structured responses before your interview will help.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain grounding. Read Hevodata's product documentation and understand what a no-code data pipeline does, how connectors work, and what problems their customers are solving. This context will make your answers feel specific rather than generic.

Week 2: Story bank. Write out five to seven STAR stories from your past experience covering: a delayed project, a cross-team conflict, a scope-change decision, a risk escalation, and a stakeholder communication win. Practice saying each story out loud in under two minutes.

Week 3: Mock rounds. Candidates report Hevodata typically runs multiple rounds. Practice with a peer or record yourself answering the 12 questions listed in this guide. Focus on cutting filler words and making your Results concrete.

Before your interview. Review Hevodata's current open roles (they currently list 40 on knok) to understand what teams are hiring. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for each interviewer: one about the team's current program challenges, one about how success is measured in the TPM role, and one about how engineering and product collaborate at Hevodata.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about your role. Saying 'we shipped the project' without explaining what you personally did is the most common mistake TPM candidates make. Interviewers are evaluating you, not your team. Use 'I' when describing your actions.

Skipping the Result. Many candidates describe the Situation and Action in detail but leave out what actually happened. Always close with a concrete outcome, even if the result was a lesson learned rather than a win.

Over-indexing on process. Describing elaborate frameworks without showing they led to a real outcome can feel academic. Tie every tool or method you mention to a specific decision or result it enabled.

Not preparing domain questions. Walking into a Hevodata interview without a basic understanding of data pipelines, connectors, or integration challenges signals low motivation. Even a few hours of product research makes a visible difference.

Failing to show stakeholder empathy. TPM interviews often test whether you can see a situation from a product manager's view, an engineer's view, and a customer's view simultaneously. Candidates who only speak from the engineering execution lens can miss this dimension.

Giving rehearsed-sounding answers. Interviewers can tell when a story is over-polished. Aim for natural, specific language. Mention a real constraint, a real team name (anonymized is fine), or a real trade-off you debated.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Hevodata TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes an initial screening call, one or two technical and domain discussions, and a final round with senior leadership. The exact number of rounds can vary based on the team and role level. It is worth confirming the structure with your recruiter at the start of the process.

Do I need a technical background to become a TPM at Hevodata?

A strong technical background helps, but you do not need to be a practicing engineer. What matters is that you can understand engineering constraints, read a system design diagram, and have informed conversations about trade-offs. Familiarity with data infrastructure concepts like ETL pipelines or API integrations is a clear plus at a company like Hevodata.

How important is knowledge of Hevodata's product for the interview?

Very important. Candidates who research the product ahead of time consistently report feeling more confident and receiving better feedback. You should understand what Hevodata does, who its customers are, and what kinds of engineering challenges a data pipeline company faces. This context makes your answers specific rather than generic.

What salary can I expect as a TPM at Hevodata?

Hevodata does not publicly post salary bands for TPM roles. Based on industry surveys, TPM compensation at mid-size product companies varies widely depending on years of experience and seniority level. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have self-reported figures from employees at similar companies that can give you a useful reference range before negotiations.

How do I stand out as a TPM candidate at Hevodata?

Candidates who stand out typically combine three things: concrete delivery stories with measurable outcomes, demonstrated comfort with the technical domain, and clear evidence they can align cross-functional teams without relying on authority. Preparing specific examples from past programs and asking sharp questions about Hevodata's current challenges will set you apart.

Is the interview process the same for remote and in-office roles?

Candidates report that the interview format is broadly similar for remote and in-office roles, with most rounds conducted over video call regardless of location. The specific team you are joining may have preferences about where you work, so it is worth asking your recruiter about this early in the process.
