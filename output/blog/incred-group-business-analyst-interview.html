01 Overview

Overview

InCred Group is a fast-growing fintech-focused NBFC (non-banking financial company) that provides personal loans, education loans, home loans, and business lending products across India. The company leans heavily on data and technology for credit decisions, which means Business Analysts here sit at the intersection of finance, product, and operations.

As of July 2026, knok's job radar shows InCred Group has 27 open roles. Across India, there are 398 Business Analyst openings tracked, with the strongest demand in Bangalore (53 open roles), Delhi (48), and Mumbai (24), where InCred has a significant presence.

Candidates typically go through 2-4 rounds covering recruiter screening, a technical or case-based round focused on SQL or analytical problem-solving, and a final conversation with a business or credit team lead. The structure varies by sub-team (credit analytics, product, or operations), so confirm the format with the recruiter early. Candidates report that InCred moves quickly and values structured, decision-oriented thinkers over people who simply describe analysis without connecting it to business outcomes.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you used data to find a problem the business did not know it had.

*Situation:* I was a BA at an NBFC where collections performance was considered stable. The team tracked overall recovery rates and nothing seemed alarming on the surface.

*Task:* My assignment was to build a monthly portfolio health report, and I decided to slice repayment data by loan vintage rather than just looking at aggregated totals.

*Action:* I ran a cohort analysis in SQL grouping borrowers by the month they received their loan. I found that loans disbursed during a specific three-month window had a noticeably higher early delinquency rate compared to other vintages. I traced this back to a brief period when an underwriting parameter was loosened. I built a clear chart showing the deviation and presented it to the credit head with a proposed tightening of the relevant rule.

*Result:* The credit team re-tightened the parameter within two weeks. The issue had been hidden because overall recovery numbers masked the vintage-level pattern. Candidates who frame this kind of proactive insight tend to stand out in InCred interviews.

---

Q: A product manager says conversion on the loan application page dropped sharply last week. How do you approach this?

*Situation:* This is a common ambiguous problem that InCred interviewers use to test structured thinking.

*Task:* The goal is to move from a vague observation to a clear, testable hypothesis without jumping to conclusions.

*Action:* I would first confirm the metric definition (is it unique sessions to submission, or clicks to submission?). Then I would check whether it is a data pipeline issue before assuming a real drop occurred. Next I would segment the drop by device type, traffic source, loan product, and geography to isolate where it is concentrated. I would then look at what changed in the same week: a UI update, a new credit policy, a shift in ad traffic bringing in a different user profile, or a backend error.

*Result:* By narrowing the drop to a specific segment, I can give the PM a clear next action rather than a vague recommendation to investigate everything. InCred interviewers typically reward this funnel-narrowing approach over a scattered list of guesses.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to present a data-backed recommendation the business initially pushed back on.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the sales team believed that offering higher loan amounts to borderline-credit customers would improve conversion and overall revenue.

*Task:* I was asked to validate this belief with data before it became policy.

*Action:* I compared repayment behaviour across customers who received higher loan amounts versus similar-risk customers who received standard amounts. The data showed a clear pattern where higher amounts increased early defaults for that credit segment. I prepared a one-page summary with a chart and walked the sales head through it privately before the wider meeting, so they could process the finding without feeling cornered in public.

*Result:* The sales head initially resisted but agreed to a small pilot. The pilot confirmed my finding and the policy was not rolled out broadly. Pre-aligning with the key stakeholder before the formal presentation was the move that made the difference.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (for behavioural questions): Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined), spend most of your answer on Action (what you specifically did, which tools you used, what logic you applied), and close with a clear observable Result. If you lack exact numbers, 'the team adopted the recommendation' or 'the process time reduced noticeably' is more credible than an invented figure.

Root cause analysis structure (for case questions): Start by confirming the metric definition. Then check for data pipeline or logging issues before assuming a real business problem. Segment the issue by time, geography, product, and user type to isolate where it is concentrated. List two to three hypotheses ranked by likelihood, then propose a concrete way to test each. InCred interviewers typically reward candidates who slow down and confirm the problem before rushing to solve it.

Metrics framework (for lending product questions): Think in three layers. First, acquisition metrics: applications, approval rate, disbursement volume. Second, portfolio health metrics: repayment rate, delinquency by bucket, early default rate. Third, collection and recovery metrics: roll rates, resolution rate. Showing this layered thinking signals that you understand how InCred's lending business actually works, not just how to build charts.

Stakeholder prioritisation tip: InCred BAs serve multiple internal customers: credit, collections, product, and operations. When answering prioritisation questions, show that you would ask each stakeholder about urgency and business impact, then communicate your sequencing and the reasoning behind it. Avoid saying you would simply work in order of who asked first.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

InCred Group interviewers, based on candidate reports, look for a specific combination of skills and attitude.

Domain curiosity about lending: Candidates who have read about NBFCs, credit scoring, or collection processes consistently have an edge. You do not need to be a credit expert, but asking a good question about InCred's portfolio during the interview signals genuine interest in the business.

SQL and Excel fluency: Expect to write at least one SQL query on paper or in a shared document. Common asks involve joins, aggregations, filtering by date ranges, and cohort-style groupings. Basic Excel comfort (pivot tables, financial formulas) is also commonly tested.

Structured analytical thinking: More than raw technical skill, interviewers want to see a clear logical process. Break ambiguous problems into steps before answering. State your assumptions out loud rather than making silent leaps.

Communication with non-technical stakeholders: InCred BAs work closely with business heads who may not be data-savvy. Interviewers assess whether you can turn a complex finding into a clean recommendation, not just describe the analysis at a technical level.

Ownership mindset: Several candidates report that InCred values people who do not wait to be told what to analyse. Stories about self-initiated analyses or proactively flagging risks in a portfolio tend to land well.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain foundation
Read publicly available material on how NBFCs operate in India: the loan lifecycle (origination, disbursement, repayment, collections), key portfolio metrics (NPA, delinquency buckets, roll rates), and how companies like InCred use credit scoring. Visit InCred's website and read any recent news about their product lines so you can reference specifics during the interview.

Week 2: SQL and analytical skills
Practice SQL questions focused on financial and transactional data: aggregations over time windows, cohort queries, identifying anomalies in repayment records. Filter for medium-difficulty problems involving joins and window functions. Separately, practice structuring case answers using the root cause and metrics frameworks described in this guide.

Week 3: Mock interviews and story preparation
Write out four to five STAR stories from your past covering: a data-driven insight you delivered, a time you challenged a flawed assumption, a stakeholder conflict you navigated, and a process you measurably improved. Practice each story out loud so it stays under three minutes. Ask a peer to push back on your reasoning so you get comfortable defending your analysis.

Day before the interview
Review InCred's recent news (new products, funding rounds, partnerships). Prepare two to three questions about the BA team's current projects or data infrastructure. Confirm the interview format with the recruiter so you know whether to expect a case, a live SQL test, or a purely conversational round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to answers without confirming the question: In case rounds, candidates often start solving before they have confirmed what metric is being measured or what success looks like. InCred interviewers typically flag this. Pause and ask one clarifying question before diving in.

Generic SQL answers: A basic SELECT with one filter is rarely sufficient. Show that you think about data quality, edge cases (null repayment dates, duplicate transaction records), and performance on large datasets.

No knowledge of the lending domain: Coming in with zero awareness of terms like NPA, delinquency, or loan-to-value ratio signals that you have done no background reading. You do not need deep expertise, but you should be able to hold a conversation about the credit business without blank looks.

Vague STAR answers: Saying 'I improved the process' without any detail about what you did, which tools you used, or what changed as a result is a very common miss. Include at least one concrete action and one observable outcome.

Listing tools without showing business impact: Naming tools (Tableau, Python, Power BI) without connecting them to a business decision does not differentiate you. InCred interviewers want to know what the analysis led to, not just how it was built.

Asking nothing at the end: Candidates who have no questions for the interviewer are seen as disengaged. Prepare at least two questions about the team's current analytical challenges or how success is measured in the BA role.

How many rounds does InCred Group typically have for a Business Analyst role?

Candidates report anywhere from 2 to 4 rounds. Does InCred Group test SQL in the BA interview?

Yes, SQL is commonly tested based on candidate reports. Expect questions involving joins across transaction and customer tables, aggregations over time periods, and cohort-style queries. You may be asked to write the query on paper, in a shared Google Doc, or in a simple online tool. Practice writing clean, readable SQL with clear aliases and brief comments explaining your logic.

Candidates who join BA roles at InCred typically come from engineering (B.Tech), MBA programs with a finance or analytics focus, or prior experience in banking, NBFCs, or fintech companies. Prior exposure to credit analytics, collections, or loan portfolio management is an advantage but is not always required for entry-level positions.
