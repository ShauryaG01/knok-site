02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have interviewed at Roko Labs and similar product-focused startups typically report questions across these areas:

  1. Walk us through how you would design and set up a CI/CD pipeline from scratch for a microservices application.
  2. How do you manage secrets and sensitive configuration inside a Kubernetes cluster?
  3. Describe a time you improved deployment reliability or reduced downtime in production.
  4. How would you design a monitoring and alerting system for a distributed architecture?
  5. What is your approach to infrastructure as code, and which tools have you used in production?
  6. Walk us through your incident response process when a production service goes down.
  7. How do you handle Kubernetes resource limits, requests, and autoscaling?
  8. Which cloud providers have you worked with, and how do you approach cost optimisation?
  9. How do you embed security into a DevOps pipeline (DevSecOps)?
  10. Describe your experience with log aggregation and distributed tracing tools.
  11. How do you decide between a rollback and a hotfix when a bad deployment reaches production?
  12. What does a good post-mortem look like to you, and how have you run one?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioural question. Here are three worked examples:

Q: Walk us through how you set up a CI/CD pipeline from scratch.

*Situation:* My previous team was releasing services manually via SSH scripts, which caused frequent configuration drift and inconsistent deployments across environments.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement a fully automated pipeline before the next product launch.

*Action:* I evaluated GitHub Actions and Jenkins, choosing GitHub Actions for its native integration with our existing repositories. I built stages for linting, unit tests, Docker image builds, container vulnerability scanning with Trivy, and a staged rollout using Helm charts: first to staging, then to production after a manual approval gate.

*Result:* Release errors dropped noticeably, the team moved to multiple deploys per week with confidence, and onboarding new engineers to the release process became straightforward because everything lived in code.

---

Q: Describe a time you handled a production outage.

*Situation:* Our primary API service began returning errors and latency climbed sharply during peak traffic hours.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer responsible for triaging and restoring service quickly.

*Action:* I checked the recent deployment history first and spotted a config change pushed shortly before the incident. The change had introduced a misconfigured environment variable pointing the service at a non-existent database endpoint. I reverted the config, verified the service recovered, and kept the team informed throughout via our incident Slack channel.

*Result:* Service was restored quickly. I wrote a post-mortem and added a config validation step to the pipeline so the same class of error would be caught before reaching production in future.

---

Q: How do you handle Kubernetes resource optimisation?

*Situation:* Our staging and production clusters were significantly over-provisioned, and cloud costs were climbing each quarter.

*Task:* I needed to right-size workloads without introducing reliability risk.

*Action:* I pulled CPU and memory utilisation data from Prometheus over several weeks and compared actual usage against declared resource requests. I adjusted limits for low-traffic services, enabled Horizontal Pod Autoscaler on high-traffic ones, and shifted nightly batch jobs to spot nodes.

*Result:* Infrastructure spend dropped meaningfully, and the team gained clear visibility into which services were costing the most. I documented the process so it became a regular review habit.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task concise, then give more detail on Action (this is where your skill shows), and close with a concrete Result. If you cannot share exact figures from a previous job, describe the direction and rough magnitude of the change.

CAR (Context, Action, Result) is a shorter variant useful when the situation is obvious, for example 'Tell me how you approach Terraform module design.' Jump straight to what you did and why you chose that approach.

For technical and system-design questions, use a structured approach: clarify requirements, state constraints, outline the key components, discuss trade-offs, then cover failure modes and observability. Interviewers at product startups typically want to hear your reasoning rather than a textbook answer.

For incident and on-call questions, frame your answer around a clear sequence: detect, triage, mitigate, communicate, resolve, prevent. This maps closely to how mature engineering teams run incidents and signals operational experience.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Ownership mindset. Interviewers at companies like Roko Labs typically want engineers who treat the platform as their own. They look for candidates who proactively identify reliability or cost issues rather than waiting to be told.

Depth over breadth. It is better to know Kubernetes and Terraform deeply than to have surface-level familiarity with a long list of tools. Be ready to go several levels deep on any tool you mention on your resume.

Clear communication during incidents. Candidates who narrate their troubleshooting process clearly, including dead ends and pivots, tend to perform better in scenario-based rounds. Interviewers often care as much about how you communicate under pressure as what you technically do.

Security awareness. With DevSecOps becoming standard practice, expect at least one question on secrets management, image scanning, or access control. Not knowing basics like RBAC in Kubernetes or secret rotation is commonly cited as a red flag in DevOps interviews.

Collaboration with product and development teams. DevOps roles at product startups sit at the intersection of infrastructure and product delivery. Show that you can translate infrastructure constraints into language developers understand and vice versa.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical revision

Review your weakest area from these three: CI/CD tooling (GitHub Actions, Jenkins, or GitLab CI), container orchestration (Kubernetes objects, networking, RBAC, autoscaling), and infrastructure as code (Terraform or Pulumi). Pick the tools you know well and prepare to go deep on them rather than skimming everything.

Week 2: Scenario practice

Practise answering the questions listed above out loud or with a peer. A good STAR answer typically runs a few minutes, so time yourself and trim where needed. Write out your strongest career stories in STAR format and practise adapting them to different question types.

Before the interview

Read Roko Labs' public engineering blog or LinkedIn posts if available. Understand what their product does and what reliability or scale challenges their platform likely faces. Prepare a few specific questions for the interviewer about the team's on-call culture, deployment frequency, or current infrastructure challenges. Asking thoughtful questions signals genuine interest and helps you assess whether the role fits you.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Listing tools without depth. Writing 'Kubernetes, Terraform, Ansible, Prometheus, Grafana' on your resume is expected at this level. The mistake is not being able to explain real decisions you made with those tools or trade-offs you considered.

Vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'things improved' is weak. Even if you cannot share exact figures, say 'release errors dropped noticeably' or 'deployment time roughly halved.' Be specific about what changed even if not the precise magnitude.

Skipping the 'why.' Candidates often describe what they did but not why they chose that approach over alternatives. Interviewers at product startups want to hear your reasoning, not just a list of your actions.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing at the end of a round often come across as uninterested. Prepare at least a couple of genuine questions about the team, the stack, or how on-call is structured.

Underestimating the HR or manager round. Even at technical companies, this round matters. Be ready to discuss how you handle disagreements with developers, how you prioritise when multiple issues hit at once, and what kind of engineering culture you do your best work in.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Roko Labs typically have for DevOps roles?

Candidates report the process typically involves two to four rounds. These commonly include an initial recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering infrastructure and system design, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or engineering manager. Round structure can vary by team and seniority level, so confirm the details with your recruiter early on.

What salary can I expect as a DevOps Engineer at Roko Labs?

Roko Labs does not publicly list salary bands, so specific figures are not available. Knok jobradar data for DevOps Engineers across India shows ranges of 6-12 LPA for entry level (0-2 years), 15-28 LPA for mid level (3-5 years), and 30-50 LPA for senior roles (6-9 years). Lead and Staff positions commonly reach 45-70+ LPA in this market. Use these figures as a reference point for your negotiation, not a guarantee of what Roko Labs will offer.

Do I need experience with all major cloud providers to clear the interview?

Generally, no. Most DevOps interviews expect depth in at least one major cloud provider, commonly AWS, GCP, or Azure, rather than surface-level exposure to all three. Check the job description for which cloud Roko Labs primarily uses. If their stack differs from your main experience, be ready to explain how your knowledge transfers and that you can learn the specifics quickly.

How important is Kubernetes knowledge for this role?

Very important at most product startups. Expect at least one or two questions covering pods, deployments, services, namespaces, RBAC, resource limits, and autoscaling. Candidates who can describe real decisions they made using Kubernetes in production tend to do better than those who only know textbook definitions. If your Kubernetes experience is thin, prioritise it in your revision.

Should I tailor my resume specifically for Roko Labs?

Yes, even a small amount of tailoring helps. Mirror the language from the job description where you genuinely have the relevant experience. If the role mentions 'infrastructure as code' and you have Terraform experience, name Terraform explicitly rather than leaving it as a vague skill. Highlight production incidents you managed or reliability improvements you drove, as these align well with what product-startup DevOps teams care about.
