01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Jane Street is a global quantitative trading and market-making firm with a reputation for hiring some of the sharpest mathematical and programming minds in the world. They trade equities, bonds, ETFs, options, and other financial instruments across offices in New York, London, Hong Kong, and Amsterdam.

As of July 2026, Jane Street has 221 open roles spanning trading, software engineering, quantitative research, and business operations. That volume signals continued growth, but every single hire goes through one of the most selective processes in the industry. Jane Street does not have an India office, so Indian candidates typically apply for positions in their international locations, most often New York or London.

Compensation at Jane Street is publicly reported on levels.fyi to be among the highest in finance, especially for traders and engineers. If you clear the bar, the offer reflects it.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Jane Street's hiring process is multi-stage and can take several weeks from application to offer. Every stage is substantive, so prepare seriously before you even submit.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. The firm looks for strong academic credentials in mathematics, statistics, computer science, or physics. A top-tier IIT, IISc, NIT, or equivalent institution helps, but demonstrated ability matters more than the college name. Relevant experience in quant finance, competitive programming, or research strengthens your file.

Stage 2: Online assessment. Most candidates receive a timed online test covering mathematical puzzles, probability problems, and sometimes a coding component. This is a genuine filter, not a formality. Expect it to be harder than a typical placement test.

Stage 3: Phone or video interview. A recruiter or trader walks you through probability questions and basic market intuition problems. Expect prompts like 'What is the expected value of...' or 'How would you price this bet?' Thinking clearly out loud matters as much as arriving at the right answer.

Stage 4: On-site or virtual on-site loop. This is a full-day or multi-session process with several interviewers. Rounds typically cover:

Round typeWhat happens
Math and probabilityProgressively harder puzzles, expected value, combinatorics
Trading simulationYou buy and sell while interviewers act as the market
ProgrammingAlgorithmic problems, often in a language of your choice
Fit and curiosityDiscussion about your thinking style and intellectual interests

Interviewers may revisit your earlier answers within the same day. This is intentional. They want to see how you update your reasoning when given new information.

Stage 5: Offer. If the loop goes well, you typically hear back within a week or two. The package includes base salary, signing bonus, and a profit-sharing component that is publicly reported on levels.fyi to be significant.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Jane Street is not looking for people who have memorised formulas. They want people who reason clearly under uncertainty.

Mathematical sharpness. Probability and expected value sit at the core of everything they do. You need to be comfortable with combinatorics, conditional probability, and quick estimation, not just at a textbook level but in fast, conversational settings.

Programming ability. For engineering and research roles, strong coding skills are essential. Jane Street is known for using OCaml internally, but interviews often allow Python or another language you know well. The emphasis is on clean logic and correctness, not syntax knowledge.

Trading intuition. Even for non-trading roles, they want people who understand how markets work and how to think about risk and reward in a quantitative way.

Clear communication under pressure. Interviewers watch how you think, not just what you answer. Candidates who say 'I am not certain, but let me break it down this way...' and reason toward an answer often do better than candidates who try to recall a memorised solution.

Intellectual curiosity. Jane Street hires people who genuinely find hard problems enjoyable. They ask what books you read, what puzzles you have worked through, and what draws you to the firm beyond the compensation.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparation for Jane Street needs to start months before you apply, not days before.

Practice probability from first principles. Work through classic problems: the secretary problem, Monty Hall, coupon collector, random walks. The book 'A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews' is commonly cited by candidates on forums as a strong match for the style of questions Jane Street asks.

Sharpen your mental arithmetic. Traders at Jane Street often do fast calculations during simulations. Practice multiplying, dividing, and estimating without a calculator until it feels natural.

Do real coding practice. Platforms like LeetCode and Codeforces build the algorithmic thinking Jane Street tests. Focus on dynamic programming, graph problems, and probability-based questions at medium-to-hard difficulty.

Understand market basics. Know what a bid-ask spread is, how options are priced conceptually, and what ETF arbitrage means. You do not need prior trading experience, but you should be able to hold a real conversation about market structure.

Run mock trading simulations. This is the step most candidates skip. Find a practice partner and simulate a market-making game where one person quotes prices and the other decides to buy or sell. This builds the intuition the trading round directly tests.

Read and watch what Jane Street publishes. They share essays, podcast appearances, and recorded talks. Interviewers notice candidates who have genuinely thought about why Jane Street specifically, not just quant finance in general.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Jane Street has a culture that is difficult to describe without reaching for the word 'intellectual.' The firm is flat. There are no formal titles like VP or Director in the way a bank uses them. People are evaluated on the quality of their thinking, not on seniority.

Collaboration over internal competition. Unlike some trading firms where desks compete against each other, Jane Street is known for a cooperative environment. People share models and insights because the firm profits as a whole, not desk by desk.

Learning is the default mode. New hires go through a structured training programme covering probability, markets, and programming. Even experienced hires report learning things they had not expected. The firm genuinely invests in making people better at what they do.

Honesty about uncertainty. Jane Street values people who say 'I do not know' clearly rather than bluffing through an answer. This is embedded in both the interview process and the day-to-day working culture.

No unnecessary hierarchy. Analysts sit near senior traders. Questions go directly to the person who knows the answer, not up a chain of managers. This appeals to people who want to learn fast and be judged on output rather than tenure.

High expectations, real reward. The culture is demanding, and hours can be long during volatile markets. Publicly reported accounts on forums like Blind suggest most employees consider the environment worth it, citing the calibre of colleagues as a consistent reason to stay.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Jane Street hire candidates from Indian universities?

Yes. Jane Street recruits globally and Indian candidates from IITs, IISc, and other strong institutions have joined the firm. Academic pedigree helps but is not the only signal. Demonstrated ability in mathematics and programming matters more, so if your skills are strong, applying directly is worth it regardless of which college you attended.

How long does the Jane Street hiring process take from start to finish?

The process commonly runs several weeks from application to offer, though timelines vary by role and hiring cycle. The online assessment and phone screen can move quickly, while scheduling the full virtual loop may take additional time. In commonly cited experiences shared on forums, total elapsed time runs anywhere from three to eight weeks.

Do I need to know OCaml before applying to Jane Street?

Not necessarily, though it helps for software engineering roles since Jane Street uses OCaml extensively in production. Most coding interviews allow you to work in Python or another language you are comfortable with. If you join, you will learn OCaml through their internal training programme. Focus on algorithmic clarity and clean logic first, not on learning a new language just to apply.

What is the salary or total compensation at Jane Street?

Jane Street does not publish official salary bands. Total compensation figures for New York-based roles are publicly reported on levels.fyi and Glassdoor, and they are consistently among the highest in the finance and technology industry for both new graduates and experienced hires. The package typically includes base pay, a signing bonus, and a profit-sharing component tied to firm performance.

Is there a Jane Street office in India, or do I need to relocate?

As of mid-2026, Jane Street does not have an India office. All roles are based in New York, London, Hong Kong, or Amsterdam. Indian candidates who want to join typically need to relocate. Jane Street has a history of sponsoring work visas for strong candidates, but you should confirm current sponsorship policy directly with their recruiter at the time of your application.

What is the hardest part of the Jane Street interview and how do I handle it?

Most candidates say the trading simulation round is the toughest because it is unlike any interview format they have encountered before. You are expected to make fast pricing decisions, adjust your positions when given new information, and stay calm under deliberate pressure from interviewers. The most effective preparation is practicing live market-making games with a partner well before your interview date, not just reading about it.
