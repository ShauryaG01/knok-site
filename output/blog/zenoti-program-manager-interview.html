01 Overview

Overview

Zenoti builds cloud software for the wellness and beauty industry, serving spas, salons, and medispas across enterprise accounts worldwide. As of mid-2026, knok's job radar shows 52 open roles at Zenoti, with Technical Program Manager positions among the most active. Across the broader Indian job market, there are 313 TPM openings tracked right now, with Bangalore leading at 41 roles.

TPMs at Zenoti sit at the intersection of engineering, product, and client delivery. You are expected to own large, multi-team programs, manage risks and dependencies proactively, and communicate clearly with both technical teams and enterprise stakeholders.

The interview process typically spans three to five rounds covering behavioral depth, program management craft, and technical grounding. Candidates report a structured experience with clear expectations communicated by recruiters upfront. This guide covers the questions that come up most often, how to frame strong answers, and what Zenoti interviewers specifically look for.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a large cross-functional program you owned end to end.

*Situation:* Our company was migrating a legacy billing system to a new microservices architecture, affecting engineering, finance, customer success, and three external vendor integrations.

*Task:* I was the program owner responsible for coordinating all workstreams, managing dependencies, and ensuring a zero-downtime cutover.

*Action:* I set up a weekly steering committee with leads from each team, created a shared RAID log (risks, actions, issues, decisions), and built a phased rollout plan with clear go/no-go criteria at each milestone. I ran daily standups for the two weeks surrounding cutover.

*Result:* We delivered on schedule with no billing disruption. Post-launch, customer success reported a meaningful drop in billing-related support tickets.

---

Q: Tell me about a program that slipped. What happened and what did you change afterward?

*Situation:* A product integration program I was managing fell three weeks behind because a third-party API dependency was not scoped correctly in the initial plan.

*Task:* I needed to recover the timeline, manage stakeholder expectations, and prevent the same gap from repeating.

*Action:* I called an unblocking session immediately with the vendor and our engineering lead, negotiated a partial API delivery that let us parallelize testing, and updated stakeholder communication to reflect the new timeline with clear reasoning.

*Result:* We recovered two of the three weeks and shipped with a one-week delay. I then added a vendor dependency checklist to our program kickoff template, which the team continues to use.

---

Q: How do you manage dependencies between multiple engineering teams with conflicting priorities?

*Situation:* Two product engineering teams both needed a shared platform team's bandwidth in the same quarter, and the platform team could only commit to one at a time.

*Task:* I had to resolve the conflict without escalating to the VP level and keep both programs on track.

*Action:* I facilitated a joint planning session with both product PMs and the platform lead, mapped the actual sequencing constraints, and proposed a phased handoff: team A got the first six weeks while team B ran a parallel 'prep phase' to reduce their dependency window.

*Result:* Both programs delivered within the quarter. Neither PM needed to escalate, and the platform team reported cleaner handoffs because of the prep phase.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR as your baseline. For program management roles, candidates report that Zenoti interviewers appreciate when you name specific artifacts you used (RAID log, program charter, RACI, OKR alignment doc) rather than speaking in generalities. Be ready to explain why you chose each tool.

For risk and trade-off questions, a simple three-part structure works well: state the risk or conflict clearly, explain the options you considered, then describe the decision and how you communicated it to stakeholders.

For 'without authority' influence questions, lead with the relationship-building step before the process step. Interviewers want to see that you built trust before you attempted to align people.

For metrics questions, separate leading indicators (are we on track?) from lagging indicators (did we deliver value?). Programs that only measure on-time delivery miss customer impact, and Zenoti's enterprise context makes outcome metrics especially important.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that Zenoti TPM interviews consistently test three things.

Program ownership depth. Did you actually drive the program, or did you mainly coordinate meetings? Interviewers probe with follow-up questions like 'what would have happened if you had not done that?' Be ready to go two or three levels deeper on any story you tell.

Stakeholder management in enterprise contexts. Zenoti serves large enterprise wellness clients, so comfort with client-facing escalations and executive communication is valued. If you have experience navigating enterprise customer expectations alongside internal engineering priorities, lead with that.

Technical fluency, not coding. You do not need to write code, but you should be able to read an architecture diagram, ask a meaningful question about API latency, and understand why a technical decision creates schedule risk. Candidates who can connect technical trade-offs to program milestones tend to advance further in the process.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

  1. Research Zenoti's product. Spend time on their website to understand their core modules: appointments, POS, marketing, and payroll for wellness businesses. Know their enterprise client profile. Expect one or two scenario questions rooted in this context.
  1. Prepare five to six STAR stories. Cover: a program that slipped, a stakeholder conflict, a technical dependency you unblocked, a metrics-driven pivot, and a cross-functional alignment win. Each story should name specific artifacts and decisions.
  1. Practice naming artifacts. For every story, be ready to say exactly which document or tool you used and why you chose it over alternatives.
  1. Review core frameworks. OKRs, RACI, RAID logs, and agile release trains. Know when each adds value and when it adds overhead without helping.
  1. Prepare questions for each interviewer. Good ones include: 'What does a program look like six months after a TPM joins it versus before?' and 'How does the TPM role interact with product managers here?'
  1. Scan recent interview reports. The day before your interview, check Glassdoor and LinkedIn for recent Zenoti TPM experiences to catch any new formats candidates have reported.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Staying too abstract. Saying 'I aligned stakeholders' without naming who, what the conflict was, or what artifact you used loses credibility quickly. Specificity is what separates strong TPM candidates from weaker ones.
  1. Claiming ownership you only partially had. Interviewers ask targeted follow-up questions specifically to test this. Own your contribution accurately and you will come across as more credible, not less.
  1. Ignoring the enterprise angle. Zenoti's clients are large wellness chains with complex operational needs. Answers that assume a pure internal-product context can miss the mark for their interviewers.
  1. Skipping the retrospective. In failure stories, 'what changed afterward' is often what interviewers remember most. Describing the process improvement signals maturity and genuine ownership.
  1. Overclaiming technical depth. If you are not hands-on with code, say so clearly, then demonstrate the technical vocabulary and curiosity that makes you effective as a TPM bridge between engineering and business.
  1. Not asking questions. Candidates who have nothing to ask at the end of a round are often read as low-engagement or underprepared.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Zenoti TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from three to five rounds, typically including a recruiter screen, one or two behavioral rounds, a technical or case discussion, and a final conversation with a senior leader. The exact structure can vary by team, so confirm the format with your recruiter after the initial screen.

Is there a coding round or technical assessment?

Most candidates report no coding test for TPM roles at Zenoti. The technical portion, if there is one, typically involves discussing a system design scenario or walking through a past technical program. Being able to read a basic architecture diagram and ask smart engineering questions matters more than writing code.

How important is domain knowledge of the wellness or beauty industry?

You do not need prior industry experience, but showing you researched Zenoti's product, their client profile (spas, salons, medispas), and their enterprise model signals genuine preparation. Candidates who reference specific Zenoti features or client challenges in their answers tend to stand out from those who give purely generic TPM responses.

What salary can I expect for a TPM role at Zenoti?

Zenoti has not published official salary bands. Glassdoor and publicly reported data for senior TPM roles at mid-size SaaS companies in Bangalore show a wide range, with figures commonly cited varying significantly by experience level and internal leveling. Ask your recruiter for the band early in the process so you can evaluate any offer clearly.

How long does the hiring process take from first round to offer?

Candidates report timelines ranging from two to four weeks for the full process, though some report longer gaps between individual rounds. Following up with your recruiter after each round is standard practice and is generally well-received.

Can I negotiate the offer at Zenoti?

Yes. Candidates report that Zenoti does engage in offer negotiation, particularly on base salary and joining bonus. Come prepared with publicly reported market data from Glassdoor or levels.fyi to anchor the conversation, and be specific about what you are asking for and why.
