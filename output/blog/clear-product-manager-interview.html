01 Overview

Overview

Clear (formerly ClearTax) is one of India's most recognised fintech companies, known for simplifying income tax filing, GST compliance, and business invoicing for millions of users. Knok's jobradar tracks 2009 open Product Manager roles across India as of July 2026, and Clear accounts for 188 of them, placing it among the more active fintech employers for PMs right now.

Bangalore leads overall PM demand in the broader market with 271 openings, and Delhi follows with 177. Clear's product and engineering teams are primarily based in Bangalore, so most of their PM roles sit there. The company's portfolio covers both B2C products (individual ITR filing, personal finance tools) and B2B SaaS (GST filing and invoicing for businesses), and interviewers expect you to think across both customer segments with equal comfort.

PM interviews at Clear typically span multiple rounds. Candidates report a product sense or case study round, a metrics and analytical thinking discussion, and a closing round with a senior leader or hiring manager. Some teams also include a take-home case study before the live rounds. Preparation of four to six weeks is commonly cited by candidates who have cleared the process.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Design a feature that helps a first-time taxpayer file their ITR without needing a CA.

*Situation:* At my previous fintech role, we found that a large share of new sign-ups dropped off before completing their first tax filing. User interviews pointed to one root cause: the product assumed prior knowledge of tax forms and income categories that first-time filers simply did not have.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the onboarding flow for first-time filers to reduce drop-off and make the process feel guided rather than overwhelming.

*Action:* I ran a discovery sprint with user researchers and a tax domain expert. We mapped the full filing journey and found three high-anxiety moments: picking the right ITR form, entering income from multiple sources, and reviewing before final submission. I designed a 'smart questionnaire' flow that asked plain questions like 'Did you switch jobs this year?' and auto-selected the correct form. We added short inline explanations in plain English and Hindi for every confusing field, plus a pre-submission checklist that flagged common errors before the user hit submit.

*Result:* The pilot showed a clear improvement in first-time completion rates. Support ticket volume for filing-related queries dropped, and post-launch feedback showed new users felt significantly more confident completing the process on their own.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you had to shut down or deprioritise a feature.

*Situation:* At my last company, we had invested several weeks building an automated expense tracker for small business users. The feature had strong internal buy-in and the engineering team was proud of the early prototype.

*Task:* When I reviewed the mid-point data from user testing, adoption intent was lower than our initial assumptions, and the engineering effort to maintain the bank-statement parsing logic was higher than originally scoped.

*Action:* I presented the findings honestly to the leadership team, including a quick competitive review showing two well-funded apps already offered this feature with far more resources behind them. I recommended pausing the expense tracker and pivoting to a simpler 'invoice uploader' that addressed the same underlying pain point with a fraction of the build effort. I framed it as a portfolio trade-off, not a project failure.

*Result:* The invoice uploader shipped in a few weeks and saw stronger early adoption than the expense tracker had projected. The freed engineering bandwidth went into a higher-priority integration that launched before the quarter ended, and the team appreciated the honest, data-backed rationale.

---

Q: Describe a time you disagreed with your engineering team on technical feasibility.

*Situation:* I was leading a real-time reconciliation feature for a GST product. I had committed to a launch timeline based on initial scoping conversations with the team.

*Task:* Mid-sprint, the engineering lead flagged that the reconciliation matching logic was far more complex than estimated, and hitting the original date would mean shipping with known quality gaps.

*Action:* Rather than pushing back on the engineering team, I called a joint session with the engineering lead, QA lead, and our product head. We broke the feature into two phases: a basic reconciliation release and an advanced matching release. I renegotiated the stakeholder commitment to deliver Phase 1 on time and repositioned Phase 2 with a separate milestone and updated success metrics.

*Result:* Phase 1 shipped on schedule. Users appreciated having partial reconciliation support immediately, and the engineering team had the space to build Phase 2 correctly without cutting corners. The phased delivery approach became a model the team applied to later complex features.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product design questions: The CIRCLES method works well here. Comprehend the situation, Identify the user segment, Report their needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, and Summarise. For Clear, always ground your answer in the regulatory or compliance context of the user's problem. A solution that ignores ITR filing deadlines or GST return cycles is incomplete, no matter how elegant the UI.

Metrics questions: Use a layered approach. Start with the North Star metric (what does success look like for this product in one number?), then define input metrics (what actions drive the North Star?), guardrail metrics (what must not break?), and diagnostic metrics (what tells you why something changed?). For Clear's consumer tax product, the North Star is often successful, error-free filings per active user per season.

Prioritisation questions: RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) works for comparing features. For Clear, 'Reach' carries extra weight because the platform serves a very large user base, so even a small improvement in a high-traffic flow has large absolute impact. Use the value-versus-effort matrix for quick triage during the interview.

Behavioural questions: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every 'tell me about a time' prompt. Keep the Situation brief, spend most of your time on the Action, and always close with a measurable or observable Result. Quantify where you genuinely can, but do not invent numbers.

Root cause and diagnostic questions: Use a structured funnel approach. Identify where in the user journey the problem appears, form two or three hypotheses, describe what data you would pull to validate each, and propose a fix. Clear interviewers reward structured thinking over landing on the 'right' answer quickly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fintech and regulatory comfort: Clear's products exist inside India's tax and GST framework. Interviewers want to see that you can design within real constraints (ITR filing deadlines, mandatory government data fields, portal integrations) without those constraints becoming an excuse for a poor user experience.

Simplification instinct: Clear's core mission is making complex financial tasks simple. Interviewers consistently reward candidates who can take a multi-step compliance process and design it into something a non-expert can complete confidently. If your answer adds complexity, expect a direct pushback.

Data-first thinking: You will be expected to define metrics, describe how you would instrument a feature, and diagnose a drop-off using data. Vague answers like 'I would look at engagement' do not land well. Name the specific metric, the query you would run, and the threshold that would trigger action.

Cross-segment thinking: Clear serves individual consumers and B2B customers on the same platform infrastructure. Interviewers want to see that you understand how a decision made for one segment can affect the other, and that you weigh those trade-offs deliberately.

Ownership and candour: Clear values PMs who own outcomes, not just deliverables. Be prepared to talk about things that did not go as planned, what you learned, and what you would do differently. Candidates who share only success stories typically score lower than those who show genuine, structured reflection.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product deeply. Use Clear's app to explore the ITR filing flow or the GST dashboard as a business user. Read their product blog and any publicly available case studies. Understand the difference between the main ITR form types, since these represent distinct user segments with very different needs and anxiety points.

Week 2: Build your metrics vocabulary. For each of Clear's major product lines (consumer tax, GST, invoicing), define a North Star metric and three supporting input metrics. Practice articulating these out loud, not just writing them down. Study how fintech companies measure activation, retention, and monetisation using publicly reported industry surveys.

Week 3: Case study practice. Solve two product design cases per day with a timer. Focus on cases involving simplification of a complex process, growth for an underserved segment (Tier 2 cities, self-employed users), and competitive response scenarios. Record yourself and listen back for clarity and structure.

Week 4: Behavioural prep. Write out your top five product stories in STAR format. Cover: a feature you killed, a cross-functional conflict you resolved, a launch that did not go as planned, a data-driven decision you made, and a time you advocated for the user against internal pressure.

Final days before the interview: Review Clear's recent product updates and the job description one more time. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for each interviewer based on their background and the team they work on. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going too generic on product design: Candidates often apply a generic consumer app framework to Clear's product questions and miss the regulatory dimension entirely. A feature for tax filing is not the same as a feature for a food delivery app. Always acknowledge the compliance or deadline constraint in your answer.

Skipping the user segmentation step: Clear serves first-time filers, salaried professionals, freelancers, and large enterprises. Candidates who jump straight to a solution without asking 'which user are we solving for?' get called out consistently by interviewers.

Vague metrics: Saying 'I would track engagement and retention' without defining what those mean for a product that most users touch only once a year is a red flag. Clear interviewers expect you to explain how you measure a seasonally used product with very specific activity windows.

Overclaiming results in STAR answers: Presenting outcomes that sound implausibly large tends to invite sceptical follow-up questions. Stick to results you can defend, and it is fine to say the impact was qualitative or early-stage.

Not asking clarifying questions before solving: Jumping into a case answer without clarifying the goal, the user, or the constraints signals poor PM instincts. Spend your first minute or two asking smart clarifying questions before you start solving.

Conflating B2C and B2B users: Clear's consumer and business products have different buying motives, usage patterns, and churn triggers. Treating them as the same user in your answers suggests you have not done your homework on the company.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Clear PM interview process typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds, though this varies by team and seniority level. The rounds commonly include a product sense or case study round, a metrics and analytical thinking discussion, and a final round with a senior leader or hiring manager. Some teams also add a take-home case study before the live rounds.

Does Clear ask coding or SQL questions in PM interviews?

Candidates generally report that Clear PM interviews do not require you to write code. SQL or basic data query questions sometimes come up in the analytical round, but being able to describe what data you would pull and why is typically sufficient. If you are targeting a more technical PM role, brushing up on basic SQL aggregations and filtering is a safe precaution.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Clear?

Based on the knok jobradar data for July 2026, PM roles in this space range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with three to six years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs, and 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and your negotiation. For the most current community-reported figures, Glassdoor and levels.fyi track fintech PM compensation in India.

Is fintech or tax domain experience required to get a PM role at Clear?

Domain experience helps but is not always a hard requirement, especially at the Associate PM or mid-level. Candidates from e-commerce, SaaS, and consumer internet backgrounds have cleared the process by demonstrating strong product thinking and doing thorough homework on Clear's products before the interview. At Senior PM level and above, some fintech or regulatory product experience becomes a stronger advantage.

How should I prepare for Clear's product design case questions?

Start by using Clear's app as a real user so you understand the actual flows, pain points, and moments of confusion firsthand. In your answer, always ground the design in a specific user segment and acknowledge any regulatory or compliance constraint that shapes the solution. Practice structuring your answer around: user, problem, solution options, trade-offs, and success metrics. Interviewers at Clear tend to push back hard on solutions that ignore the complexity of the Indian tax or GST context.

Where are most Clear PM jobs located?

Clear's product and engineering teams are primarily based in Bangalore. The broader PM job market in India, as tracked by knok's jobradar data for July 2026, shows Bangalore with 271 openings and Delhi with 177 across all companies. If you are not in Bangalore, check whether the specific Clear role has a remote or hybrid option, as policies vary by team.
