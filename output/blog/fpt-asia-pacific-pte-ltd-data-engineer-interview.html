01 Overview

Overview

FPT Asia Pacific Pte Ltd is the Singapore-headquartered arm of FPT Corporation, one of Southeast Asia's largest IT services companies. The company provides digital transformation, cloud migration, data engineering, and AI services to enterprise clients across banking, telecom, manufacturing, and retail sectors in the Asia Pacific region.

As of July 2026, FPT Asia Pacific has 30 active Data Engineer openings listed in India. Nationally, 542 Data Engineer positions are live across major cities, with Bangalore leading at 92 roles and Delhi at 66, followed by Hyderabad and Pune (23 each), Chennai (14), and Mumbai (8).

Data Engineers at FPT Asia Pacific typically build and maintain data pipelines, support cloud platform migrations, and deliver reporting and analytics infrastructure directly for enterprise client accounts. The role combines solid engineering with a client-service mindset, since outcomes are delivered to external clients rather than internal product teams.

What to expect in the interview: Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening, a technical round covering SQL, Python, and pipeline concepts, and a final conversation with a team lead or manager. Rounds and format can vary by team and seniority level. Confirm the structure with your recruiter before each stage.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in FPT Asia Pacific Data Engineer interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. Walk me through a data pipeline you designed and owned end to end. What tools did you choose and why?
  2. How do you handle late-arriving or out-of-order data in a streaming pipeline?
  3. Write a SQL query to find the second-highest value in a column without using LIMIT or TOP.
  4. What is the difference between a data lake and a data warehouse? When would you recommend each to a client?
  5. How do you ensure data quality when your source systems are unreliable or send inconsistent formats?
  6. Describe your hands-on experience with Apache Spark or PySpark. What optimisations have you applied in a real project?
  7. FPT works in client-delivery environments where requirements shift. Tell me about a time you shipped a data solution when the scope or timeline changed mid-project.
  8. What is a slowly changing dimension (SCD) Type 2, and when would you choose it over Type 1?
  9. How would you design a pipeline to ingest and process a large daily volume of records at low latency?
  10. What cloud-native data services have you worked with? Walk me through a specific setup you built.
  11. How do you document your pipelines so that another engineer can maintain them without coming back to ask you questions?
  12. How do you prioritise when you are balancing urgent pipeline maintenance issues and new development work at the same time?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a data pipeline you built end to end.

*Situation:* My team needed a daily sales reporting pipeline for a retail client. Data arrived from three sources: a transactional PostgreSQL database, a flat-file FTP drop, and a third-party REST API.

*Task:* I was responsible for designing, building, and owning the pipeline so that clean, joined data landed in our Redshift warehouse and analysts had it ready by early morning each day.

*Action:* I used Apache Airflow to orchestrate the full pipeline. I wrote Python scripts to pull from the API and FTP, used dbt for transformations in the warehouse, and added data quality checks via Great Expectations to catch nulls and schema drift before data reached the reporting layer. I set up alerting so any DAG failure sent an immediate notification to the team.

*Result:* The pipeline ran reliably for eight months. Analysts consistently had clean data before the start of business, and the team was able to stop the manual reconciliation work they had been doing each morning.

---

Q: How do you ensure data quality in a pipeline with unreliable sources?

*Situation:* At a previous role, we integrated data from a third-party logistics partner whose API returned inconsistent field formats and occasionally sent duplicate records.

*Task:* I needed the pipeline to stay accurate for downstream dashboards even when the upstream data had problems.

*Action:* I built a validation layer in Python that checked for required fields, value range anomalies, and duplicate IDs before any record touched the warehouse. Records that failed went to a quarantine table with an error code and timestamp. I also built a daily summary showing quarantine volume and failure reasons so problems were visible without anyone needing to dig through logs.

*Result:* Dashboard accuracy improved markedly. The quarantine reports also gave us concrete evidence to share with the vendor, who fixed two recurring issues on their end within a month of receiving our summaries.

---

Q: Tell me about delivering a data solution under a tight deadline.

*Situation:* A client needed a migration from on-premises SQL Server to Azure Synapse completed before their financial year close. The available window was significantly shorter than the original project plan allowed.

*Task:* My responsibility was to migrate the ETL jobs and confirm that all critical reports ran correctly in the new environment before go-live.

*Action:* I triaged the pipeline list and focused first on the ten reports the client used most. I ran parallel testing environments so we could validate without touching production. I gave the client's data lead a brief daily update so there were no surprises near the end.

*Result:* We went live ahead of the deadline. A few minor issues surfaced post-launch and were resolved quickly. The client marked the project as a success in their post-implementation review.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions: Structure your response as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences each) so most of your answer covers Action and Result. Always try to give a concrete outcome, even an approximate one. An open-ended story with no result lands weaker than one with a rough measure of success.

Three-step frame for design questions: Start by clarifying requirements (data volume, latency, SLA, known constraints). Then describe your architecture and the trade-offs you considered. Finally, explain how you would monitor and maintain it in production. This signals to FPT interviewers that you think beyond the initial build and understand what supporting a system on a long client engagement actually involves.

Think aloud for SQL and coding rounds: FPT interviewers typically evaluate how you reason, not just whether you reach the correct answer. Narrate your thinking as you write, name the edge cases you are checking for, and correct yourself calmly if you catch a mistake. Silence during a coding question often reads as uncertainty rather than focus.

For handling ambiguity: Describe how you ask clarifying questions first, propose a minimal workable solution, then iterate based on feedback. This maps directly to the agile, client-facing culture FPT operates in and signals that you will surface misalignments early rather than building the wrong thing for weeks.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have interviewed at FPT Asia Pacific describe a panel that rewards practical, hands-on experience and a service-delivery mindset over purely theoretical depth.

Client-facing communication. FPT delivers outcomes directly to enterprise clients, so interviewers look for engineers who can explain technical decisions in plain terms and handle shifting requirements without friction.

Full ownership of pipelines. Interviewers want evidence that you designed, launched, and supported a pipeline over time, not just coded something and handed it off. Bring up monitoring, alerting, and how you responded when something broke in production.

Hands-on cloud and tooling depth. Experience with at least one major cloud platform and working knowledge of tools such as Spark, Airflow, dbt, or Kafka comes up consistently in candidate feedback. Genuine depth on a few tools matters more than surface familiarity with many.

Documentation and maintainability habits. FPT teams work across time zones on multi-month client engagements. Interviewers ask about documentation because poorly maintained pipelines create real problems for the next engineer on the account.

Collaborative approach. Several candidates report questions designed to probe how you handle disagreements with teammates, pick up code someone else wrote, or respond when a colleague's change caused a data issue downstream. FPT is a services company, and team dynamics have a direct impact on client outcomes.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical revision
Revise SQL in depth, covering window functions, common table expressions, query optimisation, and multi-table joins. Practise writing queries by hand without IDE autocomplete, since some FPT technical rounds involve live coding in a plain editor. Brush up on Python for data work: writing clean, readable functions, working with pandas, and handling file and API input.

Week 2: Systems and pipeline design
Review the trade-offs between batch and streaming pipelines. Be able to explain Spark's execution model, Airflow DAG structure, and how you handle schema evolution in a pipeline that runs for months. If your cloud experience is narrow, spend time with the documentation for at least one managed data service on AWS, Azure, or GCP so you can speak to architecture decisions, not just feature names.

Week 3: FPT-specific preparation
Research FPT Asia Pacific's publicly stated focus areas: digital transformation, cloud services, and sector verticals such as banking, telecom, and manufacturing. Prepare two or three examples from your own experience that connect to these domains. Practise your answer to 'why FPT' with something specific to their client-delivery model rather than a generic response about company size or growth.

Before the interview
Prepare three STAR stories covering: a pipeline you designed and owned end to end, a data quality problem you identified and resolved, and a time you delivered under pressure or with changed constraints. Map your experience to each requirement listed in the job description. Candidates report that FPT interviewers often ask directly about items stated in the JD.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving only theoretical answers. FPT interviewers probe for real, specific experience. Saying 'Spark is used for distributed processing' without a concrete project behind it does not land well. Anchor every technical answer in something you actually built or operated.

Skipping the result. Candidates who describe what they did without saying what happened miss a key part of the answer. Even a rough outcome ('the client approved go-live on schedule', 'query time dropped noticeably after the change') is better than leaving the story unfinished.

Underestimating the SQL round. Several candidates report that FPT SQL questions go beyond basics, covering window functions, recursive CTEs, or query performance analysis. Prepare for questions at that depth, not just simple SELECT statements.

Not preparing questions to ask. FPT interviewers typically leave time for your questions. Having none signals low engagement. Strong questions include: 'What does the data infrastructure look like for the client team I would be supporting?' or 'How does the team manage schema changes on long-running client engagements?'

A generic 'why this company' answer. Responses like 'FPT is a great company with good growth opportunities' do not resonate with the panel. Reference their Asia Pacific client base, a specific sector vertical you want to work in, or their IT services delivery model.

Jumping straight to a solution on design questions. Candidates who skip clarifying questions often design for the wrong constraints. Take a moment to ask about scale, latency, and any known restrictions before proposing your architecture.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the FPT Asia Pacific Data Engineer interview typically involve?

Candidates report a process that typically involves two to three stages: an initial recruiter or HR screening, a technical round covering SQL, Python, and pipeline design, and sometimes a final conversation with a team lead or hiring manager. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level. Confirm the format with your recruiter before each stage so you are not caught off guard.

What salary can a Data Engineer expect at FPT Asia Pacific in India?

FPT Asia Pacific does not publicly disclose its pay bands. Data Engineer salaries across India broadly range from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 14-26 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 42-65+ LPA at Lead or Staff level. Cross-check FPT-specific figures on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before your negotiation, keeping in mind that sample sizes for any single employer can be small.

Does FPT Asia Pacific give a take-home assignment for Data Engineer interviews?

Candidate feedback suggests that a live SQL or Python coding exercise during the technical round is more common than a separate take-home task, though this can vary by team. Go in prepared to write and explain code on the spot. If a take-home is assigned, treat it like production code: clean structure, clear variable naming, and a short note explaining your design choices.

Which cloud platform should I focus on when preparing for FPT Asia Pacific?

FPT Asia Pacific works across AWS, Azure, and GCP depending on the client, so all three appear in job descriptions. Rather than covering all three at a shallow level, go deep on the platform where you have genuine project experience. Candidates report that interviewers value the ability to discuss architecture decisions and trade-offs on a familiar platform over broad but surface-level knowledge of all three.

How long does the hiring process at FPT Asia Pacific take from application to offer?

Candidates report that the process typically takes two to four weeks from first contact to offer, though senior roles or those requiring multiple stakeholder approvals can take longer. Following up once with your recruiter after a week of silence is standard practice and generally well received. Keep other applications moving in parallel rather than waiting on a single outcome.

Is it sensible to apply to several Data Engineer roles at FPT Asia Pacific at the same time?

FPT Asia Pacific currently has 30 open Data Engineer positions, so multiple teams are actively hiring in parallel. Applying to roles that fit your experience level and preferred city is a reasonable approach. Keep your stated background consistent across applications, since recruiters at the same company can sometimes cross-reference candidates. If you receive multiple interview invites, ask your recruiter whether the roles sit on different teams to avoid duplication.
