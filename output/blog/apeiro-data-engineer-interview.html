02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on candidate reports and the nature of the Data Engineer role at Apeiro, these are the questions most likely to come up:

  1. Walk me through a data pipeline you built end to end. What were the main bottlenecks and how did you resolve them?
  2. How do you handle late-arriving or out-of-order data in a streaming pipeline?
  3. Write a SQL query to find the top N earners in each department without window functions, then rewrite it using window functions.
  4. Explain the difference between a star schema and a snowflake schema. When would you prefer one over the other?
  5. How would you design a data warehouse for a company processing millions of events per day?
  6. What is data partitioning and how does it affect query performance on large tables?
  7. How do you ensure data quality and freshness in your pipelines? What checks do you put in place?
  8. Describe a time you had to debug a slow or failing ETL job in production. What was your process?
  9. How do you handle schema evolution in a data lake without breaking downstream consumers?
  10. What is the difference between batch and stream processing? Give a real example of when you chose each.
  11. How do you approach cost optimisation on a cloud data platform?
  12. Tell me about a time you worked with messy or incomplete source data. What did you do?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you debugged a slow or failing ETL job in production.

*Situation:* Our nightly ETL pipeline at my previous company started timing out after a routine schema change in the source database.

*Task:* I was responsible for keeping the pipeline running with zero data loss and minimal delay to the downstream reporting team.

*Action:* I checked the logs and identified the exact step where the job was hanging. A new column with an unindexed foreign key was being joined on, causing a full table scan. I coordinated with the DBA to add an index on the source side, rewrote the join to use a broadcast hint in Spark for the smaller lookup table, and added an alerting step so any future slowdown above a threshold would page the on-call engineer.

*Result:* The pipeline run time dropped from several hours to well under an hour. The downstream team had their reports on time, and the alerting caught two smaller issues in the following month before they became incidents.

---

Q: How have you handled schema evolution in a data lake without breaking downstream consumers?

*Situation:* A source system team at my previous job announced they were adding and renaming several columns in their event stream over a two-week rollout.

*Task:* I needed to absorb those changes without breaking three downstream dashboards and two ML feature pipelines that depended on the existing schema.

*Action:* I introduced schema-on-read with a compatibility layer using Delta Lake's schema evolution feature. I also wrote a column mapping config that translated old column names to new ones, so downstream queries continued to work without any code changes on their side. I communicated a deprecation timeline and held a short sync with both the ML and BI teams.

*Result:* All downstream consumers kept working through the entire rollout. We deprecated the old column aliases a month later with no incidents.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked with messy or incomplete source data.

*Situation:* I was building a customer churn model feature store, and the raw CRM export had a large portion of records missing the 'last purchase date' field.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to drop those records, impute values, or flag them, and document that decision clearly for the data science team.

*Action:* I ran a quick analysis and found the missing records were clustered in one region where the CRM had been migrated late. I cross-joined with a separate order table to recover most of the missing dates. For the remaining records, I added a boolean 'date_imputed' flag and used median imputation within the same region and customer segment. I documented all of this in the pipeline README and in the feature store metadata.

*Result:* The data science team recovered usable data for the majority of the originally missing records. The 'date_imputed' flag let them exclude or down-weight those rows in model training as they saw fit.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For SQL and coding questions: Think out loud before you type. State your approach, write the query or code, then walk through edge cases such as nulls, duplicates, and empty sets. Candidates who explain their reasoning consistently score higher than those who produce a correct answer silently.

For system design questions: Use a 4-step structure: (1) clarify the scale and constraints, (2) sketch the high-level architecture, (3) go deep on one component the interviewer focuses on most, (4) discuss trade-offs honestly. Do not jump straight into tools like Kafka or Airflow without first establishing why those tools fit the stated problem.

For behavioural questions: Use the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief at 2-3 sentences each. Spend most of your time on the Action, specifically what *you* did personally, not what the team did. Quantify the Result wherever possible using real numbers from your own work.

For data modelling questions: Start by asking about the business question the model needs to answer. Interviewers value candidates who think about the consumer of the data, not just the source system.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have done well in Apeiro Data Engineer interviews typically report that the panel looks for a few specific things.

Ownership and follow-through. Interviewers want to hear that you saw a problem to its end, not just your part of it. Saying 'I handed it off to the DBA' without explaining what happened next is a yellow flag.

Clarity over cleverness. A clean, readable SQL query with a brief explanation beats a one-liner that nobody else can maintain. The same thinking applies to pipeline design.

Practical cloud and tooling experience. Familiarity with at least one major cloud data stack (AWS Glue, Redshift, BigQuery, Azure Data Factory, or Databricks) is expected. Knowing the trade-offs between tools matters more than knowing every feature of one.

Data quality instincts. Candidates who proactively mention validation, monitoring, and alerting, without being asked, consistently stand out. Data quality is not treated as an afterthought in strong data teams.

Communication. Data Engineers at Apeiro typically sit between source system teams and analytics or ML consumers. The ability to explain technical decisions to non-technical stakeholders is valued at every level.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused 2-3 week plan covers the core areas without burning you out.

Week 1: SQL and Python foundations. Practise window functions, CTEs, and query optimisation daily. Write at least 5 PySpark or Pandas transformation exercises by hand without relying on an IDE. Review how join algorithms work under the hood, covering hash joins, merge joins, and broadcast joins.

Week 2: Pipeline and system design. Sketch 2-3 end-to-end data pipeline designs from scratch. Good examples include a CDC pipeline, a streaming analytics system, and a dimensional warehouse for e-commerce. For each design, write down the trade-offs you would discuss with an interviewer. Review Delta Lake or Apache Iceberg table formats if you have not used them recently.

Week 3: Behavioural prep and mock interviews. Write out 5-6 STAR stories from your own experience covering: a production incident you resolved, a time you improved pipeline performance, a time you disagreed on a technical approach, and a project you are most proud of. Do at least one timed mock interview with a peer or on a practice platform.

Before the Apeiro interview. Read whatever is publicly available about Apeiro's product and data use cases. Candidates report that showing genuine curiosity about the business, not just the tech stack, leaves a strong impression in the hiring manager conversation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to solutions without clarifying. Many candidates launch into a system design or coding answer before asking basic scoping questions. Interviewers notice and often treat this as a sign of poor real-world judgement.

Using 'we' throughout behavioural answers. Saying 'we built the pipeline' tells the interviewer nothing about your individual contribution. Be specific: say 'I designed the ingestion layer while my colleague handled the transformation side.'

Ignoring edge cases in code. Producing a correct SQL query for the happy path but not mentioning how it handles nulls or duplicates is a common slip. Always mention edge cases, even if you do not code them during the interview itself.

Over-engineering system design answers. Proposing a Kafka plus Flink plus Delta Lake stack for a use case that genuinely needs a simple Airflow DAG and a few dbt models is a red flag. Match the solution to the stated scale and constraints.

Not asking any questions at the end. Apeiro interviewers, like most, rate genuine curiosity positively. Prepare 2-3 specific questions about the team's current data stack, their biggest technical challenges, or how data quality is handled today.
