01 Overview

Overview

Ecso Global is a technology and digital services firm that partners with enterprise clients across sectors like banking, retail, and logistics. A Technical Program Manager role here means owning end-to-end delivery of large, cross-functional programs while keeping engineering, product, and business teams aligned.

Ecso Global currently has 3 open TPM roles listed. The interview process typically spans several rounds covering behavioural scenarios, technical depth, and program management judgment. Candidates report the process feels structured but conversational, with interviewers pressing for specific examples from your past work rather than generic answers about how you 'would' handle a situation.

This guide covers the questions you are most likely to face, how to structure strong answers, and how to prepare so you walk in confident.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from Ecso Global TPM interviews. Prepare a real example for each.

  1. Walk us through a complex program you owned from kickoff to delivery. What made it complex?
  2. How do you handle a situation where two engineering teams have conflicting priorities on a shared dependency?
  3. Describe a time you had to push back on a stakeholder's timeline. How did you manage that conversation?
  4. How do you track and communicate program risks before they turn into blockers?
  5. Tell us about a program that slipped. What happened and what did you do?
  6. How do you ensure alignment between product, engineering, and business teams across a large program?
  7. Describe your approach to building a program roadmap when requirements are still evolving.
  8. How do you manage vendors or third-party partners in a program that has hard deadlines?
  9. Give an example of a time you used data to change a program decision.
  10. How do you onboard yourself quickly into a new program or domain you know little about?
  11. Describe a situation where you had to escalate an issue. How did you decide when to escalate?
  12. How do you keep distributed or remote teams aligned across different time zones?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep each answer to two to three minutes when spoken aloud.

Q: How do you handle two engineering teams with conflicting resource needs?

*Situation:* At my previous company, two engineering teams both needed the same senior backend engineer during the same sprint cycle for separate critical deliverables.

*Task:* I needed to resolve the conflict without delaying either program's critical path or burning out the engineer.

*Action:* I called a joint session with both team leads and mapped out the dependency timelines together. We found that one team needed the engineer only at the start of the sprint and the other only toward the end. I restructured the sprint plan to sequence the work and documented a clear handoff protocol so there was no ambiguity.

*Result:* Both programs stayed on schedule. The engineer was not overloaded, and the team leads reused the sequencing approach for future conflicts.

---

Q: Tell us about a program that slipped.

*Situation:* I was managing a client-facing API integration program. Mid-sprint, the third-party provider changed their authentication specs without advance notice.

*Task:* I had to contain the impact, communicate clearly to stakeholders, and get the program back on track without losing client trust.

*Action:* I immediately convened a technical bridge call, assessed the revised engineering effort needed, and drafted an updated timeline within the day. I sent a structured status update to leadership with three options: absorb the delay, reduce scope, or bring in a contractor. Leadership chose to reduce scope, which I documented as a formal change request.

*Result:* We delivered the revised scope on the new agreed date. The client specifically noted the transparent communication. The change request format became a standard template for the team.

---

Q: Give an example of using data to change a program decision.

*Situation:* The product team wanted a simultaneous full-user launch of a new feature. I was managing the backend readiness program.

*Task:* I needed to make a risk-based case for a phased rollout instead of pushing back without evidence.

*Action:* I pulled load test results and historical incident logs from our monitoring dashboards. The data showed a consistent pattern of instability during previous full-traffic launches. I built a one-page comparison of risk scenarios and presented it in the steering committee meeting.

*Result:* The team agreed to a phased rollout. The launch went smoothly, and the phased approach caught a configuration issue before it reached all users.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (the core framework): Every behavioural answer should have a clear Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Interviewers at Ecso Global typically probe the Action step hardest, so spend the most time there. Describe exactly what you personally did, not what the team did.

The 'so what' close: After your Result, add one sentence on what you learned or what changed because of your actions. This shows reflective thinking, which TPM interviewers consistently value.

Handling 'how would you' questions: When a question is hypothetical, anchor it in a real example. Say 'I would approach it the way I did at X, where...' This keeps your answer concrete and credible.

Structuring ambiguous questions: If a question feels broad, pause and say 'I want to make sure I answer the most relevant part. Are you more interested in the stakeholder side or the technical coordination side?' This signals senior communication instincts.

Numbers and impact: Where you have real outcome data from your own work, use it. If the data is approximate, say so. Fabricating precise figures is easy to spot and damages credibility instantly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

TPM interviews at Ecso Global, candidates report, are designed to test ownership and communication above all else.

Clear ownership: Interviewers want to see that you drove programs, not just coordinated them. Phrases like 'I decided', 'I escalated', and 'I owned the timeline' signal ownership. Phrases like 'the team felt' or 'we generally tried to' signal that you were a participant, not a driver.

Technical fluency without deep coding: You do not need to write code, but you must understand engineering trade-offs well enough to hold credible conversations with architects and developers. Expect questions on API design, system dependencies, and release risk.

Stakeholder management: Ecso Global works with enterprise clients, so candidates who demonstrate experience managing upward, across teams, and with external partners score well. Show that you can say no professionally and that you document decisions carefully.

Structured communication: Interviewers notice how you structure your own answers. If you ramble or skip the result, they will wonder if your program updates are also unclear. Practise speaking in a logical sequence before the interview.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: story bank
Write out five to seven programs from your career. For each, note the complexity, the key conflict or risk you managed, and the measurable outcome. These become the raw material for every behavioural question.

Week two: technical refresh
Review concepts that come up in TPM interviews: dependency graphs, critical path method, risk registers, OKRs, and agile sprint mechanics. You do not need to be an expert, but you should be able to discuss trade-offs confidently.

Research Ecso Global
Look up Ecso Global's service lines, recent client announcements, and any publicly available case studies. Tailor your examples to the industries they serve. They currently have 3 open TPM roles, which suggests active delivery programs worth asking about in the interview.

Mock interviews
Do at least two timed mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Record yourself if no partner is available. The goal is to get your STAR answers to a clean two-minute length.

Questions to ask the interviewer
Prepare a handful of genuine questions about the programs currently running, the team structure, and how success is measured for a new TPM in the role. This signals genuine interest and helps you assess fit.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Vague answers: The most common TPM interview mistake is answering in generalities. 'I usually create a RACI and hold weekly syncs' tells the interviewer nothing. Replace every 'usually' with a specific example.

Taking credit for team outcomes only: Interviewers want to know what you specifically did. If a project succeeded because an engineer made a brilliant decision, say so and describe how you created the conditions for that to happen.

Skipping the result: Many candidates describe the Situation and Action in detail, then trail off. Always close with a concrete result, even if it is qualitative. 'The client renewed the contract' or 'the team adopted the process going forward' both count.

No questions for the interviewer: Walking out of an interview without asking questions signals low engagement. Prepare genuine questions, not generic ones.

Over-explaining technical details: TPMs lose interviewers when they go deep into implementation details that only an engineer would care about. Stay at the program level: timelines, dependencies, decisions, and outcomes.

Underselling complexity: If your program was genuinely complex, describe why. Interviewers at a firm like Ecso Global expect candidates to have managed ambiguity, competing priorities, and difficult stakeholders. Do not flatten your experience to seem humble.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Ecso Global TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically involves two to four rounds, starting with a recruiter screen and moving into technical and behavioural rounds with hiring managers and potential peers. Final rounds often include a panel or a senior leader conversation. The exact structure can vary by team and location, so confirm the format with your recruiter at the start.

Is there a case study or take-home assignment?

Some candidates report a program scenario exercise, either as a live whiteboard session or a short written case. Typically you are given a messy program situation and asked to walk through how you would diagnose it, prioritise, and communicate. Prepare by practising out-loud walkthroughs of hypothetical program crises so you can think clearly under time pressure.

What salary should I expect for a TPM role at Ecso Global?

Ecso Global does not publish salary bands publicly. For comparable TPM roles in India, Glassdoor and levels.fyi list a wide range depending on years of experience, location, and seniority level. Bangalore tends to show higher figures in publicly reported data because of the competitive talent market there. Research current rates on those platforms before your offer conversation.

Does Ecso Global hire freshers or only experienced TPMs?

TPM roles are typically mid-to-senior positions requiring hands-on experience managing cross-functional programs. Most candidates report being asked for examples from real programs they owned, which means fresh graduates without relevant internship or project management experience are rarely shortlisted. If you are early in your career, consider associate PM or project coordinator roles as a stepping stone.

How important is PMP or agile certification for this role?

Certifications like PMP, CSM, or SAFe are commonly listed as preferred qualifications in TPM job descriptions, but candidates report they carry less weight than demonstrated program outcomes. A strong story bank of real programs you managed will impress interviewers more than listing certificates. Having a certification does signal baseline familiarity with program management vocabulary, so it is worth pursuing if you have the time.
