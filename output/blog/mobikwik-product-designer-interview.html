02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the question types candidates commonly report facing in MobiKwik Product Designer interviews. Prepare a clear, structured answer for each.

  1. Walk us through your portfolio. Which project are you most proud of, and why?

  3. Tell us about a time you redesigned a payment flow. What problem were you solving?
  4. How do you approach designing for trust and security in a fintech product?
  5. Describe your process when you receive conflicting feedback from product managers and engineers.

  10. Walk us through how you would improve MobiKwik's checkout experience.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you redesigned a payment flow. What problem were you solving?

*Situation:* I was working at a digital payments company where our analytics team flagged a high drop-off rate at the confirmation step of our UPI flow.

*Task:* My goal was to reduce drop-off at that screen without removing any of the security steps compliance required.

*Action:* I ran several user interviews to understand where confusion was happening. I found that users were unsure whether their payment had gone through after entering the OTP. I redesigned the confirmation screen with a clearer progress indicator, added a real-time status message ('Your payment is being processed'), and moved the transaction ID to a more visible position. I tested two variants with internal users before shipping.

*Result:* Post-launch tracking showed improvement in completion rates over the following weeks. The redesign also reduced support tickets around 'payment stuck' queries, which the customer support team reported back to us.

---

Q: How do you design for users with varying levels of digital literacy?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were launching a feature for first-time digital wallet users in Tier 2 cities.

*Task:* I needed to design an onboarding flow that worked for someone who had never used a payments app before, without making it feel patronising to more experienced users.

*Action:* I partnered with the research team to run contextual interviews with new users in two cities. I found that icon-only navigation confused first-time users, and that regional language support mattered far more than we had assumed. I proposed a short animated tutorial on first launch, replaced abstract icons with text labels, and pushed for Hindi and Marathi support in the first release. I worked with the content team to simplify copy to match the reading level our research pointed to.

*Result:* Onboarding completion improved in the cohorts we tracked after launch. The language feature was later expanded to cover more regional languages based on the positive response from the initial rollout.

---

Q: Describe a time you pushed back on a stakeholder's request.

*Situation:* A business stakeholder wanted to add several promotional banners to our app's home screen to drive cross-sell revenue during a campaign period.

*Task:* I had to balance the business goal with the user experience. Our existing research showed the home screen already felt cluttered to users, and adding more banners risked disrupting primary flows.

*Action:* Rather than saying no outright, I prepared a short document showing our usability data and proposed an alternative: a single rotating banner with a 'see more offers' entry point. I also suggested running an A/B test so the final decision would be data-backed. I presented both the data and the alternative to the stakeholder and product lead together.

*Result:* The stakeholder agreed to test both versions. The single-banner variant performed better on the metrics we tracked, and the A/B approach became the team's standard method for evaluating similar tradeoffs going forward.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use the STAR structure: Situation (the context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did), Result (what changed). Keep each component concise. Interviewers care most about the Action and Result.

For design challenge questions (such as 'improve our checkout'), a four-step walkthrough works well. Start with the user problem you are solving. Then describe your research approach or the assumptions you would test. Walk through your major design decisions and the tradeoffs you considered. End by explaining how you would measure success after shipping.

For product critique questions, candidates report that beginning with what currently works before suggesting changes comes across as more considered than leading with criticism. Show that you understand the constraints the existing design team was working under before proposing alternatives.

For 'how would you design X' questions in fintech, always name trust signals and error states early in your answer. Payment flows fail in ways that frighten users. Showing you think about failure modes signals maturity to MobiKwik interviewers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

MobiKwik interviewers typically look for designers who can operate in a fast-moving fintech environment where user trust and business constraints both matter. Based on candidate reports, these are the signals that come up most often.

User empathy backed by evidence. Interviewers want to see that your design decisions come from research or testing, not gut feel alone. Name your methods: interviews, usability tests, analytics review.

Clear reasoning behind decisions. Saying 'I chose this because it tested better with users' is stronger than 'it felt cleaner.' Be ready to explain every significant design choice.

Fintech-specific awareness. This includes trust signals, fraud-related UX patterns, error handling in payment flows, and progressive disclosure in credit product onboarding.

Cross-functional communication skills. Product Designers at MobiKwik work closely with PMs and engineers. Show that you can translate design rationale for non-designers without becoming defensive when challenged.

Android-first thinking. MobiKwik's core user base skews Android. Candidates who understand Android design guidelines and the constraints of mid-range devices tend to stand out.

End-to-end ownership in your portfolio. Show that you were involved from problem framing through to shipping, not just the visual production phase.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Use the MobiKwik app yourself. Go through the payment flow, the BNPL onboarding, and the home screen. Note what feels intuitive, what feels confusing, and where trust signals appear. You will almost certainly be asked to critique or improve part of the app.

Prepare three to four portfolio stories. Each should have a clear user problem, your specific contribution (not the team's), and a result you can speak to: a metric, a qualitative observation, or a decision it informed.

Practice a short app critique. Pick one screen from MobiKwik and prepare a structured verbal critique. Lead with what works, then describe what you would explore changing and why, keeping it grounded in user needs.

Read about fintech UX patterns. Focus on trust signals, error handling in payment flows, progressive disclosure for credit products, and designing for low-data environments. These topics come up repeatedly in MobiKwik interviews.

Prepare questions for the interviewer. Ask about how design decisions get made, what a typical sprint looks like for designers, and how the team measures design quality. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Not explaining your specific role in portfolio projects. Interviewers want to know what you did, not just what the team shipped. If you say 'we redesigned checkout,' follow it immediately with what your personal responsibility was.

Skipping the user problem definition. Jumping straight to UI solutions without first naming the problem you are solving signals shallow thinking. Start with the user, then the design.

Designing only for premium urban users. MobiKwik serves a broad range of people, including first-time internet users in smaller cities. Show that you have thought about low-bandwidth conditions, regional languages, and varying device quality.

Avoiding numbers entirely. You do not need to share confidential metrics, but being completely vague is a missed opportunity. Reference what you tracked and what direction it moved, even without exact figures.

Arriving with no questions. Candidates who have nothing to ask about the team, the product, or the design process tend to leave a weaker impression. Prepare at least two genuine questions in advance.

Treating the portfolio review as a monologue. It is a conversation. Pause occasionally, invite questions, and respond to the interviewer's cues rather than reading through rehearsed slides without adapting.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a MobiKwik Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a portfolio review, a design exercise or take-home task, and one or two cross-functional conversations with product and engineering stakeholders. The number of rounds can vary depending on seniority. Always confirm the structure with your recruiter at the start so you can prepare each stage appropriately.

Is there a take-home design assignment in the MobiKwik interview process?

Many candidates report receiving a design exercise, either as a take-home task or a live problem-solving session. Typically this involves redesigning or critiquing an existing flow, or solving a new product problem. Treat it as a structured thinking exercise: document your reasoning as clearly as your visual output, since interviewers want to follow your thought process, not just see polished screens.

What tools should I be comfortable with before interviewing at MobiKwik?

Figma is the most commonly expected tool for Product Designer roles in Indian tech companies today. Candidates report that comfort with Figma prototyping and a working knowledge of Android design guidelines is valued. Familiarity with design systems and component libraries is an added advantage, particularly for senior and lead roles.

Do I need fintech experience to get a Product Designer role at MobiKwik?

If you do not have direct fintech experience, frame your portfolio around analogous challenges: e-commerce checkout, onboarding for first-time users, or data-heavy dashboards.
