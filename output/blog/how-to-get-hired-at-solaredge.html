01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

SolarEdge builds the power electronics and software that make solar installations smarter and more efficient. Its product range covers DC-optimized inverters, power optimizers, EV chargers, and energy storage systems. The company is headquartered in Israel with engineering hubs spread across multiple countries, and its India teams work on embedded firmware, cloud and data platforms, hardware design, and customer-facing technical support.

As of mid-2026, SolarEdge has 144 open roles globally across hardware, software, operations, and sales functions. That is a meaningful number of active openings, which points to genuine growth momentum in the clean energy sector. Roles span multiple seniority levels, so whether you are a recent graduate with strong technical fundamentals or a senior engineer with deep domain expertise, there is likely something worth applying to.

The cleantech space is competitive and SolarEdge is a recognised name within it. Getting hired here is not just about technical skills. The company looks for people who are curious about sustainable energy technology and can work effectively across hardware, software, and field operations disciplines.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The SolarEdge hiring process is structured and tends to run across several stages. The exact number and format can vary by role type, team, and seniority level. Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, engineering roles typically involve more rounds than operations or support positions.

Stage 1: Application review. Your resume is screened by a recruiter, sometimes with ATS filtering first. Align your resume language closely to the specific job description, especially for roles in embedded systems, power electronics, or cloud engineering.

Stage 2: Recruiter call. A short introductory conversation with someone from the talent acquisition team. Expect questions about your current role, notice period, and what draws you to clean energy technology. Use this time to ask about team structure and role scope.

Stage 3: Technical screen. For software roles, this commonly involves a coding assessment covering data structures, algorithms, and sometimes system design concepts. Hardware and firmware candidates are typically asked about circuit theory, embedded communication protocols, or design trade-offs relevant to the role.

Stage 4: Technical deep-dive interview. One or more rounds with senior engineers or team leads. Software engineers can expect questions on system design, concurrency, language internals, and detailed project walkthroughs. Firmware engineers may be asked about real-time operating systems, memory management, and protocol stacks like I2C, SPI, or CAN bus.

Stage 5: Hiring manager round. A conversation focused on how you approach complex problems, how you have handled ambiguous or high-pressure situations in past roles, and how your background maps to the team's current priorities. Some roles include a take-home assignment or case study at this stage.

Stage 6: Offer and HR discussion. Final alignment on compensation expectations, joining timelines, and role logistics. Come prepared with a clear picture of your expectations and any questions about team structure or career growth.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

SolarEdge sits at the intersection of power electronics, embedded software, and cloud-connected energy systems. The skills that matter most depend on the function, but a few themes come up consistently across publicly reported hiring patterns.

Technical depth over surface-level breadth. For engineering roles, SolarEdge wants people who know their domain well. Embedded candidates should be strong in C or C++ with hands-on experience in microcontrollers or DSPs. Cloud and data engineers should understand distributed systems, Python or Java, and ideally have exposure to IoT data pipelines or time-series databases.

Domain curiosity. You do not need prior experience in solar energy, but you should be able to talk about why clean energy technology is technically interesting to you. Hiring teams notice candidates who have done basic homework on how inverters work or why DC optimization matters at the system level.

Communication across disciplines. SolarEdge products involve hardware, firmware, cloud, and field teams all working together. Candidates who can explain their work clearly to people from other disciplines tend to stand out in the later interview stages.

Problem-solving under real constraints. Products deployed in the field must be reliable, cost-sensitive, and compliant with local grid standards. Interviewers often probe for how you balance performance, safety, and cost when designing or debugging a system.

Ownership mindset. SolarEdge operates in a fast-moving market. They look for people who proactively identify gaps and follow through without waiting for detailed direction.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Study the product line before any call. Spend time on the SolarEdge website understanding power optimizers, inverters, and the monitoring platform. Even a basic grasp of how the DC-AC conversion chain works will help you sound credible in technical conversations and show that you care about the domain.

Sharpen your core technical foundations. For software roles, revise data structures, algorithms, and system design. For hardware or firmware roles, review power electronics fundamentals, embedded C, and communication protocol specifications. If the role touches cloud infrastructure, be ready to discuss scalability and reliability trade-offs clearly.

Prepare strong project stories. Use a simple structure: the problem, your specific contribution, the technical challenges you solved, and the outcome. SolarEdge interviewers commonly drill into past projects to assess depth of understanding rather than surface-level familiarity with a technology.

Research the clean energy market in India. Be ready to speak to why you find the sector interesting. Generic answers like 'I want to work on green technology' are less compelling than specific observations, for example how rooftop solar adoption is changing grid load patterns or how storage costs are shifting installation economics.

Ask informed questions at each stage. Questions about the team's current technical challenges, how firmware updates are managed in deployed systems, or what the product roadmap looks like show genuine curiosity. These leave a better impression than salary-first questions early in the process.

Match your resume language to the job description. SolarEdge postings tend to be specific. If a role mentions MPPT algorithms or multi-level inverter topologies and you have relevant experience, make that visible on your resume rather than burying it in generic bullet points.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

SolarEdge describes itself as a mission-driven company focused on accelerating the shift to clean energy. Based on publicly reported employee reviews and company communications, a few cultural patterns stand out for candidates considering a role here.

Engineering-led environment. The company was founded by engineers and continues to be heavily engineering-driven. Technical quality and innovation are taken seriously, and people who enjoy going deep on hard problems tend to find the culture engaging rather than frustrating.

Global collaboration with local ownership. Teams in India often work closely with counterparts in Israel, Europe, and North America. This means some cross-timezone coordination, but local teams typically have meaningful ownership of their product areas rather than simply executing instructions from headquarters.

High performance expectations. SolarEdge operates in a competitive market and moves at a fast pace. Employees are generally expected to manage their own workload, deliver reliably, and flag issues proactively without waiting for direction.

Sustainability as a genuine motivator. Many employees cite the company's clean energy mission as a meaningful part of what keeps them engaged at work. If you have a personal alignment with sustainability goals, that tends to resonate well with the internal culture and comes through in how teams talk about their work.

Niche domain learning. Working at SolarEdge exposes you to a specialised intersection of power electronics, embedded systems, and cloud technology that is rare to find in pure software or hardware companies. For engineers interested in the energy technology space, this is a significant professional development draw.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does SolarEdge hire freshers and recent graduates in India?

SolarEdge does hire entry-level and campus candidates, particularly for engineering roles in software and hardware. The 144 open roles as of mid-2026 span multiple seniority levels, and some are specifically structured for people early in their careers. Your chances improve significantly if you have relevant internship or project experience, strong fundamentals in embedded systems or software development, and can show genuine curiosity about energy technology during interviews.

How long does the SolarEdge hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, the process from application to offer can range from a few weeks to a couple of months depending on the role, team bandwidth, and how quickly interview stages are scheduled. Engineering roles with multiple technical rounds naturally take longer than operations or support roles. Staying responsive to recruiter messages and being flexible on interview scheduling tends to help speed things up on your side.

Do I need a background in solar energy or power electronics to get hired at SolarEdge?

Not necessarily, though it helps for certain roles. SolarEdge hires software engineers, data engineers, cloud architects, and product managers who do not come from a power electronics background. What matters more is strong technical skills in your core domain and a willingness to learn the product context. That said, if you are applying for firmware, hardware design, or power systems roles, domain knowledge in those areas is generally expected and will be tested in interviews.

What programming languages or technologies does SolarEdge commonly look for?

For embedded and firmware roles, C and C++ are the primary languages, along with knowledge of microcontrollers, RTOS concepts, and protocols like I2C, SPI, or CAN bus. For software and cloud roles, Python, Java, and cloud platform experience are commonly listed in job descriptions. Data roles often call for SQL, Python, and experience with large-scale data pipelines or time-series databases. Always check the specific job posting carefully, as requirements vary significantly by team and function.

How should I handle salary discussions during the SolarEdge interview process?

Wait until the later stages, ideally the HR or offer round, before getting into specific numbers. Research publicly reported compensation data on platforms like Glassdoor or levels.fyi for SolarEdge roles in your city and function to anchor your expectations to real market data. Come prepared with a range rather than a single number, and factor in the total package including any variable or equity components the recruiter mentions.

Is the SolarEdge interview process the same for all roles?

No, the process varies noticeably by role type and seniority. Software engineering roles tend to have structured coding and system design rounds. Hardware or firmware roles focus more on domain-specific technical questions and design trade-off discussions. Non-engineering roles like sales, operations, or customer success typically involve fewer technical rounds and more emphasis on past experience, situational judgment, and communication skills. Always ask the recruiter during the first call what stages to expect for your specific role.
