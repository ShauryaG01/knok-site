01 Overview

Overview

Cendyn is a global hospitality technology company that builds CRM, eCommerce, and revenue management platforms used by hotels worldwide. Their engineering teams run cloud-heavy workloads serving real-time bookings, guest data pipelines, and analytics at scale, so Cloud Engineers here work on infrastructure that directly affects hotel operations every day.

The role typically covers AWS or Azure infrastructure, container orchestration, CI/CD pipelines, and supporting product teams with reliable and secure deployments. Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical assessment or live design session, and one or more rounds with senior engineers. Cendyn currently has 24 open roles listed, signalling active hiring across the engineering org.

In the broader India market (knok jobradar, July 2026), 102 Cloud Engineer positions are open. Bangalore and Delhi lead with 13 openings each, followed by Hyderabad (6), Pune (5), and Chennai (2). Cendyn's India roles are often remote-friendly or Bangalore-based, meaning competition is real but so is the opportunity.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are compiled from candidate reports and reflect what Cendyn engineering interviewers typically probe for. Expect a mix of cloud architecture, hands-on operations, and scenario-based questions.

  1. How have you designed or managed cloud infrastructure for a high-availability, real-time application? Walk through the key decisions you made.
  2. Cendyn builds multi-tenant SaaS products for hotels. How do you approach tenant isolation and data security in a shared cloud environment?
  3. Walk through how you would set up a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices-based hospitality platform.
  4. Describe your experience with Kubernetes or ECS in production. How have you handled scaling events, rolling updates, or cluster failures?
  5. How do you manage cloud costs without compromising reliability? Describe a specific example where you reduced infrastructure spend.
  6. How would you respond to a production incident where a guest-facing API goes down during peak hotel check-in hours?
  7. Explain how you implement observability (logs, metrics, traces, alerting) for a distributed platform. Which tools have you used?
  8. Cendyn integrates with third-party hotel systems like PMS and OTAs. How do you design fault-tolerant API integrations in the cloud?
  9. Describe a project where you used infrastructure-as-code (Terraform, CloudFormation, or Pulumi). What was the biggest challenge and how did you solve it?
  10. How do you manage secrets and credentials securely across staging and production cloud environments?
  11. How do you approach disaster recovery planning for a SaaS platform where downtime directly affects hotel revenue?
  12. Tell me about a time you had to advocate for a technical decision (such as a cloud architecture choice) with a non-technical stakeholder.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral and experience-based questions. Below are three examples you can adapt to your own experience.

---

Q: Walk through setting up a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices application.

*Situation:* My previous team had several microservices deployed on AWS, each with a manual deployment process. A release took most of a working day and often broke something because steps were done inconsistently.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement an automated CI/CD pipeline that would let engineers ship safely multiple times a day.

*Action:* I set up GitHub Actions workflows for each service: lint and unit tests on every pull request, a Docker build and push to ECR on merge to main, and a Helm-based rollout to EKS with automated smoke tests before traffic cut-over. I added rollback triggers so any failed smoke test would redeploy the previous image automatically.

*Result:* Release time dropped from a full day to a fraction of that. Rollback incidents, which had happened a few times a quarter, went to near zero over the months that followed. The team gained confidence to ship smaller, more frequent changes.

---

Q: Describe how you handled a major production incident in a cloud environment.

*Situation:* At a previous company, our primary PostgreSQL instance on RDS experienced a sudden spike in connection counts that caused the API layer to time out for end users.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and had to diagnose and restore service quickly while keeping the team and stakeholders informed.

*Action:* I checked CloudWatch metrics and identified a runaway batch job holding open a large number of idle connections. I terminated the batch job, temporarily raised the RDS connection limit, and added a PgBouncer connection pooler to prevent recurrence. I documented the full incident timeline in a post-mortem the same day.

*Result:* Service was restored quickly, well within our SLA target. The PgBouncer fix prevented similar incidents during subsequent high-traffic periods. The post-mortem led to a policy of connection pool limits on all new services.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you reduced cloud infrastructure costs.

*Situation:* Our AWS bill had grown quarter-over-quarter and leadership asked the infra team to find savings without touching production SLAs.

*Task:* I was given a short audit window to review spending and propose a concrete plan.

*Action:* I used AWS Cost Explorer and Trusted Advisor to identify three main issues: over-provisioned EC2 instances running at low utilization (industry surveys commonly cite over-provisioning as widespread in cloud environments), unattached EBS volumes left over from old experiments, and no Savings Plans covering baseline workloads. I right-sized several EC2 instances, deleted unused volumes, and purchased a Compute Savings Plan for the steady-state cluster.

*Result:* Monthly spend dropped noticeably and the Savings Plan gave leadership predictable costs for baseline workloads. The exercise led to a standing quarterly cost review process.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design and architecture questions: start by clarifying requirements (scale, latency needs, failover expectations), then sketch the high-level components (compute, storage, networking, CDN if relevant), then address the specific risk the question hints at (multi-tenancy, cost, latency, data loss). Cendyn serves hotels globally, so mentioning regional deployments or multi-AZ setups will resonate with the interviewer.

For behavioral questions ('tell me about a time...'): use STAR consistently. Keep the Situation brief (two or three sentences), spend most of your time on the Action (what YOU specifically did, not 'we'), and make the Result concrete. If you tracked a metric yourself, use it. If not, describe the direction and the mechanism clearly.

For incident or on-call questions: structure your answer as: detect, diagnose, mitigate, resolve, prevent. Interviewers want to see calm and methodical thinking, clear communication, and a habit of post-mortems. Mentioning that you would update stakeholders during the incident (not just after) scores points at a customer-facing company like Cendyn.

For cost and efficiency questions: lead with the audit approach (how you found the problem) before the fix. Engineers who can identify waste independently are more valuable than those who only fix it when pointed at it. Reference tools you have actually used (Cost Explorer, Infracost, Kubecost, or cloud-native advisors).

For security and compliance questions: hospitality companies handle PII and payment-adjacent data. Frame your answer around least-privilege access, encryption at rest and in transit, secrets management (Vault, AWS Secrets Manager, Parameter Store), and audit logging. Show that security is baked in from the start, not added afterward.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Cendyn builds software that hotel staff depend on every day. Downtime is not abstract. Interviewers are therefore looking for specific qualities beyond technical knowledge.

Reliability-first thinking. Can you design systems that survive failures gracefully? Do you naturally think about retries, circuit breakers, health checks, and rollback strategies? Mention these without being prompted.

Security awareness in a multi-tenant context. Cendyn's platform serves many hotel clients from shared infrastructure. Interviewers want to see that you understand tenant isolation, IAM scoping, and data boundary controls. Candidates who treat security as a checklist concern do not fare as well.

Operational maturity. Strong Cloud Engineer candidates talk about observability, on-call practices, runbooks, and post-mortems naturally. If you have only built systems and never operated them under pressure, practice framing your experience in operational terms.

Collaboration with product and development teams. Cendyn's engineering culture (candidates report) values infrastructure engineers as partners to developers, not gatekeepers. Show that you can explain trade-offs to non-infra colleagues and say yes to developer needs while managing risk carefully.

Comfort with the hospitality domain context. You do not need to be a hotel expert, but understanding that Cendyn's workloads are seasonal (peak travel periods), latency-sensitive (real-time booking), and data-intensive (guest profiles, analytics) will help you ask better clarifying questions and give more relevant design answers.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Ground yourself in Cendyn's stack and domain.
Read Cendyn's engineering blog and recent product announcements to understand what they are building. Study the job description closely and note every tool mentioned (AWS, Azure, Kubernetes, Terraform, specific databases). Map these against your own experience and identify gaps early.

Week 2: Drill the technical topics most likely to come up.
Practice designing a multi-tenant SaaS architecture on a whiteboard or paper. Work through several Kubernetes troubleshooting scenarios (pod crash-loops, node pressure, failed rollouts). Review your Terraform or IaC projects and be ready to explain every design decision. Set up a small cloud lab if your hands-on experience is thin in any area.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories.
Write out five to seven short STAR stories covering: a complex deployment you led, a production incident you resolved, a cost-saving initiative, a time you improved observability, and a time you worked cross-functionally with a product or dev team. Practice saying them aloud so they sound natural, not recited.

Before the interview:
Research Cendyn's current open roles and recent news (24 roles are currently listed, which signals active hiring and possible team growth). Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer: how the team handles on-call rotation, what the biggest infrastructure challenge is right now, or how cloud decisions are made. Good questions show that you are evaluating them as much as they are evaluating you.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic cloud answers. Talking about 'auto-scaling' or 'using S3 for storage' without connecting it to Cendyn's context (hospitality SaaS, multi-tenant, real-time bookings) makes you sound like every other candidate. Tie your answers to the domain.

Overcomplicating system design. Candidates sometimes propose the most sophisticated architecture possible to impress. Interviewers at product companies like Cendyn typically want the simplest design that meets the stated requirements. Add complexity only when you can justify the trade-off.

Skipping the 'why' in IaC or tooling choices. Saying 'I used Terraform' is weak. Saying 'I chose Terraform because our team had multi-cloud experience and the module ecosystem suited our needs' is what lands with interviewers.

Ignoring security in design answers. In multi-tenant SaaS, a candidate who designs an architecture without mentioning IAM roles, network segmentation, or secret management raises an immediate concern. Make security a natural part of every design answer.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'Things improved' is not a result. 'Deployment time dropped from a full day to a fraction of that' or 'on-call pages for that issue went to near zero' are results. If you do not have precise metrics, describe the mechanism and direction clearly.

Not asking questions. Cendyn has 24 open roles right now, meaning the team is growing or restructuring. Not asking about team context, roadmap, or on-call expectations signals low curiosity, which is a red flag for infrastructure roles where you will often need to investigate unknowns independently.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Cendyn Cloud Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screen, a technical round (which may be a take-home task or a live session covering cloud concepts and architecture), and one or two final rounds with senior engineers or a hiring manager. The exact structure can vary by team, so it is worth asking the recruiter upfront how many rounds to expect and what each one covers.

Does Cendyn ask LeetCode-style coding questions for Cloud Engineer roles?

Candidates report that Cendyn Cloud Engineer interviews focus more on system design, infrastructure scenarios, and operational experience than on algorithmic puzzles. You may see scripting or debugging questions in Python, Bash, or Go, but deep LeetCode preparation is generally not the priority. Spending your prep time on cloud architecture, Kubernetes, and CI/CD is likely more valuable for this role.

What cloud platform does Cendyn primarily use?

Based on publicly available job descriptions and candidate reports, Cendyn runs workloads on both AWS and Azure. Candidates report that AWS experience is the more common requirement, but familiarity with Azure and multi-cloud concepts is a plus. Always check the specific job description you are applying to, as requirements can vary across teams.

Is the Cendyn Cloud Engineer role remote or office-based in India?

Cendyn's India roles have been listed as hybrid or remote-friendly in recent postings, though arrangements can change over time. With 24 open roles currently active, the team is clearly hiring at scale across multiple work models. Confirm the arrangement with the recruiter during the first call, and ask about time zone overlap expectations if the role involves close collaboration with Cendyn's US or European offices.

How should I prepare for a multi-tenancy question in the Cendyn interview?

Practice designing a system where multiple hotel clients share the same infrastructure but their data is strictly isolated. Cover the key layers: separate IAM roles or accounts per tenant, namespace or cluster-level isolation in Kubernetes, row-level security or separate schemas in the database, and per-tenant audit logging. Cendyn builds SaaS products for the hospitality industry, so showing that you understand both the technical and compliance dimension of multi-tenancy will set you apart from other candidates.

What salary can I expect for a Cloud Engineer role at Cendyn in India?

Cendyn does not publicly disclose salary bands for India-based Cloud Engineer roles. Platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry compensation data reported by candidates and employees, and industry surveys track Cloud Engineer pay by experience level across Indian cities. Research those sources before your negotiation conversation so you enter it with a market-informed range rather than guessing.
