01 Overview

Overview

AssetPlus is a Bangalore-based fintech platform that helps independent financial advisors (IFAs) grow their mutual fund distribution business. They also serve retail investors directly. As of July 2026, AssetPlus has 18 open roles, with Product Manager positions among the most active. The PM role here is distinctive: you typically build for two users at once, the advisor who distributes and the investor who transacts. Candidates report that interviews are conversational but structured, covering product thinking, fintech domain knowledge, and past experience.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a product you built or improved end-to-end.

*Situation:* Our mobile app had a high drop-off rate during a key onboarding flow. The support team was fielding repetitive complaints about confusion at one specific step.

*Task:* I was asked to own the redesign of that flow from discovery to launch, while keeping the engineering team focused on a parallel backend project.

*Action:* I ran user interviews in a week, mapped exactly where users dropped off using funnel data, and proposed a simplified three-step flow. I wrote the PRD, worked with a single designer, and kept the engineering scope tight by deferring edge-case handling to a follow-up sprint.

*Result:* The redesigned flow went live in six weeks. Drop-off at that step fell meaningfully, confirmed via our funnel dashboard. Stakeholders approved a similar audit for two other flows at the next monthly review.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to say no to a stakeholder.

*Situation:* A senior sales leader wanted a custom reporting module built for one large enterprise client before our quarterly release.

*Task:* My job was to balance that request against roadmap commitments without damaging the relationship.

*Action:* I pulled data on how many other clients had made a similar request. The number was small. I scheduled a call with the sales leader, showed the roadmap tradeoffs clearly, and offered a workaround using our existing export feature plus a short explainer doc. I also logged it formally as a roadmap candidate with a priority score.

*Result:* The sales leader accepted the workaround. The enterprise client stayed. The reporting module shipped the following quarter as a general feature, benefiting all clients rather than just one.

---

Q: How do you measure the success of a new feature?

*Situation:* We launched a nudge feature that reminded investors to top up their SIPs when markets dipped.

*Task:* I needed to define success metrics before launch so the team had a clear target, not a post-hoc story.

*Action:* I defined a primary metric (top-up rate among nudge recipients versus a control group), a guardrail metric (unsubscribe rate from notifications), and a qualitative signal (advisor feedback on investor engagement). I set a review date four weeks after launch.

*Result:* The primary metric showed a meaningful lift in the treatment group. Unsubscribe rates stayed flat. Advisors reported that investors were more engaged in portfolio conversations, an indirect benefit the team had not fully anticipated.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the foundation for any behavioural question. Keep each element tight: one or two sentences for Situation and Task, most of your time on Action, and a concrete Result tied to a real business outcome.

CIRCLES (Comprehend, Identify, Report, Cut, List, Evaluate, Summarise) helps with product design questions such as 'design a feature for IFAs.' Start by clarifying who the user is and what job they are trying to do before proposing solutions.

The Prioritisation Matrix is useful when asked how you decide what to build. Map features on two axes: impact on the user's core job (high or low) and effort to build (high or low). In fintech, always call out compliance and regulatory dependencies explicitly, since timelines are rarely just about engineering capacity.

Metrics First is a habit interviewers notice. Before describing any product decision, name the metric you were trying to move. This signals product maturity more than any framework label.

For AssetPlus specifically, frame every answer around the dual user: the advisor who distributes and the investor who transacts. Show that you understand the IFA business model and that a poor advisor experience translates directly into lost assets under management.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Interviewers at AssetPlus typically look for three things.

Domain curiosity over domain expertise. You do not need prior wealth-tech experience, but you do need genuine curiosity about how IFAs work, how SEBI regulations shape product decisions, and why mutual fund distribution differs from direct investing.

Structured thinking under pressure. Candidates report that interviewers push back on your first answer to see whether you hold your ground with logic or fold. Prepare to defend your prioritisation choices with data and reasoning, not gut feel alone.

Bias for outcomes, not output. AssetPlus is in a growth phase. They want PMs who talk about results: retention, revenue impact, advisor activation, not just 'I shipped X feature on time.' Frame every past project around what actually moved.

Clear communication about tradeoffs is also valued. A PM who acknowledges that timelines sometimes bend to regulatory requirements comes across as credible. One who ignores compliance as a constraint does not.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product and the market.
Sign up for the AssetPlus platform if you can. Walk through the advisor and investor flows. Read publicly available material about the IFA segment in India, SEBI mutual fund regulations, and how wealth-tech platforms compete. Note the problems the platform already solves and the ones it has not yet addressed.

Week 2: Build your story bank.
Write out six to eight STAR stories covering: a product you owned end-to-end, a prioritisation decision, a stakeholder conflict, a data-driven decision, a failure, and a cross-functional win. Each story should take about two minutes to tell aloud. Practice speaking them out loud, not just reading in your head.

Week 3: Practice product design and metrics questions.
Pick two AssetPlus features and design improvements for them. For each, define the user, the problem, the solution, and the success metrics. Ask a peer to play interviewer and push back on your answers.

Day before:
Review your STAR stories once. Check the AssetPlus product for any recent changes. Prepare two or three questions to ask about the team's roadmap or how they measure advisor success.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating advisors and investors as one user. AssetPlus serves both, and conflating them signals a shallow product understanding. Always clarify which user you are designing for at the start of any product question.
  1. Ignoring compliance as a constraint. Saying 'we can just build X' without acknowledging regulatory timelines looks naive in fintech. At minimum, say you would loop in legal early and build in buffer.
  1. Vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'the product improved significantly' is not a result. Even without exact figures, say 'we measured a meaningful improvement in our funnel metric' or name the business outcome.
  1. Memorising frameworks without applying them. Mentioning CIRCLES or a prioritisation matrix is pointless if you cannot apply it to an AssetPlus-specific scenario on the spot. Practice applying, not naming.
  1. Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare specific questions about the advisor onboarding funnel, current roadmap bets, or how the PM team is structured.
  1. Underselling your domain connection. If you have worked in BFSI, insurance, payments, or B2B SaaS for SMEs, connect it explicitly to the AssetPlus context. Do not assume the interviewer will make that connection for you.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does an AssetPlus PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically spans three to four rounds, including an introductory call, one or two product thinking or case rounds, and a final leadership or culture conversation. Round structure and order can vary, so treat every interaction as evaluative. Confirm the specific process with your recruiter after you apply.

Do I need fintech experience to get a PM role at AssetPlus?

Fintech experience helps but is not always required. Candidates report that AssetPlus values structured thinking and genuine curiosity about the IFA and investor problem space as much as domain background. If you come from B2B SaaS, payments, insurance, or consumer tech, connect your experience explicitly to the advisor-investor dynamic they serve.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at AssetPlus?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level PM roles (3-6 years experience) in India typically fall in the 24-40 LPA range, while senior PM roles are commonly cited in the 40-60 LPA range. Associate PM positions typically start at 12-20 LPA. Actual offers depend on your level and negotiation, so verify current numbers against Glassdoor listings before your final round.

What kind of product case questions should I prepare for?

Candidates report questions focused on improving existing AssetPlus flows (advisor onboarding, portfolio views, SIP management), handling regulatory constraints, and prioritising a backlog with competing stakeholder demands. Practice designing for both the advisor and investor user in the same answer. Interviewers notice when you only consider one side of the two-sided platform.

How important is knowledge of SEBI regulations for this role?

You do not need to memorise SEBI circulars, but showing awareness that regulations shape product timelines and feature scope matters. Candidates who treat compliance as an afterthought tend to come across as less credible in fintech interviews. A working knowledge of how mutual fund distribution is regulated in India is a reasonable baseline to build before your first round.

Is AssetPlus a good company for an early-career PM?

Candidates report that early-career PMs at growth-stage fintech companies like AssetPlus typically get broader ownership than at larger firms, since the team is smaller and the roadmap is actively being shaped. The tradeoff is that mentorship structures are less formalised. If you are applying for an Associate PM role, be prepared to show self-direction and the ability to move fast with limited guidance.
