01 Role Overview

Role Overview

Engineering Manager (EM) is one of the most sought-after leadership roles in Indian tech right now. You own the delivery of a software team while staying close enough to technical decisions to earn your engineers' trust. Unlike a pure people manager, an EM in India is expected to understand system design, participate in architecture reviews, and step in during a production crisis.

knok jobradar tracked 975 Engineering Manager openings across India as of July 2026. Bangalore leads with 182 openings, followed by Delhi at 53, Pune and Chennai at 20 each, Hyderabad at 16, and Mumbai at 12. If you are open to relocation, Bangalore is clearly where the bulk of hiring happens.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Technical credibility is non-negotiable in India. You do not need to write production code every day, but you must understand your team's systems, review architecture proposals, set code quality standards, and speak confidently about tradeoffs. Engineers will not respect a manager who cannot engage technically.

People management is the skill most engineers underestimate before making the switch. Running effective 1:1s, giving honest performance feedback, managing conflicts, building psychological safety, helping people grow into bigger roles: these are learnable skills that require deliberate practice. You will also own hiring, from writing job descriptions to closing candidates.

Delivery and planning means owning your team's roadmap end to end. This includes breaking down product requirements into engineering tasks, tracking progress without micromanaging, surfacing blockers early, and reporting status clearly up the chain.

Stakeholder communication is a large part of the EM role, especially at product companies where you will regularly partner with Product Managers, Designers, Data teams, and Business stakeholders. Writing crisp status updates and presenting confidently in leadership reviews are worth building deliberately.

Hiring and retention has become a core EM responsibility. Attrition is commonly cited as one of the top concerns for engineering leaders in India. Learning how to hire well and how to keep strong engineers engaged will directly shape how your leadership perceives you.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

Step 1: Excel as a Senior or Staff Engineer
Technical credibility is your foundation. Own complex, cross-team projects. Mentor junior engineers. Lead technical decisions and write clear design documents. Nobody moves into a respected EM role without first being taken seriously as an engineer.

Step 2: Become the informal Tech Lead on your team
Start running standups, coordinating with the Product Manager, and unblocking your teammates without waiting for a title. Take on project coordination work voluntarily. This gives you a first real taste of the EM role and signals to your manager that you are ready for more.

Step 3: Build people management skills on purpose
Read widely on engineering leadership. 'The Manager's Path' by Camille Fournier is widely recommended in Indian EM communities. Take structured courses on Coursera or LinkedIn Learning. Find a mentor who is already an EM, ideally someone who made the same IC-to-EM transition recently.

Step 4: Have an explicit conversation with your manager
Do not hint. Say directly that you want to move into engineering management. Ask to shadow the hiring process, sit in on performance calibrations, and take on projects that require cross-team coordination. Most companies in India prefer to promote a trusted engineer over hiring an unknown external candidate.

Step 5: Apply for your first formal EM role
An internal promotion is lower risk since you already have context and relationships. If going external, target companies where the team is small and the mandate is clear. Do not wait for the perfect role. Your first EM position is a learning role, not a peak compensation role.

Step 6: Build a track record in the first year or two
Focus on three things: shipping consistently, growing the engineers on your team, and reducing the team's dependence on you for every decision. Document your wins carefully, including promotions you drove, projects you delivered on time, and attrition you managed. This evidence is your currency for the next level.

Step 7: Move toward Senior Manager or Director
With a solid record managing one team, start expanding your scope. Managing multiple teams, owning a larger product area, or starting to manage other tech leads. This is the Director track, and it requires demonstrating business impact beyond just engineering delivery.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

This roadmap assumes you are currently a strong Senior Engineer. Treat these as realistic ranges, not fixed timelines.

Years 0-3: Strong Individual Contributor
Deepen your technical expertise, take ownership of significant systems, and start mentoring others. Begin picking up coordination work informally. By the end of this phase, you should have led at least one major project that crossed team boundaries.

Years 3-5: Tech Lead or Lead Engineer
You are doing half the EM job already. You own technical direction for a feature area, engineers on the team come to you first when stuck, and you interface regularly with the Product Manager. Use this phase to actively close your people skills gap before you need them in a formal role.

Years 5-7: First EM Role
This is when most Indian engineers make the formal switch, either through internal promotion or an external move. Expect a significant learning curve in the first six months. Your primary goal here is earning your team's trust and shipping reliably, not optimising scope or compensation yet.

Years 7-10: Senior Manager
With a solid EM record behind you, you are ready to manage multiple teams or a larger product area. You will start managing other tech leads and possibly junior EMs. Salary at this level commonly falls in the 55-90 LPA range per current knok jobradar listings.

Year 10 and beyond: Director and above
At this level you are setting engineering strategy, working with VPs and CPOs, and building the management layer below you. Most Directors in India combine a strong delivery record with clear business impact and the ability to attract and retain senior engineering talent.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

The IIT, BITS, or NIT brand helps but is not a gate
Top product companies do run structured campus hiring from premier institutes. But the majority of EM openings, including at FAANG India offices, are filled through referrals and LinkedIn. Your track record and your network matter far more than your college once you are past your first job.

Use LinkedIn for product companies, Naukri for IT services
For mid-to-senior EM roles at startups, unicorns, and MNC product companies, LinkedIn is where recruiters search first. Keep your profile current, especially your 'About' section and recent project descriptions. Naukri still dominates for IT services firms and traditional Indian enterprises.

Internal promotions are very common in India
Many large Indian product companies and MNC India offices prefer to promote a trusted Senior Engineer into their first EM role rather than hire externally. If you are at a company with a growing engineering org, make your ambition clear to your skip-level manager, not just your direct manager.

Join Indian EM communities
The engineering leadership community in India is growing steadily. Look for Product and Engineering Manager communities on Slack and LinkedIn, attend events run by groups like iSPIRT or the NASSCOM Product Conclave, and connect with peers who made the IC-to-EM switch in the last couple of years. Real advice from people who recently lived through the transition is more useful than any generic guide.

Target Bangalore first if you can
Bangalore accounts for 182 of the 975 EM openings in the current data, far ahead of any other city including Delhi at 53 and Pune and Chennai at 20 each. If you are serious about an EM role at a product company or FAANG India office, being in Bangalore, or being open to relocating there, significantly expands your options.

The 'return to India' wave is opening senior doors
Indian engineers returning from the US, UK, and Singapore are taking Director and VP roles at Indian startups and growth-stage companies. If you have international experience, this is a meaningful advantage in the current market. Highlight it clearly on your LinkedIn profile.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need an MBA to become an Engineering Manager in India?

No. The vast majority of Engineering Managers in India come directly from the IC engineering track. An MBA can help if you want to move into general management or a business leadership role, but for technical EM positions at product companies and startups, your engineering depth and people track record matter far more than a management degree. Some senior leaders pursue an executive MBA part-time, but it is not a requirement or a common path to an EM title.

What is the difference between a Tech Lead and an Engineering Manager?

A Tech Lead is primarily accountable for technical direction: architecture, code quality, and technical delivery. An Engineering Manager is primarily accountable for the people: hiring, performance reviews, career growth, and team health. Many Indian companies blur this line, especially at startups, and some have a combined 'TL-M' role where one person handles both. As teams grow larger, the people responsibilities tend to dominate and the two tracks separate more clearly.

Can I become an EM at an IT services company like Infosys or TCS?

Yes, but the role looks different from a product company EM. In IT services firms, the Manager title often maps to a delivery manager function focused on client relationships, project staffing, and billing targets rather than engineering leadership. Compensation bands and career trajectories are different. If your goal is eventually to move to a product company EM role, services experience is a valid starting point, but be deliberate about building the technical leadership and people development skills that product companies specifically look for.

How do I get my first EM role when every posting asks for management experience?

The fastest path is an internal promotion. Most companies would rather promote a trusted Senior Engineer they already know than hire an unproven external EM. Make your interest explicit to your manager and start doing people-adjacent work now: own the hiring process for your team, mentor junior engineers formally, or run a cross-team project end to end. If you must go external, target early-stage startups and growth-stage companies where the first EM hire often comes without a strict prior management experience requirement.

Should I optimise for salary or scope in my first EM role?

Optimise for the quality of learning and the strength of your skip-level manager. A well-run engineering org where you will be coached and given room to make mistakes is worth more than extra LPA at a company where EMs are left to figure it out alone. Compensation catches up quickly once you have a solid two to three year EM record. Your first role sets the baseline for everything that follows, so pick the environment where you will actually learn the job well.

How do I stand out when applying for EM roles in India?

Quantify your people and delivery impact, not just your technical contributions. On your resume and LinkedIn, mention the team size you led, the engineers you helped get promoted, the projects you shipped on time, and any attrition challenges you managed. Indian recruiters for EM roles look for evidence of ownership and follow-through. A strong referral from someone inside the company still beats a cold application on LinkedIn or Naukri, so invest in building a network of senior engineers and EMs across companies well before you are actively searching.
