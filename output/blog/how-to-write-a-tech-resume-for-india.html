01 Role Overview

Role Overview

A tech resume in India in 2026 is your first and often only chance to clear the ATS filter before a human ever reads your name. Indian recruiters on Naukri and LinkedIn receive hundreds of applications per role, and most companies use automated screening to shortlist candidates. Your resume needs to do two things at once: pass the bot and impress the person who reads it next.

The good news is that Indian hiring managers are increasingly practical. They want to see what you built, what tools you used, and what impact you had, not a wall of buzzwords. A clean, honest, well-structured resume beats a padded one almost every time. Understanding the format expectations and keyword realities of Indian hiring in 2026 is what this guide is about.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

The skills that get you through ATS and into an interview room depend on your target role, but consistent patterns hold across most tech jobs in India.

For software engineers, the non-negotiables are: at least one backend language (Java, Python, Go, or C++), a frontend framework if you are full-stack (React, Angular, or Vue), a relational database (MySQL or PostgreSQL), and familiarity with at least one cloud platform (AWS, GCP, or Azure). Docker and basic CI/CD knowledge are now standard expectations even for junior roles.

For data and ML roles, you need Python, SQL, and working knowledge of at least one ML framework (scikit-learn, TensorFlow, or PyTorch). Experience with data pipeline tools (Apache Spark, Airflow) and BI tools (Tableau, Power BI) strengthens your profile further.

For QA and DevOps roles, tool-specific knowledge matters most. Selenium, Cypress, and JMeter for QA; Kubernetes, Terraform, Jenkins, and Ansible for DevOps. Certifications from AWS, Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure add credibility, especially if you are switching into these roles.

What does NOT belong in the skills section: soft skills like 'communication' or 'teamwork'. These are table stakes, not differentiators. Show them through bullet points in your experience section instead.

Competitive programming profiles on LeetCode, Codeforces, or HackerRank are not required but help significantly when targeting product companies or startups that filter on DSA skills.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

Step 1: Choose the right template. Indian ATS systems parse single-column resumes most reliably. Avoid tables, text boxes, headers and footers, and graphic elements. A plain Google Doc or a LaTeX template from Overleaf works well. Fancy Canva designs often look good on screen but fail ATS parsing.

Step 2: Write a sharp summary. Two to three sentences at the top. State your role, years of experience, core tech stack, and one standout achievement. Cut phrases like 'passionate developer', 'quick learner', and 'team player'. Recruiters skip these on sight.

Step 3: List experience in reverse chronological order. For each role, write three to five bullet points. Start every bullet with a strong action verb: built, reduced, automated, deployed, led, refactored, scaled. Add a measurable result wherever you honestly have one, whether that is a latency improvement, the number of users your system served, or the number of services you maintained. A generic bullet like 'improved performance' says nothing. A specific bullet that names a real, provable outcome says everything.

Step 4: Add a projects section if you have under three years of experience. Each project entry needs: a one-line description of what it does, the full tech stack, a GitHub or live demo link, and one outcome or learning. Recruiters at product companies read this section carefully for freshers.

Step 5: Tailor the skills section to each job description. Copy tool names exactly as written in the posting. If they say 'ReactJS', write 'ReactJS', not 'React.js' or 'React'. ATS keyword matching is often literal.

Step 6: Handle education correctly. Freshers put education near the top. Experienced candidates put it at the bottom. Include your CGPA only if it crosses commonly cited competitive thresholds for your target companies. If it is borderline, leave it out.

Step 7: Get the length right. One page for under five years of experience, two pages maximum for more. Indian recruiters rarely read beyond page two. When trimming, cut old internships and irrelevant skills first.

Step 8: Export as PDF. Send a PDF unless the application form explicitly requests .docx. PDF preserves your formatting across every recruiter's device.

Step 9: Do an ATS sanity check. Paste your resume text into a plain text editor. If it reads cleanly from top to bottom with no garbled symbols or missing sections, your ATS parse is likely fine.

Step 10: Sync your Naukri and LinkedIn profiles. Recruiters cross-check both against your resume. Inconsistencies in job titles, dates, or company names are red flags that kill otherwise strong applications.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

How long it takes to go from zero to a polished, interview-ready resume depends on where you are starting from.

Freshers (0-1 years of experience)

Month 1: Decide on your target role and primary tech stack. Build or document at least two solid projects on GitHub. These do not need to be original ideas, but they need to be genuinely yours. Set up complete profiles on Naukri and LinkedIn.

Month 2: Write your first resume draft using the steps in this guide. Get feedback from two or three people who have been hired in India recently, ideally in your target role. Send a batch of applications and track which ones get a callback.

Month 3 onward: Iterate. A run of applications with no callbacks is a signal to revise. Adjust keywords, rewrite bullet points, swap projects if needed. Resume writing is iterative, not a one-time task.

Career switchers and candidates with 2-5 years of experience

You likely have stronger material than you think, but it is buried in job-description language. The work here is translation: taking what you did and framing it as impact. Allocate two to four weeks of focused effort. The biggest wins usually come from rewriting bullet points and tailoring your skills section per application type.

Targeting product companies or unicorns

This is a parallel track. Resume work takes two to four weeks, but competitive programming preparation and open source contributions are multi-month efforts. Start both early. The resume gets you the shortlist; your DSA preparation gets you through the filter rounds.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

Naukri is still the backbone of Indian tech hiring. Even if you apply through a company's careers page, their recruiter likely sourced your profile from Naukri first. Keep your profile updated, fill in every section the platform asks for, and check that your resume is set to visible. A stale Naukri profile costs you callbacks you never even know you missed.

LinkedIn is essential for mid-to-senior roles and product companies. Recruiters at funded startups, MNCs, and product-focused companies in India rely heavily on LinkedIn searches. Your headline, about section, and skills list feed into how you appear in search results. Mirror your resume keywords in your LinkedIn profile and keep your last-updated date current.

College tier still opens some doors faster. Candidates from IITs, NITs, BITS Pilani, and top private colleges often get preferential treatment in initial screening at certain companies. If you are from a lesser-known college, compensate with stronger projects, competitive programming ratings, certifications, or open source contributions. Candidates from Tier 2 and Tier 3 colleges crack top product companies every year this way.

Communities accelerate your job search. Tech communities like HasGeek, regional developer meetups, and stack-specific Discord or Slack groups give you access to referrals. Referrals convert to interviews far more reliably than cold applications, and a referral from a current employee often bypasses ATS entirely.

Referrals are the most underused tool in Indian job search. Most candidates mass-apply on Naukri and wait. The candidates who move fastest usually have someone inside the company putting their name forward. Reach out specifically and politely, not with mass 'please refer me' messages.

Never fabricate project experience. Listing fake projects, claiming open source contributions you did not make, or describing internship work as production experience you own is risky. Interviewers in India cross-check GitHub activity, ask you to walk through code live in interviews, and sometimes contact references informally through shared networks. One caught lie can quietly close multiple doors in a city where tech circles are small.

Format your resume for both Naukri upload and direct PDF share. Naukri parses your resume and rebuilds it in its own display format, so keep your original document clean and text-based. Always have a polished PDF ready to share directly with recruiters on WhatsApp or email, which is common in India.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Should I include a photo on my tech resume in India?

No. Photos are not expected on tech resumes in India and can work against you. Most ATS systems cannot process images correctly, which can corrupt how your resume is parsed. A photo also takes up space better used for skills or achievements.

Should I include my CGPA on my resume?

Include it if it is strong relative to what your target companies expect. Check each company's stated criteria if they publish them. For candidates with two or more years of solid work experience, CGPA rarely influences hiring decisions and you can drop it entirely to free up space for more relevant content.

How do I show impact on my resume if I was only an intern or fresher?

Focus on what you built and the scope of what it touched. Even without business metrics, you can describe the system honestly: 'built a REST API used by three internal teams', or 'automated a reporting process that previously took three hours every week'. Be specific about your individual contribution, not the whole team's output.

Should I list every skill I know or only the ones I know well?

Only list skills you can discuss confidently in an interview. Listing a tool you barely touched invites questions you cannot answer, which is a fast way to lose an offer. You can separate skills by proficiency level (for example, 'primary' and 'familiar with'), but never pad the section to look broader than you are.

How often should I update my tech resume?

Update it every three to six months even when you are not actively job hunting. Add new tools, projects you have shipped, and results you can measure. Keep a running list of achievements so updates take minutes rather than hours. A resume written in a rush during a sudden job search is almost always weaker than one maintained consistently.

Is a one-page resume really better in India, or is that a myth?

For candidates with under five years of experience, one page is strongly preferred by most Indian recruiters. Beyond five years, two pages is acceptable and sometimes necessary to show the full breadth of your work. The real rule is that every line should earn its place. Cut anything a recruiter would skim past, whether that leaves you at one page or two.
