01 Overview

Overview

Premas Life Sciences is a specialist life sciences company at the intersection of genomics, diagnostics, cell biology, and scientific instrumentation in India. As a product manager here, your role sits at a unique crossroads: you are not building software from scratch but rather managing a portfolio of high-value scientific products, coordinating with global principals, and driving adoption among researchers, hospitals, and biotech firms across India.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect the themes candidates report encountering in Premas Life Sciences PM interviews. Prepare a concrete story or framework for each.

  1. How would you decide which new genomics or diagnostics products to add to the Premas portfolio for the Indian market?
  2. Premas distributes products from global principals. How would you handle a situation where the principal's global roadmap does not match what Indian customers actually need?
  3. Walk us through how you would plan and execute the India launch of a new sequencing platform from an international brand.
  4. How do you approach pricing strategy for a high-value scientific instrument targeting Indian research institutes and private labs?
  5. Describe a time you worked closely with scientists, doctors, or highly technical stakeholders to translate their requirements into a clear product or launch plan.
  6. How would you measure success for a newly launched diagnostic product in India after the first year?
  7. How do you balance the needs of two very different customer segments, for example academic researchers versus hospital-based clinical buyers, for the same product line?
  8. What does a compelling business case look like when you recommend adding a new product line to senior leadership?
  9. Describe a situation where you had to communicate complex technical product specifications to a non-technical audience, such as a sales team or a finance stakeholder.
  10. How would you assess the market opportunity for expanding Premas products into Tier 2 or Tier 3 cities in India?
  11. Tell us about a product or initiative you owned end to end. What decisions did you make, and what would you do differently?
  12. How do you stay current with trends in genomics, diagnostics, or life sciences tools, and how does that shape your product priorities?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you prioritize new products for the Indian genomics market?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were evaluating several new scientific tools to add to our distribution portfolio, but only had budget and bandwidth to launch two in the next financial year.

*Task:* My job was to recommend which two to proceed with, backed by data and a clear rationale.

*Action:* I mapped each product against three criteria: addressable demand in India (based on lab conversations and publicly reported government research spend trends), competitive intensity, and margin profile. I ran structured interviews with our field sales team to validate which gaps customers were actively asking us to fill. I then built a one-page scoring sheet and presented two options with different risk profiles to leadership.

*Result:* Leadership approved both recommendations. Within six months, one became our fastest-adopted new product line that year, with order volumes the sales head described as 'above our initial projections.'

---

Q: Describe a time you managed a conflict between a global principal and local customer needs.

*Situation:* A global principal released a new instrument version that removed a legacy data export format. Several Indian research institute customers relied on that format for existing workflows.

*Task:* I had to prevent customer churn while maintaining the principal relationship.

*Action:* I documented the specific workflows affected, with written consent from three customers, and created a business impact summary showing renewal revenue at risk. I escalated through the principal's India liaison rather than going directly to the global product team, to keep the relationship warm. I also coordinated with our internal applications team to build a short-term workaround that gave customers breathing room while the issue was resolved.

*Result:* The principal agreed to restore the export option in the next minor release. We retained all the at-risk accounts, and the principal's regional head described our escalation as 'the most structured feedback received from any regional partner.'

---

Q: Tell us about a product or initiative you owned end to end.

*Situation:* I was asked to lead the go-to-market for a new cell analysis product we were distributing for the first time in India. There was no existing playbook and the sales team had no prior experience positioning it.

*Task:* I needed to take the product from zero awareness to active pipeline within one quarter.

*Action:* I ran an internal workshop to train the sales team on key use cases, created customer-facing one-pagers in plain language with no jargon, and identified five 'lighthouse' labs to do early evaluations in exchange for a case study. I tracked each stage of the funnel in a shared CRM view so everyone could see progress weekly.

*Result:* By the end of the quarter, we had several qualified leads and two paid pilots under way. The lighthouse case studies became the primary sales tool for the next year.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For prioritization questions, use a simple scoring approach: customer demand (is someone actively asking for this?), market size (how many potential buyers exist in India?), strategic fit (does this extend existing strengths?), and margin profile. Name your criteria upfront before applying them, rather than jumping straight to a conclusion.

For product launch questions, walk through four phases: understand the customer (who buys, who uses, what problem they have), define success metrics before the launch not after, plan go-to-market with the sales team as co-owners, and build a feedback loop for the first ninety days. Premas interviewers will appreciate that you treat the sales and applications teams as key stakeholders, not just execution arms.

For stakeholder conflict questions, the STAR format works well, but add one element at the end: what you learned and what you would do differently. This signals maturity, which is particularly valued where you will often be navigating between a demanding global principal and customers with their own constraints.

For market sizing questions, be transparent about your assumptions. Say 'I would estimate X by starting with Y, because...' rather than stating a number as fact. Interviewers in life sciences tend to be scientifically trained and will respect intellectual honesty over a confident figure you cannot defend.

For metrics and success questions, separate leading indicators (early signals like demo requests and evaluation conversions) from lagging indicators (revenue and renewals). This shows you think about product health proactively, not just at year-end review time.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain comfort, not a PhD. You do not need a science degree, but you do need to show you can hold a credible conversation with a researcher or lab head without getting lost. Candidates who have read even basic material on the product categories they will manage tend to stand out.

B2B product instincts. Premas sells to institutions, not individual consumers. Interviewers want to see that you understand long sales cycles, multiple decision-makers (procurement, scientific lead, finance), and the importance of post-sale support in building repeat business.

Principal management awareness. A large part of the role involves working with global brands whose products Premas distributes. Candidates who understand this two-sided dynamic, serving the customer while also maintaining the principal relationship, tend to resonate strongly with the interview panel.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Candidates report being given open-ended scenarios where there is no single right answer. What interviewers watch for is how you break the problem down, which assumptions you state explicitly, and how you arrive at a recommendation.

Ownership mindset. Stories where you stepped beyond a narrow job description to solve a problem, even when it was not strictly your responsibility, are valued here. This is a relatively lean team, so interviewers want people who fill gaps rather than hand problems off.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the company and domain. Read Premas Life Sciences' website carefully. Note the product categories they carry and the principals they work with. Look up publicly reported information about the Indian genomics and diagnostics market. You do not need to become an expert, but you should be able to name two or three major trends, for instance publicly reported government investment in genomics infrastructure or growth in clinical sequencing.

Week 1 to 2: Prepare your core stories. Map your past experience to the twelve question areas in the previous section. For each, write a two-minute STAR answer. Practise saying them out loud, not just reading them silently. Aim for crisp delivery under ninety seconds for most answers.

Week 2: Practise product cases. Ask a friend or mentor to give you a product scenario in a domain you are less familiar with, ideally something B2B and technical. Practise framing your approach before diving into recommendations. Record yourself specifically to check whether you are stating assumptions clearly.

Day before the interview: Review your notes on Premas products. Prepare two or three specific questions for the interviewer about the product roadmap, team structure, or how success is measured in the first six months. Sharp questions signal preparation and genuine interest.

On salary: Candidates report that compensation discussions typically happen in later rounds. Knok data shows the market band for PM roles at 3-6 years experience at 24-40 LPA, which gives you a useful reference point before that conversation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating this like a software PM interview. Candidates who arrive with only consumer app or SaaS frameworks can struggle if they cannot adapt to a B2B, distribution-heavy, science-adjacent context. Your examples do not have to be from life sciences, but your frameworks should translate clearly to Premas' reality.

Ignoring the principal relationship angle. Many candidates focus entirely on the end customer and forget that a significant part of the PM role at a distributor involves managing the brand relationship upstream. Mentioning this dynamic shows you have done your homework.

Vague answers on metrics. Saying 'we improved customer satisfaction' without a specific measure sounds weak. Pick a proxy metric such as renewal rate or repeat order frequency, and be clear about why you chose it. If you cite a number from a past role, note the context or sample size so it reads as credible rather than inflated.

Overselling or apologising for your science background. Neither extreme helps. If you have domain knowledge, use it precisely. If you do not, show intellectual curiosity and explain how you get up to speed quickly in unfamiliar technical domains.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates report that Premas interviews tend to be conversational. Arriving with no questions reads as low engagement. Prepare at least two that show you have thought specifically about this role, not just PM roles in general.

Stating frameworks without applying them. Saying 'I would use a prioritisation matrix' and then not walking through how you would fill it for a Premas-specific scenario is a missed opportunity. Always anchor your framework to something real or plausible in the life sciences distribution context.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Premas Life Sciences PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves two to four rounds, though this can vary by seniority and the specific team. Earlier rounds tend to focus on product thinking and domain familiarity, while later rounds typically include a conversation with senior leadership or cross-functional stakeholders. Confirm the exact structure with the recruiter at the start of the process, as it can shift.

Do I need a science or life sciences background to apply for a PM role at Premas?

A science background is helpful but is not always a hard requirement, based on what candidates publicly report. Interviewers tend to value product thinking, B2B instincts, and the ability to engage credibly with technical users over formal credentials alone. If you do not have a science degree, prepare examples showing how you have learned technical domains quickly and earned the trust of expert stakeholders.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Premas Life Sciences?

Specific Premas compensation data is limited, so treat any single figure with caution. Across the broader Indian PM market, knok data shows PM roles at the 3-6 year experience level in the 24-40 LPA range. Glassdoor and levels.fyi sometimes carry company-specific data worth checking before your negotiation conversation. Being a specialist company in a niche domain, compensation may also reflect the value placed on domain familiarity.

How should I prepare if I am coming from a non-life-sciences industry?

Focus first on the transferable parts of your experience: stakeholder management, portfolio prioritisation, go-to-market planning, and B2B sales cycle navigation. Then spend time understanding what Premas sells, the types of customers they serve (research labs, hospitals, biotech companies), and two or three macro trends in Indian life sciences that are publicly reported. You are not expected to be a subject matter expert on day one, but you should show genuine curiosity about the domain.

Is the Premas PM role more strategic or more operational?

Based on what candidates report and the nature of distribution-focused PM roles more broadly, the role is likely a mix of both. You will own launch planning and portfolio strategy on the strategic side, while also spending real time on sales enablement, customer escalations, and coordinating with global principals on the operational side. Candidates who thrive tend to be comfortable switching between the two modes rather than strongly preferring one.

The niche life sciences context does thin the field somewhat, since not every PM candidate will be comfortable with the domain or with B2B distribution dynamics. Strong domain curiosity, well-structured STAR answers, and a clear understanding of the principal relationship model will give you a meaningful edge.
