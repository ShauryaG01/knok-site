01 Overview

Overview

Vegapay builds credit card infrastructure and embedded finance products for banks, NBFCs, and fintechs in India. Their platform handles card issuance, credit limit management, rewards, EMI conversions, and the API layer that connects lenders to card networks. This makes the PM role highly technical and deeply embedded in banking workflows.

As of July 2026, vegapay has 26 open roles. The PM function sits at the intersection of product engineering, banking partnerships, and compliance, so interviews test all three dimensions. The process typically runs 3-5 rounds: a recruiter screening, one or two product thinking discussions, a case or take-home assignment, and a final round with a senior leader or cross-functional panel. Candidates report that the bar is high on fintech domain depth.

Salary ranges from knok jobradar: PM roles (3-6 years experience) sit at 24-40 LPA, Senior PM positions reach 40-60 LPA, and Associate PM roles start at 12-20 LPA for candidates moving in from adjacent functions.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Vegapay interviews typically blend product design, behavioral, and fintech domain questions. Here are the questions candidates commonly encounter:

  1. Vegapay serves banks and NBFCs as clients, not end consumers directly. How do you define your 'customer' when building a feature for the card issuance platform?
  1. Walk us through how a credit card gets issued end-to-end. Where do you see the biggest product opportunity in that flow?
  1. You need to add a new self-serve feature to vegapay's dashboard. How do you prioritize it against a core platform reliability improvement that affects all banking clients?
  1. A banking partner asks for custom reporting that no other client has requested. How do you evaluate and respond to this?
  1. How would you measure the success of a new EMI conversion feature on the platform? What are your north star and guardrail metrics?
  1. Describe a product you have built or managed that had regulatory or compliance constraints. How did those constraints shape your decisions?
  1. A critical card-limit API that banking clients use goes down at 2 AM. As the PM, what do you do?
  1. Vegapay works with slow-moving banks and fast-moving fintechs. How do you manage roadmap tension between these two types of clients?
  1. How would you design a self-serve onboarding flow for a new NBFC partner joining the vegapay platform?
  1. Tell us about a time you used data to change a product decision that stakeholders were emotionally invested in.
  1. What KPIs would you track for vegapay's credit card rewards management module?
  1. If vegapay were to expand into a BNPL or prepaid wallet vertical, how would you structure your first 90 days of discovery?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioral questions. Here are three worked examples.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you used data to change a product decision stakeholders were emotionally invested in.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the engineering and design team had spent three months building a gamified rewards dashboard. Leadership loved the concept and had already mentioned it in a client presentation.

*Task:* I was brought in to define success metrics before launch. During this process I found that usage data from a beta showed most users visited the rewards page once after signup and never returned.

*Action:* I ran a short survey with a group of beta users and cross-referenced their responses with support tickets. The core issue was that points expiry rules were confusing, not that users did not care about rewards. I presented this finding with a clear before-and-after retention model, and proposed delaying the gamification layer to first fix the expiry communication. This was a one-week engineering task.

*Result:* Leadership agreed after seeing the data. Rewards page return visits improved noticeably within the first month. The gamification layer launched three months later on a much stronger base.

---

Q: Describe a product you managed that had regulatory constraints. How did those constraints shape your decisions?

*Situation:* I was PM for a credit line increase feature at a lending platform. RBI guidelines required that any automated credit limit change be based on a defined credit policy approved by the board.

*Task:* My goal was to ship an automated limit-increase feature that improved conversion, while staying within the compliance boundary.

*Action:* I worked with risk and legal teams to define exactly which policy parameters could be automated. I then designed a rule-based engine where credit policy owners could configure thresholds without engineering involvement. Every configuration change created an audit log for the compliance team. I kept the UI simple enough that a risk analyst could use it without any training.

*Result:* We shipped the feature in two quarters. The compliance team approved it in the first review cycle because the audit trail was built-in from day one, not added as an afterthought.

---

Q: A critical API goes down at 2 AM. As the PM, what do you do?

*Situation:* During an on-call rotation at a previous fintech role, our card-transaction API started returning errors in the early hours. Three banking partners had already escalated within minutes of the incident starting.

*Task:* I needed to coordinate the response, keep clients informed, and make sure engineering had what they needed without adding to the chaos.

*Action:* I set up a war-room channel immediately, pinged the on-call engineer, and sent an acknowledgment message to client escalation contacts within minutes. I asked engineering for a brief status update at regular intervals and drafted a communication template so I was not writing from scratch each time. I also flagged the incident to the head of partnerships so they were not caught off-guard by morning.

*Result:* The issue was resolved in about an hour and a half. Clients received a root-cause summary by early morning. One partner specifically called out the quality of our communication in their quarterly review.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product design questions: Use a structured approach: clarify the goal and user, list assumptions, map the user journey, identify pain points, propose solutions, then define success metrics. For vegapay specifically, always identify whether the user is the bank (B2B), the bank's customer (B2C), or both. This distinction often changes the entire answer.

Prioritization questions: RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) works well here. Vegapay interviewers typically want to see you weigh client-specific requests against platform-wide improvements. A quick 2x2 of 'client value vs platform scalability' is a clean way to frame this tradeoff visually.

Metrics questions: Lead with a north star metric tied to a business outcome (for example, API uptime for a platform product, or active card usage rate for a consumer-facing feature). Then add input metrics that drive the north star, and guardrail metrics that prevent regressions. For platform products, always include reliability and error rate alongside adoption metrics.

Behavioral questions: STAR format every time. Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what the team did) and Result (quantify where possible, and if exact numbers are unavailable, describe the qualitative shift clearly).

Fintech-specific framing: Regulators, banks, and end users often have conflicting needs. When asked how you balance these, show that you can hold all three simultaneously and make tradeoffs visible rather than pretending there is no tension.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fintech domain fluency. Vegapay is an infrastructure company. Interviewers want to see that you understand how credit card issuance actually works, what role network schemes play, and why compliance is a product constraint and not just a legal problem. You do not need to be a chartered banker, but you should know basic terms: card network, BIN, credit limit, EMI, settlement cycle.

B2B product thinking. Most PM interviews test consumer product instincts. Vegapay's clients are banks and NBFCs. Interviewers look for candidates who understand that the 'user' and the 'buyer' are different people, that enterprise sales cycles affect roadmap, and that a single unhappy banking client can be a very large problem.

Data comfort. You will likely be asked how you define and track metrics. Candidates who can speak about API latency, error rates, and activation funnels alongside business metrics like revenue per client stand out from those who only know consumer product metrics.

Stakeholder management under constraints. Banking partnerships involve legal, compliance, risk, and business teams on the client side. Interviewers test whether you can navigate this complexity without losing velocity on your own roadmap.

Ownership without ego. Vegapay is a growth-stage company. Interviewers look for people who can pick up unglamorous work, write detailed PRDs, talk to support teams, and still keep a strategic view of where the product is going.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain foundation

Spend the first week learning the credit card infrastructure space. Read about how card issuance works, what BIN sponsorship means, and how embedded finance platforms typically make money. Study vegapay's public website, any product blogs or interviews from their leadership, and recent fintech news about credit card infrastructure in India.

Week 2: Product and case prep

Practice product design questions using a fintech lens. For each practice question, force yourself to distinguish between the platform user (bank), the end user (cardholder), and the business goal (vegapay's revenue). Prepare your top 5-7 STAR stories covering: data-driven decisions, regulatory constraints, stakeholder conflict, prioritization under pressure, and a product that failed and what you learned from it.

Week 3: Mock rounds and company research

Do at least two full mock interviews with someone who can give honest feedback. Revisit vegapay's open roles page to understand which teams are hiring and what problems those teams are likely solving. Prepare 3-4 sharp questions to ask interviewers. Good questions about product strategy and team structure signal genuine interest and product maturity.

Before each round: Review the specific job description carefully. Map your experience to each listed requirement. Have a one-minute introduction ready that explains why fintech infrastructure specifically interests you, not just 'fintech' in general.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating vegapay like a consumer app company. Candidates who default to consumer product frameworks without adapting them to a B2B infrastructure context tend to struggle. Always anchor your answers to the platform and the banking client, not just the end cardholder.

Skipping the regulatory layer. Indian fintech is tightly regulated. Candidates who present product ideas without acknowledging RBI guidelines, data localisation rules, or compliance review cycles come across as junior. You do not need to be a lawyer, but you need to show awareness.

Being vague on metrics. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Vegapay interviewers push on specifics. Know the difference between a leading indicator and a lagging indicator, and be ready to defend why your chosen metric actually reflects product health.

Over-indexing on company names. Candidates sometimes spend too long on where they worked rather than what they built. Interviewers care about your decision-making process. Get to the 'what I did and why' part of every answer quickly.

Not asking questions. Candidates who end the interview with 'I think I covered everything' miss a real opportunity. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team's current biggest challenge, how product and engineering collaborate, and what success looks like in the first six months.
