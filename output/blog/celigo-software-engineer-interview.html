01 Overview

Overview

Celigo is a US-based integration platform (iPaaS) that helps businesses automate workflows by connecting apps like Salesforce, NetSuite, Shopify, and hundreds of others. Their engineering team builds connectors, a low-code integration builder, and the data pipelines underneath. As of mid-2026, knok jobradar tracks 2 open Software Engineer roles at Celigo.

Candidates typically go through a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final round on culture and product fit. Most candidates report hearing back within a few weeks. Celigo values engineers who understand distributed systems, third-party API behavior, and writing clean code that holds up in production.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Celigo Software Engineer interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. Walk me through how you would design a webhook listener that reliably processes events from a third-party API, even during traffic spikes.
  2. How do you handle rate limiting when your platform calls an external API at high frequency?
  3. Explain the difference between event-driven and polling-based integrations. When would you choose each?
  4. A data pipeline transforming records from one schema to another fails midway. How do you ensure no data is lost or duplicated?
  5. Have you worked with OAuth 2.0 flows? Walk us through how you would implement a secure token refresh mechanism.
  6. How do you approach testing integrations that depend on third-party APIs you cannot control?
  7. Describe a time you debugged a production issue in a distributed system. What was your process?
  8. How would you design a system that lets non-technical users configure data mappings between two apps without writing code?
  9. What strategies would you use to make a Node.js service handle a much higher load without rewriting it from scratch?
  10. How do you keep integration code maintainable when every third-party API behaves slightly differently?
  11. Tell me about a feature you built that required you to learn something entirely new on the job.
  12. How do you balance reliability against new feature velocity when both feel urgent?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How did you handle a situation where a third-party API you depended on went down unexpectedly?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a payment gateway integration processing a large volume of orders daily. The gateway API went down on a busy sale day.

*Task:* My job was to stop our system from silently dropping orders and to restore processing automatically once the gateway recovered.

*Action:* I introduced a retry queue using Redis. Failed requests were pushed into the queue with an exponential backoff policy. I added a dead-letter queue for requests that exceeded a set retry threshold, and set up alerts so the team was notified immediately. I also added a status endpoint so the support team could see which orders were pending retry.

*Result:* When the gateway recovered, all queued orders processed automatically. No orders were lost, and the support team had full visibility throughout the incident.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you improved the performance of a data pipeline.

*Situation:* We had a nightly sync job pulling records from a CRM into our data warehouse. It was taking several hours and occasionally timing out.

*Task:* I was asked to bring the runtime down so the sync finished reliably before business hours.

*Action:* I profiled the job and found we were fetching records one at a time. I rewrote the fetch logic to use batch API calls, added parallel processing for independent record sets, and switched to incremental sync using a 'last modified' timestamp instead of doing a full re-pull each night.

*Result:* Runtime dropped sharply. The job completed well within the overnight window, and we stopped getting timeout alerts entirely.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to explain a complex technical decision to a non-technical stakeholder.

*Situation:* A product manager wanted to call a third-party API synchronously during checkout. I knew this would make the checkout flow unreliable.

*Task:* I needed to convince them to use an async approach without losing them in implementation details.

*Action:* I used a simple analogy: 'Imagine checkout is a cashier. If the cashier has to wait for a call from the warehouse before handing you a receipt, one slow call holds up the entire line.' I then sketched a quick async flow showing how we could confirm the order instantly and handle the third-party call in the background with a fallback.

*Result:* The PM agreed to the async design. The feature shipped without issues, and checkout reliability stayed high even when the third-party service was slow.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions (webhook design, pipeline architecture, connector frameworks): start with the happy path, then call out failure modes. Celigo's core product value is integration reliability, so interviewers will probe what happens when things go wrong. Always cover retry logic, idempotency, and observability before wrapping up.

For coding questions: think out loud before writing. Candidates report that Celigo interviewers care about your reasoning process as much as the final answer. State your assumptions, mention edge cases, and flag trade-offs before writing a single line.

For behavioral questions: use STAR format. Keep the Situation brief, one or two sentences, and spend most of your answer on Action and Result. Quantify outcomes where you honestly can, and do not inflate them.

For 'how do you handle X API behavior' questions: structure your answer around four steps: detect the problem, isolate it, recover gracefully, prevent recurrence. This maps directly to how integration engineers think day to day at a company like Celigo.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Integration instinct: Celigo's product lives or dies on reliable app connections. Interviewers want to see that you naturally think about idempotency, partial failures, schema mismatches, and API versioning, not just the happy path.

Node.js and API fluency: You do not need iPaaS experience to get in, but comfort with async JavaScript, REST APIs, and webhook patterns matters. Brush up on Promises, the event loop, and error propagation before your technical round.

Product curiosity: Several candidates report being asked why they want to work on integrations specifically. Having a genuine answer about why the problem space interests you stands out more than a generic 'I like challenging problems' response.

Clear communication: Celigo engineers often explain technical constraints to product managers or customers. Interviewers notice candidates who articulate their thinking clearly and without heavy jargon.

Ownership mindset: Expect questions about times you spotted a problem no one else was fixing and took initiative. At a company of Celigo's size, engineers wear multiple hats and are expected to drive things to completion.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Platform and product (start here): Sign up for Celigo's free trial and map out two or three integrations, for example Salesforce to NetSuite, or Shopify to a spreadsheet. Read their developer docs on connectors and the HTTP adapter. Understanding what the product actually does will sharpen every answer you give.

Coding and data structures: Practice problems on arrays, hash maps, trees, and graph traversal. Candidates report medium-difficulty algorithmic questions, so aim for consistent accuracy over hard-problem speed. Practice in JavaScript, since that is Celigo's primary backend language.

System design: Focus on distributed system patterns: queues, retry with backoff, idempotency keys, pagination of large API responses, and webhook delivery guarantees. Draw out a design for a generic 'connect App A to App B' flow and be ready to defend your choices under pressure.

Behavioral prep: Write out five to seven STAR stories covering a production incident, a performance improvement, a disagreement with a teammate, a feature you owned end to end, and a time you learned something fast. Rehearse them out loud, not just in your head.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating integrations as simple CRUD: Candidates who design systems without accounting for third-party API failures, rate limits, or duplicate events score lower. Address these proactively, even before the interviewer asks.
  1. Skipping idempotency: In integration contexts, processing the same event twice causes real downstream problems. Not mentioning idempotency keys or de-duplication logic signals a gap in practical experience.
  1. Vague behavioral answers: Saying 'I improved performance by optimizing queries' without specifics does not land. Use concrete actions and honest outcomes, even if the outcome was partial or took longer than expected.
  1. Not exploring the product: Candidates who have not tried Celigo or read their docs come across as uninterested. A few minutes on their product page makes a visible difference in how naturally you discuss the role.
  1. Over-engineering the coding round: Writing complex abstractions for a straightforward problem suggests difficulty calibrating complexity to context. Simple, correct, and readable beats clever every time.
  1. Treating the interview as one-way: Celigo interviews are typically conversational. Not asking questions means missing the chance to show product thinking and genuine interest in the team's work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Celigo typically have for Software Engineers?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final round focused on culture and team fit. Some candidates mention a take-home assignment in place of one technical round. The exact structure can vary by team and role level, so it is worth asking your recruiter at the very start of the process.

What programming languages does Celigo use, and should I prepare in JavaScript?

Celigo's platform is heavily Node.js on the backend and React on the frontend, so comfort with JavaScript is genuinely useful. Candidates report that interviewers are usually flexible about which language you use for coding problems, but being fluent in JavaScript signals direct relevance to the role. Brush up on async patterns, Promises, and error handling in JS before your technical round.

What salary can a Software Engineer expect at Celigo in India?

Glassdoor and industry surveys place the broader Software Engineer market in India at 6-12 LPA for entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA for mid level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA for senior roles (6-9 years). Product companies with US headquarters are commonly cited as offering compensation toward the higher end of those bands. Confirm current numbers directly with the recruiter, since actual offers vary by team, location, and experience level.

Does Celigo ask competitive programming questions or more practical ones?

Candidates report a mix. Expect at least one algorithmic question at a medium difficulty level, plus one or more design questions focused on integration scenarios like webhook handling or data transformation pipelines. Pure competitive programming questions are less common than practical API and system design problems at Celigo. Preparing both sides is the safest approach.

How long does the Celigo hiring process take from first call to offer?

Timelines vary, but candidates typically report the full process wrapping up within a few weeks of the first call. Response times between rounds depend on team availability and how many candidates are in the pipeline at the same time. If you have not heard back within a week after any round, a polite follow-up email to your recruiter is completely normal and expected.

Is there anything specific about Celigo's culture I should prepare for?

Celigo is a mid-sized product company where engineers are expected to take ownership across the stack, communicate directly with product managers and sometimes customers, and move fast without waiting for fully detailed specs. Candidates who show genuine curiosity about the integration problem space and can talk about cross-functional collaboration tend to stand out. Spending time on their engineering blog or product updates before the interview gives you specific, credible talking points.
