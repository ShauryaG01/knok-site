01 Overview

Overview

Lendingkart is one of India's leading fintech lenders, focused on giving quick, collateral-free loans to small and medium businesses. As of mid-2026, the company has 72 open roles, signalling active hiring across tech and product teams. A Technical Program Manager here sits at the intersection of engineering, product, risk, and compliance, driving programs that keep the lending platform fast, reliable, and regulatory-ready.

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds, covering cross-functional program management, technical depth, and leadership principles. The interview leans heavily on real experience with large, multi-team programs, especially those involving data pipelines, compliance constraints, or platform scalability. Expect behavioural questions grounded in fintech scenarios, not generic PM theory.

Of the 313 TPM roles listed across India right now, Bangalore leads with 41 openings, followed by Delhi (14) and Pune (13). Lendingkart's Bangalore office is typically the main hub for tech leadership hiring.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through the largest program you have owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* At my previous employer, an NBFC with significant retail lending volume, our credit decisioning pipeline had several manual steps that slowed approvals and introduced inconsistencies between branches.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the automation of the entire pipeline, coordinating four engineering squads, the risk analytics team, and the compliance officer, all within a single quarter.

*Action:* In week one I ran a dependency-mapping workshop and found that compliance needed a new bureau-data field that no squad had scoped. I raised this immediately, reorganised the milestone sequence, and negotiated one sprint of buffer with the product VP. I introduced a colour-coded RAG dashboard updated every Monday so the CTO had live visibility without attending every stand-up. When one squad fell behind, I reallocated a shared QA resource to unblock them rather than slipping the date.

*Result:* We delivered on the original target date. Manual review steps dropped significantly, and the program was cited at the quarterly all-hands as a model for cross-team delivery.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a key milestone slipped. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* During a fraud-detection upgrade at a fintech startup, a third-party ML vendor missed a data-delivery commitment two weeks before our go-live.

*Task:* I needed to protect the final release date while being transparent with the CFO, who had tied a board update to our launch.

*Action:* I immediately mapped which downstream milestones depended on the vendor data and which did not. I reprioritised the squads to work on the independent features first, buying the vendor a one-week extension without touching the go-live date. I sent a written update to the CFO the same day, including a revised confidence rating and a contingency plan if the vendor slipped again.

*Result:* The vendor delivered within the extended window. We launched on schedule, and the CFO said the early heads-up had given her time to prepare alternative talking points for the board.

---

Q: Describe a program that had regulatory compliance requirements.

*Situation:* At a digital lending company, RBI issued updated data localisation guidelines that required changes across our cloud storage, API layer, and reporting stack.

*Task:* I was brought in to create and run a cross-functional compliance program within the regulatory deadline.

*Action:* I broke the requirement into three parallel workstreams: infrastructure migration, API contract changes, and audit-trail reporting. I assigned a dedicated compliance liaison to each workstream and set up fortnightly reviews with legal counsel to validate progress. Any ambiguity in the guideline wording was escalated to legal quickly rather than letting teams interpret it independently.

*Result:* We met the regulatory deadline with no audit findings. The structured workstream model was later adopted as the company's standard approach for compliance-driven programs.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR keeps your stories tight. Every behavioural question benefits from a clear Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Spend the majority of your answer on the Action section, since that is where interviewers learn how you actually work, not just what happened around you.

Influence without authority. TPMs rarely manage engineers directly, so be ready to show how you move people through alignment, data, and visibility rather than positional authority. Prepare at least two stories where you changed a team's direction without being their manager.

Program health vocabulary. Lendingkart interviewers typically want to hear concrete metrics and tools. Be ready to describe your RAG status approach, how you define milestones versus deliverables, and how you handle scope changes formally rather than informally.

Fintech-specific framing. Frame your answers around concepts relevant to a lending business: credit risk, loan lifecycle, bureau integrations, RBI guidelines, and fraud prevention. Even if your background is not fintech, connect your experience to these themes wherever honest.

The 'so what' rule. Always close a story with a result that the business cared about. 'We shipped on time' is weak. 'We shipped on time and the risk team could now process applications without manual review' is much stronger.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Operational rigour. Lendingkart moves fast and the lending platform must stay reliable. Interviewers look for candidates who have real systems for tracking dependencies, escalating blockers early, and keeping a large group of stakeholders aligned without flooding inboxes.

Comfort with ambiguity in a regulated space. Fintech programs often run into evolving RBI or SEBI requirements mid-flight. Interviewers want to see that you can absorb regulatory change into a running program without losing momentum or missing the deadline.

Technical credibility. You do not need to write code, but you should be able to discuss API design trade-offs, data pipeline architecture, and why a microservices migration is harder than it looks. Candidates who cannot engage technically with engineers typically struggle in this role.

Business orientation. At a company where every program ultimately affects loan disbursals or default rates, interviewers want TPMs who think in business outcomes, not just delivery milestones.

Clear, direct communication. Candidates report that interviewers pay close attention to how you structure your answers. Meandering, jargon-heavy answers are flagged. Plain, structured answers earn points.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundation

Start by reading Lendingkart's publicly available blog posts, LinkedIn updates, and press coverage from 2024-2026 to understand the products they have launched and the tech bets they are making. Focus on their SME lending product, co-lending partnerships, and any platform-scale announcements.

Next, audit your own resume for your three strongest program management stories. Make sure each one has a clear business outcome, not just a delivery outcome. Write out the full STAR version of each story so you can recall it cleanly under pressure.

Week 2: Practice and Depth

Practice answering the 12 questions listed above, ideally recording yourself or doing mock sessions with a peer. Pay attention to whether your answers stay under three minutes and whether the Action section is specific enough to be credible.

Revise the basics of fintech program management: loan origination systems, credit bureau APIs, KYC and AML flows, and recent RBI digital lending guidelines. You do not need deep expertise, but you should discuss these topics without hesitation.

Prepare three or four thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer. Good ones include how the TPM team is structured, how programs are prioritised against business goals, and what a successful first few months looks like in the role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about tools instead of decisions. Saying 'I used Jira and Confluence' tells the interviewer nothing. They want to know what decisions you made, what trade-offs you navigated, and what happened as a result.

Vague results. Results like 'the project was a success' or 'the team was happy' do not land. Tie your results to something the business measured: faster approvals, reduced manual steps, or fewer escalations to senior leadership.

Skipping the 'why' on scope changes. Candidates often describe what they cut from scope but not why. Interviewers want to see a principled approach to scope trade-offs, not just a habit of reducing scope when things get hard.

No fintech context. Coming into the interview with no knowledge of Lendingkart's domain is a red flag. You do not need to know every detail, but you should know what SME lending is, who Lendingkart's customers are, and why speed and risk management both matter in equal measure.

Underselling influence stories. TPMs succeed through influence, not authority. Many candidates undersell how much work went into alignment and stakeholder management. Give that part of the story the airtime it deserves.

Not asking questions at the end. Failing to ask questions signals low interest. Have two or three specific questions ready about the team, the current program portfolio, or the biggest challenges the TPM function faces at Lendingkart right now.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Lendingkart TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds. These commonly include an initial screening with HR or a recruiter, one or two rounds with hiring managers focused on program management experience, and a final round with senior leadership. The exact structure can vary depending on the level of the role and the specific hiring team.

Does Lendingkart expect TPM candidates to have a fintech background?

A fintech background helps but is not always required, based on what candidates typically report. What matters more is your ability to discuss credit risk, compliance workflows, and platform-scale programs fluently. If your background is in e-commerce, healthtech, or enterprise SaaS, frame your stories around themes that map to lending: high transaction volumes, regulatory constraints, and multi-squad coordination.

What is the typical salary range for a TPM at Lendingkart?

Lendingkart does not publish salary bands publicly. Based on Glassdoor and publicly reported ranges for TPM roles at comparable fintech companies in India, compensation is commonly cited in the mid-to-upper LPA bracket for experienced candidates, with variation based on level, prior experience, and negotiation. Confirm current numbers directly with the recruiter during your screening call.

Should I prepare system design questions for the Lendingkart TPM interview?

Candidates typically report that the focus is on program management depth rather than whiteboard system design. However, you should be able to discuss the architecture of systems you have managed, including how components depend on each other and where bottlenecks tend to arise. Being able to speak the same language as your engineering partners is important at Lendingkart, even if you are not designing systems yourself.

How do I stand out as a TPM candidate at a company like Lendingkart?

Interviewers consistently look for candidates who combine operational rigour with business thinking. Candidates who stand out can describe exactly how they tracked program health, show two or three strong stories of influencing without authority, and connect their delivery work to business outcomes like loan processing speed or customer onboarding rates. Knowing Lendingkart's product line and the broader SME lending space also signals genuine interest in the company.

Is it worth applying to multiple TPM roles at Lendingkart at the same time?

Lendingkart currently has 72 open roles, so there may be more than one TPM opening at any given time. Candidates typically apply to the role that best matches their experience level and domain. If you apply to more than one, be prepared to clearly explain your preference and how your background fits each one during the recruiter call.
