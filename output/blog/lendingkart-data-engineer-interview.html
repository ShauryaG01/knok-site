01 Overview

Overview

Lendingkart is one of India's well-known fintech NBFCs, focused on collateral-free working capital loans for small businesses. Their data engineering team is central to credit decisioning, processing loan applications, bureau pulls, repayment signals, and product telemetry at scale. A Data Engineer here works on pipelines that directly influence how loan approvals and risk models function.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Questions at Lendingkart Data Engineer interviews span three areas: hands-on pipeline experience, SQL and coding, and system design for fintech scenarios. Candidates commonly report seeing these:

  1. Walk me through a data pipeline you built end to end. What were the failure modes and how did you handle them?
  2. Lendingkart processes loan applications in near real-time. How would you design a pipeline to ingest and deduplicate borrower data arriving from multiple sources?
  3. Write a SQL query to find borrowers who made their first repayment late but all subsequent repayments on time.
  4. How do you handle schema evolution in a production Spark pipeline without breaking downstream consumers?
  5. Explain how you would build a change data capture (CDC) pipeline from a transactional loan database into a data warehouse.
  6. We use Airflow for orchestration. Describe a time when a DAG failure caused a data quality issue and how you resolved it.
  7. How would you design a data model for a lending product that tracks loan status transitions over time?
  8. What is your approach to data quality checks for a credit bureau pull pipeline where stale or missing data has direct business impact?
  9. How would you optimise a slow Spark job that processes a very large volume of transaction records daily?
  10. How do you set up monitoring and alerting for a pipeline that feeds a risk scoring model?
  11. How do you manage PII in data pipelines given RBI data localisation and compliance requirements?
  12. You notice a sudden spike in null values in a key field used by the credit model. Walk me through your investigation.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a data pipeline you built end to end. What were the failure modes?

*Situation:* At my previous company, the risk team needed a daily pipeline delivering enriched borrower profiles to the credit scoring model, but data was arriving from several source systems on inconsistent schedules.

*Task:* I owned the design and delivery of this pipeline from raw ingestion to the final curated table in our data warehouse.

*Action:* I built an Airflow DAG that pulled from each source using incremental loads, applied deduplication logic based on phone number and PAN hash, ran a suite of data quality checks before any downstream write, and used Spark for the heavy transformation step. I designed the pipeline to fail fast on quality violations rather than silently pass bad data, and set up alerting on SLA breaches with dead-letter queues for records that failed validation.

*Result:* The pipeline ran reliably for over a year. We caught multiple upstream schema changes before they reached the model, and the risk team reported higher confidence in the input data. Maintenance load stayed low because failure modes were explicit and documented.

---

Q: How do you handle schema evolution in a production Spark pipeline without breaking downstream consumers?

*Situation:* A source system at my previous company changed a field type without notice, which broke our production Spark job and caused a significant pipeline delay.

*Task:* I was asked to make the pipeline resilient to future schema changes.

*Action:* I introduced a schema registry to version all incoming schemas and updated the Spark job to read schemas at runtime rather than hardcoding them. I added a compatibility check at the start of each run: if a breaking change was detected, the job would halt and alert rather than fail mid-run. I also created a data contract shared with the upstream team to formalise expectations on both sides.

*Result:* The pipeline automatically detected several upcoming schema changes before they could cause failures. The on-call team had a clear runbook for each case, and pipeline downtime from schema issues dropped to zero.

---

Q: You notice a sudden spike in null values in a key field used by the credit model. What do you do?

*Situation:* Our data quality dashboard flagged that the 'monthly_income' field in the borrower profile table had spiked from a negligible null rate to a much higher one overnight.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly because the credit model used this field directly, making incorrect approvals or rejections a real business risk.

*Action:* I first checked whether this was a pipeline issue or a source issue. Querying the raw ingestion layer showed the nulls were already present there, ruling out a transformation bug. I traced it to a new mobile app version shipped the previous night that had made the income field optional in the form. I escalated to the product team with the evidence and, in parallel, built fallback logic using each borrower's last known income from historical records to fill nulls temporarily.

*Result:* The product team patched the app within a few hours to make the field required again. The fallback logic kept credit model inputs valid during the window, and no incorrect credit decisions were flagged by the risk team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use these three frameworks to structure your answers clearly in the Lendingkart interview.

STAR for experience questions. Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most time on Action (the specific technical choices you made), and always end with a concrete or observable Result. Interviewers at fintech companies want to hear what you personally built and decided, not what the team did in general.

Pipeline design checklist for system design questions. When asked to design a pipeline, cover these angles in order: ingestion (source, frequency, volume), transformation (tool choice and why), storage (schema, partitioning, format), data quality (where checks sit, what happens on failure), and observability (monitoring, alerting, SLA). For Lendingkart specifically, always address how you would handle PII and compliance, since the company operates under RBI guidelines.

Root cause framework for debugging questions. When asked to investigate a data issue, follow this sequence: confirm the scope (one table, one pipeline, or one source?), isolate the layer (raw ingestion, transformation, or serving?), check recent changes (deploys, upstream schema updates, volume spikes), then fix forward with a short-term patch and a long-term solution. Debugging scenarios are common in Lendingkart interviews, so having this structure ready helps you think out loud clearly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates commonly report, Lendingkart Data Engineer interviews are looking for a few specific things beyond raw technical knowledge.

Comfort with messy, high-stakes data. Fintech data is noisy: duplicate loan applications, inconsistent formats from mobile and web sources, bureau pull failures. Interviewers want to see that you have built pipelines that handle this gracefully, not just happy-path pipelines.

Ownership and debugging instinct. Candidates who clearly own their work, who can say 'I noticed X, I investigated Y, I fixed Z' rather than 'the team handled it', tend to stand out. Debugging questions are common and often open-ended to see how you reason through a problem.

Practical tool knowledge. Spark, Airflow, SQL, and at least one cloud platform (AWS or GCP) appear in most interviews. You do not need to be an expert in every tool, but you should be able to explain your choices and trade-offs clearly.

Awareness of compliance and data sensitivity. Lendingkart operates in a regulated space. Candidates who mention PII handling, data access controls, and audit trails without being prompted signal that they understand the context they are working in.

Clear communication. Data Engineers here work closely with risk, product, and analytics teams. Interviewers want to see that you can explain a technical design decision to a non-technical stakeholder, not just to another engineer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Spend a few weeks on focused preparation. Here is a practical plan.

SQL and coding foundations (first week). Practise window function problems: rank, dense_rank, row_number, lag, and lead with partitioning. Focus on loan and transaction scenarios such as repayment histories, customer timelines, and cohort analysis. If you are rusty on SQL, this is the highest-leverage investment.

Pipeline and system design (second week). Review Spark fundamentals (partitioning, shuffles, broadcast joins) and Airflow concepts (DAG dependencies, retries, sensors). Practise designing one end-to-end pipeline per session: start with batch ETL, then move to more complex scenarios like CDC, near real-time ingestion, and multi-source deduplication. Use the pipeline design checklist from the answer frameworks section.

Company and domain context (third week). Read about how NBFCs and digital lending companies use data: credit bureau integrations, collections analytics, and fraud signals. Understand RBI data localisation guidelines at a high level. Identify a few projects from your own experience that map to fintech data problems and practise telling them in STAR format.

Before each round. Review the job description again, note any tools or use cases mentioned, and prepare one question for the interviewer about the team's current data infrastructure challenges. Asking good questions signals genuine interest and curiosity.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

These are the mistakes candidates most commonly make in Lendingkart Data Engineer interviews.

Describing team work as personal work, or the reverse. Be clear about what you individually built or decided. Interviewers probe on ownership, and vague answers raise doubts.

Happy-path system design. Designing a pipeline that works when everything goes right but ignoring what happens when the source is late, data quality fails, or the job crashes midway. Always address failure modes and recovery.

Generic SQL answers. Writing a basic GROUP BY when the question calls for a window function or a self-join. Practise fintech-specific SQL scenarios before the interview.

Ignoring compliance context. Not mentioning PII handling or data sensitivity in pipeline design answers. In a fintech setting, skipping this is a red flag for interviewers.

Underselling results. Ending a STAR answer with 'it worked fine' rather than stating a concrete outcome. Even a qualitative result, such as 'the risk team stopped manually verifying the data', is better than nothing.

Over-engineering system design. Proposing a complex streaming architecture for a problem a simple Airflow DAG and database would solve. Match the complexity to the problem, and explain why a simpler option may not be sufficient before jumping to a heavier stack.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Lendingkart Data Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds. These usually include an initial screening call, one or two technical rounds covering SQL, coding, and pipeline design, and a final round with a manager or senior stakeholder. The exact number and format can vary, so confirm the process with your recruiter after the first call.

What SQL topics should I focus on for this interview?

Window functions are the most commonly reported SQL topic: rank, dense_rank, row_number, lag, and lead with partitioning. Multi-table joins with filtering and aggregations on time-series data (like repayment histories) also come up often. Practise writing queries that answer business questions about customer timelines and loan status changes, not just textbook problems.

Is Spark knowledge required, or is SQL enough?

SQL knowledge is essential and tested in almost every Data Engineer interview at Lendingkart, candidates report. Spark knowledge is also expected for mid and senior roles, particularly around performance optimisation and handling large-scale data. If your Spark experience is limited, focus on explaining concepts clearly and being honest about where you have hands-on experience versus where you have only studied the theory.

What salary can I expect for a mid-level Data Engineer role at Lendingkart?

Specific Lendingkart offer numbers are not publicly reported. The mid-level Data Engineer band (3-5 years experience) across India broadly sits at 14-26 LPA based on knok's job radar data. Actual offers depend on your experience, negotiation, and the team's budget. Check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for more recent community-reported numbers from people who have been through the process.

How important is fintech domain knowledge for this role?

You do not need deep fintech expertise to get the role, but showing domain awareness helps significantly. Interviewers appreciate when candidates understand why data quality matters in credit decisioning, or mention PII handling and RBI compliance without being prompted. Spending a few hours before your interview reading about how digital lending data flows work is a worthwhile investment.
