01 Overview

Overview

Thermondo GmbH is a Berlin-based energy-tech company that installs heat pumps and heating systems at scale across Germany. Their mobile app serves both end customers and field technicians who work in areas with variable network connectivity. As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows Thermondo has 199 open roles, signalling active growth across teams.

The Mobile Engineer interview process typically spans 3-4 stages. Candidates report an initial HR call to check communication and motivation, followed by a technical round covering coding and mobile-specific topics, then a system design or architecture session, and finally a culture and values conversation. All rounds are typically conducted in English.

Thermondo values engineers who think about the product user (a field technician or homeowner) and not just the code, so expect product-thinking questions alongside technical ones.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you approach offline-first design?

*Situation:* At my previous company we built a field-service app for technicians who visited client sites. Network coverage was unreliable.

*Task:* I needed to make sure technicians could log job updates even with no signal, without losing any data.

*Action:* I introduced a local SQLite store as the source of truth on device. Every write went to local storage first, then a background sync worker pushed changes to the server when connectivity returned. I used a 'last-write-wins' strategy for non-conflicting fields and flagged conflicting records for manual review in an admin panel.

*Result:* Field teams reported near-zero data loss during connectivity gaps. The sync queue added minimal overhead and the pattern became the standard for all offline-capable features in the app.

---

Q: How do you detect and fix a memory leak on a real device?

*Situation:* Our iOS app had a screen showing live sensor readings. QA noticed the app would crash after several minutes on that screen.

*Task:* I had to reproduce, isolate, and fix the leak without disrupting the release schedule.

*Action:* I ran the app through Instruments with the Leaks and Allocations instruments. I found a retain cycle between a view controller and a closure in our sensor data publisher. I used 'weak self' captures inside the closures and verified the deallocation with a 'deinit' print statement. I also added a unit test that checked for the deallocation pattern to prevent regressions.

*Result:* Memory growth on that screen dropped to a flat line. The fix went out in the next patch and QA closed the crash ticket.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product decision.

*Situation:* My product manager wanted to add a mandatory onboarding carousel with several screens before users could access the app. I felt it would hurt activation.

*Task:* I needed to raise my concern constructively without blocking the sprint.

*Action:* I pulled publicly reported activation benchmarks for similar apps and presented a case for a shorter, skippable version. I proposed a quick A/B test to measure drop-off before committing to either approach. I framed it as a risk reduction exercise rather than a disagreement.

*Result:* The team agreed to the test. The skippable version showed better activation in the test cohort, and that became the shipped version.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR is the most reliable structure for behavioural questions. Lead with *Situation* (one or two sentences of context), then *Task* (what you specifically needed to do), then *Action* (the steps you took, using 'I' not 'we'), then *Result* (a concrete outcome, even if qualitative).

For technical questions, try the Clarify, Design, Trade-off pattern. First clarify the constraints (network, device, user type), then describe your design, then name at least one trade-off you accepted. Thermondo interviewers typically value engineers who acknowledge trade-offs rather than presenting one-size-fits-all solutions.

For system design, open with the user journey before jumping into components. 'A field technician opens the app to log a job, so the critical path is...' anchors your answer in the product context and signals product empathy immediately.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on publicly known information about Thermondo's product and candidate reports, interviewers look for four things.

Product empathy. Thermondo's mobile users include tradespeople on job sites. Candidates who talk about users (not just APIs) tend to stand out.

Depth over breadth. Interviewers typically probe one topic deeply rather than covering everything. Expect follow-up questions when you give a high-level answer, so be ready to go into implementation detail.

Ownership. They want engineers who have shipped things, hit problems, and fixed them. 'We' answers get probed with 'What specifically did you do?'

Communication. Because the team works in English and is cross-functional, clear thinking under pressure matters as much as technical knowledge.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core mobile fundamentals. Review the lifecycle of your primary platform (activity/fragment lifecycle on Android, UIViewController on iOS, or widget tree on Flutter). Revise state management patterns you have used and be ready to compare them. Read through Thermondo's app reviews on the Play Store or App Store to understand what real users say.

Week 2, part 1: System design practice. Pick a couple of mobile system design topics such as offline sync, push notification architecture, or a real-time data feed, and sketch out a design for each. Focus on constraints before components.

Week 2, part 2: Behavioural prep. Write down a few stories covering a technical challenge, a disagreement, and a time you improved a process. Practice delivering each story concisely.

Week 3: Company and process. Spend time reading about Thermondo's product before each interview round. Look at their open roles page to understand which teams are hiring. If you receive a take-home task, test it on a real device, not just a simulator.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Talking in 'we' throughout. Interviewers need to know your individual contribution. Say 'I designed...' and save 'we' for team context.
  2. Skipping trade-offs. Every architecture decision has a cost. Naming the cost shows senior thinking.
  3. Ignoring the Thermondo context. Field technicians, poor connectivity, and energy-tech are clues about what the team cares about. Answers that ignore the domain feel generic.
  4. Over-engineering the system design. Start with the simplest thing that works, then add complexity only when a constraint requires it.
  5. Not asking clarifying questions. Jumping into an answer without confirming constraints is a red flag in design rounds.
  6. Under-preparing for behavioural rounds. Many candidates prep only for coding, but candidates report that the culture round at Thermondo carries significant weight.

Frequently asked

What tech stack does Thermondo use for mobile?

Thermondo has not publicly disclosed their full mobile stack. Candidates report seeing questions that cover both native (Android/iOS) concepts and cross-platform frameworks. Review your strongest platform in depth and be ready to discuss cross-platform trade-offs. Job descriptions for open roles typically list the required stack, so check the posting closely before your interview.

How many interview rounds does Thermondo typically have for a Mobile Engineer?

Candidates report a process of typically 3-4 rounds: an HR screen, a technical coding or take-home round, a system design session, and a final conversation with senior engineers or a hiring manager. The exact structure can vary by team. Confirm the format with your recruiter at the start of the process so you can prepare accordingly.

Does Thermondo give a take-home assignment?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home coding task as part of the technical round. If you get one, test your solution on a real device and write a short note explaining your design decisions. Reviewers typically look at code quality, architecture, and how you handle edge cases, as much as whether the feature works end to end.

Is the interview conducted in English or German?

Thermondo is a German company but candidates report that engineering interviews are conducted in English. Strong written and spoken English matters, especially for async communication in a cross-functional team. You do not need German for the interview itself, though knowing basic German can help with cultural context.

How long does the Thermondo hiring process take?

Candidates report the process typically runs a few weeks from first contact to offer, though timelines vary by team and role urgency. Following up politely with the recruiter after each round is perfectly fine. With 199 open roles as of mid-2026, hiring is active and pipelines tend to move at a reasonable pace.

What salary can a Mobile Engineer expect at Thermondo?

Thermondo has not publicly disclosed salary bands for this role. For market benchmarks, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for Berlin-based mobile engineering roles. Compensation at funded climate-tech scale-ups is commonly cited as competitive for the Berlin market, but always negotiate based on your experience level and any competing offers you hold.
