01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Khatabook is one of India's most recognised fintech startups, building digital bookkeeping and payment tools for small merchants and shop owners across the country. The company's mission sits at the intersection of financial inclusion and mobile-first technology, which makes it an exciting place to work if you care about real-world impact at scale.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar is tracking 61 open roles at Khatabook across functions like engineering, product management, design, sales, and operations. That breadth means whether you write code, close deals, or manage projects, there is likely a fit worth exploring.

Khatabook hires through a mix of sources: direct applications through their careers page, referrals from existing employees, and platforms that aggregate fintech jobs. Competition is real, especially for engineering and product roles, but the volume of open positions signals an active growth phase where the company is genuinely looking to add people.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The stages below reflect commonly reported patterns for growth-stage Indian fintechs. Khatabook's exact process can vary by role and team, so treat this as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Stage 1: Resume Shortlisting
Your application is reviewed by the talent team or a hiring manager. For tech roles, a GitHub profile or portfolio link can matter. For business roles, clear outcomes in your resume (growth you drove, team sizes, processes you built) help you stand out. Shortlisting can take a few days to a couple of weeks depending on applicant volume.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR Call
If shortlisted, expect a short call with someone from HR or talent acquisition. This round is about understanding your background, your interest in Khatabook specifically, your current notice period, and salary expectations. Be honest here, especially about notice periods. Startups often move fast, and a mismatch on timelines can derail things late in the process.

Stage 3: Technical or Skills Assessment
For engineering roles, this is usually a coding round on a platform like HackerRank or a take-home assignment. For product roles, you might get a product case or an analytical exercise. For sales or operations, a short situational test or a mock pitch is common. This round filters heavily, so preparation here pays off the most.

Stage 4: Technical or Domain Interview
This is a deeper conversation with someone from the team you would join, and there may be more than one such round depending on seniority. Engineers can expect data structures, system design, and questions about past projects. Product managers are typically asked how they have handled roadmap decisions, worked with engineers, or prioritised conflicting demands. Business and ops candidates can expect questions about market knowledge and problem-solving approach.

Stage 5: Cross-functional or Leadership Round
Many Khatabook roles include a round with a senior leader or someone from a different function. This tests how well you think beyond your own domain and whether you communicate clearly with people who do not share your technical background. Candidates who connect their work to business outcomes tend to do well here.

Stage 6: HR and Offer Discussion
The final stage is a culture and values conversation, followed by the offer. Salary negotiation happens here. It is reasonable to ask for a few days to consider the offer, and to ask questions about your team, your manager, and what the first few months would look like.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Khatabook's product serves small business owners, often in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, who may be using digital tools for the very first time. This context shapes what the company values in its people.

Ownership mindset. Startups at this stage do not have a manager watching every task. Interviewers often probe whether you have taken initiative in past roles, pushed a project forward without being asked, or handled a difficult situation with limited guidance.

Comfort with ambiguity. The product roadmap can shift, priorities can change, and the data you need might not exist yet. Candidates who show they can work through unclear situations, make reasonable assumptions, and iterate quickly tend to impress.

Understanding of the target user. Khatabook's user is a kirana shop owner or a small trader, not a tech-savvy urban professional. Showing genuine curiosity about this user segment, whether in a product interview or a sales conversation, signals strong cultural fit.

Strong fundamentals. For engineers, this means clean code and sound system design thinking. For product managers, it means structured thinking and the ability to prioritise ruthlessly. For business roles, it means knowing your numbers and being able to tell a story with data.

Clear communication. Across all roles, the ability to explain your thinking clearly is valued. You do not need to be polished, but you do need to be coherent and direct, whether you are speaking in English or Hindi.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know the product before your interview. Download the Khatabook app, use it as a merchant would, and note what works well and what could be improved. Being able to speak concretely about the product is one of the fastest ways to stand out, and interviewers will notice if you have not done this step.

Research the market they operate in. Khatabook competes in the digital bookkeeping and fintech-for-SMB space. Knowing who else operates in this space and what makes Khatabook's approach distinct will help you answer 'why Khatabook?' convincingly and without sounding like you read a summary five minutes before the call.

Prepare your past-work stories. Use a simple format: the situation you faced, what you did specifically, and what the result was. Have several of these ready for different contexts, such as a time you handled a conflict, a time you improved a process, or a time something failed and what you learned from it.

Practice for the technical round. If you are applying for an engineering role, spend time on problem-solving platforms focusing on arrays, strings, recursion, and basic system design. For product roles, practice structuring product case answers: start with the user, move to the problem, then solutions, then trade-offs.

Prepare smart questions to ask. At the end of most rounds, you will be invited to ask questions. Asking about the team's current biggest challenge, how success is measured in the role, or what the onboarding process looks like shows genuine interest and helps you evaluate the opportunity at the same time.

Sort your documents early. Have your current offer letter, recent salary slips, and any relevant certifications ready. Startups often move quickly once they decide to make an offer, and delays on your end can create friction at the worst possible moment.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Khatabook sits firmly in the startup culture bracket: fast-moving, relatively flat in hierarchy, and focused on measurable outcomes. If you are coming from a large IT services company or a public sector background, the shift can feel significant at first. Meetings tend to be shorter and more action-oriented, and the expectation is that you drive your own work rather than wait for detailed briefs.

Mission alignment matters. The company's focus on financial inclusion and helping underserved small businesses is something many employees cite as a motivating factor. Candidates who talk about this sincerely, rather than as an interview talking point, tend to connect better with interviewers.

Collaboration across functions. Engineers work closely with product managers, and sales teams work closely with operations. Siloed thinking is a red flag. Showing that you have worked across teams, or are comfortable doing so, will land well in any round.

Pace and adaptability. The product and business priorities can change quickly in response to market conditions. People who thrive here tend to be comfortable with change and good at re-prioritising without losing momentum or morale.

Learning culture. Khatabook values people who are curious and keep up with their domain, whether in payments regulation, UX for low-literacy users, or emerging fintech infrastructure. Showing that you read and think about your field, not just execute tasks, is a positive signal at every level.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the entire interview process at Khatabook usually take?

Based on commonly reported experiences at similar Indian fintechs, the end-to-end process from application to offer typically spans a few weeks. Urgent or senior hires can move faster. If you have not heard back after a week at any stage, a polite follow-up email to the recruiter is reasonable and usually welcomed.

Does Khatabook hire freshers or is it mostly for experienced candidates?

Khatabook does hire freshers, particularly for engineering roles where strong fundamentals and competitive programming performance carry real weight. Business and operations roles tend to prefer candidates with some prior experience, though internship experience can count. Check the specific job description carefully since requirements vary by team and level.

How important are referrals at Khatabook?

Referrals carry weight at most Indian startups, and Khatabook is no exception. A referral from a current employee can help your resume get noticed faster in a crowded applicant pool. If you have a connection at the company, reaching out before applying is worth doing. If you do not, a well-written direct application with a clear cover note can still get you through.

What salary can I expect at Khatabook?

Specific compensation figures are not publicly reported in a reliable way for all roles and levels at Khatabook. Platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi have community-sourced data for Indian fintech startups that can give you a rough benchmark by function and seniority. Going into the HR round with a number based on your market research and your current CTC is the most grounded approach.

What kinds of roles are currently open at Khatabook?

As of July 2026, knok jobradar is tracking 61 open roles at Khatabook. These span engineering, product, design, sales, and operations functions. The breadth of openings suggests hiring is active across the company, not just in one team, so it is worth browsing the full list rather than assuming only tech roles are available.

How should I follow up after an interview round?

A short thank-you note to the recruiter or interviewer within a day of a round is a good habit. It does not need to be long: a couple of sentences confirming your interest and mentioning one thing you found interesting about the conversation is enough. If you have been waiting more than a week for feedback, a single polite follow-up is appropriate and rarely comes across as pushy.
