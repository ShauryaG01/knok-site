01 Overview

Overview

Experian is a global information services company with significant operations in India, focused on credit data, analytics platforms, and financial technology products. As of July 2026, Experian has 2 open Technical Program Manager roles.

Candidates typically report a process that includes a recruiter or HR screening call, one or two technical and behavioral rounds with engineering or program leadership, and a final discussion with a hiring manager or panel. The exact structure varies by team and seniority level.

TPMs at Experian sit at the intersection of technology delivery and business outcomes, coordinating across product, data engineering, compliance, and operations teams. Because Experian handles sensitive credit and financial data, interviewers pay close attention to whether you have worked in regulated environments and whether you think proactively about risk. Come prepared with specific examples from your past work, especially those involving cross-functional alignment, compliance-aware delivery, and measurable results.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you managed a program with strict compliance requirements.

*Situation:* My team was tasked with migrating a core lending platform to a new cloud infrastructure. The platform processed customer financial data, so every change had to pass internal audit and regulatory review.

*Task:* I needed to coordinate six teams across engineering, security, legal, and operations while keeping the timeline intact and ensuring zero compliance gaps.

*Action:* I set up a compliance checkpoint at every major milestone before any code moved to production. I created a shared risk register that every team updated weekly, and I ran a fortnightly review with the legal and audit leads so issues surfaced early. When one team's deliverable slipped, I re-sequenced the dependency chain rather than pushing the entire program out.

*Result:* The migration completed on schedule, passed the external audit on the first review, and the process was cited internally as a model for future migration programs.

---

Q: Describe a program that went off track. What did you do?

*Situation:* A data pipeline rebuild I was managing fell three weeks behind because a vendor delivered incomplete APIs.

*Task:* I had to protect the downstream teams waiting on the pipeline and keep leadership confident without hiding the reality of the delay.

*Action:* I flagged the delay immediately in the weekly status report with a clear impact statement and three recovery options, each with trade-offs explained. I escalated the vendor issue through procurement while having the engineering team build mock APIs so downstream work could continue in parallel. I also restructured the schedule so non-dependent tracks stayed on their original dates.

*Result:* Final delivery came in only one week later than the original target. Stakeholders appreciated the transparency, and the vendor evaluation checklist was updated based on this experience.

---

Q: How do you prioritize competing stakeholder requests?

*Situation:* At a previous company, three business units escalated their programs to 'critical' in the same quarter, each expecting dedicated engineering resources.

*Task:* I needed a fair, transparent way to resolve the conflict without damaging trust with any of the three teams.

*Action:* I proposed a structured prioritization session with all three business leads and the CTO. I brought a scoring matrix covering business impact, regulatory obligation, and engineering effort. Each stakeholder scored their own program and we compared results openly. Two programs were combined into one workstream because their technical needs overlapped significantly.

*Result:* All three teams accepted the outcome because the process was visible and objective. We delivered both workstreams within the quarter and avoided a significant amount of duplicated engineering effort, as confirmed in our delivery retrospective.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the foundation for every behavioral question. Keep the Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on the Action, and always close with a concrete Result tied to business or delivery impact.

RACI for alignment questions: When asked how you drive cross-functional alignment, structure your answer around who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed. This shows you think systematically about ownership rather than relying on informal influence alone.

Risk-first framing: For program delivery questions, open with how you identified the top risks before talking about execution. Experian values proactive risk thinking given its regulated, data-sensitive environment.

Communication layering: Demonstrate that you adjust the level of detail by audience: high-level progress and blockers for leadership, technical specifics for engineering, and business impact for product and commercial teams.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Experian TPM interviewers typically focus on four qualities.

Regulated-environment experience: Experian deals with credit and financial data under significant compliance pressure. Candidates who have worked in BFSI, healthcare, or other compliance-heavy sectors carry a clear advantage.

Cross-functional influence without authority: TPMs at Experian do not directly manage engineering teams. Your ability to lead through clarity, credibility, and relationship-building matters more than technical depth alone.

Structured thinking under pressure: Interviewers often probe what you did when things went wrong. They want to see a methodical, calm response rather than improvisation or blame-shifting.

Data-driven program management: Experian is an analytics business at its core. Candidates who track program health with clear metrics and use data to make prioritization decisions fit the culture well.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Build your story bank. Write out 8-10 STAR stories covering program delivery, stakeholder conflict, risk management, and failure recovery. Make sure at least two involve compliance-heavy or regulated environments.

Step 2: Research Experian's India business. Read their publicly available annual reports and press releases to understand their product lines (credit bureau, analytics, decisioning platforms) and any recent technology initiatives publicly reported.

Step 3: Practice out loud. Run mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Time your answers. Most candidates over-explain the Situation and under-explain the Action and Result.

Step 4: Prepare smart questions. For each round, have two or three questions ready about the team's current program challenges, how TPM success is measured in the first six months, and how engineering and product teams collaborate at Experian.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague answers without outcomes: Saying 'the project was successful' is not enough. Quantify your outcomes wherever you honestly can, using real data from your own work history.

Treating the TPM role like a PM role: TPMs at companies like Experian are expected to engage more deeply with technical architecture trade-offs and engineering constraints than a product manager would. Show that technical depth.

Ignoring the compliance and data sensitivity context: Candidates who give generic program management answers without acknowledging Experian's regulated environment miss what interviewers are listening for.

Describing only easy wins in alignment questions: Interviewers want to hear how you handled genuine conflict, resistance, or competing priorities, not situations where everyone agreed from the start.

Not asking any questions: Arriving with no questions signals low interest. Ask about the team's biggest delivery challenge, how success is measured for this role in the first six months, or what the most significant program risk is right now.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Experian TPM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: an initial HR or recruiter call, one or two technical and behavioral discussions with engineering or program leaders, and a final round with a hiring manager or panel. The exact number of rounds varies by team and the seniority of the role. Some candidates also report a written case study or take-home scenario, though this is not universal. Always confirm the process structure with the recruiter before your first interview.

Does Experian ask coding questions in TPM interviews?

Typically, no. Candidates report that Experian TPM interviews focus on program management experience, cross-functional leadership, and system design thinking at a conceptual level rather than hands-on coding. You should be comfortable discussing technical trade-offs, architecture choices, and engineering constraints. Being able to speak the language of engineering teams with credibility matters more than writing code in an interview setting.

Is Experian a good company for TPMs in India?

Publicly reported reviews on platforms like Glassdoor suggest that Experian India offers exposure to large-scale data and analytics programs, a relatively stable work environment, and products used by banks and financial institutions across the country. As with any company, experience can vary significantly by team and manager. Speaking to current or former Experian employees on LinkedIn before accepting an offer is always a smart step.

How should I prepare for technical questions in an Experian TPM interview?

Experian TPM interviews are unlikely to ask you to design a system from scratch the way a software engineer would. Prepare instead to discuss how you break down a large technical program into workstreams, how you manage dependencies between platform teams, and how you make trade-off decisions when engineering capacity is limited. Familiarity with data pipeline architectures, API integration challenges, and cloud migration programs will help you speak credibly to common Experian technology contexts.
