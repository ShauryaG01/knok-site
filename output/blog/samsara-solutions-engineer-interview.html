01 Overview

Overview

Samsara is a US-listed IoT and fleet management company used by logistics, construction, utilities, and food delivery businesses to track vehicles, equipment, and field workers in real time. Their Solutions Engineer (SE) role sits at the intersection of technical depth and customer-facing communication. You will be expected to run technical demos, lead proofs-of-concept, answer integration questions, and partner with sales to win deals.

The interview process typically spans several rounds: an initial recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a technical or demo round, and a final round with cross-functional stakeholders. Candidates report that at least one round involves a live roleplay or scenario exercise. Samsara currently has 350 open roles globally, and the Solutions Engineer position is a key part of their go-to-market engine.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and reflect the competencies Samsara typically evaluates in SE interviews.

  1. Walk me through how you would run a discovery call with a fleet operations manager who has never used telematics software before.
  2. Samsara's platform covers GPS tracking, AI dashcams, asset monitoring, and driver safety scores. How would you tailor a demo for a mid-size logistics company evaluating multiple vendors?
  3. Describe a time you took a complex technical topic and explained it clearly to someone without a technical background.
  4. A proof-of-concept is two weeks in and the customer says they are not seeing the value they expected. What do you do?
  5. Tell me about a time you worked closely with an account executive to close a competitive deal. What was your specific contribution?
  6. A prospect says your pricing is higher than a competitor. How do you handle that conversation?
  7. How do you learn a new product quickly when you join a new company?
  8. Describe a situation where you managed priorities across several active customer accounts at the same time.
  9. Samsara serves industries like transportation, construction, and food and beverage. How would your demo differ across these verticals?
  10. Walk me through a technical objection you received from a customer and how you resolved it.
  11. How do you keep up with developments in IoT, fleet technology, or connected vehicles?
  12. Describe a time you went out of your way to help a customer succeed during an implementation.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you took a complex technical topic and explained it clearly to someone without a technical background.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we sold an API-based integration platform. A regional sales head needed to present the product to a government client's procurement committee, none of whom had a software background.

*Task:* I had to prepare her for technical questions and make sure the committee could understand the value of the platform without getting lost in jargon.

*Action:* I built a one-page visual showing data flow as a 'pipe' from their existing systems into a central dashboard. I replaced every technical term with a business outcome. I then ran two practice sessions with the sales head, role-playing as a committee member asking basic questions.

*Result:* The presentation went well. The sales head told me afterward that the committee asked sharper business questions rather than expressing confusion, and the deal moved to the next stage.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked closely with an account executive to close a competitive deal.

*Situation:* We were in a three-way competitive evaluation for a mid-size logistics firm. The prospect was leaning toward a cheaper competitor after the first demo round.

*Task:* My job was to show technical superiority in a live bake-off while helping the AE frame our total cost of ownership story.

*Action:* I mapped every prospect pain point from the discovery call to a specific product capability. During the demo, I walked through exactly how our platform addressed each one. I also prepared a one-page integration FAQ the AE could leave behind, covering the IT team's main concerns.

*Result:* We won the deal. The customer told the AE that our demo felt the most relevant to their actual situation, compared with the other vendors they had evaluated.

---

Q: A proof-of-concept is going off-track midway through. What do you do?

*Situation:* At a previous role, a manufacturing client flagged mid-POC that our platform's reports did not match the format their ops team used internally.

*Task:* I needed to quickly understand the gap and either fix it or set realistic expectations before the evaluation window closed.

*Action:* I set up a call with the customer's ops lead and their IT admin the same day. I asked them to walk me through the exact reports they were comparing ours against. It turned out two key fields were mapped to different column names during data import. I reconfigured the mapping during the same call and walked them through the updated output. I also sent a short written summary of what changed and why.

*Result:* The customer confirmed the reports now matched their workflow. The POC continued and eventually converted to a paid contract.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Discovery call (SPIN-style). Start with Situation questions (current fleet size, tools already in use), move to Problem questions (what breaks, what costs time), then Implication (what does that problem cost the business), and finally Need-payoff (if this were solved, what would it mean for operations?). This structure mirrors how Samsara typically trains its own SE team, so using it signals alignment.

Technical objection (ACE). Acknowledge the concern without dismissing it. Clarify what specifically is driving it (is it a feature gap, an integration worry, or a past bad experience?). Then Explain with evidence, either a product demo, a customer reference story, or relevant documentation.

Demo structure. Open with a business outcome, not a feature list. Show the customer's own scenario inside the demo, not a generic walkthrough. Close each module by asking: 'Does this match what you were imagining, or should we adjust?' This keeps the conversation collaborative rather than one-directional.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Samsara interviewers are typically looking for four things in an SE candidate.

Technical credibility without arrogance. You do not need to know Samsara's stack on day one, but you must show you can learn fast, ask the right questions, and translate technical details into customer-facing language. Candidates who over-engineer answers or lead with jargon tend to score lower.

Customer empathy. SE interviews at Samsara often include a roleplay or scenario question. Interviewers want to see that you listen before you pitch. Asking clarifying questions before jumping to a solution is seen as a strength, not hesitation.

Sales partnership. Samsara SEs work closely with account executives. Interviewers look for candidates who treat AEs as partners, share pipeline context proactively, and can coach AEs on technical points without making them feel talked down to.

Resilience and ownership. Fleet and industrial IoT deals can be long and involve multiple stakeholders. Interviewers want to see that you take ownership when things go sideways, rather than attributing problems to the product or the customer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: product and industry depth.
Spend time with Samsara's public website, product documentation, and any customer case studies you can find. Focus on the three core pillars: fleet tracking, driver safety, and asset monitoring. Read about how logistics and construction companies use telematics. For each feature you read about, note the business outcome it drives, not just what the feature does.

Week two: practise your story and scenarios.
Pick three or four experiences from your own background that map to these competencies: customer empathy, technical explanation, sales collaboration, and handling setbacks. Write a STAR outline for each. Practise saying them aloud until they sound natural rather than memorised.

Demo preparation. If you can access a public demo video of any fleet management or IoT platform, practise narrating it as if you are presenting to a logistics operations manager. Focus on leading with business outcomes rather than feature names.

Day before the interview. Review Samsara's recent news, any publicly reported product launches or partnerships, and the job description one more time. Prepare two or three questions for your interviewers that show genuine curiosity about how the SE team operates internally.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Leading with features, not outcomes. Candidates often describe what the product does rather than what the customer gets. Samsara interviewers want to hear 'your drivers get home safer' before they hear 'the AI dashcam flags harsh braking events.'

Skipping discovery in roleplay questions. When given a scenario, many candidates jump straight to a solution. Taking a moment to ask one or two clarifying questions almost always scores higher than an immediate pitch.

Being vague about your own contribution. In collaboration stories, be specific about what you personally did. 'We closed the deal' is weak. 'I built the integration FAQ that addressed the IT team's main concerns' is strong.

Ignoring the vertical. Samsara sells to very different industries. An answer that works for a trucking company does not automatically fit a construction firm or a food delivery fleet. Show that you understand the difference between their pain points.

Underestimating the business acumen bar. SEs at Samsara are expected to understand pipeline stages, deal context, and commercial outcomes. If you have not engaged with CRM data or deal reviews before, brush up on basic sales process vocabulary before your interview.

How many rounds does the Samsara SE interview typically have?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screen, a hiring manager round, a technical or demo-focused round, and a final panel or stakeholder round. Some candidates also mention a written discovery exercise or a take-home scenario. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level, so confirm the format with your recruiter after the first call.

Do I need to know Samsara's product before the interview?

You do not need to know every feature, but you should understand what Samsara does and who it sells to. Spend time on their public website and read a few customer case studies. Interviewers typically assess your ability to learn and communicate, not your ability to memorise a product catalogue before day one.

Is there a live demo or roleplay in the Samsara SE interview?

Candidates commonly report at least one roleplay or scenario exercise, which may involve running a mock discovery call or responding to a customer objection. Some candidates report a demo component using Samsara's platform or a generic tool. Practise narrating a product walkthrough out loud before your interview so you are comfortable thinking aloud in front of an interviewer.
