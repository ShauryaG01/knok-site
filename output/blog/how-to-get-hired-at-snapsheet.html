01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Snapsheet is a Chicago-based insurtech company that builds cloud-native claims management software for insurance carriers. Their platform covers the full claims lifecycle digitally, from first notice of loss to final settlement. They are well known in the US insurance tech space for virtual appraisal tools and digital payment solutions.

As of mid-2026, Snapsheet has 15 open roles, which signals active hiring across multiple functions. You will typically find openings in software engineering, product management, customer success, and operations. Most roles are designed for the US market. If you are based in India, verify remote eligibility and visa sponsorship status on each individual listing before investing time in the application process.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process varies by role and team, but candidates commonly report a four-to-five stage journey. Here is what to expect, based on publicly reported experiences.

Application review. The recruiting team screens resumes against the job description. Tailoring your resume to the role, especially using keywords from the listing, improves your chance of moving forward.

Recruiter phone screen. A recruiter reaches out to confirm your background, discuss salary expectations, and explain the role. This is commonly cited as a short call of around 20-30 minutes. Be ready to talk about why you want to work in insurance tech and what draws you to Snapsheet specifically.

Technical or skills assessment. For engineering roles, this is typically a coding challenge or a take-home assignment. For product or business roles, expect a case study or a short functional exercise. Publicly reported timelines suggest candidates get one to two weeks to complete take-home tasks.

Hiring manager interview. A deeper conversation with the person you would report to directly. Expect questions about your past work, how you handle ambiguity, and how you would approach problems specific to that team.

Panel or final round. You meet two to four people from the broader team, including cross-functional partners. Questions blend technical depth with signals around culture and collaboration.

After the final round, candidates publicly report hearing back within one to two weeks with a decision or an offer.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Snapsheet operates at the intersection of insurance and technology, so they tend to value a specific mix of skills and mindsets.

Technical depth with product thinking. Engineers are expected to write clean, maintainable code but also understand how their work connects to the end user. Familiarity with cloud infrastructure and API-driven architectures is frequently mentioned in their job listings.

Insurance domain knowledge (a plus, not a must). You do not need an insurance background to get hired, but candidates who can speak the language of claims, carriers, and adjusters tend to stand out. If you have worked on enterprise SaaS or B2B products in regulated industries, draw those parallels in your interviews.

Clear communication across teams. Snapsheet's platform is used by large insurance carriers, which means teams deal with complex stakeholder relationships. The ability to explain technical decisions to non-technical partners is valued across all roles.

Ownership mindset. Interview questions often probe for examples where you took initiative, fixed something without being asked, or kept a project moving when it stalled. Have specific stories ready before you walk in.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product first. Spend time understanding how Snapsheet's claims platform works. Read their blog, check their press releases, and watch any product demos available on their site. The more specific you can be about the problem they solve, the better your interviews will go.

Brush up on insurance basics. You do not need to be an underwriter, but knowing terms like 'first notice of loss,' 'subrogation,' and 'claims adjudication' will help you ask smart questions and signal genuine interest in the domain.

Prepare your behavioral stories. Snapsheet interviews typically include competency-based questions. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and have three to four strong stories ready that show ownership, collaboration under pressure, and handling of ambiguity.

Practice system design if you are in engineering. Publicly reported feedback suggests design rounds focus on scalability and reliability. Practice designing systems that handle high-volume, event-driven workflows, as that maps closely to what a claims platform deals with.

Prepare smart questions. Ask about the team's biggest current technical challenge, how product priorities are set, or how success is measured for the role. These signal genuine curiosity and preparation rather than just going through the motions.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Snapsheet is on a mission to modernize the insurance claims experience, an industry that has historically been slow to change. That context shapes the culture in a few meaningful ways.

Pace and adaptability. As a growth-stage tech company serving large enterprise clients, teams often move quickly on product decisions while navigating the slower pace of the insurance world. Employees need to be comfortable working at both speeds depending on the context.

Collaboration across functions. Claims management involves legal, operations, customer success, and engineering working closely together. The culture values people who communicate clearly across team boundaries rather than staying strictly in their own lane.

Customer closeness. Snapsheet puts a strong emphasis on understanding the end user, whether that is the carrier, the adjuster, or the policyholder. Teams are encouraged to stay close to customer feedback and let it drive decisions rather than building in isolation.

Flexible work arrangements. Snapsheet has embraced remote work for many roles, primarily within the US. If you are outside the US, confirm the arrangement directly with the recruiter rather than assuming it applies to your situation.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Snapsheet hire candidates from India or offer visa sponsorship?

Most Snapsheet roles require US work authorization, and visa sponsorship is not always mentioned in the job listing. The best approach is to apply and ask the recruiter directly during the initial phone screen. Do not assume sponsorship is unavailable without asking, as policies often vary by role and by how urgently the team needs to fill the position.

How long does the full hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on publicly reported experiences, the end-to-end process typically runs anywhere from three to six weeks, depending on how quickly rounds are scheduled on both sides. The recruiter usually reaches out within one to two weeks of applying. After the final round, publicly reported timelines suggest one to two more weeks before you get a decision.

What kinds of roles are most commonly open at Snapsheet?

Software engineering roles appear most consistently, particularly backend and full-stack positions. Product management, customer success, and data roles also come up regularly. As of mid-2026, Snapsheet has 15 open positions across these functions, so there is a reasonable spread of opportunity beyond just engineering.

Is prior insurance industry experience required to get hired?

No, it is not required for most roles. Snapsheet hires engineers, designers, and product managers from general tech backgrounds without an insurance background. That said, candidates who show genuine curiosity about the domain and take time to learn the basics tend to perform better in interviews and receive stronger feedback from interviewers.

What is the interview style like at Snapsheet, formal or conversational?

Publicly reported experiences describe Snapsheet interviews as relatively conversational rather than highly formal. Technical rounds include real assessments and are taken seriously, but the overall tone is collaborative. Interviewers tend to ask about real situations you have actually handled rather than abstract puzzles or trick questions.

How should I follow up after submitting my application?

If you have not heard back within two weeks, a brief follow-up to the recruiter via LinkedIn or email is completely appropriate. Keep it short: confirm your interest, reference the specific role you applied for, and ask if there is any update on the timeline. Avoid following up more than once or twice, as repeated messages can work against you.
