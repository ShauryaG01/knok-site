01 Overview

Overview

Smartsheet is a cloud-based work management platform used by enterprise teams worldwide. With 117 open DevOps roles as of mid-2026, it is one of the more active hirers tracked by knok jobradar. Candidates report a process that typically spans a recruiter screen, a technical phone round, a take-home or live infrastructure exercise, and a panel discussion with the engineering team.

The DevOps role centers on AWS infrastructure, CI/CD pipelines, Kubernetes-based deployments, and site reliability practices. Interviewers typically focus on real work you have owned rather than hypothetical scenarios, so concrete examples from your own experience carry the most weight.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

The following questions are commonly reported by candidates who have interviewed for DevOps roles at Smartsheet. They reflect the company's emphasis on AWS depth, automation, reliability thinking, and cross-team collaboration.

  1. Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you designed end to end. What were your key decisions and why?
  2. How have you used Terraform or another infrastructure-as-code tool to manage cloud resources at scale? What challenges did you run into?
  3. Describe a production incident you were part of. How did you detect it, respond, and prevent it from recurring?
  4. How do you manage secrets and credentials securely across environments? What tools have you relied on?
  5. Tell us about your experience with Kubernetes. How did you handle cluster upgrades or capacity changes without downtime?
  6. Smartsheet runs primarily on AWS. How have you optimized cloud costs without compromising reliability?
  7. How do you work with developer teams to shift security into the delivery pipeline earlier in the process?
  8. Describe a time you reduced deployment time or increased deployment frequency. What changed between before and after?
  9. How do you define and monitor SLIs and SLOs for a critical service? Walk us through a real example from your own work.
  10. Tell us about an automation you built that replaced a manual, repetitive operational task. What was the impact on your team?
  11. How do you handle database migrations safely in a continuous delivery environment?
  12. How do you make sure new services ship with proper observability (logs, metrics, traces) built in from the start?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a CI/CD pipeline you designed end to end.

*Situation:* My previous team deployed a monolith to production using a manual, step-by-step process that took several hours and regularly caused issues over weekends.

*Task:* I was asked to modernise the pipeline and bring deployment time down significantly, with automated rollback built in so failures would not reach production.

*Action:* I designed a pipeline in GitLab CI with separate stages for linting, unit tests, integration tests, Docker image build, and Kubernetes deployment via Helm. I added automated smoke tests post-deploy and a rollback step that triggered on failure. I worked with developers to set up branch protection rules and mandatory peer review gates before any merge to main.

*Result:* Deployment time dropped from a multi-hour manual process to under 30 minutes, as confirmed by internal team metrics. Production incidents from bad deployments reduced noticeably over the following quarter, and the team was able to ship multiple times a day with confidence.

---

Q: Describe a production incident you were part of.

*Situation:* A memory leak in a Node.js service caused our API gateway to return errors during peak hours on a weekday morning. The team noticed customer-facing failures before any alert fired.

*Task:* I was on-call and responsible for identifying the root cause, restoring service, and writing the post-mortem.

*Action:* I checked dashboards in Datadog and spotted memory climbing steadily on a specific pod group. I restarted the affected deployment to restore service quickly. I then reviewed recent commits and found a new middleware that was not releasing event listeners. I raised a patch PR the same day and also added a high-memory alert so the team would catch similar issues before they caused customer impact.

*Result:* Service was restored within minutes of detection. The patch shipped the same day. The new alert has since fired twice, both times before any customer-visible impact occurred.

---

Q: How have you used Terraform to manage cloud resources at scale?

*Situation:* Our team had AWS infrastructure spread across 3 accounts, all managed manually through the console. Drift between environments caused constant 'it works on staging' problems that slowed releases.

*Task:* I was responsible for migrating all existing infrastructure to Terraform and enforcing infrastructure-as-code-only changes going forward.

*Action:* I used 'terraform import' to bring existing resources under state management and split the config into reusable modules covering networking, compute, and databases. I set up a Terraform Cloud workspace with per-environment variable sets and added a plan-on-PR check so engineers could review infrastructure changes before merge. I also added a policy to block direct console changes to tagged production resources.

*Result:* Within 2 months all 3 accounts were fully managed via Terraform. Environment drift dropped to near zero, and the team stopped seeing deployment failures caused by config differences between staging and production.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the primary framework Smartsheet interviewers use for behavioral questions. Structure every story with a clear setup, your specific responsibility, the concrete steps you took, and a measurable or observable outcome. Avoid vague results like 'it improved performance.' Connect your results to team impact wherever possible, even if the numbers are rough.

Clarify, design, trade-off works well for technical design questions. Start by asking one clarifying question about scope or scale, then walk through your design, then proactively name at least one option you considered but did not choose and briefly say why. Candidates report this signals senior-level thinking and is a consistent differentiator in Smartsheet technical rounds.

Detect, isolate, resolve, prevent is a strong structure for incident and debugging questions. Start with how you noticed the problem, narrow to the root cause, explain the fix, and close with what changed to prevent recurrence. This flow resonates well with Smartsheet interviewers, who value operational maturity and forward-looking reliability thinking.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Smartsheet DevOps interviews typically assess four areas.

Ownership mindset. Interviewers probe whether you drove outcomes or simply executed tickets. Use 'I' when describing your specific contribution, then separately explain how you collaborated with others. Candidates report that answers framed entirely as team efforts, without a clear individual role, tend to score lower.

AWS depth. The company's product runs primarily on AWS, and interviewers typically go beyond naming services. Be ready to explain why you chose one approach over another, with real reasoning from your own experience rather than textbook answers.

Reliability thinking. Questions about SLOs, alerting, and post-mortems appear consistently across rounds. Interviewers want to see that you think about failure modes before incidents happen, not only in response to them.

Collaborative communication. DevOps at Smartsheet involves working closely with product engineers, security teams, and sometimes customer-facing teams. Interviewers pay close attention to whether you can explain technical decisions clearly and handle cross-team disagreements constructively.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Refresh core technical areas. Review Terraform state management and module design, Kubernetes rolling updates and pod disruption budgets, and AWS services commonly used in SaaS platforms such as EKS, RDS, CloudWatch, IAM, and VPC networking. Practice explaining these topics out loud, not just reading about them.

Week 2: Build your story bank. Write at least 6 STAR stories from your own work covering a pipeline you built, an incident you resolved, an automation project, a reliability improvement, a cross-team collaboration, and a cost-saving initiative. Practice each story until you can deliver it clearly in under 3 minutes.

Week 3: Smartsheet-specific research. Search for Smartsheet engineering blog posts or conference talks (by name, not URL) to understand how they approach infrastructure and reliability. Review their open roles page to see which skills they currently emphasise. Candidates report that referencing specific product features or integration patterns makes a positive impression with interviewers.

Day before: Record yourself answering 3 practice questions and watch it back. Focus on cutting filler words and staying on point. Prepare 2-3 thoughtful questions for your interviewer about team practices, on-call culture, and how success is measured in the first few months.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being vague about impact. Saying 'I improved the deployment process' without any supporting detail is the most common gap candidates report. Even a rough before-and-after description of the workflow, without exact figures, is far more convincing than a general claim.

Treating DevOps as purely tooling. Listing tools (Jenkins, Terraform, Docker) without connecting them to business or reliability outcomes often leads to early filtering. Interviewers want to hear the reasoning behind your choices, not just the names.

Skipping the trade-off. Candidates who jump straight to a solution without acknowledging alternatives signal junior-level thinking. Always mention one option you considered but did not choose, and briefly explain the reasoning.

Ignoring the collaboration angle. Smartsheet DevOps teams work across product, security, and customer-facing engineering. Candidates who focus only on infrastructure and skip cross-team dynamics miss a key part of the evaluation.

Memorising scripts. Practiced stories are valuable. Memorised scripts sound robotic and fall apart when an interviewer asks a follow-up. Know your stories well enough to enter from any point in the narrative and adapt as the conversation moves.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Smartsheet typically have for a DevOps role?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter call, a technical phone screen, one or two technical rounds (live or take-home), and a final panel interview. The exact number can vary by team and level. Treat each stage as a chance to demonstrate both technical depth and clear communication, since Smartsheet interviewers evaluate both throughout the process.

What AWS services does Smartsheet focus on in DevOps interviews?

Candidates most commonly report questions around EKS, IAM, RDS, CloudWatch, S3, and VPC networking. Interviewers typically go deeper than basic service names and ask about real decisions you made and why. Experience with cost visibility and optimization tools is also frequently mentioned in candidate feedback.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps role at Smartsheet in India?

Based on knok jobradar data (July 2026), DevOps Engineers in India see typical ranges of 6-12 LPA at entry level, 15-28 LPA at mid level, and 30-50 LPA at senior level, with Lead and Staff roles often above 45 LPA. Individual offers depend on your specific experience, skills, and negotiation. For Smartsheet-specific compensation data, community discussions on Glassdoor and levels.fyi tend to have more granular detail.

Does Smartsheet ask coding questions in DevOps interviews?

Candidates report that Smartsheet DevOps interviews focus more on scripting (Python, Bash, or Go) and infrastructure tasks than on algorithmic coding. You may be asked to write a script to automate a deployment step or parse log output. Brushing up on clean, readable scripting is more useful than grinding algorithmic problem sets.

How should I prepare for a Smartsheet take-home infrastructure task?

Candidates report that take-home tasks typically involve writing Terraform configurations or creating a Dockerfile with a CI setup. Focus on readable, well-structured code and include a short README explaining your decisions and any trade-offs you made. Interviewers typically value clear reasoning as much as a working solution.

Is Smartsheet open to remote DevOps candidates in India?

Smartsheet's remote posture varies by team and seniority level. Candidates report that some roles are fully remote while others prefer candidates near a hub city. Check the specific job listing for location details and ask the recruiter directly in your first call so you have clarity before investing significant time in the process.
