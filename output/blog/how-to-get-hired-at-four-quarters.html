01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Four Quarters is a fintech company focused on employee financial wellness, offering salary-on-demand and financial planning tools that help working professionals manage their cash flow between paydays. As of mid-2026, the company has around 60 open roles across product, technology, sales, and operations, which points to an active growth phase rather than a handful of isolated openings.

For Indian job seekers, Four Quarters sits at an interesting crossroads: the pace and culture of a startup, but the domain (employee benefits, HR tech, and consumer finance) means you will work alongside large enterprise clients while building tools for everyday workers. Roles span early-career to senior levels, and hiring appears to cover both metro locations and remote or hybrid arrangements.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The stages below are a general map based on what candidates commonly report. The exact sequence can differ by role and team, so treat this as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Resume screening. The talent team reviews your application against the job description. For tech roles, specific skills and past project outcomes carry more weight. For sales or operations roles, domain experience and measurable results from previous jobs matter most. Mirror the keywords in the job posting so your resume clears the initial filter.

Recruiter or HR call. This is a brief introductory conversation where the recruiter checks your background, current role, notice period, and salary expectations. Come ready to state these clearly. The recruiter may also give you a quick overview of the role and the team you would be joining.

Technical or functional round. This is typically the most demanding stage. For engineering candidates, expect coding problems, system design questions, or a take-home assignment. For product, business, or operations roles, this may be a case study, a role-play, or scenario-based questions tied to the company's domain. Brushing up on fintech and HR tech concepts helps if the role touches those areas.

Managerial round. A discussion with the hiring manager or a senior team member. This round digs into how you have handled real situations at work, how you think through problems, and how you collaborate across teams. Behavioural questions are common here, so prepare specific examples from past jobs.

Final HR discussion. Once the functional rounds clear, HR typically reaches out to align on compensation, joining timelines, and any remaining details before the offer letter is issued. Be ready to negotiate clearly and professionally at this stage.

With around 60 open roles as of mid-2026, the hiring team is running multiple processes at once. Response times between stages can vary, so a polite follow-up to the recruiter is perfectly reasonable if you have not heard back in about a week.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Four Quarters operates where product thinking, sales ability, and operational discipline all need to work together. Across roles, a few qualities tend to matter consistently.

Ownership mindset. Startups at this stage need people who will pick up a problem and see it through without a detailed playbook. If you have examples of initiatives you led or situations where you stepped up beyond your official scope, highlight them.

Domain curiosity. The company works in employee financial wellness and HR tech. You do not need to be a finance expert for every role, but showing genuine interest in the problem space (why employees run short before payday, how salary-on-demand works in practice, what corporates care about when choosing benefits vendors) will set you apart from candidates who treat this as just another job.

Clear communication. Whether you are in tech, sales, or operations, the ability to explain ideas simply is valued. The company sells to enterprise HR teams while also serving everyday employees, so speaking to both audiences is a real skill here.

Comfort with ambiguity. Early and mid-stage startups shift priorities quickly. Candidates who thrive here usually have experience navigating changing requirements and building processes where none existed before.

Role-specific skills. Engineers will be expected to write clean, working code and think about scale. Sales candidates should show they can build and close a pipeline. Operations candidates need to demonstrate process thinking and attention to detail.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product first. Before any interview, spend time understanding what Four Quarters actually offers. Read their website, look for founder interviews or LinkedIn posts, and try to understand the core value proposition. Interviewers notice when a candidate has genuinely done this versus someone who only skimmed the job description.

Prepare your stories. For behavioural rounds, have a handful of strong examples from your past ready to describe. Each should cover what the situation was, what you specifically did, and what the result was. Avoid vague answers like 'we worked together as a team' and make your individual contribution clear.

Start technical prep early. Do not leave coding or case prep for the night before. For engineers, practice problems focused on data structures and algorithms, and revisit system design if you are going for a senior role. For business roles, practice walking through a business problem out loud so your thinking is visible to the interviewer.

Prepare questions that show depth. Good questions to ask include: what does success look like in the first few months in this role, how does the team measure impact, what is the biggest challenge the team is working through right now, and how has the product evolved over the past year. These signal that you are thinking about actually doing the job, not just landing the offer.

Sort out logistics in advance. Know your notice period, your current CTC, and your expected CTC before the recruiter call. Having vague answers here can slow down the process or send the wrong signal.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Four Quarters operates as a growth-stage startup, which shapes the day-to-day experience significantly. People who tend to do well here are comfortable with a faster pace, willing to wear multiple hats, and find satisfaction in solving problems that have a direct impact on how Indian workers manage their financial lives.

Mission-driven work. The company's core focus is financial wellness for employees, especially helping workers who live paycheck to paycheck access their earned wages when they need them most. If you care about building something with a practical, positive impact on working-class and middle-class India, that shared purpose tends to show up in how teams operate.

Collaborative but independent. Teams are small and interdependent. You will likely work closely across product, tech, and business functions rather than staying in a narrow lane. At the same time, there is an expectation that you can manage your own work without constant hand-holding.

Compressed learning curve. For candidates earlier in their careers, working at a company like Four Quarters can pack a lot of learning into a short period. The tradeoff is that support structures and documentation may be thinner than at larger companies, so self-direction matters.

Room to grow. With 60 open roles as of mid-2026, there are real opportunities to step into positions with meaningful scope, and potentially to grow within the organisation as it scales.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the Four Quarters hiring process usually take from application to offer?

Timelines vary by role and how many candidates are in the pipeline at the same time. Candidates commonly report a process that spans a few weeks from the first recruiter call to offer. Tech roles with multiple rounds may run a little longer. If you have not heard back after about a week between stages, a brief follow-up email to the recruiter is perfectly reasonable.

Does Four Quarters hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

With around 60 open roles as of mid-2026, there is a mix of seniority levels across the company. Some roles are explicitly for experienced professionals, while others are open to candidates with less experience who show strong fundamentals and the right attitude. Check the specific job description carefully, as requirements vary significantly across teams and functions.

What kind of coding questions can I expect for engineering roles?

Candidates for engineering roles commonly report questions on data structures, algorithms, and problem-solving, along with system design discussions for more senior positions. The exact difficulty and format can vary by team and interviewer. Practicing on standard coding platforms and reviewing concepts like databases, APIs, and service scalability will serve you well going in.

Is the Four Quarters interview process conducted online or in person?

Based on what candidates report, most early rounds (recruiter calls and technical screens) happen online. Final rounds may be in person depending on your location and the specific role. Confirm the format with the recruiter when you schedule each stage so you can prepare and plan accordingly.

How should I handle salary discussions with Four Quarters?

Be upfront and specific. Know your current CTC and have a clear expected CTC in mind before the recruiter call. Check publicly reported salary data on platforms like Glassdoor or levels.fyi so your expectation is grounded in the market for similar roles. Vague answers like 'I am open to anything' rarely work in your favour and can slow down the process unnecessarily.

What questions should I ask the interviewer at the end of my rounds?

Ask questions that show you are thinking about doing the job, not just getting the offer. Good examples include: what does success look like in the first few months, how does the team measure its impact, what is the biggest challenge the team is navigating right now, and how the product roadmap has shifted recently. Avoid saving all your questions for compensation and leave policy in early rounds, as that can signal the wrong priorities to the interviewer.
