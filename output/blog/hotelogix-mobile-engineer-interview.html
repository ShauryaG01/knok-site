01 Overview

Overview

Hotelogix is a Chandigarh-based SaaS company building cloud property management software for hotels worldwide. Their mobile app lets front-desk and housekeeping staff handle check-ins, room assignments, and guest requests on handheld devices during busy shifts. As a Mobile Engineer here, you would work on building and improving these hospitality-focused mobile experiences.

Hotelogix currently has 44 open roles, suggesting active hiring across teams. Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening, a technical round (coding exercise or take-home assignment), and one or two final conversations with engineering leads. The technical focus is on mobile fundamentals, real-world reliability, and understanding how your code affects hotel staff who depend on the app daily.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would build an offline-first feature for a hotel check-in screen.

*Situation:* At my previous job, hotel clients worked in areas with patchy Wi-Fi, and the check-in screen would freeze or throw errors when connectivity dropped even briefly.

*Task:* I needed to make the check-in flow work reliably even when the device had no internet for a few minutes at a time.

*Action:* I introduced a local SQLite database using WatermelonDB to cache reservation data synced nightly. On check-in, the app wrote the action to a local queue. A background sync service retried queued actions once connectivity returned, using exponential backoff. I also added a clear 'offline mode' banner so staff could see exactly what state the app was in, and I communicated the sync status to the product manager through a simple dashboard.

*Result:* Check-in failures on the client property dropped noticeably. Staff reported fewer complaints during peak morning hours when hotel Wi-Fi was strained, and the client renewed their contract citing improved reliability.

---

Q: How do you optimise a long flat list of reservations in React Native?

*Situation:* A reservations screen at a mid-size hotel showed hundreds of bookings and scroll performance was visibly janky, especially on budget Android phones used by front-desk staff.

*Task:* I was asked to fix the lag without removing any booking data from the list.

*Action:* I replaced the basic ScrollView with FlatList, added 'getItemLayout' to avoid dynamic measurement, set 'removeClippedSubviews' to true, and memoised each list item using React.memo. I also reduced image sizes loaded per row and moved heavy date-formatting logic out of the render cycle into a pre-computed lookup.

*Result:* Scroll performance improved significantly on a mid-range Android device, verified using Flipper's performance panel. Staff stopped raising complaints about the reservations screen, and the product team noted faster task completion during morning check-in rushes.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved app stability after crashes on older Android devices.

*Situation:* Our hospitality app was crashing on Android 8 devices used by housekeeping staff at a budget hotel chain. Staff were losing mid-shift data and restarting the app repeatedly.

*Task:* I owned the crash-reduction effort for that release cycle.

*Action:* I pulled crash logs from Firebase Crashlytics and identified that crashes were caused by a memory leak in a camera-based room-inspection feature. Camera resources were not being released in the onPause lifecycle method. I added proper cleanup code, tested on an emulator set to Android 8, and wrote automated tests for the camera component to catch any regression in future releases.

*Result:* Crash-free sessions for Android 8 improved meaningfully within two weeks of the fix going live, as shown in Crashlytics. The housekeeping team stopped losing inspection data mid-shift, and the client's operations manager escalated positive feedback to our customer success team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical design questions, use a 'context, constraints, choices' flow. Name the real constraint first (offline reliability, performance on older devices, security of guest data), list two or three options with trade-offs, then explain why you chose one for that specific situation. Avoid jumping straight to your favourite library without explaining why it fits the hotel-ops context.

For debugging questions, describe a systematic process: reproduce the issue, isolate the layer (UI, state, network, native), use the right tool (Flipper, Crashlytics, Chrome DevTools for React Native), fix, verify, and add a test so the issue does not return. Mention how you communicate progress to product managers or customer success teams, since hotel staff are the end users and downtime has direct business impact.

For behavioural questions, use the STAR structure: Situation (one sentence of context), Task (what you specifically owned), Action (what YOU did, not the whole team), Result (a concrete outcome tied to uptime, speed, or staff experience). Results framed around real operational impact land well with interviewers at a B2B hospitality company.

For product trade-off questions, show you understand B2B constraints. A hotel-staff app prioritises reliability and speed over visual novelty. Mention that you check with product or customer success before changing workflows that staff have already memorised, since disrupting familiar flows directly affects hotel operations and can generate support tickets.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hotelogix interviewers typically look for engineers who can build reliable, practical mobile experiences rather than flashy ones. Candidates report that interviewers respond well to people who explain their reasoning clearly, acknowledge trade-offs honestly, and have worked on apps used in real operational environments such as logistics, field service, or hospitality.

On the technical side, solid understanding of React Native internals (the JavaScript bridge or the newer JSI), native modules, and the Android or iOS activity or view lifecycle is important. Experience with offline sync, API authentication flows, and list performance comes up frequently in technical rounds. For senior roles, candidates report questions about CI/CD pipelines for mobile, App Store review cycles, and managing backward compatibility for older OS versions found on budget hotel hardware.

Soft-skill signals that interviewers watch for include clear communication (hotel staff are your end user, not engineers), an ownership mindset when things break in production, and comfort working with a product team that serves hotel clients across multiple countries and time zones.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Start with core mobile fundamentals. Revise React Native architecture (bridge vs. JSI), the component lifecycle, state management options (Redux, Zustand, Context API), and how to handle network errors and retries gracefully. Build a small offline-capable prototype if you have not done so recently, even a simple one, so you can speak from direct experience.

Then focus on Hotelogix-specific prep. Download the Hotelogix mobile app or watch their product demo videos to understand the UI patterns and workflows hotel staff use daily. Think through how you would improve one specific screen. Prepare a short, genuine answer to 'What interests you about building software for hotels?' because interviewers ask it to filter candidates who care about the domain.

In the final stretch, practise and polish. Do two or three mock interviews focused on mobile system design topics: offline sync, real-time updates, and push notifications. Review your past projects and pick two or three examples with concrete results you can describe in STAR answers. Prepare questions to ask the panel about their tech stack, release cadence, and how mobile engineers collaborate with the backend team on API design.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to a framework without reasoning. Saying 'I use Redux' without explaining why it fits the hotel-ops context is a red flag. Interviewers at product companies want to see the thinking behind your choices, not just library names.

Ignoring the end user. Front-desk and housekeeping staff are not tech-savvy users. Answers that skip over usability, error messaging, or what happens when the app behaves unexpectedly miss an important signal that interviewers are specifically watching for.

Claiming offline support without knowing the details. Many candidates say 'we used AsyncStorage' without explaining conflict resolution, sync strategy, or what happens when two devices modify the same booking simultaneously. Interviewers at Hotelogix will probe on exactly this.

Not asking about the stack. Failing to ask which version of React Native the team uses, whether they have native modules, or how they handle over-the-air updates makes you look unprepared for a role where these details affect your day-to-day work immediately.

Giving vague results in STAR answers. 'Performance improved' is forgettable. Specific outcomes tied to tools like Crashlytics, Flipper, or App Store review data are memorable. Always prepare at least one result you can describe concretely, even if you are paraphrasing from memory.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Hotelogix typically have for a Mobile Engineer?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screening call, a technical round involving a coding exercise or take-home assignment, and one or two final conversations with engineering leads or a hiring manager. The exact number varies by seniority and team. Ask your recruiter to confirm the structure upfront so you can prepare the right material for each stage.

Is the Hotelogix interview conducted in Hindi or English?

Candidates report that Hotelogix interviews are conducted in English, which makes sense given that their product serves international hotel clients. The atmosphere is typically conversational rather than formal. Clear, structured communication matters more than vocabulary or accent, so focus on organising your answers well.

Does Hotelogix expect React Native experience, or will a native Android or iOS background work?

Most candidates report that Hotelogix's mobile team primarily uses React Native. Having hands-on React Native experience is a strong advantage going in. If your background is native Android or iOS, be ready to explain concretely how your experience translates to cross-platform development and show genuine willingness to pick up React Native quickly.

What salary can I expect for a Mobile Engineer role at Hotelogix?

Hotelogix does not publish salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and AmbitionBox list ranges for Hotelogix engineering roles, but sample sizes are small so treat them as rough indicators rather than precise benchmarks. Research the role on both platforms, factor in your years of experience and the city, and use that as your starting point for negotiation.

How important is hotel or hospitality domain knowledge before the interview?

It is not required, but candidates who show genuine curiosity about the hotel industry make a stronger impression. Spend an hour understanding basic concepts such as PMS (property management system), housekeeping workflows, and front-desk check-in flows before your interview. It signals that you care about the people using your software, not just writing the code.

What questions should I ask the interviewer at the end?

Good questions include: 'Which version of React Native are you on, and do you use JSI or the classic bridge?', 'How do you handle app releases, and do you use any OTA update mechanism like CodePush?', and 'How does the mobile team collaborate with backend engineers on API contract design?' These show technical depth and genuine interest in how the team works day-to-day rather than just whether you got the offer.
