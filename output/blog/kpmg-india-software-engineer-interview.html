01 Overview

Overview

KPMG India is one of the Big Four professional services firms, with a rapidly growing technology and digital advisory practice. 02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on what candidates report from KPMG India Software Engineer interviews, these questions come up most often:

  1. Tell me about yourself and walk me through your most impactful project.
  2. How do you approach debugging a production issue when you have limited information?
  3. Explain the difference between REST and GraphQL. When would you pick one over the other?
  4. How have you maintained code quality in a fast-moving team?
  5. Describe a time you had to pick up a new technology quickly for a project deadline.
  6. How do you handle a technical disagreement with a colleague or senior?
  7. What is your experience with cloud platforms such as AWS, Azure, or GCP?
  8. Walk me through how you would design a system to support a large number of concurrent users.
  9. How do you prioritize tasks when multiple deadlines overlap?
  10. What do you know about KPMG's technology practice, and why do you want to join?
  11. Describe a time you spotted a security or compliance risk in your code or a teammate's code.
  12. How do you keep up with new frameworks, tools, and industry trends?

KPMG interviewers typically tie at least one or two questions back to the consulting context, so be ready to explain how your technical decisions would affect a client's business.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three STAR-format answers for the questions candidates report most often at KPMG India:

Q: Describe a time you debugged a critical production issue.

*Situation:* Our payment microservice started throwing intermittent errors on a Friday evening, just before a major client demo.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and needed to find the root cause and restore service quickly.

*Action:* I pulled the error logs, traced the spike to a third-party API that was timing out under load, added a circuit breaker with a sensible fallback, and pushed a hotfix through an expedited code review.

*Result:* Error rates dropped sharply after deployment. We also added a dedicated alert so the team could catch similar degradation early, before customers noticed.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly.

*Situation:* Midway through a project, the client asked us to migrate our data pipeline from a relational setup to a graph database the team had not used before.

*Task:* I was responsible for the migration plan and had only one week before the next sprint review.

*Action:* I spent the first two days on official documentation and a focused online course, built a small proof-of-concept query to validate my assumptions, then paired with a colleague who had prior graph-database experience to review the schema design.

*Result:* We delivered a working prototype by the sprint review. The client approved the approach, and the full migration completed on schedule in the following sprint.

---

Q: How do you handle a technical disagreement with a colleague?

*Situation:* A senior teammate and I disagreed on whether to use a message queue or direct API calls for a new notification feature.

*Task:* We needed a decision quickly because the feature was on the critical path.

*Action:* I proposed a short technical spike where each of us wrote a brief document outlining trade-offs for our preferred approach, then we presented both options to the team together. I focused on use-case fit and data rather than personal preference.

*Result:* The team chose the message queue approach after seeing the comparison. The senior colleague said the structured discussion felt collaborative rather than top-down, and we adopted the same format for future architectural decisions.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most of your answer on Action, and always close with a concrete Result, even if it is qualitative.

For system design questions, candidates report that KPMG interviewers respond well to a structured walk-through: clarify requirements first, then discuss trade-offs at each layer (storage, compute, APIs, failure modes) before committing to a design. Avoid jumping straight to a tech stack.

For debugging questions, use a 'narrow-the-search' approach: describe how you would isolate the layer (network, application, database), what signals you would look for, and how you would verify the fix. KPMG values engineers who think systematically, not just engineers who happen to know the right answer.

For 'why KPMG?' questions, tie your answer to the firm's consulting and enterprise context. Mention specific things you know about their digital transformation work or the industry verticals they serve. Generic answers about 'learning and growth' rarely stand out in Big Four interviews.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

KPMG India interviewers are typically looking for three things beyond raw technical ability.

Communication clarity. Because KPMG engineers often work alongside non-technical consultants and client stakeholders, interviewers pay close attention to whether you can explain technical decisions in plain terms. Practise answering questions out loud, not just in your head.

Problem-solving process. Interviewers want to see how you think, not just what you know. Articulate your reasoning as you go, flag assumptions explicitly, and show that you consider edge cases before finalising an approach.

Professional maturity. Big Four firms value engineers who handle ambiguity calmly, take ownership of mistakes, and collaborate across teams. Behavioural questions at KPMG are often deliberately open-ended to see whether you take initiative or wait to be told what to do.

Domain awareness. Knowledge of enterprise software, data privacy regulations such as India's DPDP Act, or cloud compliance frameworks is a meaningful differentiator for KPMG roles compared to product company interviews.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week plan based on what candidates report works for KPMG India Software Engineer interviews:

Week 1: Technical foundation
Review core data structures, algorithms, and system design concepts. Focus on practical applications: designing APIs, handling concurrency, and choosing between SQL and NoSQL. Practice at least one system design problem per day. If the role mentions a specific cloud platform, spend extra time on that.

Week 2: Behavioural and firm-specific prep
Write out four to six STAR stories from your own experience covering: a technical challenge, a disagreement, a time you learned something fast, and a mistake you handled well. Research KPMG India's technology and advisory practice areas. Prepare a crisp 'why KPMG' answer that references their consulting context, not just Big Four prestige.

Throughout both weeks, practise explaining your answers out loud to catch jargon or unclear reasoning. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about the team and projects. Review your own resume line by line, as every bullet point is fair game.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the clarifying questions. Candidates who jump straight into coding or designing without asking about scale, constraints, or use cases often solve the wrong problem. KPMG interviewers typically expect a short clarifying conversation before you start.

Talking only about what you built, not how you decided. KPMG values decision-making and trade-off thinking. For every project you mention, be ready to explain why you chose that approach over alternatives.

Generic 'why KPMG' answers. Saying you want to 'grow in a reputed firm' does not distinguish you. Connect your answer to what KPMG specifically does in technology, consulting, or the domain you are interviewing for.

Silence during technical rounds. If you are stuck, thinking out loud is far better than going quiet. Interviewers want to see your reasoning process, not just the final answer.

Underestimating behavioural rounds. Many candidates over-prepare for coding and underprepare STAR stories. At KPMG, the behavioural round can carry as much weight as the technical round, particularly for mid and senior roles.

Not asking questions at the end. Interviewers at consulting firms often interpret zero questions as low interest or low curiosity. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team, projects, or role scope.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the KPMG India Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds. This usually includes an HR screening call, one or two technical rounds covering coding or system design, and a final conversation with a manager or senior leader. The exact structure can vary by team and level, so confirm the format with the recruiter after you apply.

Does KPMG India ask DSA or competitive programming questions?

Candidates report that KPMG's technical rounds tend to focus more on applied problem-solving and system design than on competitive programming puzzles. You may encounter data structure and algorithm questions, but they are typically practical in nature. Strong system design thinking and clear communication tend to matter more here than at pure product companies.

What salary can I expect as a Software Engineer at KPMG India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Software Engineer salaries across India range from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 40-65+ LPA at lead or staff level. KPMG-specific figures are not publicly reported in a reliable sample, so check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for peer comparisons alongside these ranges.

Is there a coding assignment or take-home test before the interviews?

Some candidates report an online assessment or take-home assignment before the live interview rounds, while others move directly to a technical interview. This typically depends on the team and hiring volume at the time. Confirm with the recruiter after you receive the invite so you can prepare the right way.

How long does the KPMG India hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report the end-to-end process typically takes a few weeks, though it can vary with team availability, notice periods, and background verification timelines. Following up politely with the recruiter after each round is a reasonable way to stay informed without appearing pushy.

Does KPMG India offer remote or hybrid work for Software Engineers?

Work arrangements vary by team and project. Candidates report that KPMG India commonly offers hybrid options, but client-facing project roles may require more in-office or on-site time. Clarify the specific team's arrangement during the interview process rather than assuming a single blanket policy applies.
