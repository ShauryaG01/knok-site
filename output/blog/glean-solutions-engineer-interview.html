01 Overview

Overview

Glean builds an AI-powered enterprise search and knowledge assistant used by large companies to help employees find information across tools like Slack, Confluence, Salesforce, and Google Workspace. A Solutions Engineer at Glean sits at the intersection of sales and engineering: you run product demos, lead proof-of-concept (POC) projects, handle technical objections, and help customers get measurable value from the platform.

With 146 open roles at Glean as of mid-2026, the company is actively growing, and the SE position is among the most competitive because it demands both technical depth and clear communication. Candidates typically go through multiple rounds covering product knowledge, technical troubleshooting, customer scenario handling, and cultural fit. Preparing well across all four dimensions is what separates shortlisted candidates from those who stall after the first screen.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from what candidates typically report across Glean SE interview processes:

  1. Walk us through how you would run a discovery call with an enterprise IT leader who has never heard of Glean.
  2. A customer's employees are complaining that Glean search results feel irrelevant. How do you diagnose and fix the issue?
  3. Explain how Glean's permissions-aware indexing works to a non-technical stakeholder such as a VP of HR.
  4. How have you managed a proof-of-concept project that was at risk of going off-track?
  5. A prospect asks: 'How does Glean prevent sensitive documents from surfacing to the wrong people?' How do you answer?
  6. Describe a time you had to learn a completely new technical product quickly and then present it confidently to a customer.
  7. A customer wants to integrate Glean with a custom internal tool that has no out-of-the-box connector. What is your approach?
  8. How would you handle a situation where the account executive and the customer have misaligned expectations about what the POC will prove?
  9. A large enterprise prospect says a competitor's product is cheaper and does the same thing. What do you say?
  10. Describe how you have used usage data or metrics to help a customer see value from a product you supported.
  11. A critical connector breaks mid-demo in front of a senior customer team. What do you do?
  12. You are simultaneously supporting five active POCs. How do you decide where to spend your time?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How have you managed a proof-of-concept that was at risk of going off-track?

*Situation:* I was running a POC with a mid-sized manufacturing company. Midway through, the customer's champion kept adding new success criteria, creating scope creep that threatened the agreed timeline.

*Task:* I needed to protect the schedule and keep both the sales team and the customer aligned, while making the customer feel heard rather than dismissed.

*Action:* I called a formal alignment meeting with the customer's IT head and our account executive. I put the original success criteria in writing and shared them before the meeting. During the call, I proposed a 'Phase 2 list' for every new request, so nothing felt rejected outright. I set up a shared weekly tracker visible to both sides so progress stayed transparent throughout.

*Result:* The POC closed on schedule, the customer signed, and the Phase 2 list became the upsell roadmap going into the first contract renewal.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to learn a new technical product quickly and present it to a customer.

*Situation:* My company acquired a data observability tool and asked me to demo it to a key account within two weeks, with no prior exposure to the product at all.

*Task:* I needed to build enough technical depth to handle live questions confidently, not just deliver a scripted walkthrough.

*Action:* I spent the first week inside the product daily, intentionally broke connectors to understand failure modes, and scheduled a dry run with a senior SE to stress-test my explanations. I read all available documentation and joined the internal product team's Slack channel to surface edge cases before the customer could find them first.

*Result:* The demo went well. The customer raised several edge-case questions I had specifically prepared for. The account moved to a paid trial within a week of the session.

---

Q: Describe how you have used data or usage metrics to help a customer realize value.

*Situation:* A customer was three months into using our platform but had not expanded their license, and their champion was under internal pressure to justify the investment to leadership.

*Task:* I needed to build a data-backed business case before their upcoming quarterly business review.

*Action:* I pulled search query logs, identified which teams were heavy users and which had near-zero adoption, and built a simple slide showing time saved on information retrieval compared to their pre-product baseline. I flagged low-adoption teams and proposed a targeted training session tailored to each group's workflow.

*Result:* The champion presented the data internally, secured budget approval, and the account expanded to a full company rollout at the next renewal.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions
Most Glean SE rounds include behavioral questions. STAR keeps your answer tight: Situation (brief context), Task (your responsibility), Action (the specific steps you took, spend most time here), Result (outcome). Keep Situation and Task short. Interviewers are listening hardest during Action, so do not rush past it.

PTSC for troubleshooting scenarios (Problem, Thinking, Solution, Check)
When given a technical scenario, structure your answer as: Problem (confirm you understand what is broken), Thinking (walk through your diagnostic approach out loud), Solution (what you would actually do), Check (how you verify the fix worked and communicate back to the customer). This format signals structured thinking even when you do not know the exact answer immediately.

The Bridge for competitive objections
Acknowledge the competitor's strength briefly, then pivot to Glean's differentiation. For example: 'That product is strong on X, and where Glean stands out is its permissions-aware search across connectors, which is typically the deciding factor for security-conscious enterprises.' Never dismiss a competitor outright. Interviewers notice when a candidate sounds dismissive rather than confident.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without engineering arrogance. You need to speak comfortably about APIs, connectors, SSO, and data pipelines. But you must also simplify those concepts for a business leader who does not care about architecture. Candidates who go too deep too fast, or who cannot adjust their explanation level for the audience, often lose points on this dimension.

Customer empathy. Glean's product is used by employees across an entire company, not just IT administrators. Interviewers want to see that you understand the end user's frustration (irrelevant results, missing context) as much as you understand the IT admin's technical concerns.

Ownership and follow-through. SE roles sit between sales and engineering, and issues fall through the cracks easily in that gap. Expect behavioral questions that probe whether you chase problems to closure or hand them off and move on.

Genuine curiosity about AI and enterprise search. Glean operates in a fast-moving space. Candidates who have spent real time in the product, have a perspective on AI-powered search, and can articulate why this problem matters to large organizations stand out significantly from those who give a generic 'I love AI' answer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product and domain depth
Explore Glean's product through publicly available demo videos and any trial access you can get. Understand the core experience: how employees search across connected tools, how permissions are respected at query time, and how results are ranked. Read about enterprise search concepts, knowledge graphs, and SSO so you can speak about them without hesitation in a live interview.

Week 2: Interview-specific prep
Write out STAR answers for at least six behavioral scenarios: managing a difficult customer, handling a technical failure during a demo, learning a new product quickly, resolving conflict between sales and engineering teams, driving adoption in a reluctant account, and handling a competitor objection. Practice explaining Glean's value proposition in under two minutes, in language a non-technical HR director would understand immediately.

Week 3: Mock interviews and competitive context
Do at least two mock interviews with someone who can give hard feedback on filler words and over-explanation. Research Glean's main competitors in the enterprise search and AI assistant space and prepare to articulate clearly why a prospect should choose Glean. Review Glean's publicly reported funding milestones and recent product announcements so you can speak to the company's direction with confidence.

On the day: Bring two or three specific questions for your interviewers: how the team structures POCs, how SE success is measured in the first year, and what the biggest challenges are for new hires. Strong questions signal that you have done your homework and are evaluating the role seriously.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Treating it like a pure sales interview.
Glean SE roles are technical. Candidates who only talk about closing deals and skip product depth typically do not advance past the technical round. Be ready to walk through a troubleshooting flow or explain an API integration concept in plain terms.

2. Giving vague STAR answers.
'I led a project to successful delivery' is forgettable. Ground every answer in the specific actions you took, the real friction you encountered, and a concrete outcome. Vague stories read as low ownership to experienced interviewers.

3. Not exploring the product before the interview.
Candidates report that interviewers notice quickly when someone has not actually used or explored Glean. Spend time in the product before your first round, even if only through publicly available demo videos.

4. Pitching before listening.
Solutions Engineers are expected to ask questions and fully understand the customer's problem before proposing anything. In role-play scenarios, jumping straight to a feature demo before diagnosing the need is one of the most commonly cited reasons candidates do not progress to the next round.

5. A weak answer to 'Why Glean?'
Every SE interview will touch on motivation. 'I like AI' is not sufficient. Have a specific, honest answer about why enterprise search matters and why Glean's particular approach to solving it interests you.

6. Asking no questions at the end.
Candidates who ask nothing, or ask generic questions like 'What does the culture look like?', signal low engagement. Prepare sharp, specific questions about the team's current challenges, the POC process, and what success looks like in the first six months.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Glean typically have for SE roles?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a technical round covering product knowledge and troubleshooting scenarios, a customer role-play round, and a final panel or leadership conversation. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and location, so confirm the format with your recruiter after the first screen. Planning your preparation around each stage separately makes the process less overwhelming.

Does Glean ask coding questions in SE interviews?

Candidates report that SE interviews at Glean are not typically coding-heavy the way a software engineering role would be. You may be asked to read or interpret a simple API call, discuss an integration approach, or describe a technical architecture. Focus your prep on REST APIs, JSON structures, SSO protocols, and connector concepts rather than algorithm-style coding problems.

What salary can I expect for a Glean SE role in India?

Glean does not publicly publish detailed India-specific SE compensation bands. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list commonly cited figures for SE roles at enterprise SaaS companies in Bangalore, but ranges vary significantly by experience level and negotiation. Research those platforms for current benchmarks before your offer conversation, and be ready to state your expected range clearly during the HR screen.

How should I prepare for the customer role-play round?

Practice running a short discovery call where you ask questions before pitching anything. In the role-play, interviewers typically play the role of a customer and will introduce objections or shifting requirements mid-way through. Listen first, clarify the problem fully, then position the product. The most commonly cited mistake is rushing to show features before understanding what the customer actually needs, so train yourself to slow down in that moment.

Is Glean actively hiring Solutions Engineers in India right now?

Yes. As of mid-2026, Glean has 146 open roles globally, with Solutions Engineer positions listed across Indian metros. Based on knok jobradar data, Bangalore has the highest concentration of SE openings among Indian cities, followed by Mumbai and Delhi. Check Glean's careers page directly for the most current postings since the mix of open roles changes frequently.

How long does the Glean hiring process typically take from first screen to offer?

Candidates report the process can range from a few weeks to over a month, depending on team availability and how quickly rounds get scheduled. After each round, follow up with your recruiter to ask about next steps and expected timelines. Staying proactive and responsive on your end is one of the few variables you can actually control during the wait.
