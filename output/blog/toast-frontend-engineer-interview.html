01 Overview

Overview

Toast builds restaurant management software, from point-of-sale terminals to online ordering and kitchen display systems. Frontend engineers at Toast write code that restaurant staff tap under pressure during a busy dinner rush, so the bar for reliability, speed, and usability is very real. The knok jobradar tracked 375 open roles at Toast as of July 2026, signalling active hiring across experience levels.

The interview process typically spans a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering live coding or a short take-home, a system design round for mid and senior candidates, and a behavioural panel. Candidates report the process taking a few weeks end to end. Toast teams tend to care deeply about React, TypeScript, accessibility, and performance on the low-power handheld devices used in real kitchens.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would architect a React component that renders live order updates without causing unnecessary re-renders.

*Situation:* At my previous company we had a live order board that updated every few seconds via WebSocket. It had many order cards and was noticeably laggy on the older tablets used on the restaurant floor.

*Task:* I needed to reduce re-renders without breaking the real-time feel of the board.

*Action:* I split the board into a container that held WebSocket state and individual OrderCard children wrapped in React.memo. I moved the WebSocket subscription into a custom hook that published updates through a Zustand store slice keyed by order ID, so only the card for the changed order would re-render. I also used useCallback on event handlers passed as props to prevent reference churn.

*Result:* The React DevTools Profiler confirmed significantly fewer re-renders on a typical busy board, and staff reported the board felt 'snappy' again on the restaurant's older Android tablets.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you had to push back on a design spec because it was not technically feasible within the sprint.

*Situation:* A designer shared a spec for a multi-step checkout flow that included a full-page animation sequence between each step. The sprint was two weeks and we already had three other stories in scope.

*Task:* I had to communicate the constraint clearly without killing the designer's vision entirely.

*Action:* I set up a short sync and walked through a rough estimate comparing the full animation approach against a simpler CSS transition that would cover most of the visual polish. I proposed shipping the lighter version now and flagging the full animation as a follow-up ticket. I also put together a quick prototype in CodeSandbox so the designer could see exactly what we would ship.

*Result:* The designer agreed to the phased approach. We shipped on time, the lighter transition tested well with users, and the full animation story was prioritised in the next planning cycle.

---

Q: How do you write frontend tests for a component that depends on real-time WebSocket data?

*Situation:* We had a kitchen display screen component that consumed a WebSocket connection and I needed to write tests without spinning up a real server.

*Task:* Create a test suite that was fast, deterministic, and covered the main state transitions.

*Action:* I used Jest's manual mocking to replace the WebSocket constructor with a controllable fake. In each test I would instantiate the component, then programmatically emit mock message events from the fake socket to drive state transitions. For integration tests I used MSW (Mock Service Worker) to intercept the WebSocket and replay recorded message sequences. I also wrote snapshot tests for the main display states: idle, loading, active order, and error.

*Result:* The test suite ran quickly in CI, caught two edge-case bugs around message ordering before they reached staging, and the pattern was adopted by other teams in the org.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions, start by restating the constraint that makes Toast's context unique: the frontend runs on tablets and handhelds in a kitchen, not a desktop browser. That framing alone shows the interviewer you have done your homework.

For performance questions, lead with measurement before solution. Name the tool (Lighthouse, React DevTools Profiler, Chrome Performance tab) and the metric (LCP, INP, re-render count) before describing the fix. Interviewers want to hear that you do not optimise blindly.

For behavioural questions, use STAR but keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action, calling out specific decisions and the tradeoffs you considered rather than just what you did. Toast behavioural rounds typically probe for collaboration, disagreement handling, and shipping under pressure.

For coding rounds, think out loud from the first moment. Candidates report that Toast interviewers want to see your reasoning process, not just a correct answer. If you spot a trade-off (say, normalising state versus keeping it flat), name it even if you do not resolve it immediately.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Ownership over code quality. Toast engineers work on software that restaurant businesses depend on every day. Interviewers look for candidates who have genuine opinions on testing, code review, and reliability, not ones who say 'I just follow the team's standards.'

Empathy for the end user. Restaurant staff are not developers. Candidates who talk about usability, accessibility, and real-world usage conditions stand out clearly compared to those who only discuss technical elegance.

Comfort with ambiguity. Toast ships fast. Interviewers value candidates who can make a reasonable call with incomplete information and communicate their assumptions clearly rather than waiting for perfect specs.

React and TypeScript depth. The stack is primarily React with TypeScript. Candidates report that interviewers go beyond surface-level syntax into topics like reconciliation, concurrent features, and type narrowing in complex generics.

Cross-functional collaboration. Toast's frontend teams work closely with product managers, designers, and backend engineers. Expect questions about how you handle conflicting priorities and design feedback.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Ground yourself in Toast's product. Download or watch demos of Toast POS, Toast Go (the handheld device), and Toast Now (the manager app). Note the UI patterns: real-time order updates, offline banners, dense data tables. These will come up in system design rounds and show you have done your research.

Week 2: Sharpen React and TypeScript. Revisit React's rendering model (reconciliation, Fiber, concurrent mode), common performance patterns (memoisation, code splitting, virtualised lists), and TypeScript generics and utility types. Write at least one small side project that uses all three together.

Week 3: Practice system design for frontends. Pick one system design prompt (a live order board, a kitchen display system, a reporting dashboard) and talk through it out loud for an extended session. Focus on component architecture, state management, offline handling, and accessibility.

Week 4: Mock interviews and behavioural prep. Write out five STAR stories covering: a performance win, a conflict with a stakeholder, a bug you caught before production, a time you improved a process, and a feature you shipped under pressure. Practice each one out loud until it flows naturally.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the product context. Candidates who treat Toast like a generic SaaS company miss a big opportunity. The restaurant environment (tablets, ambient noise, offline risk, high staff turnover) is a real design constraint, and ignoring it signals you did not prepare.

Optimising before measuring. A very common mistake is jumping straight to 'I would use React.memo and useMemo everywhere.' Interviewers want to hear you profile first, then optimise the actual bottleneck.

Thin STAR answers. Saying 'I improved performance on a dashboard' is not enough. Interviewers want the specific decision you made, what you considered and rejected, and a concrete outcome with some evidence.

Ignoring accessibility. Toast's users include kitchen staff with varying literacy levels, working in poor lighting, often using gloves. Accessibility is not a nice-to-have at Toast. Leaving it out of your design answers is a red flag.

Not asking questions. Candidates report that Toast interviewers appreciate thoughtful questions about the team's current challenges, tech debt priorities, and how decisions get made day to day. Having no questions reads as low interest.

Assuming your previous stack maps perfectly. Toast uses specific libraries and internal tools that may differ from your last role. Ask about the stack early in the process rather than assuming everything carries over.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the Toast interview process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report a multi-week timeline from first recruiter call to offer. The pace can vary depending on team availability and how many rounds are needed for your level. If you have not heard back within a week of each round, a polite follow-up email to your recruiter is entirely reasonable.

Does Toast do take-home assignments or live coding?

Candidates report seeing both formats depending on the team. Some teams give a short take-home while others do a live coding session with a shared editor. Ask your recruiter which format to expect so you can prepare accordingly, as the two require different kinds of practice and mindset.

What salary can I expect as a Frontend Engineer at Toast in India?

Toast's India-based salary bands are not widely published. For the broader Frontend Engineer market in India, Glassdoor and industry surveys commonly cite ranges of 5-11 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 12-22 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 24-40 LPA at senior level, and 38-58+ LPA at lead or staff level. Negotiate based on your specific experience, the role's location, and any competing offers you hold.

Is Toast actively hiring Frontend Engineers right now?

Yes. The knok jobradar tracked 375 open roles at Toast as of July 2026, out of 405 Frontend Engineer openings across India in total. Bangalore had the highest concentration of frontend roles with 102 openings tracked across all companies, making it the strongest city for this role right now.

What tech stack does Toast use for frontend?

Candidates report React and TypeScript as the core stack, with GraphQL for data fetching on some teams. Toast also builds for Android-based handheld devices, so some teams work with React Native or web views optimised for Android tablets. Confirm the specific stack for your team during the recruiter screen, as it can vary across product areas.

How important is system design for a mid-level Frontend Engineer role at Toast?

Candidates at the mid level (3-5 years) typically report at least one system design question, though it is often scoped narrower than a senior-level round. Expect component architecture and state management questions rather than full distributed systems discussions. Framing your answers around Toast's real-world product constraints (offline handling, tablet hardware, real-time order data) makes a noticeably strong impression.
