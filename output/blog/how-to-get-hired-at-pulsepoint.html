01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Pulsepoint is a health data and programmatic advertising technology company that connects pharma brands with healthcare professionals and patients across digital channels. As of July 2026, Pulsepoint has 16 open roles spanning engineering, data science, product management, sales, and client success. The company sits at the crossroads of health data, machine learning, and digital advertising, so hiring managers tend to look for people who are comfortable moving between technical depth and business context.

Getting hired here is not just about coding or domain knowledge in isolation. Pulsepoint values candidates who understand how data flows through an ad tech or health tech pipeline and who can communicate findings clearly to both technical and non-technical stakeholders. If you come from a software, data, or analytics background, several of the 16 open roles are worth exploring. Sales and client-facing positions tend to favour people with prior experience in digital media or health marketing specifically.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Pulsepoint's hiring process typically runs across four to five stages, based on publicly available candidate feedback. Each stage can vary by role and seniority, so treat this as a general roadmap rather than a guarantee.

Application and Resume Review. Your resume goes through an initial screen by a recruiter or an automated system. Tailor it to the specific role, and make sure your experience with data, ad tech, or health-related platforms is visible in the first few lines.

Recruiter Call. If shortlisted, a recruiter will reach out for a short video or phone call. This is mostly about your background, your interest in Pulsepoint, and a quick check on salary expectations and availability. Be ready to explain why health data or ad tech appeals to you.

Technical or Skills Round. For engineering and data roles, this is usually a take-home exercise or a live coding session. For product or strategy roles, you may get a case study involving campaign analytics or product prioritisation. For sales roles, expect a mock pitch or a structured competency interview.

Hiring Manager Interview. This is a deeper conversation about your past work, how you handled specific challenges, and how you think about problems relevant to the role. Managers tend to ask behavioural questions alongside technical ones, so prepare concrete examples from your experience.

Final Panel or Loop. Senior or specialised roles often close with a panel interview involving multiple team members across functions. This round usually covers collaboration style, cross-functional scenarios, and sometimes a presentation of prior work.

With 16 open roles currently listed, there is a reasonable spread of seniority levels and functions to target. Response times between stages can vary, so following up politely with your recruiter after each stage is a good habit.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Pulsepoint's open roles tend to cluster around a few core skill sets.

Technical depth with business context. Engineering and data science candidates are expected to write clean, production-ready code and understand how their work connects to business outcomes. Knowing how to work with large datasets and build or maintain data pipelines is consistently valued across technical roles.

Familiarity with digital advertising or health data. You do not need to have worked in pharma, but understanding programmatic advertising, DSPs, DMPs, or health audience segmentation gives you a clear edge. Candidates who can reference real experience with ad tech platforms or health data standards tend to move faster through the process.

Communication and stakeholder management. Pulsepoint works with pharmaceutical clients and media agencies, so the ability to translate technical findings into plain business language matters. This applies even to engineering roles, where cross-functional communication is increasingly expected.

Attention to data governance and quality. Given that health data is involved, an awareness of privacy standards and data quality signals maturity to hiring teams. You do not need to be a compliance specialist, but showing you take data responsibility seriously goes a long way.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research Pulsepoint's product and positioning. Spend time understanding what Pulsepoint actually does: health data activation, programmatic advertising for pharma, and identity resolution for healthcare audiences. Read their blog and any publicly available case studies. Going into an interview with even a basic grasp of their core product puts you ahead of most candidates.

Sharpen your SQL and Python skills. For data and engineering roles, SQL is non-negotiable. Practice writing queries that involve joins, aggregations, window functions, and CTEs. If the role touches machine learning or data science, review model evaluation, feature engineering, and common statistical concepts.

Practise behavioural answers using real examples. Pulsepoint interviewers commonly ask about situations where you worked with incomplete data, managed a difficult stakeholder, or had to pivot mid-project. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and keep your examples concise.

Quantify your past work honestly. Statements like 'I improved pipeline performance significantly' are weaker than specific numbers from your own experience. Use real figures from roles you have held. Do not invent or inflate them.

Prepare thoughtful questions for each stage. Ask about team structure, how success is measured in the role, and what the biggest challenge the team is working through right now. Good questions signal genuine interest and help you assess fit from your side too.

For sales or client success roles, research who Pulsepoint's typical clients are (pharma brands, healthcare media agencies) and think about how you would approach a consultative sale in a regulated industry.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Pulsepoint describes itself as a data-first company, and that ethos appears to carry through into how teams operate day to day. Decisions tend to be grounded in measurable outcomes, and employees are generally expected to back up recommendations with data rather than intuition alone.

The company sits within the IPG (Interpublic Group) family, which gives it access to larger agency infrastructure while maintaining a focused product identity. This means the culture sits somewhere between a nimble tech company and a structured media organisation. There is process and hierarchy, but also room for individual contributors to shape how problems get solved.

Collaboration across functions is a recurring theme in publicly available employee reviews. Sales, product, and engineering teams often work in close contact on client solutions, which means strong communication habits matter as much as domain expertise. If you prefer siloed, heads-down work, the cross-functional nature of many roles here may feel like an adjustment.

Pulsepoint's focus on health advertising also gives the work a sense of purpose that candidates in the health tech space often find motivating. Helping the right health information reach the right patients or professionals, through data, is a pitch that resonates with many employees who choose to stay long-term.
