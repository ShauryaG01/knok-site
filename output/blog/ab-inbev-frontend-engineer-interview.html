02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have interviewed at AB InBev for Frontend Engineer roles commonly report questions in these areas:

  1. Walk me through a complex React component you built from scratch. What trade-offs did you make?
  2. How do you handle state management in a large application? When would you choose Redux over Context API?
  3. Describe a time you improved the performance of a slow-loading page. What did you measure and what did you change?
  4. AB InBev builds tools used across many countries. How have you handled internationalisation in a frontend project?
  5. How do you approach writing tests for React components? What is your strategy for deciding what to test?
  6. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product manager or designer on a UI decision. How did you resolve it?
  7. How do you ensure your UI works well for users with disabilities? Walk me through your approach to accessibility.

  11. How do you think about micro-frontends or module federation? Have you worked in a micro-frontend architecture?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you improved the performance of a slow-loading page.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a key sales analytics dashboard was taking several seconds to load, and field sales reps said it was unusable on mobile networks.

*Task:* I was asked to investigate and reduce load time without a full redesign.

*Action:* I used Chrome DevTools and Lighthouse to profile the page. I found the entire charting library was loading upfront even on pages that did not use charts. I introduced code splitting with React.lazy and Suspense, moved heavy chart components behind dynamic imports, and added skeleton loaders to improve perceived performance. I also found large images that were not compressed and switched them to WebP format.

*Result:* The initial load time dropped noticeably, and the sales team reported a much better experience on field devices. The fix shipped within the same sprint.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product manager on a UI decision.

*Situation:* A product manager wanted a floating pop-up to auto-appear every time a user opened the app, to promote a new feature.

*Task:* I felt this would hurt the user experience and wanted to push back in a constructive way.

*Action:* I pulled user feedback from our support tickets showing that pop-up fatigue was already a complaint. I proposed an alternative: a small persistent banner that users could dismiss, and offered to run a split test of both approaches. I put together a short doc with examples from other products and presented it to the PM.

*Result:* The PM agreed to the split test. The banner version showed higher engagement and was chosen for the final release. The PM appreciated the data-backed approach, and we built a stronger working relationship going forward.

---

Q: How have you handled internationalisation in a frontend project?

*Situation:* I worked on a B2B platform that needed to support English, Hindi, and Tamil for different regional teams.

*Task:* I was responsible for setting up the internationalisation architecture from scratch.

*Action:* I used a React internationalisation library to manage translation files, created a folder structure separating translations by language and namespace, and added a language-switcher in the app header. I also wrote a small lint rule to flag hardcoded strings in the codebase and coordinated with translators to review the initial Hindi and Tamil strings before launch.

*Result:* The platform launched with three languages on schedule. The lint rule caught several hardcoded strings during code reviews, which saved rework later. When additional languages were needed, the setup made each one a fraction of the initial effort.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Most behavioural questions at AB InBev fit the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep each spoken answer to roughly two minutes.

For technical questions, a useful structure candidates report is: state your default approach, explain the trade-offs, then describe when you would deviate. For example, if asked about state management, start with what you reach for by default, such as local state or Context API, then explain when you would add Redux or Zustand, and back it up with a real example from your experience.

For system design questions, structure your answer around four areas: requirements (what does this dashboard need to do?), component breakdown (how do you split the UI?), data flow (where does state live?), and performance considerations (what breaks at scale?). Start simple and build up. Do not jump to advanced patterns before covering the basics.

For 'tell me about a time' questions, pick examples where you can describe a clear impact on the business or the team, not just technical elegance. AB InBev interviewers value candidates who connect engineering decisions to outcomes that matter to the organisation. Phrases like 'the sales team could now do X' land better than 'my code was clean.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report and the nature of AB InBev's products, interviewers are typically looking for these qualities.

Strong React and JavaScript fundamentals. They want to see you can reason about rendering, hooks, and the event loop, not just produce working code. Expect follow-up questions that probe the 'why' behind your choices.

Product thinking. AB InBev builds internal tools used by sales and operations teams across multiple markets. Interviewers value candidates who ask 'who uses this and how?' before jumping to a technical solution.

Cross-functional communication. You will work with designers, data engineers, and product managers. Being able to explain a trade-off in plain language without jargon is something interviewers actively assess.

Ownership and initiative. Look for chances in your answers to show you identified a problem on your own and drove it to resolution, rather than waiting for a ticket to be assigned.

Comfort with ambiguity. Enterprise-scale products come with legacy code, unclear requirements, and shifting priorities. Candidates who show they can navigate this without freezing, and who ask the right clarifying questions, make a strong impression.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

First week: core skills audit

Revise React fundamentals: hooks, lifecycle, reconciliation, and performance patterns like memoisation and list virtualisation. Review JavaScript closures, the event loop, and async patterns. Pick two or three projects from your own experience that show business impact and practise telling them in STAR format.

Second week: AB InBev-specific prep

Look at AB InBev's public engineering content and LinkedIn to understand what kinds of products they build. Practise one or two dashboard or data-heavy UI problems. Revise accessibility basics such as ARIA roles and keyboard navigation, and review internationalisation patterns. These topics come up often for roles that serve global, multi-language markets.

Third week: mock interviews and polish

Do at least two timed mock interviews, ideally with a peer. Record yourself answering behavioural questions and check that your answers are concrete, not vague. Prepare a few questions to ask your interviewer about the team's tech stack, deployment process, and how they handle technical debt. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest and seniority.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Vague answers without outcomes. Saying 'I improved performance' is weaker than describing what changed and who noticed. Use concrete results even if you do not have exact metrics. A stakeholder reaction or a team outcome counts.

Skipping the 'why' on technical choices. Interviewers do not just want to hear what you built. They want to know why you chose React Query over a custom hook, or why you reached for Redux instead of Context API. Always explain the trade-off.

Treating accessibility and internationalisation as afterthoughts. AB InBev products serve global, diverse users. Candidates who wave these topics away miss a signal that interviewers actively look for in mid and senior roles.

Talking only about code. Frontend engineers at AB InBev work closely with product and design. Behavioural answers that never mention collaboration or communication leave an important gap in the interviewer's picture of you.

Not asking questions at the end. Silence at the 'any questions?' stage reads as low engagement. Prepare at least two thoughtful questions about the team's challenges, their deployment pipeline, or how they handle design system governance.

Overcomplicating system design. Start simple, validate your assumptions, then add complexity if the interviewer prompts you. Jumping straight to micro-frontends or edge caching before covering the basics can signal shaky foundations rather than seniority.

How many rounds does the AB InBev Frontend Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: an initial screening call with HR, one or two technical rounds covering React, JavaScript, and possibly a take-home or live coding task, and a final behavioural or culture round. The exact structure can vary by team, so it is worth asking your recruiter at the start of the process.

What salary can I expect as a Frontend Engineer at AB InBev in Bangalore?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level Frontend Engineers (3-5 years of experience) typically see ranges of 12-22 LPA, while senior profiles (6-9 years) see 24-40 LPA. AB InBev India roles tend to skew toward mid and senior profiles. For more current benchmarks specific to this company, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi, as compensation can shift quickly and sample sizes on public sites vary.

Does AB InBev ask DSA questions for Frontend Engineer roles?

Candidates report that AB InBev Frontend interviews focus more on practical JavaScript and React problems than on competitive programming or whiteboard algorithms. You may see a problem involving array manipulation or a simple data transformation, but deep DSA prep is typically less critical here than strong framework knowledge. Reviewing common JavaScript array and object methods is still a worthwhile use of your time.

Is there a take-home assignment in the AB InBev Frontend interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home task, often a small React project such as a dashboard component or a form with API integration. The task is typically scoped to a few hours. Focus on clean component structure, readable code, and at least some test coverage. Reviewers tend to pay attention to how you make decisions, not just whether the app runs correctly.

How important is TypeScript for AB InBev Frontend Engineer roles?

AB InBev's engineering teams are known to use TypeScript in their frontend codebases, and candidates report being asked about it in technical rounds. You should be comfortable with interfaces, generics, and type narrowing. If your current experience is mostly in plain JavaScript, practise converting a personal project to TypeScript before your interview to build confidence.
