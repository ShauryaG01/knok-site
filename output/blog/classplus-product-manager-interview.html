01 Overview

Overview

Classplus is an EdTech platform that helps coaching institutes and independent tutors go digital, managing content, students, payments, and marketing from a single app. As of mid-2026, Classplus has 13 open Product Manager roles, making it one of the more active EdTech hirers in the current market.

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to five rounds: an initial HR or recruiter call, one or two product thinking rounds, a case study or take-home assignment, and a final leadership or cultural fit conversation. Round names and order vary, so treat any description of the process as approximate.

PM roles at Classplus span a wide range. Publicly reported salary bands from sources like Glassdoor and industry surveys show Associate PMs earning 12-20 LPA, PMs with 3-6 years of experience earning 24-40 LPA, Senior PMs at 40-60 LPA, and Group or Principal PMs at 55-90+ LPA. Classplus-specific compensation may differ; verify with your recruiter.

Interviews lean heavily on EdTech domain knowledge, product sense for the creator and institute economy, and your ability to think about metrics that matter in a subscription and retention-driven business.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Product Sense and Strategy

  1. Classplus serves two sides: coaching institutes and their students. How would you decide which side to prioritise when their needs conflict?
  2. If you were to design a feature that helps a first-time solo tutor get their first ten paying students, what would you build and why?
  3. Classplus competes with players like Teachmint and Graphy. How would you think about differentiation, and which product bets would you place over the next 12-18 months?
  4. Walk us through how you would improve the content-delivery experience for a tier-2 city coaching institute with patchy internet.

Metrics and Analytics

  1. What is the single most important metric for Classplus's business health, and how would you track it?
  2. Monthly active institutes on the platform dropped this quarter. Walk us through how you would diagnose the problem.
  3. A new batch-management feature shipped two months ago. How do you decide if it was successful?

Execution and Cross-functional

  1. Describe a time you had to say no to a feature request from a major client. How did you handle it?
  2. You are building a roadmap for the next quarter with limited engineering bandwidth. How do you prioritise across three competing initiatives?
  3. How would you work with the sales team at Classplus to gather product feedback without letting the loudest customer dominate your roadmap?

EdTech Domain

  1. How does the unit economics of a coaching institute differ from a direct-to-consumer edtech app, and how does that change what you build?
  2. What trends in the creator or coaching economy do you think Classplus should be building for right now?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A new batch-management feature shipped two months ago. How do you decide if it was successful?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we launched a scheduling tool for tutors that promised to reduce no-shows and simplify attendance tracking.

*Task:* Two months post-launch, leadership asked me to present a success assessment before deciding whether to invest further or sunset the feature.

*Action:* I started by revisiting the goals we had set at launch: reduce tutor-reported admin time, improve session attendance rates, and increase weekly active usage of the core product. I pulled usage data segmented by institute size and tenure, ran a short survey with a sample of active users, and spoke directly with five institutes that had not adopted the feature. I also checked support ticket volume to see if the feature had created new problems.

*Result:* The data showed strong adoption among larger institutes but near-zero uptake from solo tutors, who found the setup flow complex. I recommended a simplified onboarding for solo tutors and got sign-off to build it. Adoption among that segment improved meaningfully in the following quarter.

---

Q: Monthly active institutes on the platform dropped this quarter. Walk us through how you would diagnose the problem.

*Situation:* This is a classic metric-drop question that Classplus interviewers typically use to test structured thinking.

*Task:* The goal is to show you can isolate root cause before jumping to solutions.

*Action:* I would start by checking if the drop is real or a measurement artifact (a tracking change or definition change). Then I would segment: is it a specific cohort (new versus tenured institutes), a specific geography, or a specific plan tier? I would look at the funnel: did fewer institutes activate, did previously active ones churn, or did active ones simply reduce usage below the activity threshold? I would cross-reference with any product changes, competitor moves, or external events (exam season, for example, naturally reduces tutor activity in certain months). Finally, I would talk directly to churned institutes.

*Result:* This structured approach, rather than guessing at solutions, is what interviewers typically want to see. It shows you treat ambiguity as a diagnosis problem, not a brainstorm prompt.

---

Q: Describe a time you said no to a feature request from a major client.

*Situation:* A large enterprise client at my previous company requested a deeply custom reporting module that would have consumed several weeks of engineering time.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to build it and how to communicate the decision without damaging the relationship.

*Action:* I audited our roadmap and found the request served only that one client and conflicted with a platform-wide analytics revamp already in progress. I set up a call with the client's account lead and their operations head. I showed them how the upcoming analytics revamp would cover most of their stated needs within two quarters, and offered a workaround using our existing export functionality in the interim. I also logged their specific requirements as direct inputs to the revamp.

*Result:* The client accepted the plan. The revamp shipped on schedule and covered their core needs. The relationship held and the account renewed.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for Behavioural Questions

Most behavioural questions on conflict, prioritisation, and stakeholder management respond well to Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined) so you spend most of your time on Action and Result. Classplus interviewers are reportedly focused on how you made the decision, not just what the outcome was.

A Simple Product Sense Structure

For 'design a feature' or 'improve this product' questions, candidates report that a clear structure lands better than a free-form brainstorm. A workable approach: clarify the goal and user, identify two or three user pain points, propose solutions ranked by impact and feasibility, define success metrics, and call out risks. Resist the urge to jump to solutions before you have defined the problem.

Metric-Drop Diagnosis

For any 'a metric went down' question, move in this order: confirm the data is accurate, segment to isolate where the drop lives, look at the funnel to find where users are falling off, cross-reference with internal changes and external events, then propose hypotheses and how you would test them. Only then suggest fixes.

Prioritisation Frameworks

For roadmap or prioritisation questions, pick a framework and justify it rather than just naming it. RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) works well for feature prioritisation. For strategic bets, an impact-versus-effort quadrant communicates trade-offs clearly. At Classplus, always factor in whether a feature serves the institute (your paying customer) or the student (the end user), since those can pull in different directions.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

EdTech and SMB Empathy

Classplus's customers are coaching institutes, many of them small businesses run by a single educator or a small family. Interviewers want to see that you understand the constraints of this customer: limited tech literacy, price sensitivity, high dependency on word-of-mouth, and a business that runs on exam cycles. Generic product thinking without this context will not land well.

Comfort with Two-Sided Platform Thinking

The platform connects institutes and students. Candidates who can articulate how a feature affects both sides, and who can navigate trade-offs between them, stand out. If you have worked on marketplace or platform products before, bring those examples forward.

Metric Fluency

Classplus is a SaaS business with subscription revenue. Interviewers reportedly care about whether you understand retention, activation, and expansion metrics in that context. Know the difference between activity metrics and business-outcome metrics, and be ready to discuss which matters more in a given situation.

Decisiveness Under Ambiguity

Candidates report that interviewers push back on answers to see if you fold or hold. They are not necessarily looking for the 'right' answer. They want to see that you can defend a position with logic while remaining open to new information.

Communication Clarity

PMs at Classplus work across engineering, design, sales, and support. Interviewers assess whether you can explain a complex trade-off simply. Avoid jargon. If you use a framework, explain why you chose it, not just what it is.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the Product

Sign up for a free Classplus trial or explore their public demo. Map the core user journeys: how does an institute onboard, create a course, manage batches, and collect fees? Read recent coverage of Classplus in business and EdTech outlets to understand their growth story, funding history, and stated strategic priorities. Look at their app-store reviews to surface real user pain points.

Week 2: Domain and Metrics

Study the EdTech SaaS model: how institutes acquire students, what drives retention, and what causes churn. Understand the difference between the institute's goals (revenue, student outcomes, admin efficiency) and the student's goals (learning, exam results, convenience). Build a mental model of the key metrics Classplus would track, such as monthly active institutes, net revenue retention, and feature adoption rates.

Week 3: Structured Practice

Practice answering the question types in this guide out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself and review for clarity and pacing. Do at least two timed mock interviews, one product sense and one behavioural, with a peer or a practice partner. Prepare three to five strong STAR stories from your work history that can flex to cover prioritisation, conflict, metrics, and stakeholder management questions.

Week 4: Final Polish

Prepare five to seven sharp questions to ask your interviewers. Good questions show you have done your research: ask about the PM team's current priorities, how product decisions are made when sales and product disagree, or what success looks like in the first six months. Review your resume so every claim on it is backed by a story you can tell in the room.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Leading with solutions before understanding the problem

The most commonly cited mistake in product interviews is jumping to a feature idea before clearly defining the user and the problem. Classplus interviewers are specifically looking for structured thinking. Slow down, ask a clarifying question, and state your assumptions before proposing anything.

Treating institutes and students as the same user

Classplus has two distinct user types with different goals and different willingness to pay. Conflating them in your answers signals that you have not done your homework on the product.

Naming frameworks without applying them

Saying 'I would use RICE' without actually running through the RICE logic does not demonstrate analytical skill. Either apply the framework to the specific question or explain why you chose a different approach.

Being vague about metrics

Saying 'I would look at engagement' is not enough. Name the specific metric, explain how it is measured, and explain why it is the right proxy for the business outcome you care about.

Not asking clarifying questions

Candidates sometimes accept a question at face value when it is intentionally ambiguous. Asking a clarifying question before answering is a positive signal, not a sign of weakness. Interviewers typically want to see how you handle incomplete information.

Underestimating the SMB and tier-2 context

Classplus's core market includes institutes in smaller cities with limited bandwidth, lower price points, and less tech-savvy operators. Solutions that assume high-end devices, fast internet, or sophisticated users will feel out of touch with the real customer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Classplus PM interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from three to five rounds, though this varies by level and team. A typical sequence includes an HR screening call, one or two product thinking discussions, a case study or take-home assignment, and a final round with senior leadership. Round count and format are not fixed, so confirm the structure with your recruiter after the first call.

Does Classplus give a take-home case study?

Many candidates report receiving a take-home case study, often focused on a real product problem related to the institute or student experience. You are typically given a few days to prepare a structured presentation. Focus on clear problem framing, a defensible recommendation, and specific success metrics rather than exhaustive feature lists.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Classplus?

Classplus-specific compensation is not publicly reported in detail, so verify with your recruiter. For context, Glassdoor and industry surveys show the broader PM market in India at 24-40 LPA for PMs with 3-6 years of experience and 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs. Your actual offer will depend on level, team, and negotiation.

Do I need an EdTech background to interview at Classplus?

No, but you do need to demonstrate that you understand Classplus's customers and business model. Candidates with SaaS, marketplace, or SMB product backgrounds frequently clear the process by showing strong customer empathy and a willingness to learn the EdTech context. Spend time using the product and reading about the coaching institute economy before your interviews.

How important is data analysis in the Classplus PM interview?

Classplus does not publicly specify a SQL requirement for PM roles, but candidates report that metric fluency is heavily tested. You should be comfortable defining metrics, diagnosing drops, and discussing how you would use data to make decisions. Basic SQL is a plus, especially if the role description mentions data-driven decision making.
