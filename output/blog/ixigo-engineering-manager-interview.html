01 Overview

Overview

ixigo is one of India's leading travel-tech platforms, built around train bookings, flight search, bus ticketing, and real-time travel intelligence powered by AI. Since its stock market listing, the company has been expanding its engineering teams, and Engineering Manager roles sit at the heart of that growth. An EM here typically owns a squad working on high-traffic, mobile-first products and is expected to balance fast delivery with strong technical standards.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report a mix of behavioural, technical leadership, and product-context questions. The themes that come up most at ixigo:

  1. Peak traffic and scale: 'ixigo sees huge spikes during Diwali, summer, and IPL season. Walk us through how you have handled a sudden traffic surge in a system you owned.'
  1. Third-party API reliability: 'Our train booking depends on IRCTC APIs that can go down. How do you manage reliability and user experience when external dependencies fail?'
  1. Delivery under pressure: 'Your team's velocity has dropped two sprints before a major travel-season launch. What do you do?'
  1. Cross-functional collaboration: 'ixigo uses ML heavily for price prediction and seat availability. How do you work with a data science team that does not report to you?'
  1. Low-bandwidth users: 'A large share of our users are on 2G or patchy networks in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. How do you ensure your team's backend and API decisions reflect that reality?'
  1. On-call and incident management: 'How would you design an on-call rotation and incident response playbook for a team owning a payment or booking flow?'
  1. Post-acquisition team integration: 'ixigo has grown through acquisitions. How would you bring an acquired engineering team with its own culture and tech stack into your existing squad?'
  1. Technical debt vs. roadmap: 'How do you balance paying down technical debt when your product team has an aggressive feature roadmap?'
  1. Difficult feedback: 'Tell us about a time you gave tough feedback to a strong senior engineer who was struggling with collaboration or ownership.'
  1. Engineering health metrics: 'Beyond sprint velocity, how do you track the health of your team and the quality of what it ships?'
  1. Mobile-first backend decisions: 'ixigo is mobile-first. How do you ensure backend and infrastructure choices account for mobile app performance on constrained devices?'
  1. Hiring for domain plus skill: 'How do you approach hiring for a role where you need both system design expertise and domain knowledge in travel or consumer tech?'

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: ixigo sees huge traffic spikes during peak seasons. Walk us through how you handled a sudden traffic surge.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we ran a flash-sale feature that drove a sharp, sudden spike in requests to our order service within minutes of launch.

*Task:* My team owned the order processing service. I needed to keep the system stable, protect the user experience, and recover quickly if something failed.

*Action:* We had prepared in the weeks before by running load tests, setting auto-scaling policies with conservative thresholds, and adding circuit breakers on downstream payment and inventory APIs. During the spike, I kept the team focused on metrics rather than assumptions, made the call to shed non-critical background jobs to protect the core booking path, and kept a rollback plan ready in case a recent deploy was implicated.

*Result:* The service handled the surge without a full outage. We caught elevated error rates on one downstream service early because of our alerting, communicated proactively with the product team, and ran a post-incident review that produced three concrete reliability improvements carried into the next season.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you gave difficult feedback to a strong senior engineer who was struggling with collaboration.

*Situation:* A senior engineer on my team was technically one of the best contributors, but team members were starting to disengage from design reviews because this person dismissed alternative ideas quickly.

*Task:* I needed to address this before it damaged morale and our ability to retain other strong engineers.

*Action:* I set up a private one-on-one and opened with specific observations rather than labels. I said something like 'In the last two design reviews, I noticed three instances where you moved past a suggestion before others had finished explaining it.' I asked for their view first, listened, and then described the impact on the team clearly. We agreed on a concrete behaviour to try: asking at least one follow-up question before responding to any proposal in a group setting.

*Result:* The change was gradual but real. Within the next quarter, two team members independently said that design reviews felt more open. The engineer's technical output did not dip, and they later told me the feedback was useful even if uncomfortable at first.

---

Q: How do you balance technical debt against an aggressive feature roadmap?

*Situation:* At a prior company, the product roadmap for a consumer app was packed, and the engineering team kept flagging that a core data pipeline was brittle and slowing down every new feature.

*Task:* I had to find a way to keep shipping while reducing the risk the tech debt posed to reliability.

*Action:* I made the debt visible by linking it to delivery outcomes. Instead of asking for a separate 'tech debt sprint', I showed the product manager data on how many incidents in the past quarter traced back to that pipeline, and how much time the team spent on workarounds each sprint. We agreed to a standing allocation per sprint for reliability work, not as a favour but as a delivery enabler. The team then triaged the debt so we tackled the highest-impact items first.

*Result:* Incident frequency on that part of the system dropped over the following two quarters. The team reported spending less time on firefighting, and the product roadmap actually moved faster because we were no longer constantly unblocking workarounds.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation brief (two or three sentences), spend most of your time on Action (the choices you made and why), and make the Result specific even if you cannot share confidential numbers.

For technical leadership questions, structure your answer as: context (what the system or team looked like), constraints (what made it hard), decisions (what you chose and the trade-offs you weighed), and outcome.

For cross-functional conflict or stakeholder questions, lead with what your goal was, then show how you understood the other party's priorities before proposing a path forward. ixigo interviewers typically want to see that you build bridges rather than escalate first.

For product-thinking questions, anchor your answer in the user (ixigo's travellers, often from smaller cities, on mobile) before jumping to technical solutions. Showing you understand who uses the product makes a strong impression.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that ixigo interviewers look for a consistent set of traits.

Ownership without title: They want to see that you treat reliability, code quality, and team health as personal responsibilities, not just the team's problem.

Comfort with constraints: ixigo operates in a cost-conscious, mobile-first, high-scale environment. Interviewers probe whether you have made pragmatic trade-offs under real constraints, not just designed for ideal conditions.

People depth: Beyond process talk, they want to hear how you have actually changed individual behaviour, grown junior engineers, and handled underperformance with care and clarity.

Product curiosity: EMs are expected to push back on requirements and ask 'why'. Showing genuine interest in the travel domain and ixigo's user base stands out.

Low ego, high collaboration: The culture values directness but not aggression. Candidates who show they can disagree and then commit tend to do well.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the company. Use ixigo's app for a train or flight booking. Read the company's investor relations updates and recent product announcements. Map out the core products (trains, flights, buses, hotels) and think about which squad you might be joining.

Week 2: Build your story bank. Write out six to eight STAR stories covering: handling a production incident, giving difficult feedback, managing a missed deadline, improving a team process, and a cross-functional win. Practise saying each one aloud in under three minutes.

Week 3: Technical readiness. Brush up on distributed systems concepts relevant to booking flows: idempotency, rate limiting, circuit breakers, and eventual consistency. Review how to design for low-bandwidth mobile clients. Be ready to discuss monitoring and alerting strategy, including what you track beyond uptime.

Ongoing: Look at recent ixigo job descriptions for clues about current technical priorities. Reach out to current or former ixigo engineers on LinkedIn to understand the team culture and interview style before your loop begins.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic answers. 'I run agile ceremonies' does not tell an ixigo interviewer anything useful. Tie your answers to travel-tech realities: seasonal spikes, mobile-first constraints, third-party API fragility.

Talking about process, not people. Interviewers want to hear how you changed someone's trajectory or resolved a real conflict. Process descriptions without human outcomes fall flat.

Over-indexing on technical depth. EM candidates who spend all their time on system design and skip people and product questions often do not clear the full loop. Show range across all three areas.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'It went well' is weak. Even without confidential numbers, make the result concrete: 'the team went from deploying once a week to deploying daily' is a real signal.

Not asking good questions. Candidates who ask nothing, or jump straight to salary, signal low interest. Ask about the team's biggest current engineering challenge or how success is measured in the first six months.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the ixigo Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically spans four to five rounds, though this varies by team and seniority. These usually cover an initial recruiter screen, a technical or system design discussion, one or more people and culture rounds, and a final round with senior leadership. Treat each round as a chance to show both technical leadership and people depth.

What salary can I expect as an Engineering Manager at ixigo?

Based on knok jobradar listings, Engineering Manager roles in India range from 35-60 LPA, Senior Manager roles from 55-90 LPA, and Director-level from 90-150+ LPA. Actual offers at ixigo depend on your experience, the specific team, and negotiation. For company-specific benchmarks, Glassdoor and levels.fyi have publicly reported ixigo compensation data worth checking.

Does ixigo ask system design questions in the EM interview?

Candidates report that system design does come up, though the focus is on decision-making and trade-off thinking rather than low-level implementation. You may typically be asked to design something relevant to ixigo's domain, such as a seat-availability service or a PNR status notification system. Focus on reliability, scale patterns, and how you would guide your team through the design rather than trying to spec every detail.

How important is travel-domain knowledge for this role?

You do not need to be a travel industry expert, but familiarity with ixigo's products and users helps significantly. Interviewers typically respond well to candidates who have used the app and can speak to the challenges of serving mobile-first users on constrained networks. Reading up on how IRCTC integrations work and why real-time seat data is hard in Indian rail is a practical starting point.

Is there a coding round for Engineering Manager candidates at ixigo?

Candidates at the EM level typically report that live coding is not the main focus, though some teams include a light coding or code-review exercise to confirm technical fluency. The heavier weight falls on system design, people management scenarios, and leadership philosophy. Confirm the exact format with your recruiter once you enter the process.
