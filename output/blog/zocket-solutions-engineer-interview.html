01 Overview

Overview

Zocket is an AI-powered advertising platform that helps businesses create and launch ads on social media without needing a dedicated design or media buying team. The platform is built for SMBs and growing brands that want to go from brief to live campaign quickly using AI-generated creatives and automated media buying.

A Solutions Engineer at Zocket sits at the crossroads of technical knowledge and client communication. The role involves helping prospects understand how Zocket fits their advertising workflow, supporting new account onboarding, and working with the product and engineering teams when clients run into technical problems.

As of mid-2026, Zocket has 10 open roles, with Solutions Engineer among them. This signals active scaling of the company's client-facing technical function. Across the broader market, knok jobradar tracked 1,270 Solutions Engineer jobs in India at the same time, with Bangalore (55), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20) leading for this profile.

Candidates typically go through a recruiter screen, one or two technical and product-knowledge rounds, and a final conversation with a senior stakeholder. Zocket is a startup, so interviewers tend to value clear thinking and a bias toward action over textbook-perfect answers.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Solutions Engineer interviews at Zocket and similar AdTech companies. Candidates report a mix of technical, situational, and product-knowledge questions.

  1. How would you explain Zocket's ad automation platform to a client who has never used AI-based advertising tools?
  2. Walk us through how you would onboard a new SMB client from the first call to their first live campaign.
  3. A client says their ads are not delivering the reach or conversions they expected on Zocket's platform. How do you troubleshoot this?
  4. How comfortable are you with REST APIs? Describe a time you used API documentation to help a client with an integration.
  5. How would you adjust your communication style when speaking to a technical CTO versus a non-technical marketing manager at the same company?
  6. Tell us about your experience with Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads, or similar advertising platforms. How does that apply to this role?
  7. A prospect asks you to compare Zocket directly with a competitor. How do you handle that conversation?
  8. Describe a time you identified a recurring issue affecting multiple clients and worked with the product team to resolve it.
  9. How would you build a structured success plan for a new enterprise client, and what milestones would you track in their first few months on the platform?
  10. Zocket's pitch is speed: from business idea to live ad in minutes. How would you demonstrate this value during a product demo?
  11. Tell us about a technically complex client escalation you have handled. What was the issue and how did you resolve it?
  12. How do you stay updated on changes to platforms like Meta or Google that might affect clients using Zocket?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral questions. Here are three examples tailored to the Solutions Engineer role.

Q: Tell us about a technically complex client escalation you handled.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a mid-sized e-commerce client saw a significant drop in ad performance after a platform update and was threatening to pause their subscription.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly, keep the client calm, and coordinate internally to fix the problem before the end of their active campaign cycle.

*Action:* I first reproduced the issue using the client's own account settings to confirm it was a platform bug and not a configuration error on their side. I documented the reproduction steps, raised a priority ticket with the engineering team, and gave the client a clear update within a few hours: what the problem was, what we were doing, and when to expect a fix. I set up a daily check-in with the client until the issue was resolved.

*Result:* The bug was patched within two working days. The client stayed on the platform and later expanded their subscription. The incident also led the team to build a post-update testing checklist for ad delivery.

---

Q: How would you onboard a new SMB client to the platform?

*Situation:* A small retail brand had just signed up after a sales demo. They had no prior experience with AI-based ad tools and a small team with limited bandwidth.

*Task:* My goal was to get them to their first live campaign quickly while making sure they felt confident enough to manage the platform on their own.

*Action:* I started with a focused kickoff call to understand their immediate goal: the one campaign they most wanted to launch. I shared a simple onboarding checklist, helped them connect their Meta Business account step by step, and walked through the ad creation flow live on screen. I deliberately held back the full feature set for later sessions so they were not overwhelmed.

*Result:* They launched their first campaign within three days of signing. They renewed at their next billing cycle, and I used their onboarding experience to refine our standard SMB checklist.

---

Q: Describe a time you helped a client who was comparing your product to a competitor.

*Situation:* During a late-stage evaluation, a prospect told me they were leaning toward a competing AdTech tool because of a specific feature we did not have.

*Task:* I needed to reframe the conversation without dismissing the competitor or overpromising on our roadmap.

*Action:* I acknowledged the feature gap honestly and then asked the client to walk me through their actual use case. I found that the missing feature addressed a scenario they rarely encountered, while our platform's core strength (speed and creative automation) directly solved their main problem. I offered a short focused trial and connected them with a reference client in a similar industry.

*Result:* The prospect chose our platform after the trial. They told us the reference call was the deciding factor, which led me to build out a more structured reference program for competitive evaluations.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'explain the product' questions: Use a three-part structure: what the product does in one sentence, who it is for, and one concrete example of value it delivers. Start with the client's problem, not the product's features. Avoid technical jargon in your opening sentence.

For troubleshooting questions: Describe your process in steps. First, confirm whether the issue is on the client's side or the platform side. Then, explain what information you would gather (logs, account settings, campaign data). Finally, describe how you would escalate if you could not resolve it yourself. Interviewers want to see systematic thinking and clear communication under pressure.

For behavioral questions: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on Action, and always close with a concrete Result. If the outcome was not fully positive, mention what you learned and what you would do differently.

For 'working with the product team' questions: Show that you can translate client pain into actionable product feedback. Describe how you document an issue, how you prioritize it relative to other requests, and how you close the loop with the client once the team has responded.

For competitor comparison questions: Acknowledge the competitor's strength, pivot to understanding the client's actual priority, and anchor your response on the specific value your product delivers for that priority. Avoid speaking negatively about competitors by name.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Solutions Engineer interviews at Zocket typically assess four things.

Product and domain fluency. You do not need to have used Zocket before the interview, but you should understand how AI-based ad creation works, what problems SMBs face with advertising, and how platforms like Meta and Google Ads fit into the picture. Candidates who have done clear homework on Zocket's product stand out.

Technical communication. The role requires you to explain complex concepts to non-technical clients and communicate clearly with engineering teams. Interviewers watch whether you can switch between these modes depending on your audience.

Client empathy and problem-solving. Expect scenario-based questions where a client is unhappy or stuck. Interviewers want to see that you start with the client's goal, not the product's limitations. Strong candidates show patience and a structured approach under pressure.

Startup mindset. Zocket is a growth-stage company. Interviewers value candidates who can move fast, take ownership across loosely defined boundaries, and work without heavy process support. Past experience in fast-moving startup or SaaS environments is a genuine advantage.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Understand the product.
Spend time on Zocket's website and any publicly available product walkthroughs or case studies. Understand how a business goes from signing up to launching a campaign. Be ready to explain this flow in your own words.

Step 2: Brush up on AdTech fundamentals.
Get comfortable with the basics of Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads: campaign structure, targeting options, creative formats, and performance metrics like CTR and ROAS. You do not need to be a media buyer, but you should know the vocabulary.

Step 3: Prepare your stories.
Identify three to five situations from your past where you solved a client problem, handled a technical escalation, or improved a process. Write them in STAR format and practice saying them aloud so they sound natural, not rehearsed.

Step 4: Prepare smart questions.
Interviewers at early-stage companies notice when candidates ask thoughtful questions. Ask about how the solutions team collaborates with product, what a typical client escalation looks like, or what success looks like in the first few months on the job. Prepare at least two genuine questions before each round.

Step 5: Practice live demos.
If there is a demo round, practice screen-sharing a product walkthrough with a colleague acting as a skeptical client. Being comfortable on screen and thinking on your feet is a skill in itself.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Explaining the product before understanding the client's problem.
Many candidates jump straight into features. Strong Solutions Engineers start with questions. In interview scenarios, always clarify the client's goal before talking about what the product can do.

Being vague about technical depth.
Saying 'I am comfortable with APIs' is not enough. Be ready to describe a specific integration you worked on, what the challenge was, and how you solved it. Concrete details build credibility far more than general claims.

Avoiding the competitor question.
Some candidates dodge this out of caution. Interviewers expect you to handle it with confidence. Practice a structured response: acknowledge the competitor's strengths, pivot to the client's actual priority, and anchor on your product's differentiated value.

Not asking questions at the end.
Candidates who ask no questions in a startup interview signal low enthusiasm or poor preparation. Prepare genuine questions about the team, the product roadmap, or the client base before each round.

Overpromising during roleplay scenarios.
When an interviewer plays a difficult client, it can be tempting to agree to things the product cannot do just to ease the tension. Be honest about limitations and redirect to what the product does well. Interviewers are testing your integrity as much as your communication skills.

Focusing only on technical skills.
The Solutions Engineer role is as much about relationships and communication as technical depth. Candidates who skip the client empathy side often struggle in later rounds.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Zocket Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two technical and product-knowledge rounds, and a final conversation with a senior team member. The total can vary depending on the seniority of the role. Zocket is a startup, so the process tends to move faster than at large enterprises.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer at Zocket?

Zocket does not publicly publish salary bands for this role. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list compensation for Solutions Engineer positions at Indian SaaS and AdTech startups of similar stage, and publicly reported ranges vary widely by experience level. Research comparable roles on those platforms before your final round and be prepared to discuss your expectations clearly.

Do I need AdTech or advertising experience to apply?

Direct AdTech experience is a plus but is not always a hard requirement, especially for candidates with a strong SaaS Solutions Engineer or technical account management background. Familiarity with Meta Ads Manager or Google Ads is useful and can be built through self-study before the interview. Showing that you understand the SMB advertising problem Zocket solves matters as much as prior industry experience.

Will there be a live product demo in the interview?

Candidates report that some rounds include a roleplay or demo exercise where you walk through the platform as if presenting to a prospect. If this comes up, focus on the client's problem first rather than listing features. Practicing screen-sharing a walkthrough before your interview will help you feel comfortable in the moment.

How important is coding or scripting knowledge for this role?

The Solutions Engineer role at Zocket is primarily client-facing and consultative. You are unlikely to be asked to write production code, but comfort with REST APIs, reading API documentation, and understanding basic integration concepts is expected. Being able to help a client debug an API connection or understand a webhook setup is more relevant than algorithmic problem-solving.

Where are most Zocket Solutions Engineer openings located?

Zocket is headquartered in Bangalore, so most in-person and hybrid roles are likely based there.
