01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Tailscale builds networking software that makes it easy for teams to connect their devices and services securely, without wrestling with firewalls or complex VPN configurations. The product is built on WireGuard and is used by engineering teams at companies ranging from early-stage startups to large enterprises.

As of July 2026, Tailscale has 45 open roles across engineering, product, design, go-to-market, and support. The company is fully remote by design, which means Indian candidates can apply, but each job listing specifies the locations it accepts. Many roles are open only to candidates in the US or Canada, so read the location requirements before investing time in an application.

Tailscale is a privately held company that is still relatively small and focused. Hiring tends to be deliberate: they look for people who can work independently, communicate well in writing, and care deeply about the craft of building software.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Tailscale's interview process typically has four to five stages, though the exact steps can vary by role and team. The details below are based on publicly available candidate accounts and may not reflect every team's process exactly.

Stage 1: Application review. Your resume and any written answers are reviewed by a recruiter or hiring manager. Tailscale values clear, concise writing, so tailor your resume to the role and avoid filler.

Stage 2: Recruiter call. A short introductory call, commonly cited as around 30 minutes, to check your background, confirm location and work authorisation, and explain the role. Be ready to talk about why you want to work at Tailscale specifically.

Stage 3: Technical screen or take-home. For engineering roles, this is usually a coding or system design task. Some teams send a take-home problem; others do a live coding session. Take-home assignments are common at remote-first companies because they let you work in your own environment without time pressure.

Stage 4: Team interviews. A set of conversations with engineers, a product manager, or a team lead. Expect questions on your past projects, your technical depth in the relevant area (networking, security, distributed systems), and how you collaborate remotely.

Stage 5: References and offer. If the team decides to move forward, reference checks happen, followed by a verbal offer and then a written one.

The full process commonly takes three to six weeks from first application to offer, based on publicly reported candidate timelines.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Tailscale is building a networking product, so depth in relevant technical areas matters a great deal for engineering roles.

For software engineers: Strong Go programming skills are important as Go is Tailscale's primary language. Comfort with networking concepts (TCP/IP, DNS, routing, WireGuard, NAT traversal) gives you a clear edge. Security awareness matters too, since the product is fundamentally about secure connectivity.

For all roles: Written communication is treated as a core skill. Because the team is fully remote, almost everything happens asynchronously over text. Candidates who write clearly and concisely tend to do better at every stage of the process.

Attitude and approach: Tailscale looks for people who can own a problem from start to finish without needing constant guidance. They want engineers who ask good questions, document their thinking, and can work across time zones without losing momentum.

For Indian candidates: If you have shipped production code in Go, worked on infrastructure, security, or developer tools, or contributed to open-source networking projects, highlight those clearly on your resume. Prior remote work experience across time zones is also a genuine plus.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product first. Download Tailscale and use it. Set up a personal tailnet, connect two devices, and explore the admin console. Candidates who have actually used the product ask sharper questions and give more grounded answers in interviews.

Strengthen your Go and networking knowledge. If you are targeting an engineering role, practise writing idiomatic Go. Review how WireGuard works, how NAT traversal is handled, and how split tunneling and exit nodes work conceptually. You do not need to memorise the Tailscale codebase, but familiarity with the problem space helps.

Polish your written communication. Before interviews, practise writing clear, concise explanations of past projects. Tailscale's recruiters read a lot of text from candidates, so sharp writing stands out.

Prepare project stories. For each major project on your resume, be ready to explain: what the problem was, what you specifically built or fixed, what trade-offs you made, and what you would do differently now. Avoid vague or high-level answers.

Research the company narrative. Read Tailscale's engineering blog and changelogs, and look for public talks from the founders. Understanding why they built what they built helps you frame your answers in terms of their actual priorities.

Check the location requirement early. With 45 open roles, there is a reasonable range to explore, but some roles are restricted to specific geographies. Confirm this before you spend hours on a take-home assignment.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Tailscale is a remote-first company, which means the culture is shaped heavily by how the team communicates in writing. Long-form writing is valued over quick chat. Decisions get documented. Code reviews and design discussions happen asynchronously. If you have worked in a fast, in-office environment where most decisions happen in meetings, this is a shift worth preparing for.

Small team, real ownership. Tailscale is not a large company. Engineers tend to own significant chunks of the product. That means more impact per person, but also more responsibility. People who thrive here tend to be comfortable making decisions without a lot of approval layers.

Security and correctness matter deeply. Because the product handles network access and authentication, there is a strong culture of thinking carefully before shipping. Cutting corners on correctness is not rewarded here.

Transparency. Tailscale has been relatively open about how the product works, publishing detailed technical explanations of their architecture publicly. This transparency tends to carry into the internal culture as well, based on publicly available employee accounts.

For Indian professionals: The company is headquartered in Canada and most of the team is in North America. If you join remotely from India, expect significant time zone overlap challenges. Tailscale typically expects some availability during North American business hours for collaboration, so factor that into your decision before accepting an offer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Tailscale hire engineers from India?

Tailscale does not have an India office, but some roles are open to global remote candidates. Each job listing specifies the accepted locations, so check carefully before applying. If a role says 'US only' or 'North America,' it typically means they cannot process work authorisation for India-based candidates at this time. Roles that say 'remote' without a country restriction are your best opportunity.

What programming language does Tailscale mainly use?

Tailscale's core product is written in Go, and if you are targeting an engineering role, Go proficiency is practically a requirement. Some roles in adjacent areas like infrastructure or tooling may involve other languages, but Go is the primary language mentioned consistently in Tailscale's public engineering content. If your background is in Python, Java, or another language, spend time getting comfortable with Go before you apply.

How long does the Tailscale hiring process take?

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, the process commonly runs three to six weeks from initial application to offer. This can vary depending on the role, the team's current bandwidth, and how quickly you move through each stage. Take-home assignments, if part of the process, add time since you need to complete and submit them before the next step begins.

Does Tailscale give take-home assignments?

Take-home coding assignments are commonly cited for engineering roles at remote-first companies like Tailscale, and publicly available candidate accounts suggest this is part of their technical evaluation. The advantage is that you work in your own environment at your own pace. Treat the assignment as you would production code: write clean, readable Go, include comments where the logic is not obvious, and do not rush through it.

What salary can I expect from Tailscale?

Tailscale does not publicly publish salary bands, and data specifically for India-based remote roles is thin. For North America roles, Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry some data points, but these numbers may not translate directly to candidates hired remotely from India. Your best move is to raise compensation expectations early in the recruiter call so there are no surprises later in the process.

Are there non-engineering roles at Tailscale worth applying for from India?

Tailscale's 45 open roles as of July 2026 include positions in product, design, sales, and customer support, not just engineering. Some support and community roles are more likely to be open to global remote candidates. For sales roles, there is usually a geographic territory attached, so a fully remote position from India may be less common in that function. Check each listing for location restrictions before applying.
