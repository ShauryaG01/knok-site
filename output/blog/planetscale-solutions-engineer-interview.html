01 Overview

Overview

PlanetScale is a serverless MySQL-compatible database platform built on Vitess, the open-source technology originally developed to scale MySQL at YouTube. A Solutions Engineer at PlanetScale sits at the intersection of pre-sales technical consulting and post-sales enablement. You demo the product, guide customers through proof-of-concepts, troubleshoot migrations, and help engineering teams get the most out of features like database branching and non-blocking schema changes.

The role is remote-first, so candidates across India are eligible regardless of city. As of July 2026, PlanetScale has 12 open roles across all functions. For context, the broader Solutions Engineer market in India shows the most activity in Bangalore (55 openings), followed by Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20), with Pune at 12 openings across all employers.

Candidates typically experience a four-to-five stage process: a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a technical interview covering databases and systems, and a final panel that often includes a mock customer call or a short presentation. The full loop usually runs two to four weeks from first contact to offer, candidates report. PlanetScale values engineers who can speak fluently to both a DBA and a VP of Engineering in the same conversation.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in PlanetScale SE interviews, based on what candidates report and the nature of the product:

  1. Walk me through how you would help a customer migrate a large MySQL database to PlanetScale with zero downtime.
  2. Explain PlanetScale's database branching workflow to a CTO who has never heard of it. Keep it under two minutes.
  3. A customer gets an error after enabling foreign key emulation. How do you diagnose and fix it?
  4. How does Vitess route queries across shards, and what MySQL features does this make unavailable?
  5. A prospect's lead engineer says, 'We cannot run without foreign key constraints at the database level.' How do you respond?
  6. Describe a proof-of-concept you ran for a complex technical product. What was your process and what did you learn?
  7. A customer reports slow queries after moving to PlanetScale. Walk me through your troubleshooting steps.
  8. How would you explain PlanetScale's connection pooling to a developer whose only experience is a single-server MySQL setup?
  9. Tell me about a time you worked closely with a sales team. How did you balance being thorough technically with moving the deal forward?
  10. A customer's ORM generates queries that PlanetScale does not support. How do you handle that conversation?
  11. How do you stay current with the MySQL and broader database ecosystem? Share something recent that caught your attention.
  12. You have three customers all needing urgent help at the same time. How do you decide who gets your attention first?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would help a customer migrate a large MySQL database to PlanetScale with zero downtime.

*Situation:* At a previous role, a client ran a high-traffic production MySQL database and wanted to move to PlanetScale to take advantage of branching and autoscaling, but could not afford any service interruption.

*Task:* I was the SE responsible for designing and guiding the migration plan end to end.

*Action:* I started with a schema audit to identify unsupported patterns, specifically foreign key constraints and any MySQL-specific functions that Vitess does not pass through. I then used PlanetScale's import tool to run an initial data sync to a new PlanetScale database. Once the replica was in sync, I worked with the client's engineers to configure dual writes so both databases stayed consistent. We monitored replication lag closely and agreed on a cutover window with a documented rollback plan ready.

*Result:* The migration completed over a weekend with no customer-facing downtime. The client told me it was the smoothest database migration they had run, and they went live on PlanetScale without a single rollback.

---

Q: A prospect says they cannot run without foreign key constraints. How do you respond?

*Situation:* During a proof-of-concept with an e-commerce company, the lead database engineer raised this concern in front of his CTO. He had read that PlanetScale does not support foreign key constraints at the database level.

*Task:* I needed to address the objection honestly and keep the evaluation moving without overselling.

*Action:* I acknowledged the limitation directly. I then explained that PlanetScale enforces referential integrity at the application layer, which is the same pattern used by publicly reported high-scale systems. I showed a live demo of PlanetScale's foreign key emulation feature and walked through how their ORM could handle the enforcement. I also pulled up a publicly reported case study so the engineer could see how another team had solved the same problem.

*Result:* The engineer was satisfied and moved the evaluation forward. The team completed the migration within that quarter, and the CTO later cited the honest handling of the limitation as a reason they trusted PlanetScale.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked closely with a sales team to close a deal.

*Situation:* A strategic prospect had been in evaluation for longer than expected. The account executive asked me to help accelerate the technical decision without rushing the customer.

*Task:* I needed to design a focused technical session that would get the customer's engineering team to a clear decision quickly.

*Action:* I planned a half-day workshop covering a schema review, a live migration demo, and a structured Q and A with the customer's DBA team. I coordinated with the product team to get early access to a feature the customer specifically needed. I also prepared a concise one-page technical summary the AE could share with the economic buyer who would not attend the technical session.

*Result:* The customer's CTO approved the purchase after the workshop. The AE shared that it was the fastest final-stage close on his team that quarter, and it became a format we reused for other late-stage deals.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions. Every 'tell me about a time' question expects a clean arc: Situation sets the scene briefly, Task names your specific responsibility, Action is the bulk of your answer with concrete steps, and Result shows measurable or clearly described impact. Avoid vague endings like 'it went well' and instead name what changed: the migration completed by a specific date, the customer moved to production, the deal progressed to the next stage.

Explain, Demo, Confirm for technical explanation questions. When asked to explain a PlanetScale concept such as branching, sharding, or non-blocking schema changes, lead with a plain-English one-sentence summary, follow with a concrete analogy or live walkthrough, then confirm understanding by inviting a question. This mirrors what you will do on actual customer calls and shows interviewers you have experience with technical selling.

Problem, Diagnosis, Fix, Prevention for troubleshooting questions. When asked about a customer issue such as slow queries, migration errors, or connection limits, structure your answer in four beats: name the problem clearly, describe how you would gather data to pinpoint the cause, explain the fix, then mention what you would do so the same issue does not recur. This shows systematic thinking rather than guesswork.

The 'Yes, and' objection framework. For objection-handling questions around the foreign key limitation or the 'why not just use RDS' concern, always acknowledge the concern before pivoting. Interviewers are checking whether you will paper over real limitations. Candidates who admit constraints and then explain the workaround come across as more trustworthy than those who deflect.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep database fundamentals, not just product knowledge. PlanetScale SEs are expected to explain Vitess internals, MySQL replication, and schema design tradeoffs. Knowing the product's features is table stakes. Knowing why the product is built the way it is, including its constraints, sets strong candidates apart.

Customer empathy and communication clarity. Every technical question carries an implicit second question: 'Can you explain this to someone who is not a DBA?' Practice explaining concepts like connection pooling or schema branching to a non-technical stakeholder before your interview. Candidates who can simplify without losing accuracy do well here.

Honest handling of product limitations. PlanetScale does not support all MySQL features, and interviewers will probe to see if you acknowledge this. Candidates who try to spin or minimize limitations lose credibility quickly.

Sales awareness. This is not a pure engineering role. Interviewers want to see that you understand deal cycles, can read a room on a customer call, and know when to escalate to the account executive versus when to keep troubleshooting. Candidates with experience in pre-sales, developer relations, or technical consulting have an advantage here.

Ownership and follow-through. SEs at smaller companies typically carry work end to end. Interviewers look for candidates who take full responsibility for a customer outcome rather than handing off at the first sign of complexity.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your technical foundation.

Start with Vitess documentation and the PlanetScale docs. Understand how PlanetScale differs from standard MySQL: no foreign key constraints at the database level by default, Vitess query routing, non-blocking schema changes using gh-ost, and the branching and deploy requests workflow. Spin up a free PlanetScale account and go through the quickstart. Create a database, make a branch, run a schema change, and merge it. You will be asked about this workflow, and hands-on experience makes your answers concrete.

Review the basics of MySQL replication, indexing strategy, and connection pooling. These topics come up in troubleshooting questions.

Week 2: Practice communication and behavioral prep.

Prepare STAR answers for at least four behavioral questions: a migration you led, a technical objection you handled, a time you worked with a sales team, and a time you had to prioritize under pressure. Record yourself and check whether your answers run two to three minutes and end with a specific result.

Practice explaining PlanetScale's core features out loud as if to a CTO who has never seen the product. Time yourself. Aim for under two minutes per concept without losing accuracy.

Research PlanetScale's publicly available customer stories. Knowing which types of companies use PlanetScale and why gives your answers real-world grounding.

In the final days before your interview, prepare two or three questions that show you understand the SE role: ask about the customer mix, the handoff process between SE and customer success, and how PlanetScale measures SE impact.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going too broad on database answers. When asked about MySQL or Vitess, candidates sometimes give a textbook definition instead of a practical, customer-facing explanation. Interviewers want to hear how you would explain it on a customer call, not how you would define it in documentation.

Ignoring PlanetScale's real limitations. Candidates who claim PlanetScale is perfect for every use case immediately lose credibility. Know the scenarios where PlanetScale is not the right fit and be ready to name them honestly.

Treating this like a pure engineering interview. SEs are evaluated on commercial awareness and communication skills, not just technical depth. Candidates who stay entirely in deep technical mode without mentioning customers or deal dynamics miss half the evaluation criteria.

Skipping hands-on product time. Candidates who have never actually used PlanetScale give noticeably thinner answers on the branching workflow and schema change process. Spend at least a few hours in the product before your interview.

Weak results in STAR answers. Ending an answer with 'it worked out' or 'the customer was happy' is not enough. Name what changed: the migration completed on schedule, the customer moved to production, the deal progressed to the next stage. Even qualitative outcomes should be specific.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the PlanetScale SE interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs four to five stages: a recruiter screen, a hiring manager call, a technical interview focused on databases and systems, and a final panel that may include a mock customer presentation or live demo. Some candidates also report a separate culture or values conversation. The full process usually runs two to four weeks from first contact to offer.

Do I need to know Vitess internals to pass the technical interview?

You do not need to be a Vitess contributor, but you should understand the basics: how Vitess routes queries, why it restricts certain MySQL features, and how PlanetScale builds its branching and schema change features on top of it. Candidates who can explain why PlanetScale works the way it does, rather than just what it does, tend to stand out. Reviewing the Vitess documentation and PlanetScale's technical blog before your interview is a good starting point.

Is prior sales engineering experience required?

Formal SE experience is not always required, candidates report, but you should be able to demonstrate that you understand how a pre-sales process works. Experience in developer relations, technical consulting, or customer-facing engineering roles translates well. What matters most is showing that you can run a customer conversation, handle technical objections honestly, and collaborate with an account executive toward a commercial outcome.

How should I prepare for the mock customer call portion?

Practice explaining PlanetScale's core value (serverless MySQL, database branching, non-blocking schema changes) in under two minutes, as if speaking to a CTO seeing the product for the first time. Anticipate the foreign key objection and the 'why not just use RDS' concern and prepare honest, concise responses. Run through the explanation with a friend or record yourself. Interviewers are checking whether you can simplify without losing accuracy and whether you listen and adapt when the customer pushes back.
