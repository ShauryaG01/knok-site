01 Overview

Overview

Their platform helps businesses manage last-mile delivery, freight forwarding, and warehouse operations. An Engineering Manager at Shipsy is expected to own both the technical direction of a team and the career growth of individual engineers, while working closely with product and business teams that serve demanding enterprise clients.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you manage technical debt when the business is pushing for constant feature delivery?

*Situation:* At my previous company, our core order-management service had grown significantly over two years of rapid product development. It was becoming a bottleneck: deployment cycles had stretched, and new engineers struggled to ramp up on the codebase.

*Task:* I needed to address this without blocking a busy roadmap that the product and business teams depended on.

*Action:* I introduced a 'health budget' concept with the team. We reserved a consistent portion of each sprint for refactoring and documentation, negotiated upfront with the product manager rather than after the fact. I made the debt visible by tracking it in the same tool as features, so leadership could see the trade-offs clearly. We prioritised the parts of the code touched most frequently in production first.

*Result:* Within a few quarters, deployment cycles shortened noticeably, onboarding of new engineers became faster, and we had zero unplanned rollbacks in that module. The product team eventually started advocating for the health budget themselves because they saw fewer bugs in that area.

---

Q: Tell us about an outage or critical incident you handled as an EM.

*Situation:* A third-party carrier API we depended on for real-time shipment tracking went down unexpectedly on a high-volume sale day for one of our enterprise clients.

*Task:* I had to manage the engineering response while also keeping the client and internal stakeholders informed without creating panic.

*Action:* I immediately set up a war-room channel, assigned one engineer to provide client-facing status updates at regular intervals and the rest to root cause and mitigation. I spoke directly with the client's operations lead to set honest expectations. We activated a fallback polling mechanism we had built earlier for exactly this kind of scenario and restored partial tracking within a short window.

*Result:* The client appreciated the transparent communication and the fact that we had a fallback ready. We ran a thorough post-mortem and added this carrier's reliability patterns to our monitoring stack. The client renewed their contract the following quarter and specifically mentioned incident handling as a reason.

---

Q: How have you approached hiring and retaining strong engineers in a competitive market?

*Situation:* After a funding round, my team needed to scale headcount significantly in a short window, but we were a growth-stage company competing against larger tech firms for the same talent pool.

*Task:* I needed a hiring strategy that could attract strong candidates while keeping the bar high.

*Action:* I rewrote our job descriptions to be specific about the technical problems candidates would work on (not just generic 'scalable systems'), which improved applicant quality immediately. I ran referral drives with engineers I respected from past roles. For retention, I set up quarterly career conversations, ensured engineers had visibility into product and business decisions, and started an internal tech-talk series so people could build their own reputation within the company.

*Result:* We filled the open positions within the target window, and attrition on the team stayed low for the year that followed. Several engineers cited 'learning and visibility' as top reasons for staying in our internal engagement survey.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for every behavioral question at Shipsy. For EM roles, the 'Action' section must make clear what YOU decided and drove, not what the team did collectively. Interviewers are assessing your judgment and decision-making, not team output.

The 'so what' close: After your Result, add one sentence on what you learned or would do differently. Engineering leaders who reflect on outcomes stand out over those who only report them.

For system design questions, use a scope-first approach: restate the scale and constraints before jumping into design. For a logistics platform like Shipsy, this means calling out real-time requirements, multi-tenant data isolation for enterprise clients, and reliability expectations upfront.

For people and process questions, use the 'problem, principle, practice' frame:
- Name the actual problem you saw (not a generic one)
- State the principle that guided your decision (for example: 'I believe engineers own quality, not just QA')
- Describe the specific practice you put in place

For cross-functional and stakeholder questions, show both sides: describe how you understood the other team's constraints before proposing your solution. Shipsy's EMs work closely with product and operations teams, so interviewers specifically look for empathy across functions, not just technical assertiveness.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Shipsy is a product company in a complex, fast-moving domain. Their EM interviewers are typically looking for a few specific signals.

Domain curiosity, not domain gatekeeping: You do not need a logistics background, but candidates report that interviewers respond well to people who engage seriously with the logistics domain rather than treating it as generic enterprise SaaS. Spend time understanding last-mile delivery, freight management, and supply chain visibility before your rounds.

Engineering depth that informs leadership: Shipsy's platform involves real-time data processing and integrations with many external systems. Interviewers want to see that you can hold a credible technical conversation with your engineers, not just manage a project board.

Clear ownership mindset: In a startup environment, ambiguity is the norm. Interviewers probe whether you step up when things are unclear or wait for direction. Use examples that show you identified a problem, formed a view, and acted on it.

Client-aware thinking: Because Shipsy serves enterprise clients, reliability and communication during incidents matters enormously. Be ready to discuss how you have handled external-facing pressure during technical crises.

People development with evidence: Saying 'I care about my team's growth' is expected. What stands out is specific examples: an engineer you promoted, a performance issue you resolved constructively, or a career conversation that changed someone's trajectory.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the company and domain

Read Shipsy's product pages and recent press coverage to understand their core platform, the logistics problems they solve, and their key enterprise clients. Understand the difference between last-mile delivery management, freight forwarding, and warehouse management, as these are Shipsy's main product areas. Note any recent funding rounds, geographic expansions, or product launches from 2024-2026.

Week 2: Build your story bank

List your strongest EM experiences across several themes: technical decision-making, incident handling, team building, cross-functional collaboration, and delivery under pressure. For each experience, draft a concise STAR narrative. Tag each story with the question types it can answer so you can retrieve the right example quickly under interview pressure.

Week 3: Practice out loud and do targeted prep

Practice your answers out loud, ideally with a peer who can challenge your reasoning. For system design, practise designing a real-time shipment tracking system or a multi-carrier integration layer, as these map closely to Shipsy's actual platform problems. Prepare a few thoughtful questions for each round that show you have done your research on the team and the product.

On logistics domain basics: Familiarise yourself with terms like first-mile, mid-mile, last-mile, NDR (non-delivery report), RTO (return to origin), and 3PL. You do not need to be an expert, but using this vocabulary naturally in conversation signals genuine interest in the domain.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic EM answers: Saying 'I set OKRs and do one-on-one meetings' without connecting it to a real situation reads as surface-level. Shipsy interviewers have seen many EM candidates. Specificity in your examples is what separates strong candidates.

Ignoring the product and client context: Some candidates talk entirely about internal engineering health without acknowledging that Shipsy's engineering decisions directly affect enterprise client operations. Weave in client impact wherever it is relevant.

Underselling technical depth: Some EM candidates overcorrect into pure people-management mode and avoid technical detail. For a product-engineering role at Shipsy's stage, being able to discuss system design trade-offs, reliability patterns, and code quality is still important.

Not having questions ready: Arriving without prepared questions signals low interest. Ask about engineering challenges specific to their platform, how they handle SLA pressure from enterprise clients, or what the team is currently working to improve on the technical side.

Claiming team outcomes as personal wins: Interviewers will probe for your specific contribution. 'We delivered X' is weaker than 'I made the call to do X because of Y, and the team executed.' Own your decisions without erasing your team's work.

Being vague about failures: Most EM interview panels include a question about something that went wrong. Candidates who give sanitised, non-specific answers miss the chance to demonstrate self-awareness and learning, two qualities Shipsy's leadership values highly.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Shipsy typically have for an EM role?

Candidates report the process typically involves multiple rounds covering a recruiter screening, one or more technical and system design discussions, a managerial and behavioral panel, and a final discussion with senior leadership. The exact structure can vary by team and the seniority of the role. Confirm the round format with your recruiter after the first call so you can prepare appropriately for each stage.

What salary can I expect for an Engineering Manager role at Shipsy?

Based on the knok job radar salary bands, Engineering Manager roles in India typically range from 35-60 LPA, with Senior Manager positions at 55-90 LPA. Exact compensation at Shipsy will depend on your level, years of relevant experience, and the specific team you join. Discuss the band early with the recruiter so you are aligned well before the offer stage and avoid surprises on either side.

Do I need a logistics or supply chain background to get hired at Shipsy?

Candidates report that a logistics background is not a hard requirement for the EM role. However, showing genuine curiosity about the domain makes a strong impression on interviewers. Spend time understanding Shipsy's core product areas, including last-mile delivery, freight management, and warehouse operations, and connect your engineering experience to the problems they solve. Using logistics terminology naturally in conversation signals that you have done your homework.

What is the difference between an EM interview and a senior engineer interview at Shipsy?

As an EM candidate, you will still face system design questions, but the focus shifts from 'how would you build this?' to 'how would you lead a team to build this, make trade-off decisions, and ensure quality and delivery?' Behavioral and people management questions carry much more weight at the EM level. Interviewers also assess how you handle ambiguity, stakeholder pressure, and team conflict, not just technical correctness.

How should I prepare for Shipsy-specific system design questions?

Focus on distributed systems problems relevant to logistics: real-time shipment tracking, high-volume event processing, multi-carrier integrations, and multi-tenant SaaS architecture. Practice framing your designs around reliability and SLAs first, since Shipsy's clients are large enterprises where downtime has direct business impact. Bring up failure handling, alerting, and graceful degradation proactively rather than waiting to be prompted by the interviewer.
