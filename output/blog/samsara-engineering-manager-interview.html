01 Overview

Overview

Samsara builds the connected operations cloud, helping companies monitor fleets, equipment, and frontline workers through IoT sensors and AI software. If you are interviewing for an Engineering Manager role at Samsara in 2026, you are stepping into a company with serious engineering scale. Samsara currently has around 350 open roles, and EM positions are central to how they grow their product and platform teams.

Candidates report a multi-round interview process that covers people leadership, technical judgment, and cultural alignment. Samsara places strong emphasis on customer obsession, operational reliability (their products are used in safety-critical environments), and your ability to develop engineers who can build durable, high-quality systems.

Market data for Engineering Manager-track roles at Samsara shows 35-60 LPA at the Manager level, 55-90 LPA at Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at Director.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up regularly in Samsara EM interviews, based on candidate reports and the company's publicly shared values around customer focus and operational excellence.

  1. Walk me through how you have built or significantly grown an engineering team.
  2. Samsara's products affect real-world safety. How do you build a culture of quality and reliability in your team?
  3. Describe a situation where you had to make a hard technical trade-off under time or resource pressure.
  4. How do you identify and hire engineers who can work on problems that involve both hardware and software complexity?
  5. Tell me about a time you managed an underperforming engineer. What did you do, and what was the outcome?
  6. How do you balance shipping new features quickly against addressing technical debt?
  7. Describe how you have partnered with product managers and customer-facing teams to shape engineering priorities.
  8. Tell me about a time you drove alignment across multiple engineering teams on a shared initiative.
  9. How do you set technical direction for your team and get buy-in without becoming a bottleneck?
  10. How have you handled a major production incident that had direct customer or business impact?
  11. How do you retain strong engineers when competitors are actively recruiting your people?
  12. Tell me about a project you delivered successfully despite incomplete information or shifting requirements.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioral questions. Below are three example answers built around themes Samsara interviews commonly surface.

Q: Tell me about a time you built or scaled an engineering team.

*Situation:* Our team was assigned ownership of a new product line in a domain the company had not built in before, with no dedicated engineers yet and a hard customer-facing deadline.

*Task:* I needed to staff the team, establish an engineering culture from scratch, and deliver a working MVP without pulling excessive bandwidth from other critical teams.

*Action:* I started by defining the core skills the team would need, then partnered with recruiting to write targeted job descriptions and ran structured interviews to assess both technical depth and growth mindset. I also moved two senior engineers from adjacent teams with their managers' agreement to seed the group. From week one, I held regular 1:1s, introduced a lightweight RFC process for technical decisions, and set up a clear on-call rotation.

*Result:* The team shipped the MVP on schedule, and on-call incidents were low from launch because we had built reliability habits early. Samsara interviewers probe specifically whether you can show concrete outcomes, so tie your story to a visible result.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you managed an underperforming engineer.

*Situation:* A senior engineer on my team was consistently missing delivery commitments, and the rest of the team had started quietly absorbing the impact.

*Task:* I needed to address this directly without damaging team morale or losing someone who had valuable domain knowledge.

*Action:* I opened a candid 1:1 to understand the root cause first. It turned out the engineer was struggling with scope creep and poor task breakdown, not with technical ability. I worked with them to build a structured weekly plan, set explicit mid-week checkpoints, and paired them with a teammate on complex deliverables. I also gave clear, written feedback on what 'good' looked like at their level so expectations were no longer implicit.

*Result:* Delivery consistency improved within a quarter and team trust was restored. Samsara interviewers typically want to see that you gave honest feedback early, not just at the end when damage was already done.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to make a hard technical trade-off.

*Situation:* We were building a data pipeline that needed to handle real-time telemetry at high volume. Two architecture options existed: a simpler design that could ship faster but had known scalability limits, and a more complex distributed approach that would take significantly longer.

*Task:* I had to choose a path and get alignment from product, infrastructure, and leadership, all of whom had different priorities.

*Action:* I ran a structured trade-off session with the team and wrote a short decision document covering the risks, costs, and realistic growth projections for each option. We chose the simpler approach, but with a clearly documented migration plan and agreed thresholds that would trigger the switch.

*Result:* We shipped on time, and the migration was executed later without disruption because the plan had been in place from the start. Samsara values this kind of deliberate, documented decision-making over gut-feel choices.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for Samsara behavioral questions. Lead with context, be specific about what you personally were responsible for, go deep on your actions rather than your team's, and close with a concrete result.

Influence without authority. Samsara EMs typically work across product, design, data science, and operations teams. Interviewers will probe how you move things forward when you do not have direct control. Prepare examples where you aligned stakeholders through data and shared goals, not positional authority.

Technical depth calibration. Samsara EMs are expected to engage meaningfully with technical decisions. A useful way to frame your role is: 'what I delegated, what I guided, what I decided.' Be ready to articulate where you drew those lines and why. Interviewers want to see that you are neither disconnected from the technical work nor micromanaging your engineers.

The scale narrative. Samsara is a growth-stage company and interviewers respond well to before-and-after framing: what was the state of the team or system when you took over, what decisions you made, and what measurably improved. Avoid vague claims about impact. Tie them to delivery pace, reliability, or team growth wherever possible.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Samsara interviewers are evaluating Engineering Manager candidates on specific dimensions that reflect what the company actually needs at its stage of growth.

Customer and safety obsession. Samsara's products are deployed in physical operations where reliability affects real people. Interviewers want to see that you treat engineering quality as a customer issue, not just a technical preference. Bring examples where your team's work had direct, visible impact on end users.

Hiring bar ownership. Samsara places high value on EMs who actively own their team's hiring standards. Candidates report interviewers asking detailed questions about how you define what 'good' looks like for your team and how you personally contribute to recruiting.

Clarity under ambiguity. The connected operations space moves fast and requirements shift. Interviewers want evidence that you can make decisions with incomplete information, communicate clearly to stakeholders, and course-correct without creating drama.

Engineering culture building. Beyond delivery, Samsara wants EMs who build durable team cultures: code review habits, post-incident review practices, career development conversations, and psychological safety. Be specific about the systems or rituals you put in place.

Data-informed decisions. Samsara's product generates enormous amounts of telemetry. Interviewers respond well to EMs who use data to make engineering decisions, whether that is around deployment frequency, incident trends, or engineer health.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Understand the product deeply. Read Samsara's engineering blog and product pages. Understand what connected operations means, how their IoT devices interact with the cloud platform, and what engineering challenges are involved. This context will make your answers feel specific rather than generic.

Step 2: Map your experience to their domain. Samsara values experience with high-reliability, high-throughput systems. If you have worked on infrastructure, data pipelines, or safety-critical software, lead with that. If not, be ready to show how your experience translates to their constraints.

Step 3: Build your story bank. Write out at least six to eight stories covering: building or scaling a team, managing a difficult person situation, making a hard technical call, driving cross-functional alignment, handling a production incident, and hiring.

Step 4: Prepare for the technical discussion. Samsara EMs typically go through at least one round with a technical focus. Review system design concepts relevant to IoT data ingestion, real-time processing, and fleet-scale telemetry. You do not need to code, but you should discuss architecture trade-offs fluently.

Step 5: Prepare smart questions. Ask about how engineering teams are structured, how technical roadmap decisions are made, and what the on-call culture looks like. These questions signal that you are thinking practically about the role, not just trying to land an offer.

Step 6: Know your compensation expectations. Market data shows 35-60 LPA for Manager, 55-90 LPA for Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at Director level. Cross-check with Glassdoor and levels.fyi for current self-reported figures so you walk into the offer conversation with a clear number in mind.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about your own contribution. Saying 'we did X' instead of 'I decided X' is one of the most common mistakes. Samsara interviewers are evaluating you, not your team. Be specific about what you personally drove.

Skipping the 'why' behind technical decisions. Candidates who describe what they built without explaining the trade-offs they considered do not land well at Samsara. The company values deliberate engineering judgment. Always explain what options you considered and why you chose one over another.

Underplaying people management. Some EM candidates from strong IC backgrounds spend too much time on technical war stories and not enough on how they develop engineers. Samsara interviewers probe for this specifically. Prepare detailed examples of coaching, feedback, and career development.

Not connecting to customer impact. Generic answers about 'improving system reliability' without tying it to real customer or business outcomes fall flat at Samsara. Their culture is heavily customer-facing. Always close your STAR answers with the customer or product impact, not just the engineering metric.

Ignoring the company's physical-world context. Engineering Manager candidates sometimes answer as if Samsara is a standard SaaS company. It is not. Their systems interact with physical assets and frontline workers in real time. Show in your answers that you understand why reliability and trust matter at a level beyond typical software products.

Arriving unprepared on compensation. Not knowing your range can hurt you in final stages. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for self-reported figures and be ready with a number before you reach the offer stage.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Samsara Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs four to six rounds, though this can vary by team and level. Common stages include an initial recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, technical and behavioral panels, and a final leadership round. Samsara may also include a cross-functional or 'bar raiser' style interview. Confirm the exact structure with your recruiter at the start of the process.

Does Samsara expect Engineering Managers to write code?

Samsara does not typically ask EMs to complete coding exercises, but candidates report being expected to discuss technical trade-offs, system design decisions, and architecture approaches in depth. You should be comfortable talking about distributed systems, data pipelines, and reliability patterns. The expectation is technical fluency, not daily coding output.

What is the salary range for Engineering Manager roles at Samsara in India?

Market data for Engineering Manager-track roles shows 35-60 LPA at the Manager level, 55-90 LPA at Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at Director level. For current self-reported figures from employees, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi. Compensation also varies by the specific team and scope, so treat these as reference ranges rather than fixed offers.

How important is domain knowledge in IoT or fleet management?

Domain knowledge helps but is not strictly required, based on what candidates report. Samsara cares more about your ability to lead engineering teams that build reliable, scalable systems than about prior IoT or fleet experience specifically. That said, showing genuine curiosity about their domain and doing your homework on their product before interviews gives you a clear edge over candidates who arrive with a generic pitch.

How should I talk about failures or mistakes in a Samsara interview?

Samsara values honesty and learning over polished storytelling. Candidates report that interviewers respond well when you describe a failure clearly: what happened, what your role was, what you did to fix it, and specifically what you changed afterward. Avoid blame-shifting or over-sanitising the story. A genuine account of a hard situation handled maturely is far more compelling than a perfectly packaged success story.

Is there a take-home assignment or case study in the Samsara EM process?

Some candidates report receiving a short written exercise or a scenario to think through before a panel, but this is not universal across all teams. Typically these are designed to ground the conversation rather than to evaluate output quality in isolation. Ask your recruiter upfront whether there is any written component so you are not caught off guard on the day.
