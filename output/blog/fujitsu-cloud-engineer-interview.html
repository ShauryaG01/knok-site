01 Overview

Overview

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a cloud migration you handled end to end.

*Situation:* My previous employer needed to move a legacy on-premise ERP system to AWS for a manufacturing client. The system had multiple dependencies and zero downtime tolerance during business hours.

*Task:* I was responsible for planning the migration strategy, setting up the target environment, and coordinating the cutover window with the client's IT team.

*Action:* I chose a phased lift-and-shift approach, starting with non-critical modules. I used AWS DMS for database migration and set up parallel environments for testing. I ran load tests on each module before switching traffic, and I scheduled the final cutover on a weekend after getting client sign-off.

*Result:* The migration completed with zero unplanned downtime. Post-migration, the client saw faster query performance because we right-sized the instances and enabled auto-scaling.

---

Q: How do you manage cloud costs for a client with multiple business units?

*Situation:* At my last role, a large client had multiple teams spinning up resources independently, and their monthly cloud bill kept climbing with no clear ownership.

*Task:* I was asked to bring visibility into spending and reduce waste without blocking any team's work.

*Action:* I implemented tagging policies so every resource was linked to a business unit. I set up AWS Cost Explorer dashboards for each team and configured billing alerts. I also identified idle resources and recommended reserved instances for predictable workloads.

*Result:* The client's teams could finally see their own spend. Within two billing cycles, overall waste from idle resources dropped significantly, and budget conversations became data-driven.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you worked with a cross-functional team under a tight deadline.

*Situation:* A Fujitsu-style managed services project required delivering a new staging environment for a banking client before their audit cycle began.

*Task:* I needed to coordinate with the networking team, the security team, and the client's application owners to have the environment ready and compliant.

*Action:* I created a shared task board, held daily stand-ups, and personally handled the Terraform scripts for provisioning. When the security team flagged a firewall rule conflict, I worked with them the same day to resolve it rather than waiting for the next sprint.

*Result:* The environment was delivered two days before the audit deadline. The client's compliance team approved it on first review, and the same setup was later reused as a template for future environments.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for every behavioural question. Structure your answer as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Fujitsu interviewers value clear communication because their Cloud Engineers often work directly with enterprise clients.

The 'Why This Way' framework for design questions. When asked how you would architect something, state your approach, then explain why you chose it over alternatives. For example: 'I would use a multi-AZ deployment with auto-scaling because the client needs high availability, and this approach avoids the complexity of a multi-region setup for their current scale.'

The Diagnose-Act-Verify pattern for troubleshooting. First, explain how you would narrow down the root cause (logs, metrics, recent changes). Then, describe the fix. Finally, mention how you would verify the fix worked and prevent recurrence. This shows you think beyond the immediate problem.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Practical cloud skills, not just theory. Fujitsu delivers managed services to enterprise clients. Interviewers want to hear about real infrastructure you have built, migrated, or maintained. Textbook definitions without project context will not stand out.

Client-facing mindset. Many Fujitsu Cloud Engineers interact with client stakeholders. Showing that you can explain technical decisions in simple terms, manage expectations, and handle feedback calmly is a real advantage.

Structured problem-solving. When given a scenario, break it down step by step. Jumping straight to an answer without showing your reasoning can work against you.

Awareness of compliance and governance. Fujitsu's enterprise clients, especially in banking and manufacturing, have strict requirements. Familiarity with commonly cited standards like ISO 27001 and general data protection practices will strengthen your answers.

Team collaboration. Fujitsu operates in a delivery-oriented model. Interviewers look for candidates who work well within structured teams and can coordinate across functions.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core cloud fundamentals. Revise key services on your primary platform (AWS or Azure): compute, storage, networking, IAM. Practice drawing architecture diagrams for common patterns like three-tier web apps and disaster recovery setups.

Week 2: Hands-on automation. Write or revisit Terraform or CloudFormation templates. Deploy a small project end to end. Be ready to talk through your code in an interview.

Week 3: Scenario practice. Pick five questions from the list above and answer them out loud using the STAR format. Time yourself to keep answers under two minutes each. Practice explaining a technical decision to a non-technical person.

Week 4: Company research and mock interviews. Read about Fujitsu's cloud partnerships and recent projects in India. Do at least one mock interview with a friend or mentor. Focus on clarity and confidence, not on memorising scripts.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Giving generic answers. Saying 'I used AWS' without specifics about which services, what scale, or what problem you solved does not impress. Always tie your answer to a concrete project.
  1. Ignoring the client context. Fujitsu is a services company. Talking only about internal projects and never mentioning how you worked with clients or stakeholders misses what the role actually involves.
  1. Skipping the 'why' in design questions. Stating a solution without explaining your reasoning makes it hard for the interviewer to assess your depth. Always mention trade-offs you considered.
  1. Over-engineering answers. Proposing a multi-region, Kubernetes-based, microservices architecture for a simple use case signals that you do not tailor solutions to actual requirements.
  1. Not asking questions at the end. Asking about the team structure, client domains, or tech stack shows genuine interest. Saying 'no questions' leaves a flat impression.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What cloud platforms does Fujitsu typically use in India?

Fujitsu works across multiple cloud platforms, with AWS and Azure being commonly cited by candidates. The specific platform depends on the client and project. Being comfortable with at least one major provider and open to learning others is a safe approach.

How many interview rounds can I expect for a Cloud Engineer role at Fujitsu?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds: an initial screening, a technical interview focused on cloud and infrastructure skills, and a final discussion with a hiring manager. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level.

Is Terraform experience required for Fujitsu Cloud Engineer roles?

Infrastructure-as-code experience is highly valued. Terraform is commonly mentioned in Fujitsu job descriptions, but experience with CloudFormation or Ansible can also be relevant depending on the project.

Are there Fujitsu Cloud Engineer openings outside Bangalore?

Yes. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked Fujitsu Cloud Engineer roles across multiple cities. Delhi had 13 openings, Hyderabad had 6, Pune had 5, and Chennai had 2, alongside 13 in Bangalore.

Should I prepare for coding questions in a Fujitsu Cloud Engineer interview?

Light scripting questions (Python, Bash) may come up, especially around automation tasks. However, the focus is typically on cloud architecture, troubleshooting, and infrastructure tooling rather than competitive-style coding problems.

How important is certification for getting hired as a Cloud Engineer at Fujitsu?

Certifications like AWS Solutions Architect or Azure Administrator are a plus and can help your resume clear initial screening. However, candidates report that hands-on project experience matters more during the actual interview rounds.
