01 Overview

Overview

Braze is a customer engagement platform that helps brands send personalised messages across push notifications, email, SMS, and in-app channels. A Solutions Engineer (SE) at Braze sits at the crossroads of sales and technical depth: you demo the product, handle deep integration questions, run proof-of-concept builds, and help prospects understand exactly how Braze fits their existing stack.

Braze currently has 252 open roles, signalling active hiring across their global team. Across all companies, knok tracked 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 55 listings, followed by Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20).

The interview process typically spans four to five rounds, and candidates report a mix of behavioural screens, technical deep-dives, and a live demo exercise. Preparation needs to cover both platform knowledge (Canvas, Liquid, SDK basics) and commercial awareness about how your work moves deals forward.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report encountering in a Braze Solutions Engineer interview. Expect both behavioural and technical formats, sometimes in the same conversation.

  1. Walk us through how you would technically qualify a prospect who claims they already use a competing customer engagement platform.
  2. Braze uses Liquid templating for message personalisation. Describe a complex Liquid use case you have built or would build for a customer.
  3. Tell us about a time you ran a proof-of-concept for a sceptical technical buyer. How did you structure it and what was the outcome?
  4. A customer's iOS push open rate has dropped after integrating the Braze SDK. Walk us through your debugging approach step by step.
  5. How would you explain Braze Canvas Flow to a non-technical marketing manager in under five minutes?
  6. A prospect asks how Braze handles data residency for their EU customers. What do you tell them?
  7. You are mid-demo and the feature you planned to showcase stops working unexpectedly. What do you do?
  8. How do you handle a situation where a prospect asks for a feature that Braze does not currently support?
  9. Describe your hands-on experience with REST APIs and webhooks, and give a specific integration example relevant to customer messaging.
  10. How would you map a retail brand's omnichannel customer journey across email, push, SMS, and in-app channels inside Braze Canvas?
  11. A sales rep believes the prospect is ready to close. You believe there are unresolved technical blockers. How do you handle the disagreement?
  12. What metrics would you track to show a new customer is getting genuine value from Braze in their first three months post-launch?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioural question. The three examples below show how to tie technical detail to commercial impact.

Q: Tell us about a proof-of-concept you ran for a sceptical technical buyer.

*Situation:* A mid-size e-commerce company was evaluating whether Braze could replace their in-house messaging tool. The engineering lead was openly sceptical about SDK performance on low-end Android devices common in their user base.

*Task:* I needed to design a focused PoC that addressed his exact concern rather than deliver a generic product walkthrough.

*Action:* I scoped the PoC to a single use case (abandoned cart push notification) and set up a test environment with throttled network conditions to mirror their target devices. I instrumented the SDK and shared latency logs in real time with the engineering lead so he could see the data himself rather than take my word for it.

*Result:* He moved from openly sceptical to 'this works for our constraints' within two sessions, and the deal progressed to legal review the following week.

---

Q: A customer's push open rate dropped after SDK integration. Walk us through your debugging approach.

*Situation:* A customer reported a noticeable drop in push open rates three weeks after going live on Braze and assumed the SDK was broken.

*Task:* My job was to isolate the root cause quickly without making the customer feel blamed for a misconfiguration.

*Action:* I asked them to share their Canvas settings, segmentation filters, and send logs from the past two weeks. I spotted that their quiet hours setting was active but locked to the wrong timezone, meaning pushes were being silenced during peak engagement windows. I also checked their iOS provisional authorisation status, which had been misconfigured during a recent app update.

*Result:* Both issues were fixed in under an hour, open rates recovered on the next send cycle, and the customer's confidence in the integration was fully restored.

---

Q: A sales rep and you disagree on whether a prospect is ready to close. How do you handle it?

*Situation:* My sales partner was confident a fintech prospect was ready to sign. I had identified two open questions around PII handling that I felt could create onboarding problems after signature.

*Task:* I needed to raise the concern without undermining my sales partner or stalling a deal that might genuinely be ready.

*Action:* I requested a brief internal sync before the next customer call and framed the gaps as 'questions that will surface in onboarding anyway, so better to resolve them now.' We agreed on two direct questions to ask the prospect on the call to get written clarity before closing.

*Result:* The prospect answered both questions confidently on the call. The deal closed on schedule and onboarding started without any integration surprises.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Every story needs a Situation (context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you did, not what 'we' did), and Result (a concrete outcome, ideally tied to deal progress or customer success). Practice each story until you can tell it cleanly in under two minutes.

DEEP for technical walkthroughs. Define the problem clearly, Explore the possible causes or approaches, Execute by walking through your chosen path step by step, and Present the outcome plus what you would do differently next time. This structure shows methodical thinking without turning the interview into a lecture.

The 'So What' check for demo scenarios. After any technical explanation, ask yourself whether the interviewer knows why this matters commercially. If not, add one sentence connecting the feature to a business outcome. For example: 'Liquid templating saves the customer from maintaining dozens of separate message variants, which directly reduces the marketing team's workload.'

Discovery-first for demo role-plays. Braze interviewers often run a simulation where they play the prospect. Candidates who launch straight into features without asking discovery questions first typically score lower. Open with something like: 'Before I show you anything, can I understand what problem you are trying to solve and who the end user of these messages is?'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without jargon overload. You should be comfortable discussing Braze Canvas, Liquid templating, SDK event tracking, REST APIs, and webhook integrations. But interviewers also watch whether you can translate these concepts clearly to a non-technical audience without losing accuracy.

Commercial awareness. Solutions Engineers at Braze are part of the revenue team. Interviewers want evidence that you understand where you sit in the sales cycle and that you actively help deals move forward rather than waiting to be told what to do next.

Ownership and initiative. Strong candidates describe moments where they went beyond the brief: proactively identifying a customer risk, preparing a tailored PoC instead of a generic demo, or flagging a product gap before it became a post-sale support issue.

Clarity under pressure. The live demo exercise (which candidates typically report as part of later rounds) is partly a composure test. Staying calm, narrating your thinking, and recovering gracefully from an unexpected problem matters as much as technical accuracy.

Genuine curiosity and humility. Braze publicly describes their culture as one of building with heart and humility. Interviewers watch for candidates who are curious, collaborative with internal teams, and honest about what they do not yet know.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Build platform knowledge first. Explore Braze's public documentation and, if you can access a sandbox or trial environment, build something hands-on. Focus on Canvas Flow (the multi-step journey builder), Liquid templating syntax, and how the iOS and Android SDKs track custom events and attributes. Reading alone is not enough; build at least a simple abandoned cart or re-engagement flow so you can speak from direct experience.

Map your past experience to Braze scenarios. List five or six past projects where you ran a PoC, debugged an integration, or handled a technically sceptical buyer. For each, write a STAR story you can deliver in under two minutes. Practice saying these out loud, not just writing them down.

Run mock demos under live pressure. Ask a friend or colleague to play a sceptical marketing director while you demo a Braze Canvas. Practice your discovery questions, your feature walkthrough, and your recovery when the 'prospect' throws an unexpected objection. Candidates report that Braze interviewers specifically test how you handle live ambiguity.

Research Braze's public positioning. Read their recent product announcements and customer case studies, available on their public website. Knowing which verticals (retail, fintech, media) they serve helps you tailor your demo scenarios to the interviewer's world.

Know your numbers. Be ready to discuss the size of deals you have supported, the number of integrations you have handled, and what measurable outcomes improved for your customers. Interviewers often probe for specificity over general claims.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Demoing features without asking discovery questions. Jumping into a feature walkthrough before understanding the prospect's problem is one of the fastest ways to lose a Braze interview. Always lead with two or three focused discovery questions before opening Canvas.

Assuming the interviewer knows your past tools. Saying 'we used our in-house CDP' without explaining what it did leaves the interviewer guessing. Translate every past tool into what it achieved for the customer, not what it was called internally.

Focusing only on technical depth. Solutions Engineers are evaluated on commercial impact too. An answer that ends with 'the integration worked' is weaker than one that ends with 'the customer launched ahead of schedule and expanded their contract at renewal.'

Mixing up 'I' and 'we.' Interviewers want to understand your individual contribution. Use 'I' when describing your own actions and 'we' only for broader team outcomes. A story where everything is 'we did this' makes it impossible to assess your specific role.

Underestimating the live demo exercise. Candidates who treat the demo as a product knowledge test often miss the point. It is also a communication and composure test. Practice recovering from a broken flow or an unexpected question without losing your narrative thread.

Generic answers about passion for technology. Braze interviewers are experienced enough to spot filler. Ground every claim in a specific example from your own work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does the Braze Solutions Engineer process typically have?

Candidates typically report four to five rounds, starting with a recruiter screen and a hiring manager conversation, then moving into a technical deep-dive, a live demo or pitch exercise, and sometimes a final panel or senior leader interview. Round structure varies by region and team, so confirm the sequence with your recruiter early in the process. Do not assume a standard order until you have asked.

Do I need prior Braze product experience to apply?

Prior Braze experience helps but is not always required. Candidates report that interviewers care more about your ability to learn a complex SaaS platform quickly and your track record running technical pre-sales cycles. Building familiarity with Braze's public documentation and sandbox environment before your first interview gives you a real, visible advantage.

What technical skills matter most for this role?

Candidates consistently highlight REST APIs, webhooks, basic mobile SDK knowledge (iOS and Android), JSON, and familiarity with HTML and Liquid templating as the most important areas. SQL for audience segmentation also comes up. You do not need to be a full-stack developer, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation and explaining an integration end to end without hand-holding.

What salary can I expect for a Braze Solutions Engineer role in India?

Salary data specific to Braze India SE roles is limited at the time of writing. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and community forums for SE roles at enterprise SaaS companies at a comparable level vary widely based on experience, city, and negotiation. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi specifically for Braze India to find the most current community-reported numbers before your negotiation conversation.

Is there a take-home assignment in the Braze SE interview process?

Some candidates report a take-home scenario where they are asked to design a customer journey or write a brief technical proposal for a fictional prospect. Others go straight to a live demo exercise without a written assignment. Confirm with your recruiter whether a take-home is part of your specific process, as this varies by team and hiring region.

How should I handle the 'do you have any questions' moment at the end of an interview?

Ask questions that show you have done your research: for example, how the SE team collaborates with product on feature gap feedback, what the customer onboarding process looks like in the first months, or how deals are typically divided between the SE and the account executive. Avoid asking only about compensation or leave in early rounds. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest in the role, not just the job title.
