01 Overview

Overview

MoEngage is a fast-growing B2B SaaS customer engagement platform that helps consumer brands run personalised campaigns across push notifications, email, SMS, in-app messages, and WhatsApp. Clients span e-commerce, BFSI, edtech, and media. As of mid-2026, MoEngage has 32 open PM roles, making it one of the more active SaaS hirers in product management right now.

The PM team at MoEngage is typically organised around product pillars: campaign orchestration, analytics and insights, AI-powered features (the Sherpa suite), channel integrations, and platform or developer experience. Your interview panel will likely include a PM, a senior PM or director, and sometimes an engineering or design lead.

MoEngage PM interviews test three things hard: platform-level product thinking (you build for brands, not end consumers), comfort with engagement and retention metrics, and the ability to prioritise in a competitive SaaS market. Broad market data for PM roles in India is shown below.

  4. Walk me through how you would prioritise the next set of features for MoEngage's AI-driven segmentation engine.

  11. How do you measure whether a personalisation feature is actually working? What would a 'false positive' look like?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you reduce push notification opt-out rates on the MoEngage platform?

*Situation:* In my previous role at a consumer internet startup, our product used a third-party push notification tool. We noticed our app's opt-out rate was climbing and hurting retention numbers.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the investigation and ship a solution within one quarter.

*Action:* I first segmented users by notification frequency, time of day, and content category to find where opt-outs were highest. I ran a survey with a small sample of opted-out users. The clearest signal: users who received notifications above a certain weekly threshold were opting out at a much higher rate (notification fatigue is widely cited in industry surveys as a top driver of opt-outs). I redesigned our logic to cap frequency per user segment, introduced a 'quiet hours' preference, and A/B tested subject-line personalisation using browsing history.

*Result:* Over the following two months, our opt-out rate dropped measurably according to internal dashboards, and click-through on the notifications we did send improved. For MoEngage, I would frame this at the platform level: smarter throttling defaults for brands, a send-time optimisation model, and clear dashboards showing opt-out trends by campaign type.

---

Q: A large e-commerce client says their campaign conversion dropped after a recent SDK update. How do you investigate?

*Situation:* At a B2B analytics company I worked at, a similar escalation came in: a key client reported a drop in a core metric right after a platform update.

*Task:* I had to triage quickly because this was a strategic account and the client was considering escalating to their own leadership.

*Action:* I started with a timeline check: did the drop start exactly on the update date, or slightly before or after? I pulled event logs to check for a tracking gap (events not firing vs. events firing but not converting). I looped in engineering to see if the SDK update had changed any default event-batching behaviour or cookie handling. I also asked the client for their own analytics data to cross-check. The root cause turned out to be a changed default for event batching that delayed attribution, not an actual conversion problem.

*Result:* We shipped a hotfix within a few days, the client's reported numbers recovered, and we added a regression test for attribution integrity to our release checklist. For MoEngage, I would use the same structured approach: separate a data collection bug from an actual business metric drop before drawing any conclusions.

---

Q: How would you prioritise the next features for MoEngage's AI-driven segmentation engine?

*Situation:* At my last company, I owned the customer data platform's segmentation module and had a large backlog of feature requests from sales, clients, and internal teams.

*Task:* I needed to cut this to a short sprint roadmap for the quarter.

*Action:* I used RICE scoring (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) but adapted 'Reach' to mean how many MoEngage clients would use the feature, not end users. I interviewed several enterprise clients and a few mid-market clients to calibrate impact scores. I also reviewed support tickets and churn reasons to find pain points actively costing retention. Features that addressed a known churn reason received a multiplier on impact score.

*Result:* We shipped the top-ranked features on time. One of them (a self-serve lookalike audience builder) became one of the most-used new additions that quarter according to product analytics. I would apply the same client-informed RICE approach at MoEngage, since the platform's value is directly tied to how easily brand teams can create precise segments without developer support.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'improve a product' questions (most common at MoEngage): Use the CIRCLES method as a skeleton: Comprehend the situation, Identify the customer, Report the customer's needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarize. Always anchor the 'customer' step by naming both the brand (MoEngage's direct client) and the brand's end user, since decisions often need to balance both.

For metric or north-star questions: Start by defining what the product is trying to do for users, then pick a metric that moves only when real value is delivered (not a vanity metric). For MoEngage, good north star candidates are things like 'campaigns delivering a positive ROI for the client' rather than 'total campaigns sent.' Be ready to explain leading vs. lagging indicators.

For prioritisation questions: RICE works well and MoEngage interviewers recognise it. Adapt it to a B2B context by defining 'Reach' as number of clients or client MAU affected, not end-consumer reach. If you prefer a simpler framework, a 2x2 of Impact vs. Effort is acceptable as long as you define the axes clearly before using them.

For root cause or analytics questions: Use a structured funnel approach. Break the metric into its components (for example: campaign sent, delivered, opened, clicked, converted), identify where the drop-off occurs, then generate hypotheses for each layer. Always separate 'is the data correct' from 'is the product broken' before jumping to solutions.

For build-buy-partner questions: Frame your answer around three things: strategic fit (is this core to MoEngage's differentiation?), time-to-market (how fast does the client need this?), and total cost of ownership. MoEngage interviewers value candidates who think about platform ecosystem effects, since many features involve third-party integrations.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Platform-level product thinking. MoEngage builds for brands, not consumers. Interviewers want to see that you understand the 'two-sided' nature: your product decisions affect both the brand's marketing team (who configure campaigns) and the brand's end users (who receive them). Candidates who only think in consumer product metrics often get tripped up here.

Data fluency. MoEngage is an analytics-heavy product. You should be comfortable defining metrics, spotting data quality issues, and explaining A/B test design. Candidates report that at least one round focuses heavily on metrics and analytical thinking.

Opinionated prioritisation. Interviewers want to see you make a call, not just list trade-offs endlessly. State a recommendation clearly, explain your reasoning, and defend it when pushed. 'It depends' is not an answer; 'It depends on X, and here is my bet' is.

Customer empathy with a B2B lens. MoEngage's clients are paying customers with SLAs and procurement cycles. Interviewers value candidates who understand enterprise client pain points: ease of integration, reliability, ROI measurement, and speed of onboarding.

Product knowledge. You do not need to have used MoEngage directly, but you should understand how customer engagement platforms work, what push notification deliverability means, and why segmentation accuracy matters to a marketing team. Showing this knowledge signals you will ramp up quickly.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product.
Sign up for MoEngage's free trial or watch their official product walkthrough videos. Understand the core modules: flows (journey builder), campaigns, analytics, and Sherpa (AI features). Read a few of their public case studies to understand what problems their clients bring to them. Note the specific language they use for features, since mirroring it in your answers signals familiarity.

Weeks 1 and 2: Know the domain.
Read up on multi-channel marketing, push notification best practices, and customer lifecycle stages (acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, referral). Understand what terms like 'deliverability,' 'segmentation,' 'A/B testing at campaign level,' and 'churn prediction' mean in practice for a brand's marketing team.

Week 2: Build your story bank.
Prepare several STAR stories covering: a product you launched, a data-driven decision you made, a time you pushed back on a stakeholder, a cross-functional challenge, and a product failure you learned from. Map each story to the types of questions MoEngage typically asks.

Weeks 2 and 3: Practice frameworks.
Work through a few product improvement questions using the CIRCLES method. Time yourself and aim for a clear structure within the first minute of answering. Practise metrics questions by picking any SaaS product at random, defining its north star metric, and drilling down into sub-metrics.

Before each round: Review MoEngage's latest blog posts, press releases, and LinkedIn posts. They often hint at where the product is headed, and knowing recent launches signals genuine interest.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating MoEngage like a consumer app company. Their product is B2B SaaS. Answers that frame the 'user' purely as a smartphone end-consumer will miss the mark. Always bring in the brand's marketing team as a core user persona.

Vague metric definitions. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Interviewers want a specific metric, a formula, and an explanation of why it measures real value rather than just activity.

Jumping to solutions before diagnosing. For case and root cause questions, candidates who skip straight to 'I would build X' without establishing what the actual problem is tend to be filtered out early. Always spend time clarifying the goal and the data before proposing anything.

Ignoring the competitive context. MoEngage operates in a crowded market. If you are asked about a feature decision, acknowledging what competitors do (and why MoEngage's approach should differ or match) shows strategic thinking that generic frameworks alone cannot.

Not knowing your own stories well enough. Candidates sometimes prepare stories but stumble when interviewers probe deeper ('What exactly did you do vs. your team?', 'What was the number?'). Know each story at a level of detail where follow-up questions feel comfortable, not threatening.

Forgetting to ask clarifying questions. For open-ended design questions, jumping straight into an answer without clarifying the goal, constraints, and target user is a red flag. Interviewers value structured thinking from the very first question you ask back.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does MoEngage typically have for PM roles?

Candidates report the process typically runs across several rounds. A commonly reported sequence includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager discussion, one or two product sense rounds, a metrics or analytical round, and a final leadership discussion. MoEngage does not publicly publish a fixed interview structure, so confirm the format with your recruiter after clearing the first screen, as it can vary by level and team.

Is there a take-home assignment or case study?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home case study, typically a product teardown or a metrics analysis exercise, while others go straight into live rounds. When a take-home appears, candidates report it is usually due within a couple of days. Be ready to present your findings in a follow-up discussion, since interviewers will probe your assumptions and the trade-offs you made.

What salary can I expect as a PM at MoEngage?

MoEngage does not publicly disclose salary bands in detail. Based on broader market data, mid-level PM roles in India are commonly cited in the 24-40 LPA range, while senior PM roles are commonly cited in the 40-60 LPA range. Levels.fyi and Glassdoor have some MoEngage-specific data points, though sample sizes are small. Factor in ESOPs, which are typically a meaningful part of compensation at growth-stage SaaS companies.

How important is prior SaaS or B2B product experience?

Candidates with prior B2B SaaS PM experience tend to ramp faster and often clear product sense rounds more easily, because platform-level thinking already feels natural to them. That said, MoEngage has reportedly hired PMs from consumer internet backgrounds who could demonstrate strong analytical skills and a genuine willingness to learn the B2B context. If you come from a consumer background, invest extra preparation time in understanding enterprise client needs and SaaS metrics like NRR and time-to-value.

Does MoEngage hire freshers or APMs for PM roles?

MoEngage occasionally opens Associate PM roles aimed at early-career candidates, often from engineering or MBA backgrounds. Knok currently tracks 32 open PM roles at MoEngage across levels. Check each job description carefully for experience requirements before applying, since mid-level and senior roles are more common than APM openings at any given time.

How long does the full interview process take end to end?

Candidates report the end-to-end process typically takes a few weeks from first contact to offer, though timelines can vary depending on the team's hiring urgency and the time of year. If you have a competing offer with a hard deadline, candidates report that flagging it early to the recruiter often helps compress the schedule. Do not wait until the last day to mention a deadline.
