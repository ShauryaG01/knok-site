01 Overview

Overview

SolarSquare Energy is one of India's leading rooftop solar companies, helping homeowners and businesses switch to clean energy through end-to-end installation, financing, and after-sales service. The PM role here is genuinely different from a typical SaaS position: you are working across a physical product (solar panels and inverters), a financial product (EMI and subsidy navigation), and a digital product (customer app and installer tools) all at once.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 8 open Product Manager roles at SolarSquare Energy, making this an active hiring window. Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds: an HR screening call, one or two case and product-thinking rounds with a senior PM or hiring manager, and a final round with leadership. Rounds are rarely given formal names, so treat every call as a chance to show structured thinking alongside real business context.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from SolarSquare Energy PM interviews. The pattern is clear: interviewers want to see how you handle complexity at the intersection of physical delivery, consumer trust, and data.

  1. How would you redesign the post-sale customer experience after a homeowner confirms a solar installation?
  2. A significant portion of leads are dropping off at the financing or government subsidy explanation stage. How would you diagnose and fix this?
  3. How would you prioritize a feature roadmap for SolarSquare's homeowner app, which tracks solar generation and bill savings?
  4. SolarSquare is planning to expand into Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. What product changes would you make to support that growth?
  5. What is the single most important metric you would use to measure the health of SolarSquare's installation funnel, and why?
  6. Design a referral product for existing SolarSquare customers. Walk the interviewer through your full thinking.
  7. A low-cost competitor has entered the rooftop solar market. How does product strategy respond without competing purely on price?
  8. How would you build a mobile tool for field installation technicians to reduce errors and speed up turnaround?
  9. Tell us about a time you had to align engineering, sales, and operations teams on a single product decision.
  10. What would you change about how SolarSquare communicates expected savings and payback period to a first-time buyer?
  11. Describe a consumer product you genuinely admire. What one idea from it would you bring into SolarSquare's customer experience?
  12. How would you measure whether SolarSquare's in-app education content is actually changing customer behaviour after installation?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioral questions. The three examples below are written for the kind of work SolarSquare PMs typically handle.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you had to align multiple teams on a difficult product decision.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were building an EMI calculator feature for a fintech product. The sales team wanted the tool to show the most attractive (lowest) EMI first. The compliance team flagged that displaying only the best-case number could mislead customers.

*Task:* My job was to ship a version that satisfied both sales performance goals and compliance requirements, without delaying the launch.

*Action:* I put together a short async doc laying out three options with tradeoffs, tagged both teams for input, and set a two-day decision window. I also pulled recent user research showing that customers who saw a range of EMI options converted better because they felt more in control of the decision.

*Result:* We launched with a 'best to standard' EMI range view. Conversion held steady, compliance signed off without escalation, and the data point I surfaced became a standing input for future finance-feature decisions.

---

Q: How would you improve a customer-facing metric that was declining?

*Situation:* At a previous role, our post-installation satisfaction score dropped noticeably over one quarter and leadership wanted a clear diagnosis fast.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the investigation and present a fix plan within three weeks.

*Action:* I segmented the score by city and by installer team to find where the drop was concentrated. The problem was almost entirely in installations completed after a specific date, pointing to a change in how handover documentation was being shared. I spoke with several affected customers and confirmed they felt 'left in the dark' after the work was done. I then worked with operations to add a WhatsApp-based post-install checklist that walked customers through what to expect in the first month.

*Result:* The score recovered to its earlier level within two months. More importantly, we now had a repeatable playbook for diagnosing score drops by segment rather than in aggregate.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to make a product decision with very limited data.

*Situation:* We were about to launch a new feature for small business owners but had almost no behavioral data on how they used the existing product, because that segment was brand new.

*Task:* I needed to choose between two design approaches with a two-week deadline and no time for a full research cycle.

*Action:* I ran five short calls with users in three days, focusing on one core question: what does a successful week look like for your business? I mapped the answers to both design options, found that one clearly matched the mental model these users already had, and documented my reasoning so the team could revisit if post-launch data contradicted it.

*Result:* We launched with the choice backed by qualitative signal. Post-launch usage data validated the direction, and the call notes became a foundation for a proper research sprint the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and feature questions, move from user to problem to solution to measurement. Start by naming the user clearly: is it the homeowner, the field installer, or an internal operations team? Then define the core problem in one sentence before moving to solutions. SolarSquare interviewers reward candidates who pause to ask 'who exactly is struggling here' before listing features.

For metric and funnel questions, lead with your north star metric, then show you understand what sits upstream and downstream of it. For a company like SolarSquare, the funnel has both digital steps (lead form, app activation, document uploads) and physical steps (site survey, installation, handover). A strong answer names both sides of that chain.

For strategy and competitive questions, use a simple jobs-to-be-done lens: what is the customer hiring SolarSquare to do, and is a competitor threatening that specific job or a different one? Candidates who frame competitive threats around a concrete customer need tend to perform better than those who speak abstractly about market share.

For behavioral questions, STAR is your default. Keep Situation and Task short (two to three sentences each) and spend most of your time on Action and Result. SolarSquare interviewers particularly look for cross-functional collaboration stories, so make sure your Action section describes how you worked with other teams, not just what you personally executed.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Comfort with physical-plus-digital complexity. SolarSquare's product is not purely software. A panel gets installed on a roof, a government subsidy gets filed through a portal, and a customer checks their savings on an app. Interviewers want to see that you understand this full chain and do not treat the role like a pure SaaS PM position.

Deep customer empathy for a high-stakes purchase. Buying solar is a large, infrequent decision for most homeowners. It involves trusting a brand they may not have heard of before, navigating government subsidy processes, and committing to a multi-year financial decision. Candidates who show they understand this emotional and financial weight stand out clearly.

Data mindset without data worship. SolarSquare is still in a high-growth phase, which means clean data is not always available for every decision. Interviewers value candidates who make principled calls with limited information and can articulate when they would invest in better data versus when they would ship and learn.

Cross-functional credibility. PM roles here sit at the junction of sales, operations, engineering, and customer success. Interviewers look for evidence that you can build trust with non-product teams, not just write good specs.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the business.
Explore SolarSquare's website, read app store reviews from real customers, and look at any publicly available press coverage to map what a customer experiences from lead inquiry to post-installation. Try to identify two or three friction points you would address as a PM. Read broadly about the rooftop solar market in India, including government subsidy schemes and state-level net metering policies, because these topics come up regularly in case discussions.

Week 2: Practice the question types.
Pick three questions from the 'Most Asked Questions' section above and write out full structured answers. For case questions, time yourself: aim to frame a clear answer in two to three minutes without rambling. For behavioral questions, identify four or five stories from your past work that each cover a different theme (cross-functional alignment, data-driven decision, low-data decision, customer insight driven change).

Week 3: Mock interviews and refinement.
Do at least two mock interviews with someone who can give honest feedback. After each session, review where you spent too long on setup versus action. Prepare two or three specific questions for your interviewers about the team's current roadmap and how PM success is measured. Strong candidates always come with good questions.

On the day: Be ready to sketch a simple funnel or framework on a shared screen if the round involves a case discussion. Candidates report that showing your thinking visually, even roughly, helps interviewers follow along and offer more useful prompts.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure software PM role. The most common feedback candidates report is coming in with a SaaS mindset and ignoring the physical delivery and financing layers. Every answer should acknowledge that SolarSquare's complexity is partly operational, not just digital.

Jumping to solutions before defining the user. In case and design questions, many candidates start listing features within the first minute. SolarSquare interviewers reward candidates who pause, clearly define who the user is and what the core problem is, and only then move to solutions.

Vague cross-functional stories. In behavioral rounds, saying 'I worked with the engineering team' is not enough. Interviewers want to hear specifically what the disagreement was, how you built alignment, and what the outcome looked like.

Not knowing the product. Candidates who have not explored SolarSquare's app or website before the interview come across as unprepared. Spend at least an hour going through their customer-facing surfaces before any round.

Ignoring the Tier 2 and rural context. SolarSquare's growth is increasingly outside the major metros. If every answer assumes a Bangalore or Mumbai customer, you may miss a central part of what the company is actively building toward.

Asking no questions at the end. Interviewers notice when a candidate has nothing to ask. Prepare specific, informed questions that show you have researched the company and are genuinely curious about the team's work.
