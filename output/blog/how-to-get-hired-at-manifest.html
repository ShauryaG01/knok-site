Hiring Overview

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The exact stages can vary by role, team, and the interviewer's preference, but the process at companies of Manifest's size typically follows a pattern like this. Use this as a guide, not a guarantee.

Stage 1: Application and Resume Screen
A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume to check whether your background matches the role. A well-tailored resume that mirrors the job description in language and substance gets through this stage faster. With 8 open positions currently listed, competition exists but the hiring team is actively looking to fill seats.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR Call
A short introductory call to confirm your interest, notice period, location, and salary expectations. Have a clear two-minute career summary ready, and know the specific reason you want to join Manifest. Vague answers here can stall your process.

Stage 3: Technical or Domain Assessment
For engineering roles, this is commonly a take-home assignment or a live coding session focused on practical problem-solving. Product and operations candidates often face a case study or are asked to walk through a piece of past work in detail. The emphasis is usually on how you approach the problem, not just whether you arrive at the right answer.

Stage 4: System Design or Deep-Dive Round
Senior candidates typically face a system design or architecture discussion. Expect questions about tradeoffs, past decisions, and what you would do differently. Junior candidates may skip this stage or face a lighter version of it.

Stage 5: Culture and Fit Round
A conversation with a senior leader or founding team member to check whether your working style aligns with the team. Expect questions about how you handle ambiguity, prioritise competing demands, and work through disagreement. Specific stories from past experience land much better than general statements here.

Stage 6: Offer and Reference Check
If all rounds go well, Manifest typically moves to an offer. Reference checks may happen before or after the verbal offer depending on the role and urgency.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Ownership and initiative. In a small team, there is no one behind you to pick up what you drop. Interviewers want evidence that you have taken responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks. Come with specific examples where you identified a problem and drove it to resolution without waiting for direction.

Clear thinking under pressure. Whether it is a coding problem or a case study, the way you structure your thinking matters as much as the final answer. Walk through your reasoning out loud. Interviewers at lean companies value clarity because team discussions move fast and ambiguity is expensive.

Strong communication skills. You will work across functions, which means explaining technical ideas to non-technical people and vice versa. Show that you can do this in writing and in speech during the interview itself.

Genuine interest in the company's work. Candidates who have done their research stand out immediately. Knowing what Manifest builds, who their customers are, and what challenges the product is solving signals that you chose this application carefully.

Adaptability. Priorities shift at growth-stage companies. People who can adjust without losing momentum are the ones who thrive. If you have examples of navigating unexpected change at work, keep them ready.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Start with deep research on Manifest. Read everything publicly available, including blog posts, LinkedIn updates, product descriptions, and any press coverage. The more specific your understanding of what the company does and why it matters, the better your answers will land across every round.

Prepare your 'why Manifest' answer early. This question comes up in almost every stage. Tie your answer to something concrete about their product, their mission, or a specific problem you find interesting. 'Good growth opportunity' is too thin to stand out.

For engineering roles, sharpen the right skills. Practice data structures, algorithms, and system design at a level appropriate to your target role. Focus on problems that test practical reasoning, since that is what most growth-stage companies prioritise over abstract puzzles.

Build a story bank from your past experience. Prepare four to five specific examples using a simple situation, action, and result format. Cover ownership, handling conflict, working under pressure, learning from a mistake, and a time you influenced without authority. These stories will carry you through multiple rounds.

Prepare sharp questions for your interviewers. Asking about what success looks like in the first three months, or what the team's biggest unsolved challenge is, signals engagement and helps you evaluate the role honestly.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Manifest operates with the pace and intensity common to companies focused on growth. Teams are small, decisions move fast, and the impact of individual contributions is visible quickly. This environment suits people who find that energising, and can feel overwhelming for those used to large, process-heavy organisations.

Collaboration over hierarchy. Titles matter less than ideas at companies of this scale. You are expected to have an opinion, back it up with reasoning, and update your view when presented with better information. Passive participation is noticed and rarely rewarded.

Speed with accountability. The expectation is that you ship, learn, and iterate, but you own the quality of what goes out under your name. There is little patience for diffused ownership when something goes wrong.

Transparency in communication. Smaller teams typically share context broadly, and this tends to be true at Manifest. Leadership communicates openly about company direction and challenges, which means you get real information but also real responsibility to act on it.

Growth through breadth. Because the team is lean, your role will likely expand beyond its original description over time. This creates fast career growth for people who embrace the stretch, and friction for people who prefer clearly bounded responsibilities.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the full Manifest interview process take from application to offer?

Most candidates move from application to offer in two to four weeks, though this varies depending on scheduling availability on both sides. Manifest tends to move faster when a role is urgent, so responding promptly to recruiter messages and completing any assignments without delay keeps your process on track. If you have not heard back within a week of completing a round, a polite follow-up email to the recruiter is appropriate.

Is there a coding round in the Manifest interview process?

For engineering roles, a technical assessment is standard. This is commonly a take-home assignment or a live coding session, depending on the team. The focus tends to be on practical problem-solving rather than purely algorithmic challenges. For product, operations, or other non-engineering roles, a case study or a deep-dive into past work is more likely than a coding test.

What salary does Manifest offer?

Manifest has not published official salary bands publicly, so precise figures are difficult to verify independently. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list compensation data submitted by employees at similar companies, and those are worth checking to build a realistic range before your HR call. Going into salary discussions with a specific range in mind, anchored to market data for your role level and experience, is more effective than waiting to hear a number first.

Does Manifest hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

With 8 roles open as of July 2026, the mix spans different seniority levels. Growth-stage companies like Manifest typically hire freshers for entry-level and associate positions while bringing in experienced professionals for senior and specialist roles. Read each job description carefully before applying, as requirements and expectations differ significantly from one opening to the next.

How important is the culture round and how do I prepare for it?

The culture round carries significant weight at a small company because one misaligned hire affects the whole team. Interviewers are looking for authentic examples of how you have actually worked, not textbook answers. Prepare specific stories about how you handled conflict, ambiguity, and failure. Trying to sound ideal without grounding your answers in real experience tends to be obvious and counterproductive at this stage.

How can I improve my chances if I do not have a referral at Manifest?

Search LinkedIn for current Manifest employees and send a short, specific message explaining your interest in a particular role and asking if they would be willing to pass along your resume. A referral moves your application higher in the review queue at most companies. Even a second-degree connection who can make an introduction is worth reaching out to. A well-tailored resume and a strong cover note close much of the gap when no referral is available.
