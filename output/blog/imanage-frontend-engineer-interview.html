02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these topics coming up frequently in iManage Frontend Engineer interviews. The questions reflect their enterprise product context: large file handling, security, performance, and reliability.

  1. Walk us through how you would build a document viewer component that handles large PDFs and Office files inside a browser.
  2. iManage Work is used by legal professionals who need fast, reliable search. How have you optimised search or filtering on the frontend?
  3. Describe your experience with React and TypeScript in an enterprise application. What patterns do you rely on for state management at scale?
  4. How do you approach accessibility (WCAG compliance) when building interfaces for professional users who may rely on assistive technology?
  5. iManage handles sensitive legal documents. How have you built frontend features with security in mind (XSS prevention, token handling, content security policy)?
  6. Tell us about a time you improved the performance of a slow frontend feature. What did you measure, and what was the outcome?
  7. How do you handle real-time updates or collaborative editing in a document management context?
  8. Describe your approach to writing and maintaining unit and integration tests for complex UI components.
  9. Have you worked with REST or GraphQL APIs in a document-heavy application? How did you handle large payloads or pagination?
  10. iManage serves enterprise clients who cannot tolerate downtime. How do you approach error boundaries, graceful degradation, and fallback UIs?
  11. How do you keep a large frontend codebase maintainable as the team and feature set grow?
  12. Tell us about a time you disagreed with a design or technical decision. How did you handle it, and what was the outcome?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you improved the performance of a slow frontend feature.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the document list page became very slow when a user had thousands of files in a single folder.

*Task:* My task was to reduce the load time so users could navigate their files quickly without frustration or switching to a different tool.

*Action:* I profiled the component using Chrome DevTools and found we were rendering all items at once. I replaced the list with a virtualised scroll using react-window, added memoisation to prevent unnecessary re-renders, and moved the data fetching to a pagination model so we only loaded what was visible on screen.

*Result:* Initial render dropped significantly, and user complaints about slowness disappeared in the following sprint review. The virtualisation approach was later adopted as a standard in the component library.

---

Q: How have you built accessible interfaces for professional users?

*Situation:* Our enterprise client, an accounting firm, reported that screen reader users on their team could not navigate our document upload modal.

*Task:* I needed to make the modal fully accessible without rebuilding the entire component from scratch, and without disrupting the release timeline.

*Action:* I audited the component using axe and NVDA, added proper ARIA roles and labels, ensured focus was trapped inside the modal when open, and added keyboard shortcuts for common actions. I also wrote a test using jest-axe so regressions would be caught automatically in CI.

*Result:* The client confirmed the issue was resolved. The fix was added to our design system as a standard so all future modals would follow the same pattern, making the team faster on accessibility going forward.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you disagreed with a technical decision.

*Situation:* My team decided to introduce Redux for state management in a new feature that was relatively self-contained and did not share state with the rest of the app.

*Task:* I felt this would add unnecessary boilerplate and slow down development, so I needed to make the case for a simpler approach without creating conflict.

*Action:* I put together a quick comparison: a React Context plus useReducer solution versus the Redux setup. I showed the team how Context would reduce boilerplate, be easier to test, and keep the feature isolated. I listened to their concerns about future scalability and proposed a clear migration path if the state ever grew complex.

*Result:* The team agreed to use Context for this feature. It shipped cleanly, the code stayed easy to maintain, and we never needed to migrate, which validated the initial call.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions, use the STAR structure: Situation (set the scene briefly), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (the steps you personally took), Result (the outcome, ideally with a business or user impact). Keep the Situation and Task short. Spend most of your answer on Action and Result.

For 'how would you build X' questions, follow this structure: state your requirements and constraints first (file size, browser support, number of users), then sketch the component or architecture, then talk through performance and edge cases. iManage interviewers typically want to see that you understand the 'why' behind your choices, not just the implementation.

For trade-off questions, name at least two options, explain what each costs and what it gains, then state which you would choose and why. Avoid presenting one option as obviously correct. Enterprise engineering is full of genuine trade-offs, and showing you can reason through them signals maturity.

For questions about past failures or disagreements, be direct. Describe what went wrong or what you disagreed with, what you did, and what you learned. Candidates who gloss over mistakes or deflect blame do not build trust with interviewers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

iManage builds software for legal and financial professionals, so interviewers typically look for engineers who understand enterprise requirements: reliability, security, and performance at scale.

Strong technical fundamentals. Deep React and TypeScript knowledge is expected. Interviewers want to see you can build components that hold up under real usage, not just pass a quick demo.

Security awareness. iManage handles sensitive client documents. Candidates who can speak to XSS prevention, secure token handling, and content security policy stand out. You do not need to be a security specialist, but you should not be caught off guard by the topic.

Accessibility and compliance. Enterprise clients, especially in legal and financial services, often have strict accessibility requirements. Knowing WCAG basics and being able to implement them is a practical differentiator.

Clear communication. Candidates report that iManage interviewers pay close attention to how you explain technical decisions. If you can frame a trade-off clearly for a non-technical stakeholder, that matters as much as the technical answer itself.

Honesty about gaps. Candidates report that interviewers appreciate directness when you have not worked with a specific technology. Explain how you would approach learning it and draw on analogous experience you already have.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core skills review. Go through React patterns you use daily: hooks, context, lazy loading, error boundaries, and performance optimisation. Practise explaining them out loud, as if to a colleague. Review TypeScript generics and utility types. Pick two or three past projects that had clear technical challenges and write out STAR summaries for each.

Week 2: iManage-specific context. Spend time on iManage's website and recent blog posts to understand their product, their clients (law firms and accountancies), and what problems they solve. Look for any engineering content or open-source contributions. Prepare answers for document management scenarios: large file handling, search, real-time collaboration, and security.

Week 3: Practice and polish. Do live coding practice on a platform you are comfortable with, focusing on component design and performance problems. Review accessibility basics (WCAG AA and ARIA) and be ready to discuss them. Read up on frontend security patterns. Do at least one mock interview with a peer or a practice platform.

On the day: Join the call a few minutes early. Share your screen before they ask. Think aloud during technical problems so the interviewer can follow your reasoning. Prepare two or three genuine questions to ask about the team, the product, or the engineering culture.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Skipping the 'why'. Candidates who only describe what they built, without explaining why they made those choices, do not stand out. Always connect your technical decisions to a business or user need.
  1. Ignoring the enterprise context. iManage's users are legal and financial professionals. Generic answers about consumer app development miss the point. Frame your experience around reliability, compliance, and working with large organisations.
  1. Staying silent during coding rounds. Interviewers want to follow your thinking. A quiet screen share is a missed opportunity. Talk through your approach before writing a single line of code.
  1. Underselling testing experience. Enterprise teams care deeply about test coverage. If you have written unit, integration, or end-to-end tests, bring it up even if they do not ask directly.
  1. Not preparing questions to ask. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team, the product roadmap, or the engineering culture.
  1. Overcomplicating simple problems. If the question is straightforward, a clean simple solution beats an over-engineered one. Show you can judge the right level of complexity for the problem at hand.

How many rounds does the iManage Frontend Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final round with senior engineers or a hiring manager. iManage's frontend work typically centres on React and TypeScript. Candidates report questions around state management, performance optimisation, and component design. Familiarity with REST APIs, browser security basics (XSS, content security policy), and accessibility standards is also commonly mentioned in interview feedback.

How competitive is the iManage hiring process for Frontend Engineers?

iManage currently has 43 open Frontend Engineer roles, which points to active hiring across multiple teams rather than a highly selective single funnel.
