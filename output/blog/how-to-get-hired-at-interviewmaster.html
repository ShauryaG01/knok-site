01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

InterviewMaster is in the business of helping people crack interviews, which means they hold their own hiring bar high. As of July 2026, 16 open roles are listed at InterviewMaster across functions like content, product, engineering, and sales. Whether you are a freshly minted graduate or a working professional looking for a career shift, understanding how InterviewMaster hires can give you a real edge.

The company sits at the intersection of edtech and career coaching, so they value people who can walk the talk. They want candidates who understand the job market, communicate clearly, and genuinely care about learner outcomes. Roles span full-time positions in curriculum design, software development, sales, and operations.

If you are preparing to apply, read on for a stage-by-stage breakdown of the interview process, what they look for in candidates, and how to put your best foot forward.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at InterviewMaster typically runs through four to five stages, though the exact sequence can vary by role and team.

Application and Resume Screening: After you apply, a recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume. For content or coaching roles, they pay close attention to writing samples or teaching experience. For tech roles, your GitHub profile or portfolio matters. Turnaround on screening commonly takes one to two weeks.

Recruiter Call: If your resume clears the first filter, expect a short call with a recruiter or HR. This covers your background, why you want to join, and basic role expectations. It is also your chance to ask about the team structure and growth path.

Technical or Functional Round: This is the core evaluation. For engineering roles, expect coding problems and system design discussions. For content or coaching roles, you may be asked to create a sample lesson plan, review a mock interview, or critique a piece of candidate advice. Some teams send a take-home assignment first, so be prepared for either format.

Managerial or Panel Round: A senior manager or cross-functional panel interviews you next. Expect behavioural questions using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result), plus deeper dives into your domain knowledge. For customer-facing roles, they may role-play a live interaction to see how you handle pressure.

HR and Offer Discussion: The final step covers compensation, joining timelines, and any role-specific expectations. Offers at edtech companies vary widely across experience levels, so use Glassdoor or levels.fyi to benchmark before you negotiate.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

InterviewMaster looks for people who combine domain expertise with a learner-first mindset.

Communication skills sit at the top of the list. Because the product is built around helping people express themselves better in interviews, everyone at the company, not just coaches, is expected to write and speak with clarity. Vague or jargon-heavy answers in your interview will work against you.

Product empathy matters a lot. They want people who have personally felt the frustration of job searching, whether as a fresher or as someone switching careers mid-journey. That lived experience shapes better content, better tools, and better sales conversations.

Ownership and self-direction are valued across all teams. Startups in this space move fast, and managers prefer candidates who can pick up a brief and run with it rather than waiting for step-by-step guidance. Be ready to give concrete examples of projects you drove independently.

Data awareness is a plus for almost every role. Whether it is tracking course completion rates or monitoring which job categories are trending, comfort with numbers and basic analysis sets candidates apart from those with equal qualifications.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product deeply before your interview. Sign up for the free tier or explore the platform as a user. Be ready to speak about one feature you found useful and one you would improve. This shows genuine interest and critical thinking, two things interviewers notice immediately.

Prepare your STAR stories early. Behavioural rounds are a near-certainty. Have three to four strong examples ready that cover: leading a project under pressure, handling a difficult stakeholder, learning a new skill quickly, and a time you failed and recovered. Generic stories that could apply to any company will not impress here.

For content or coaching roles, bring samples. A short video, a written explainer, or even a mock interview you recorded can do more than any resume line. If you do not have existing samples, create something specific to the role you are applying for before the interview.

For tech roles, practise mock interviews out loud. A company in the interview-prep space will notice immediately if you struggle under interview pressure. Use platforms focused on live mock interviews to sharpen your performance before the real thing. Silent LeetCode grind alone is not enough.

Ask thoughtful questions. Good questions about their learner success metrics, their content roadmap, or how they measure coach effectiveness signal that you think like an insider, not just an applicant.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

InterviewMaster's culture is shaped by the problem it solves: helping people land jobs in a tough market. That creates a team that is broadly mission-driven and empathetic toward people in career transition.

Pace is startup-like. Teams are lean, decisions are made quickly, and priorities can shift as market trends change. People who enjoy ambiguity and can adapt tend to thrive here more than those who prefer rigid structures and fixed processes.

Learning is baked in. Because the company teaches interview skills for a living, there is a natural culture of feedback, iteration, and skill-building. Internal knowledge-sharing and peer coaching are commonly cited as part of the work culture at edtech companies of this type, according to industry surveys.

Collaboration across functions is the norm. Content teams work closely with product and tech to build assessment tools. Sales teams feed learner insights back to curriculum designers. Expect your role to touch other functions regularly, so being a good collaborator matters as much as being a subject expert.

Frequently asked

How many jobs are currently open at InterviewMaster?

As of July 2026, 16 open roles are listed at InterviewMaster. These span functions like engineering, content, sales, and operations. The number changes regularly, so checking a live job aggregator or the company's own careers page gives you the freshest picture before you apply.

How long does the InterviewMaster hiring process take end to end?

The full process typically takes two to four weeks from application to offer, based on patterns commonly seen at edtech startups of this size. Timelines can stretch if the role is senior or if the team is running parallel searches. Following up politely after each round is perfectly acceptable and shows continued interest.

Do I need a background in HR or recruitment to work at InterviewMaster?

Not necessarily. Many roles, especially in content, product, and engineering, do not require HR experience. What matters more is that you understand the job search experience from the candidate's side. Having personally navigated a tough job hunt is often seen as a genuine asset at companies that serve job seekers.

Is there a coding test for tech roles?

For engineering roles, a coding assessment is very likely, either as a take-home problem or a live session. The difficulty level at edtech companies of this size is commonly cited as moderate to medium-hard in industry surveys. Practising data structures, algorithms, and basic system design will serve you well before you go in.

How should I negotiate my salary at InterviewMaster?

Come to the offer discussion with a number backed by market data. Glassdoor and levels.fyi are good starting points for benchmarking roles in the edtech and SaaS space in India. Focus on the total package, including learning budgets, work flexibility, and equity if offered, not just the fixed component.
