01 Overview

Overview

Fortive is a global industrial technology company with brands including Fluke, Tektronix, Gordian, and ServiceChannel. A Solutions Engineer here sits at the intersection of pre-sales consulting, technical demos, and customer success, working closely with account executives to win and retain enterprise customers.

As of July 2026, Fortive has 38 open roles on knok jobradar. The Solutions Engineer title is in strong demand across India, with 1,270 openings across the broader market. Bangalore leads with 55 openings, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), and Pune (12).

Fortive runs on the Fortive Business System (FBS), a continuous improvement framework influenced by lean manufacturing. FBS shapes how teams run meetings, handle escalations, and measure progress. It comes up in almost every Fortive interview regardless of role. Candidates report that the hiring process typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two competency or technical interviews, a demo or presentation round, and a final conversation with a senior manager. The exact steps vary by business segment, so ask your recruiter early.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

  1. Walk us through how you prepare and run a technical demo for a skeptical enterprise customer.
  2. Describe a time you turned a customer's vague business problem into a concrete technical proposal.
  3. How familiar are you with the Fortive Business System? Give an example of applying lean or continuous improvement thinking in your work.
  4. Tell us about a deal you lost. What did you learn, and what changed in your process afterward?
  5. How do you manage your workload when multiple sales cycles are all in the technical evaluation phase at the same time?
  6. Describe your experience with Fortive's product areas: test and measurement equipment, industrial safety, or enterprise facility management software.
  7. How do you handle a gap between what your product can do and what the customer actually needs?
  8. Walk us through a proof-of-concept you led from first customer call to final readout.
  9. How do you coach a non-technical account executive so they can handle basic technical questions without you in the room?
  10. How do you build a credible technical relationship with a senior engineering or operations stakeholder?
  11. How do you track competitor moves in your product space? Give a recent example.
  12. Tell us about a time customer feedback directly shaped a product or process improvement at your company.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you turned a customer's vague business problem into a concrete technical proposal.

*Situation:* A plant operations manager told me their team was 'losing too much time on calibration,' but they had no formal measurement of the problem and no clear idea where the bottleneck was.

*Task:* I needed to define the real problem before proposing anything, or I risked pitching the wrong solution entirely.

*Action:* I arranged a half-day on-site visit with their maintenance and quality leads. I mapped their current calibration workflow step by step, identified that manual data entry after each check was the main time drain, and built a demo focused on eliminating that step. I used their own shift data in the demo so the time savings appeared in numbers they already trusted.

*Result:* They approved a pilot for two production lines within three weeks. The project later expanded across the facility.

---

Q: Tell us about a deal you lost. What changed in your process afterward?

*Situation:* We were finalists for a large enterprise contract with a facilities management company. I was confident our platform covered the customer's core requirements.

*Task:* I owned the technical evaluation track while the account executive handled commercial terms.

*Action:* I had focused the demo on features the customer said they wanted, but missed a deeper concern: they needed the platform to connect with a legacy work-order system that we did not support natively. The competitor had a pre-built connector for it. I only discovered this after the loss call.

*Result:* I built a 'Day 1 discovery checklist' that puts integration questions in the very first call. My team adopted it, and we caught a similar requirement early in the next competitive deal, which we won.

---

Q: How do you handle a situation where your product cannot meet a customer's requirement?

*Situation:* A customer asked for a real-time data export format that our platform did not support natively.

*Task:* I needed to be transparent without losing the deal or over-promising on the product roadmap.

*Action:* I acknowledged the gap directly, walked them through a workaround using our REST API, and looped in the product manager to formally log the feature request. I gave the customer a realistic timeline range for when it might be addressed, with a clear caveat that roadmap items are not guarantees.

*Result:* The customer signed the contract because they appreciated the honesty. The feature shipped in a later release, and the customer became one of our reference accounts.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Customer-back framing for technical and product questions. Start every answer with the customer's business problem, not the product feature. Name the problem in the customer's language first, then explain how the solution addresses it. Fortive interviewers are trained to spot candidates who lead with features instead of outcomes.

STAR for behavioral questions. Use Situation, Task, Action, Result and keep each section tight. Be specific about what you personally did, not what the team did. If you do not have exact numbers, describe the direction and scale of the outcome clearly.

FBS alignment for process and improvement questions. You do not need prior Fortive experience to answer these well. Describe any time you spotted a recurring problem, made a change, and tracked whether things improved. That pattern is the core of FBS, even if you have never used the term before.

Segment specificity for 'why Fortive' questions. Generic answers about 'industrial technology' are a red flag. Name the specific brand or product line the role relates to (Fluke, Tektronix, Gordian, etc.) and explain why that segment interests you. This signals you have done real research.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fortive Solutions Engineers are expected to earn trust with both technical buyers (engineers, IT leads) and business buyers (operations heads, finance stakeholders). Candidates report that interviewers typically look for four things.

Customer curiosity. Can you ask questions that surface the real problem, not just the stated problem? Expect scenario questions where the customer gives you incomplete or misleading information on purpose. Interviewers want to see you probe before you propose.

Technical depth without losing the room. You do not need to be a product engineer, but you must explain how the product works in a way that earns trust from a technical audience. Be ready to walk through a product you have sold or supported in real detail.

FBS mindset. Fortive promotes a culture of structured, measurable improvement. Candidates who can point to a specific process they improved, even a small one, consistently stand out. Candidates who speak only in generalities about 'being process-oriented' do not.

Business impact orientation. Can you connect your technical work to a commercial result? Interviewers want to see that you think about deal outcomes, customer retention, and revenue impact, not just whether the demo ran smoothly.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Learn the Fortive Business System. Read publicly available material on FBS and lean continuous improvement. Understand kaizen events, daily management, and policy deployment at a surface level. Prepare one personal story where you improved a process, measured the result, and repeated the improvement.

Step 2: Know your segment. Fortive spans test and measurement (Fluke, Tektronix), sensing, industrial safety (Industrial Scientific), and software (Gordian, ServiceChannel). Identify which segment the specific role sits in and prepare examples relevant to that product space and customer type.

Step 3: Build your demo story. Prepare a two-minute narrative of the best technical demo you have ever run. Cover how you prepared, how you handled tough questions in the room, and what the business outcome was.

Step 4: Prepare for the loss question. Every serious SE interview includes a question about a deal you lost. Have a real example ready with a clear lesson and a concrete change you made afterward. Vague answers here are a common reason candidates do not advance.

Step 5: Research the competitive landscape. Know the two or three main competitors in the segment you are interviewing for. Be ready to describe how you would handle a customer who is leaning toward a competitor's solution.

Step 6: Prepare sharp questions for them. Ask how FBS shows up in the Solutions Engineer role day to day, how success is measured in the first six months, and how the SE team feeds product feedback back to the engineering team. Good questions signal you are already thinking like an insider.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Leading with features, not problems. Interviewers at Fortive are trained to catch candidates who demo features without anchoring them to a business outcome. Always start with the customer's problem.

Vague answers on continuous improvement. Saying 'I believe in continuous improvement' without a real example is a clear red flag in a Fortive interview. Have a specific, small story ready.

Confusing Fortive with Danaher. Fortive spun off from Danaher years ago and operates as an independent company. Mentioning Danaher without context can signal you have not done current research.

Not asking which segment you are interviewing for. SE roles differ significantly across Fortive's portfolio. Candidates who skip this question often prepare for the wrong product type and miss the mark on technical examples.

Over-promising on unsupported requirements. Interviewers sometimes present a scenario where the product cannot fully meet the customer's need. Saying 'yes, we can do that' when the honest answer is 'here is how we would handle this transparently' is a mistake with real consequences.

A generic 'why Fortive' answer. Saying you are excited about 'industrial technology' without naming a specific brand, customer segment, or business problem signals that you have not researched the role seriously.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What does the hiring process for a Solutions Engineer at Fortive typically look like?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter call, one or two competency or technical interviews, and a demo or presentation round. Some segments add a final conversation with a business unit leader. The number of steps and structure vary by segment, so ask your recruiter for a clear breakdown at the start of the process.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at Fortive in India?

Fortive does not publish India-specific compensation bands publicly. Glassdoor and industry surveys for Solutions Engineer roles at comparable industrial technology companies in India show a range that varies by experience level, city, and business segment. Bangalore and Mumbai roles are commonly cited as being at the higher end of the range. Ask the recruiter directly at the first call and cross-check with the specific segment you are targeting.

Do I need an engineering degree to apply for a Solutions Engineer role at Fortive?

Not necessarily. For hardware-focused segments like Fluke or Tektronix, a background in electronics, instrumentation, or electrical engineering is a strong advantage. For software segments like Gordian or ServiceChannel, a background in SaaS, facilities management, or enterprise software is often more relevant. Read the specific job description carefully to understand what the role requires.

How important is knowing the Fortive Business System before the interview?

You are not expected to be an FBS expert before joining. Being able to describe a time you identified a recurring problem, made a process change, measured the result, and repeated the improvement will strongly resonate with interviewers. They are looking for the mindset and habit of structured improvement, not familiarity with Fortive's specific terminology.

What is the difference between a Solutions Engineer and a Sales Engineer at Fortive?

The titles are often used interchangeably at Fortive and across the industry. Both roles support account executives with technical depth during the sales process. In some segments, Solutions Engineer leans more toward post-sale implementation and customer success, while Sales Engineer is more purely pre-sales. The specific job description will clarify the balance for the role you are applying to.

Are there Solutions Engineer openings at Fortive outside Bangalore?

As of July 2026, Fortive has 38 open roles tracked on knok jobradar. Across the broader Solutions Engineer market in India, 1,270 openings are active, with the largest concentrations in Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, and Pune. For Fortive specifically, check directly on the company careers page and on major job platforms for the most current city-level listings.
