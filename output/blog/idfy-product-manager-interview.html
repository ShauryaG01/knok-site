01 Overview

Overview

IDfy is one of India's leading identity verification and background check companies. They help banks, NBFCs, gig platforms, and large enterprises verify customer and employee identities through KYC, video KYC, document verification, and background screening APIs. Product managers at IDfy own core API products and work closely with compliance, engineering, and enterprise sales teams.

As of July 2026, IDfy has 13 open roles listed on knok jobradar. Salary bands for PM roles in the Indian market run 12-20 LPA for Associate PM, 24-40 LPA for PM with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PM, and 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level.

The interview process typically spans several rounds covering product thinking, data analysis, domain knowledge in identity and verification, and a behavioral round. Candidates report that IDfy interviewers pay close attention to how you understand regulated industries, API product design, and enterprise customer pain points.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a product you have built end to end. What metrics did you use to define success?

*Situation:* At my previous company, our mobile KYC onboarding step had a high drop-off rate that was commonly cited internally as a major growth blocker.

*Task:* I was asked to own the redesign of the KYC onboarding flow as the sole PM on the project.

*Action:* I ran user interviews with people who had dropped off, mapped the friction points, and worked with design and engineering to significantly reduce the number of steps. I also added asynchronous document review so users did not have to wait in-app.

*Result:* Completion rates improved measurably over the following cohort, as tracked in our internal dashboards. I learned to define success metrics before starting, not after shipping.

---

Q: How would you prioritize features when regulatory requirements and customer requests conflict?

*Situation:* While working on a lending product, a new RBI circular required changes to our KYC re-verification flow, which conflicted with several high-priority customer feature requests already in the sprint.

*Task:* I had to sequence the work within fixed engineering bandwidth.

*Action:* I mapped each item against two axes: compliance deadline (non-negotiable) and customer contract risk. I held a rapid sync with legal, sales, and engineering leads to align on what could slip without SLA breach. I moved the regulatory change to the top, deferred some customer features by one sprint, and fast-tracked a smaller customer ask that fit within the same sprint.

*Result:* We met the RBI deadline and retained all enterprise contracts. I documented the decision in a one-pager so the team could reuse the prioritization logic next time.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with engineering on scope or timelines.

*Situation:* My engineering lead wanted to rebuild our document parsing module from scratch, which would have taken several sprints. I felt the business could not wait that long.

*Task:* I needed to align on a shared plan without damaging the team relationship or escalating unnecessarily.

*Action:* I asked the team to walk me through the technical debt driving the rebuild. I then proposed a phased plan: a short stabilization sprint to protect key clients, followed by a structured rewrite running in parallel with new feature work. I brought in the business context (an upcoming enterprise renewal) to help the team see the trade-offs clearly.

*Result:* Engineering agreed to the phased plan. The stabilization patch shipped quickly, the renewal was secured, and the rewrite completed in the following quarter without rushing.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for behavioral questions at IDfy. Keep each story under three minutes when spoken aloud.

For product design questions, use a structured flow: start with the user (who is the customer, developer, or compliance officer?), define the problem clearly, list a few solution options with trade-offs, then pick one and explain the build sequence. IDfy interviewers typically reward candidates who name specific user personas in their domain, such as NBFC compliance teams, HR ops leads at large enterprises, or gig platform operations managers.

For metrics and analytical questions, use a goal-signal-metric tree: state the business goal, name the leading signals, then define specific metrics. For an IDfy product like background verification, a goal might be 'reduce time-to-hire for enterprise clients,' with signals like verification turnaround time and re-submission rate.

For prioritization questions, use a simple impact-effort matrix or a weighted scoring model. At IDfy, always add a third axis: compliance risk. A feature that creates regulatory exposure should drop in priority regardless of business impact, and interviewers will notice if you skip this step.

For competitor questions, follow a positioning framework: acknowledge the competitor's strength honestly, then articulate IDfy's defensible moat (data network, compliance depth, API reliability, or enterprise integrations). Avoid vague answers like 'we have better quality.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

IDfy interviews are typically designed to test three things: domain fit, product thinking depth, and how you handle ambiguity in a regulated industry.

Domain fit means showing you understand identity verification, KYC, and background checks at a working level. You do not need to be a compliance lawyer, but you should know terms like video KYC, Aadhaar-based e-KYC, and cKYC. Candidates report that interviewers quickly move past candidates who treat IDfy as a generic SaaS company.

Product thinking depth means going beyond surface-level frameworks. Interviewers want to see you define a real user, name a specific pain point, and propose a measurable outcome. Generic answers score poorly.

Handling ambiguity is tested through questions with incomplete data. IDfy's products serve regulated industries where requirements change frequently. Interviewers want to see a structured, calm approach to making decisions with partial information, and a habit of documenting your reasoning.

Candidates also report that IDfy values PMs who can speak the language of enterprise sales, since most of their products are sold B2B. Being able to discuss churn risk, SLA commitments, and integration timelines will set you apart.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain foundation. Read IDfy's public product pages, developer documentation, and any publicly available case studies. Understand their core products: background verification, video KYC, identity API suite, and onboarding automation. Learn the basics of RBI KYC guidelines, Aadhaar-based verification, and what UIDAI regulations mean for product decisions.

Week 2: Product thinking practice. Pick two of IDfy's products and do a mock product critique. For each, define the primary user, the core job to be done, a couple of gaps you notice, and one feature you would prioritize next quarter with reasoning. Practice saying this out loud in under four minutes.

Week 3: Behavioral and case prep. Prepare five STAR stories covering: a product you shipped, a prioritization trade-off, a conflict with a cross-functional team, a failure and what you learned, and a data-driven decision. For case prep, practice funnel analysis and API product design with a friend or in writing.

Week 4: Mock interviews and logistics. Do at least two mock interviews with someone who can give candid feedback. Review your stories for specificity (names, timelines, outcomes) and cut any vague language. Prepare a couple of thoughtful questions for the interviewer about IDfy's roadmap or team structure.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating IDfy as a generic tech startup. Candidates who use generic frameworks without connecting them to identity verification, compliance, or enterprise sales tend to score lower. Ground every answer in IDfy's actual business context.

Skipping the regulatory layer in prioritization. A common slip is prioritizing purely on customer demand or business impact, ignoring compliance risk. At IDfy, any product decision touching KYC or identity data must factor in regulatory constraints.

Vague metrics. Saying 'user satisfaction improved' without naming a specific signal (like re-submission rate, verification turnaround time, or API error rate) signals a lack of analytical depth.

Over-explaining frameworks, under-explaining decisions. Interviewers want to know what you decided and why, not a lecture on how RICE scoring works. Use frameworks as a tool, not a script.

Not asking questions. Candidates report that IDfy interviewers expect you to ask at least one or two substantive questions. Asking nothing reads as low curiosity or low interest in the role.

Underestimating B2B complexity. IDfy's customers are enterprises, not consumers. Candidates who default to consumer product thinking (DAU, viral loops, onboarding funnels for individuals) without adapting to enterprise buying cycles and integration-heavy sales tend to miss the mark.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the IDfy PM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves multiple rounds, often covering a resume and background discussion, a product thinking or case round, an analytical or metrics round, and a final leadership or cross-functional round. Round count and structure can vary by level and team, so confirm the format with your recruiter before your first call. Preparing for both written case assignments and live problem-solving is advisable.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at IDfy?

Salary bands for PM roles in the Indian market, based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, run 12-20 LPA for Associate PM, 24-40 LPA for PM with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PM, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PM levels. Actual offers at IDfy will depend on your experience, the specific role, and negotiation. For more current benchmarks, Glassdoor and levels.fyi India listings are a useful reference.

Does IDfy ask coding or SQL questions in PM interviews?

Candidates report that IDfy PM interviews are not primarily technical in the coding sense, but analytical and data-oriented questions are common. Being comfortable reading a basic SQL query or interpreting a data table will help you in the analytical round. For API-facing PM roles, understanding REST API concepts and being able to read documentation is an advantage, even if you are not expected to write code.

How much do I need to know about KYC and identity verification before the interview?

You do not need deep legal expertise, but a working knowledge of terms like video KYC, Aadhaar e-KYC, cKYC, and RBI digital KYC guidelines will signal genuine domain interest. Candidates who arrive without this baseline tend to struggle with product design questions specific to IDfy's space. Reading IDfy's public developer documentation and a few RBI circulars on KYC norms is usually sufficient preparation.

Where are most IDfy PM roles located?

IDfy is headquartered in Mumbai and has a significant presence in Bangalore, which together account for a large share of their hiring activity in the Indian market. The role you apply for may be fully on-site, hybrid, or remote depending on the team, so confirm the working arrangement with the recruiter. As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 13 open IDfy roles across platforms.

What is the best way to show product sense for an identity verification company?

The clearest way is to tie your product thinking directly to IDfy's customer segments: banks, NBFCs, gig platforms, and enterprises with large-scale employee verification needs. Practicing a mock product critique of IDfy's video KYC or background verification product before your interview will sharpen your ability to name real users, real pain points, and measurable outcomes. Generic consumer product examples without a B2B or regulated-industry angle tend not to land well with IDfy interviewers.
