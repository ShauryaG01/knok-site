01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Baseten builds the infrastructure layer that lets companies deploy and serve machine learning models in production. Their platform is used by ML engineers and data science teams who need reliable, low-latency model serving without building everything from scratch. As of mid-2026, Baseten has 74 open roles across engineering, go-to-market, and operations, which signals strong hiring activity. The roles span remote and hybrid setups, with many positions open to candidates across the US and, in some cases, India-based applicants for remote work.

If you have a background in ML infrastructure, distributed systems, backend engineering, or developer tooling, Baseten is worth a close look. Competition is real because these roles attract experienced engineers, so preparation matters.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Baseten's interview process typically moves through several stages, though the exact flow can vary by role and team. Here is a general picture based on publicly available information from candidate reports:

Step 1: Application and resume screen. A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume for relevant experience. Roles often require strong engineering fundamentals, so a clear and specific resume helps.

Step 2: Recruiter conversation. If your profile matches, a recruiter reaches out for a brief intro call. Expect questions about your background, why Baseten interests you, and basic logistics like location and availability.

Step 3: Technical screen. This is usually a coding or systems design conversation depending on the role. For engineering roles, expect questions on data structures, algorithms, or system design topics relevant to ML infrastructure. The interviewer wants to see how you think through problems, not just whether you get the right answer.

Step 4: Deep-dive or take-home assignment. Some roles include a take-home project or a deeper technical discussion. For ML platform roles, this might involve designing a serving architecture or debugging a distributed system scenario.

Step 5: Final loop. This typically involves conversations with several team members covering technical depth, past experience, and cross-functional collaboration. You may also speak with a member of the leadership team.

Timelines vary, but candidates commonly report hearing back within a couple of weeks between stages. If you do not hear back after a week, a polite follow-up to your recruiter is reasonable.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Baseten hires for depth in the infrastructure and systems space. Here is what tends to matter most across their engineering roles:

Strong systems fundamentals. Whether you are applying for backend, ML infrastructure, or platform engineering, comfort with distributed systems, low-latency design, and production debugging is expected.

Hands-on ML experience. You do not need to be a researcher, but understanding how models are trained, served, and monitored in production is valued. Familiarity with frameworks like PyTorch or TensorFlow, and tools like Triton or ONNX, comes up frequently.

Product thinking. Baseten's customers are developers. Engineers who understand developer experience, good APIs, and how infrastructure choices affect end users stand out.

Communication. Baseten is a relatively small team moving fast. They want people who can write clearly, flag blockers early, and collaborate across functions without heavy process.

For go-to-market and operations roles, deep knowledge of the ML tooling ecosystem and the ability to speak the language of ML engineers matters as much as the typical sales or ops skills.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A focused preparation plan gives you a real edge. Here is how to approach it:

Understand the product first. Spend time with Baseten's documentation and blog before any interview. Know what Truss is, how their model deployment flow works, and what problems they solve for customers. Being able to say specifically why their approach interests you is far more convincing than a generic answer.

Brush up on systems design. For engineering roles, review how you would design a low-latency model serving layer, handle traffic spikes, and manage GPU resource allocation. Practice explaining these out loud, not just in your head.

Review ML fundamentals. Know the lifecycle of a model from training to inference. Be comfortable discussing batching strategies, model quantization, and common failure modes in production ML systems.

Prepare strong past-project stories. Baseten values people who have shipped real systems. For each major project on your resume, be ready to explain what you built, the tradeoffs you made, what broke, and what you learned.

Practice communication. Their process often involves explaining complex technical decisions to non-specialists. Practice narrating your thinking out loud.

If you are applying from India for a remote role, clarify timezone overlap expectations early in the recruiter call. Most of their team is US-based, so being clear about your availability and async communication style helps. 06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Baseten operates with the focus and pace you would expect from a growth-stage infrastructure company. A few things stand out from public accounts and their own writing:

They care about craft. Engineering quality matters here. People who take pride in the details of how systems are built fit in well. Cutting corners to ship fast is not the norm.

Customers are developers. The culture is shaped by deep respect for the developer experience. If you have ever been frustrated by a poorly designed API or unclear docs, that instinct is valued.

Small team, real ownership. With a compact team, individuals take on meaningful scope. This suits people who want to own problems end to end, not just work a small slice of a large codebase.

Direct communication. Feedback is expected to be honest and specific, given and received without excessive softening. This is common in infrastructure companies where unclear communication leads to outages.

Baseten has grown steadily through 2024 and 2025, and with 74 open roles as of mid-2026, they are in an active expansion phase. That means real opportunities, but also the reality of a company still building out its processes and ways of working.
