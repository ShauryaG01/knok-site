01 Overview

Overview

Cyera is a cloud data security company built around data security posture management (DSPM), helping enterprises discover, classify, and protect sensitive data across multi-cloud environments. As of July 2026, they have 120 open Data Engineer roles, making them one of the more active technical hirers in India right now.

Data Engineer interviews at Cyera sit at the intersection of large-scale pipeline engineering and data security. Interviewers are not just testing whether you can build fast pipelines. They also want to know how you think about who accesses data, how sensitive information is handled, and how compliance gets built into pipeline design from the start.

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-5 rounds: an initial recruiter screen, a technical phone or video round, one or two deeper technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final round with a hiring manager or cross-functional panel. Round structure varies by team and seniority.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and reflect Cyera's product focus on data security, cloud-scale pipelines, and enterprise compliance. Expect a mix of coding, system design, and behavioural questions across rounds.

  1. Walk me through how you would design a pipeline to ingest, classify, and store sensitive customer data securely in the cloud.
  2. How do you detect and handle PII (personally identifiable information) in a large, heterogeneous data lake?
  3. Explain the difference between batch and stream processing. When does each approach make sense for a security-focused use case?
  4. How would you build a metadata catalog to track sensitive data assets across a multi-cloud environment?
  5. Write a SQL query to identify duplicate records in a large table. How would you handle removal at scale without causing downtime?
  6. How do you implement role-based access control (RBAC) for data assets in a cloud warehouse or data lake?
  7. Describe a production data quality incident you handled. What went wrong, how did you find it, and what changed afterwards?
  8. How do you manage schema evolution in a pipeline that serves multiple downstream consumers?
  9. What is data lineage? Have you built or used a lineage system? Walk me through your approach.
  10. Compare two cloud data platforms you have used (for example Snowflake vs Databricks). What drove your choice in a specific project?
  11. How would you design a near-real-time alerting system that flags anomalous data access patterns across an enterprise?
  12. How do you build GDPR or SOC 2 compliance requirements into a pipeline from day one, rather than adding them after the fact?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for behavioural questions. Here are three examples tailored to Cyera's interview themes. Adapt the details to your own real experience.

Q: Describe a time you found and fixed a data quality issue in production.

*Situation:* Our analytics team flagged that a customer churn dashboard was showing a large, unexpected spike for one segment that the business team could not explain with any real-world signal.

*Task:* I owned the ingestion pipeline feeding that dashboard and needed to trace whether the spike was real or a data artefact.

*Action:* I queried the raw source tables and found that an upstream CRM migration had changed a status field from numeric codes to string labels. Our pipeline was treating unmapped strings as null, and our churn logic counted null as churned. I wrote a backfill script to re-map the historical values, added a schema validation step with alerting at the ingestion layer, and documented the field mapping contract with the source team.

*Result:* The dashboard corrected itself after the backfill. We added automated schema drift checks, and that pipeline has had no silent mapping failures since.

---

Q: How have you handled PII in a data pipeline?

*Situation:* At a previous employer we were building a data warehouse from customer support tickets. The tickets contained free-text fields with names, phone numbers, and occasionally payment details typed in by agents.

*Task:* We needed the ticket data for model training but could not expose raw PII to analysts or training jobs.

*Action:* I integrated a PII detection library that scanned free-text fields using regex patterns and a named-entity recognition model. Detected PII was replaced with typed tokens such as [PHONE_NUMBER]. I also applied column-level encryption for structured PII fields and restricted the raw data to a quarantine zone accessible only to the data governance team.

*Result:* Analysts and ML engineers could use the ticket data safely. The approach satisfied our legal team's GDPR requirements without blocking any feature work.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you managed schema evolution without breaking downstream consumers.

*Situation:* We were adding a new field to a high-traffic events table that three downstream teams read in production, including a billing job.

*Task:* Roll out the new field without any downstream job failing or requiring coordinated downtime.

*Action:* I introduced the column with a safe default value so existing rows and consumers saw a valid value immediately. I wrote migration notes in our internal data catalog, notified each downstream team with a clear deprecation notice, and kept the old derived logic running in parallel for two weeks while teams migrated on their own schedules. I also added a contract test that would fail CI if any consumer referenced a column that no longer existed.

*Result:* All three consumers migrated without incidents. The contract test pattern became our team standard for future schema changes.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard for behavioural questions. Cyera interviewers want concrete examples from real projects, not generic hypotheticals. Keep the Situation brief and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Problem-Solution-Trade-off works well for system design questions. State the problem clearly, propose a solution, then name what you traded away and why that was acceptable. At a security-focused company, always include the security and compliance implications of your trade-offs alongside the performance ones.

Clarify-then-Design is useful for open-ended design prompts. Spend the first few minutes asking about scale, latency requirements, compliance constraints, and who consumes the data. This signals that you understand pipeline design is driven by context, which is central to Cyera's product domain.

For coding questions, walk through your approach before writing a single line. State the time and space complexity when you finish. If the question involves SQL on large tables, mention partitioning, indexing, and query plan considerations proactively rather than waiting to be asked.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Security-first thinking. Because Cyera's product protects sensitive data, interviewers want engineers who instinctively ask 'who should be able to see this?' and 'what happens if this data escapes the intended boundary?' You do not need a security engineering background, but you should be comfortable reasoning about access control, encryption at rest and in transit, and compliance requirements.

Pipeline depth, not tool name-dropping. Knowing that Spark or Kafka exists is not enough. Interviewers want to hear that you understand why a tool is appropriate for a given scale, latency, and cost profile. Be ready to explain the trade-offs behind your past architecture decisions.

Ownership and production instincts. Cyera builds enterprise products where pipeline failures have direct customer impact. Stories about how you monitored, debugged, and improved reliability in past roles carry significant weight.

Cloud fluency. Most questions assume a cloud-native context. Be comfortable discussing managed services, IAM policies, and cost trade-offs on at least one major cloud platform.

Clear communication. Data engineers at Cyera work closely with product, security research, and customer-facing teams. Interviewers will note whether you can explain a technical decision clearly to a non-engineering audience.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations and Cyera context

Read Cyera's product pages and any publicly available blog posts to understand what DSPM is and the data problems they solve for enterprise customers. Revisit Python fundamentals: generators, context managers, and writing efficient PySpark or Pandas transformations. Solve several SQL problems focused on window functions, CTEs, and query optimisation.

Week 2: System design and security topics

Practise designing two or three end-to-end pipelines: one batch ETL, one streaming, one focused on data classification and masking. For each design, explicitly call out the security and compliance considerations. Review RBAC patterns in cloud data platforms and how column-level security works in Snowflake, BigQuery, or whichever platform you know best.

Week 3: Behavioural prep and mock rounds

Write out STAR stories for five or six scenarios: a production incident, a cross-team collaboration, a time you pushed back on a requirement, a performance optimisation, and a project you owned end-to-end. Do at least one full mock interview covering both coding and system design with a peer. Review your weakest area from mock feedback before the actual interview.

On interview day, confirm the round structure with your recruiter in advance. Prepare two or three specific questions about the team's current infrastructure challenges or how they handle data governance internally. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it as a generic data engineering interview. Candidates who do not mention data privacy, access control, or compliance miss a key signal Cyera interviewers look for. Even for a straightforward pipeline design question, name the security implications.

Naming tools without explaining trade-offs. Saying 'I would use Kafka' is not an answer. Explain why Kafka over a simpler queue, what the operational overhead is, and when you would not use it.

Weak SQL fundamentals. Candidates consistently underestimate how deep SQL questions go. Practise window functions (RANK, LAG, LEAD), recursive CTEs, and reading query execution plans. Cyera works with enterprise-scale data volumes, so performance awareness matters.

Vague STAR answers. Answers like 'we improved pipeline reliability' without specifics leave interviewers with nothing to evaluate. Use concrete details from your own projects, state what you measured, and be clear about your individual contribution versus the broader team effort.

Not asking questions. A candidate who asks nothing at the end signals low curiosity or low interest. Prepare specific questions about the team's current infrastructure challenges or how they approach data governance internally.

Ignoring the compliance angle. Questions about GDPR, SOC 2, or data residency are not trick questions at a company like Cyera. If you have built pipelines with compliance requirements in mind, say so explicitly and early in your answers.
