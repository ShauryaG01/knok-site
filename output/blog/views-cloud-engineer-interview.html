01 Overview

Overview

VIEWS is one of the more active hirers for Cloud Engineers right now, with 40 open roles in its current pipeline. Across India, the broader Cloud Engineer market shows 102 active openings (as of July 2026), with Bangalore and Delhi tied at 13 openings each, followed by Hyderabad (6), Pune (5), and Chennai (2).

Candidates report that VIEWS interviews are thorough and technically focused. The process typically includes a resume screening, one or two technical rounds covering cloud architecture and hands-on tooling, and a final discussion on experience and cultural alignment. Rounds may be conducted over video call or in a panel format. Expect questions on AWS, Azure, or GCP depending on the team's stack, Infrastructure as Code, container orchestration, and cloud security.

Cloud Engineer roles at VIEWS typically sit at the intersection of platform engineering and DevOps, so demonstrating both design thinking and practical scripting skills matters. Prepare to discuss real projects you have owned, not just tools you have used.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a cloud migration project you owned from planning to go-live.

*Situation:* Our product team was running on ageing on-premise servers. Unplanned downtime during traffic spikes was a recurring problem and the ops team spent most weekends firefighting.

*Task:* I was given ownership of migrating our core application stack to AWS, with a hard deadline tied to a contract renewal.

*Action:* I started with a dependency mapping exercise to understand every service, database, and third-party integration. I used Terraform to codify all target infrastructure so the environment could be version-controlled and reproduced. I broke the migration into phases: stateless services first, then databases with a parallel-run period to validate data consistency, then DNS cutover. I ran daily standups with the app and QA teams to catch blockers early.

*Result:* We completed the migration on schedule. Post-migration monitoring showed a clear reduction in incident volume during peak hours, and the team stopped having weekend on-call emergencies tied to hardware failures.

---

Q: Describe a production outage you have debugged in a cloud environment.

*Situation:* Our payment service went down on a Friday evening. Alerts fired across CloudWatch and PagerDuty simultaneously.

*Task:* As the on-call cloud engineer, I had to identify the root cause and restore service with minimal downtime.

*Action:* I pulled CloudWatch logs and noticed a spike in database connection errors coinciding with an auto-scaling event. A misconfigured security group rule was blocking new EC2 instances from reaching the RDS cluster. I corrected the rule, verified connectivity, and confirmed the scaling group was healthy. I then wrote a post-mortem and added a Terraform validation check to catch that rule pattern in future.

*Result:* Service was restored shortly after identifying the root cause. The post-mortem was shared with the wider team and the Terraform check was merged into our CI pipeline to prevent recurrence.

---

Q: How do you manage cloud security: IAM policies, secrets management, network segmentation?

*Situation:* At a previous company, a security audit flagged overly permissive IAM roles across several services, and secrets were stored in environment variables rather than a vault.

*Task:* I was asked to lead remediation before our compliance review.

*Action:* I audited every IAM role using AWS IAM Access Analyzer and applied least-privilege policies, removing all wildcard permissions. I migrated all secrets to AWS Secrets Manager and updated application code to fetch them at runtime. I also restructured VPC subnets to place databases in private subnets with no public route, and tightened security group rules to allow only the minimum required traffic.

*Result:* The compliance review passed with no critical findings in the cloud infrastructure section. The team adopted the new patterns as internal standards going forward.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for all experience-based questions. Situation gives context, Task clarifies your specific role, Action is where you show depth (use 'I' not 'we'), and Result shows business impact. Keep the Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your answer on Action and Result.

For architecture and design questions, use a layered approach: start with requirements (availability, latency, scale), then propose a high-level design, then drill into specific components. Show you can reason about tradeoffs, not just recall AWS service names.

For troubleshooting questions, follow a structured debugging story: what the alert told you, what you checked first, how you narrowed down the root cause, what you fixed, and what you changed to prevent recurrence. Interviewers want to see disciplined thinking under pressure.

For cost and optimisation questions, frame your answer around measurement first: you cannot optimise what you do not monitor. Mention tools like AWS Cost Explorer or Azure Cost Management, then talk about specific actions such as right-sizing, reserved instances, spot usage, and storage tiering.

For security questions, default to least-privilege and defence-in-depth as your anchor principles. Concrete examples of IAM, secrets management, and network segmentation carry more weight than general statements.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that VIEWS interviewers care most about practical, hands-on cloud experience. Knowing service names is table stakes. What stands out is being able to describe a real system you built, or broke and fixed, the tradeoffs you made, and what you would do differently.

Ownership mindset. Cloud Engineers at product companies are expected to take end-to-end responsibility. Interviewers notice when a candidate attributes every action to 'the team'. Use 'I' where you genuinely led or decided.

Infrastructure as Code fluency. Terraform comes up in almost every technical round. Be ready to discuss module structure, state management, and how you handle configuration drift.

Security awareness. Cloud security is not a separate team's problem at most modern companies. Expect at least one question on IAM, encryption, or network design, even in rounds that are not labelled as security-focused.

Communication. Cloud engineers often work across app, data, and product teams. Interviewers watch for whether you can explain a technical decision clearly to a non-cloud audience.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Solidify your cloud fundamentals.
Review core services on your primary platform (compute, storage, networking, databases, IAM). Practice drawing architecture diagrams by hand. If you have a certification, revisit the exam guide to refresh areas you have not used recently.

Week 2: Go deep on IaC and DevOps tooling.
Spend time writing and reviewing Terraform code. Practice writing a module from scratch, managing remote state, and using workspaces. Review your CI/CD pipeline experience and be ready to walk through a real pipeline you built or maintained.

Week 3: Prepare your project stories.
List the cloud projects you have owned. For each, prepare a STAR story covering what you built, the key decisions, the tools used, and the outcome. Have at least one story each for: migration, outage response, cost optimisation, and security hardening.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about tools without talking about problems. Saying 'I have used Kubernetes' tells an interviewer nothing. Tell them what problem Kubernetes solved for your team and what tradeoffs you accepted.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'The system improved' is weak. Even if you cannot share specific figures, say what changed: fewer incidents, faster deployments, a compliance audit passed. Give the interviewer something concrete to hold onto.

Over-explaining the Situation. Candidates often spend too long on context and rush the Action. Flip the ratio: brief context, detailed actions.

Skipping the 'what would you do differently' angle. Interviewers often follow up with this. If you raise it yourself, it signals maturity and self-awareness.

Ignoring cloud security in non-security questions. Weave security thinking into architecture and migration answers. Not mentioning IAM or encryption in a design answer is a flag for senior-level roles.

Not asking clarifying questions in design rounds. Jumping straight into an answer without asking about scale, budget, team size, or compliance requirements suggests you design in a vacuum. Ask first, then design.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the VIEWS Cloud Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves a resume or recruiter screening, one or two technical rounds, and a final round covering experience and team fit. The exact number of rounds can vary by level and team. It is common for at least one round to include a live architecture or whiteboarding exercise.

What cloud platforms does VIEWS focus on in interviews?

Based on what candidates report, AWS is most commonly tested, but interviewers typically care more about your depth on one platform than surface knowledge across all three. Be ready to discuss your primary platform in detail and mention any multi-cloud or hybrid experience as a bonus.

Is Terraform knowledge required for Cloud Engineer roles at VIEWS?

Terraform comes up consistently in Cloud Engineer interviews at companies of VIEWS's profile. Candidates report being asked to write or review Terraform code, discuss state management, and explain how they handle infrastructure drift. If you have used another IaC tool like Pulumi or CDK, be ready to explain it and draw comparisons to Terraform.

How important are cloud certifications for getting shortlisted at VIEWS?

Certifications help with initial screening but are not a substitute for project experience. Candidates with strong hands-on project stories tend to perform better in technical rounds than those who rely only on certification knowledge. A relevant certification combined with real project examples is a strong combination.

What salary can I expect for a Cloud Engineer role at VIEWS?

Salary data specific to VIEWS is limited in public sources. Industry surveys and Glassdoor listings for Cloud Engineer roles in India commonly cite ranges that vary widely by experience level and city. For a more accurate picture, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi filtered to VIEWS or companies of a similar size and sector.

How do I stand out if I am switching from a sysadmin or on-premise background?

Highlight any cloud projects you have completed, even personal or freelance ones. Frame your on-premise experience in terms of problems you solved (availability, scaling, security) and explain how you would solve the same problems using cloud-native tools. Show that you understand the shift in thinking from managing servers to managing platforms, and that you are actively building cloud skills.
