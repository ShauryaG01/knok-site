01 Overview

Overview

MyGlamm, now part of the Good Glamm Group, is one of India's fastest-growing D2C beauty and personal care brands. The company blends content, community, and commerce across its platform, so its engineering team works on high-traffic consumer apps, product recommendation systems, and e-commerce infrastructure. As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 4 open Software Engineer roles at MyGlamm.

The interview process typically spans 3-4 rounds: a coding screen, one or two technical interviews covering algorithms and system design, and a final managerial or culture-fit conversation. Candidates report the focus is on practical problem-solving, product thinking, and the ability to ship quickly in a startup-paced environment. Given the consumer-facing nature of the product, interviewers tend to appreciate engineers who connect technical decisions to user experience.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you improved the performance of a slow database query or data pipeline.

Situation: At my previous company, our product listing page was timing out during peak hours because of a slow SQL query joining three large tables.

Task: I needed to cut query time without changing the product's visible behavior for users.

Action: I profiled the query, identified a full-table scan as the bottleneck, added composite indexes on the most-filtered columns, and introduced a Redis cache layer for the most common filter combinations.

Result: Page load time dropped sharply and timeouts during peak traffic disappeared within two weeks of the fix going live. The change also improved the experience for mobile users on slower connections.

---

Q: Tell us about a production incident you handled.

Situation: Our checkout service started returning errors for a portion of users on a Friday evening, right before a sale campaign was supposed to go live.

Task: As the on-call engineer, I had to identify and fix the issue with minimal downtime and user impact.

Action: I checked application logs, traced the error to a third-party payment gateway that had changed its response schema without notice, wrote a hotfix to handle both the old and new schemas, and deployed it after a quick peer review.

Result: Errors stopped within the hour. I followed up with a postmortem and added schema validation tests to catch similar upstream changes earlier.

---

Q: How do you balance speed of delivery with code quality in a fast-moving team?

Situation: During a major product launch sprint, my team was under pressure to ship several features in a short window.

Task: I needed to keep velocity high without accumulating technical debt that would hurt the team in future sprints.

Action: I introduced lightweight PR checklists covering tests, edge cases, and security basics. I also flagged two features that needed a design review before implementation, which saved rework later and kept the codebase clean.

Result: We shipped on time with no critical bugs in the first month post-launch, and the checklists became a team standard adopted across the squad.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief, one to two sentences each. Spend most of your time on Action, since that is where your technical depth shows. Close with a concrete Result, and if you cannot share exact numbers, reference industry surveys for comparable benchmarks.

For system design questions, follow a structured flow: clarify scope and expected scale first, then define the main components, walk through data models, discuss key trade-offs (SQL vs. NoSQL, sync vs. async, caching at which layer), and zoom into the parts the interviewer probes on. Because MyGlamm is a consumer product, always connect your design choices to reliability and user experience, not just engineering elegance.

For coding rounds, think out loud from the start. Candidates report that product company interviewers value your reasoning process as much as the final solution. State a brute-force approach first, give its time and space complexity, then optimize. Ask clarifying questions about input size, edge cases, and constraints before you write a single line of code.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

MyGlamm's engineering team ships products used by a large consumer base, so interviewers look for a blend of technical depth and genuine product sensibility. They want to see that you understand the downstream impact of your code on real users, not just on system metrics.

Ownership. Be ready to talk about problems you spotted and fixed proactively, not just tickets assigned to you. Engineers who show initiative and care about outcomes beyond their immediate task tend to stand out in the process.

Comfort with ambiguity. The company moves at a startup pace. Interviewers value engineers who can make sound decisions with incomplete information and course-correct quickly when priorities shift.

Communication clarity. Because engineering works closely with product and business teams at a D2C company, the ability to explain a technical decision in plain terms is a real advantage. Practice summarizing complex work so a non-technical stakeholder can follow it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Technical Foundations. Review core data structures and algorithms, focusing on arrays, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. Practice coding problems at medium difficulty, since MyGlamm interview questions are typically at that level. Brush up on SQL optimization, indexing strategies, and query planning.

Week 2: System Design and Stack. Study common system design patterns including caching, message queues, API gateways, and microservices decomposition. If the job description lists specific technologies such as Node.js, React, Python, or AWS, spend extra time there. Review how e-commerce platforms handle product catalog, cart, checkout, and order management at scale.

Week 3: Behavioral Prep and Product Awareness. Write out STAR stories covering leadership, handling conflict, improving performance, and shipping under pressure. Practice explaining past projects clearly, first to a non-technical listener and then to a technical one. Read about D2C e-commerce trends in India to show genuine product interest during interviews.

Final Days. Review your resume line by line, since interviewers often dig into specific projects listed there. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions about the team's current technical challenges and how success is measured in the role. A candidate who asks good questions signals real curiosity.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping clarification in system design. Candidates who jump straight into drawing architecture often solve the wrong problem. Spend a minute aligning on scale, read/write patterns, and key constraints before proposing a solution.

Vague behavioral answers. Saying 'I improved performance' without any context does not land. Prepare specific stories with concrete outcomes. If you cannot share exact numbers, describe the impact qualitatively or reference industry surveys for comparable benchmarks.

Ignoring the product layer. MyGlamm is a consumer brand. Engineers who talk only about code without connecting it to user experience or business value miss what the company cares about most.

Not asking questions at the end. A candidate who asks nothing signals low interest. Prepare at least two genuine questions about team challenges, technical roadmap, or how engineering decisions get made.

Memorizing answers word for word. Interviewers notice scripted responses. Internalize the key points of each story and adapt them naturally to the question being asked, rather than reciting a rehearsed speech.
