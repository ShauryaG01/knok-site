01 Overview

Overview

Grab is Southeast Asia's leading super-app, operating across ride-hailing, food delivery, payments, and financial services. For Indian PMs, landing a role at Grab means joining a company whose product decisions are deeply tied to local market behaviour, inclusion, and serving users across a wide range of income and connectivity levels.

The interview process typically spans four to six rounds, candidates report, mixing an initial recruiter screen with product sense exercises, analytical case studies, a leadership and behavioural panel, and a final round with senior leadership. Some candidates also report a take-home product design or metric investigation exercise between rounds.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 2,009 PM openings in India, with Bangalore (271 roles) and Delhi (177 roles) leading. Grab alone carries 365 open PM roles across levels. Salary bands, based on publicly reported figures and industry surveys, run roughly 12-20 LPA for Associate PMs, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA at the Senior PM level, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PMs.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Grab interviews test product thinking, analytical depth, and cross-cultural user empathy. These are the questions candidates most commonly report:

  1. Walk me through how you would improve Grab's food delivery experience for first-time users in a Tier-2 Southeast Asian city.
  2. A key metric (say, daily active rides) has dropped sharply week-over-week. How do you investigate and respond?
  3. How would you prioritise the next three features for GrabPay given limited engineering bandwidth?
  4. Design a product that helps Grab driver-partners increase their monthly earnings.
  5. Grab wants to expand into a new vertical (for example, grocery or healthcare). How would you evaluate the opportunity?
  6. Tell me about a time you shipped a product that failed. What did you learn?
  7. How would you measure the success of Grab's subscription programme (GrabUnlimited)?
  8. A senior stakeholder wants to add a feature you believe will hurt retention. How do you handle it?
  9. Describe how you would set up an A/B test for a redesigned home screen on the Grab app.
  10. How would you define and track 'trust' as a metric across Grab's driver and passenger sides?
  11. Grab operates across multiple countries with very different regulatory environments. How do you build a product roadmap that works across markets?
  12. What do you see as Grab's biggest competitive threat in the next two years, and how should the product team respond?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A key metric has dropped sharply week-over-week. How do you investigate?

*Situation:* At my previous company, daily order volume on our food platform fell sharply after a backend infrastructure migration.

*Task:* I was the PM on call and needed to identify root cause fast, with engineering and data teams available.

*Action:* I first confirmed the data was real by checking multiple dashboards and ruling out a tracking bug. Then I segmented by city, device type, user cohort, and order type to isolate the drop. The dip was concentrated on Android users in two cities. I checked the release notes and found a payment SDK update had shipped the same day. I pulled error logs with the engineering lead, confirmed a payment failure spike, and we rolled back the SDK within four hours.

*Result:* Order volume recovered within 24 hours. We added a pre-release payment flow smoke test to the deployment checklist so the same class of issue would catch before reaching production.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you shipped a product that failed. What did you learn?

*Situation:* I led the launch of an in-app loyalty badge system meant to reward power users and increase weekly active usage.

*Task:* My goal was a meaningful lift in weekly sessions among the top user cohort within two months of launch.

*Action:* We shipped on time with a small notification campaign. I had prioritised speed to market over qualitative testing, skipping a user research round because of a tight deadline.

*Result:* Sessions did not move. Exit surveys showed users found the badges confusing and felt the rewards were not meaningful. We sunset the feature after three months. The lesson: I now always run at least five user interviews before committing an engineering sprint to a gamification mechanic, even when timelines are tight.

---

Q: How would you prioritise the next three features for GrabPay?

*Situation:* During a practice case prep session I framed this as a real prioritisation exercise with a mock squad and a 12-week window.

*Task:* Choose three features from a backlog of eight that maximise user growth and transaction volume.

*Action:* I applied a RICE-style scoring: Reach (how many users affected), Impact (effect on core metric), Confidence (how validated the idea is), and Effort (engineering weeks). I also added a 'regulatory risk' column given GrabPay operates under multiple central bank frameworks. The top three features were: a fast peer-to-peer transfer for Southeast Asian markets, a bill-split feature for group rides, and a savings round-up product for driver-partners.

*Result:* In the mock session the interviewer probed my regulatory column, which led to a strong discussion about market-specific compliance timelines. The exercise reinforced that at Grab, regulatory feasibility is a first-class prioritisation input, not an afterthought.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions respond well to a structured user-first approach. Start by clarifying the goal (growth, retention, monetisation). Then define your users and their core jobs-to-be-done. List pain points, generate solutions, prioritise using RICE or a simple impact-effort matrix, and close with success metrics.

Metric investigation questions follow a drill-down pattern. Confirm the data is accurate. Segment by time, geography, platform, and user cohort. Form two or three hypotheses. Match each hypothesis to a data source or experiment. Propose a fix and a safeguard.

Prioritisation questions work best when you make your constraints explicit first: team size, time horizon, and strategic goals. RICE scoring (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) is widely understood. For Grab specifically, add a 'market applicability' column because a feature that works in Singapore may not work in Vietnam without localisation.

Behavioural questions at Grab typically map to themes of customer obsession, data-driven decision making, and cross-functional influence. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and keep your result specific, even if the number is approximate and you disclose that.

Design questions benefit from a CIRCLES-lite flow: Comprehend the situation, Identify the user, Report on needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Grab interviewers, candidates report, are looking for four qualities above all else.

Southeast Asian market empathy. Grab's users are not uniform. A PM who talks only about urban, smartphone-first users without acknowledging cash-first behaviour, low-bandwidth connectivity, or multi-language households misses the point. Bring genuine curiosity about the region.

Comfort with ambiguity in data. Grab operates across fragmented markets with inconsistent data pipelines. Interviewers want to see that you can make a defensible decision with imperfect information rather than waiting for a perfect dataset.

Cross-functional influence without authority. Grab's PM culture typically expects you to align engineering, design, operations, and legal teams without formal power. Stories that show you built consensus across functions land better than stories where you had top-down authority.

Speed and learning loops. Grab moves fast. Interviewers look for candidates who can describe how they shorten the time between hypothesis and evidence, whether through rapid experiments, user interviews, or instrumentation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product deeply. Use Grab across every available vertical: rides, food, mart, pay, and insurance if accessible. Note every friction point. Read Grab's engineering and product blogs. Study their investor materials for strategic priorities.

Week 2: Practise the core question types. Work through at least two metric investigation cases, two prioritisation exercises, and two product design prompts per day. Use the frameworks above. Time yourself: interviewers expect structured answers within a focused 8-10 minute window.

Week 3: Build your story bank. Map your career experiences to the four qualities Grab looks for: market empathy, data comfort, cross-functional influence, and learning speed. Prepare a STAR story for each. Practise with a peer who can push back on your results.

Week 4: Sharpen Southeast Asia context. Read public case studies on super-app growth in Indonesia, Vietnam, and Thailand. Understand how Grab's financial services layer differs from pure payments apps. Prepare a one-minute view on Grab's biggest opportunity and biggest risk in 2026.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Ignoring Southeast Asian context. Candidates who answer Grab product questions as if the users are in a typical metro tech environment rarely get through. Always anchor your user definition to the actual Grab customer base across the region.

Skipping the data-accuracy check. In metric questions, jumping straight to hypotheses without first confirming the measurement is correct signals inexperience. Interviewers at data-driven companies notice this immediately.

Over-engineering the framework. Using a seven-step framework for a simple question wastes time and sounds rehearsed. Match your structure to the complexity of the problem.

Weak or vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'the product did well' without any qualifier leaves interviewers with nothing to evaluate. Be as specific as your NDA allows, and note the sample size or conditions if the number is directional.

Not asking clarifying questions. Grab PMs operate in ambiguous environments. Starting to answer before clarifying the goal, the user segment, or the time horizon signals you are not comfortable with ambiguity, the opposite of what they want.

Treating all Grab markets as identical. A feature or metric that makes sense in Singapore may need significant rethinking for rural Indonesia. Show that you understand this without being asked.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Grab PM interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from four to six rounds, typically starting with a recruiter screen followed by a product sense round, an analytical or metrics round, a behavioural panel, and a final round with senior leadership. Some candidates also report a take-home exercise between rounds. The exact structure can vary by level and team, so confirm with your recruiter after you apply.

What salary should I expect as a PM at Grab in India?

Based on publicly reported figures and industry surveys, PM compensation at Grab in India tends to align with the broader market. Associate PMs can expect around 12-20 LPA, mid-level PMs (3-6 years) around 24-40 LPA, Senior PMs around 40-60 LPA, and Group or Principal PMs around 55-90+ LPA. Grab's offers, candidates report, often include an equity component on top of base salary. Always verify current numbers through Glassdoor or levels.fyi, as bands shift with market conditions.

Do I need prior Southeast Asian work experience to get a PM role at Grab?

You do not need to have worked in Southeast Asia, but you do need to demonstrate genuine understanding of the region's users and markets. Candidates who have studied Southeast Asian consumer behaviour, done product deep-dives on Grab's app, or worked on products serving price-sensitive or connectivity-constrained users tend to perform better. Show intellectual curiosity about the region rather than assuming your Indian market experience maps directly.

How important is SQL or data skill for the Grab PM interview?

Quite important. Grab, candidates report, runs a data-driven culture and expects PMs to be comfortable reading and interpreting data, understanding funnel analysis, and asking the right questions of their analytics partners. You are unlikely to be asked to write raw SQL in the interview, but you should be fluent in the logic behind queries and comfortable discussing cohort analysis, retention curves, and A/B test design without needing a data scientist to translate.

What is the best way to prepare for Grab's product design questions?

Start by using the Grab app extensively and documenting every point of friction and every moment of delight. Then practise designing solutions for Southeast Asian users specifically, not generic users. Use a structured approach: clarify the goal, define the user, identify pain points, generate solutions, prioritise, and define success metrics. Practise out loud so your structure is clear under time pressure.

How many PM jobs are open at Grab right now?

As of early July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 365 open PM roles at Grab. Across the broader India PM market there were 2,009 openings at that time, with Bangalore (271) and Delhi (177) seeing the highest volume. Grab's headcount can fluctuate, so check current listings directly or through a tool that aggregates postings nightly.
