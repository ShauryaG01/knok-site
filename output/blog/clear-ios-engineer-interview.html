01 Overview

Overview

Clear (formerly ClearTax) is one of India's leading fintech platforms, built for income tax filing, GST compliance, and personal finance management. Millions of individuals and businesses file their returns through Clear every year, which means the iOS app must handle high seasonal traffic while keeping sensitive financial data tightly secured. Engineers here are expected to own features end to end, from design review to App Store release.

As of July 2026, Clear has 188 open roles across all functions. Separately, the knok jobradar shows 70 iOS Engineer openings live across the Indian market, with Delhi leading at 21 postings, Bangalore at 6, Mumbai at 3, and Chennai at 2.

The interview process typically runs 3 to 5 rounds. Candidates report a recruiter screen, a coding round (live or take-home), one or two deep technical interviews, and a final discussion with a senior engineer or hiring manager. Interviewers focus heavily on Swift fundamentals, iOS security architecture, and your ability to make sound decisions for a compliance-sensitive, data-heavy product.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Clear iOS interviews, based on what candidates report and the nature of the product.

  1. Optimising a slow list view. How do you fix scroll jitter in a UITableView or UICollectionView showing large tax or transaction data? Be ready to discuss cell reuse, height caching, and background image loading.
  1. Secure local storage. Clear handles PAN numbers, Aadhaar-linked data, and bank details. How do you choose between the Keychain, encrypted Core Data, and NSFileProtection for different sensitivity levels?
  1. App lifecycle and crash debugging. Walk through UIApplicationDelegate states. How would you track down a crash that appears only when the app resumes from background?
  1. Offline-first architecture. Have you built features using Core Data, Realm, or SQLite that work without a network connection? Clear's filing flow must survive spotty connectivity.
  1. Complex multi-step form architecture. Clear's ITR filing is a long, branching form. How do you design navigation, state management, and field validation in Swift across many screens?
  1. Unit testing with network dependencies. How do you write testable ViewModels or Interactors that call APIs without hitting real endpoints during tests?
  1. App size and launch time. Have you profiled and reduced IPA size or cold-launch time? Walk through the tools you used and what improved.
  1. API versioning and backward compatibility. In a financial app with mandatory updates and wide OS support, how do you handle breaking backend changes without crashing older builds?
  1. Push notifications end to end. Explain the APNs flow from server certificate to on-device handling, including silent notifications and interactive notification actions.
  1. Memory management in scroll-heavy screens. What tools do you use to find retain cycles? How do you structure delegates and closures to avoid leaks?
  1. Accessibility. How do you approach VoiceOver support and Dynamic Type in an app where the content is complex and data-dense?
  1. Retry and error handling in critical flows. In a tax submission screen, a dropped request cannot fail silently. How do you design retry logic, idempotency handling, and user-facing error states?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you handle secure local storage of sensitive financial data on iOS?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built a personal finance app where users stored bank account numbers and credit scores on-device for offline access.

*Task:* I needed a storage approach that was fast for read-heavy dashboards and met the security requirements our compliance team had laid out.

*Action:* I used the iOS Keychain for high-sensitivity fields like session tokens and account numbers, wrapped in a small Swift abstraction so the rest of the team never touched Keychain APIs directly. For larger structured data, I used Core Data with NSFileProtection set to 'CompleteUnlessOpen', which keeps files encrypted when the device is locked. I wrote a short internal doc explaining the threat model so new engineers understood the reasoning behind each choice.

*Result:* The app passed a third-party security audit with no findings related to local storage. New engineers onboarded to the security architecture noticeably faster because the decisions were already documented and justified.

---

Q: How would you optimise a slow UITableView showing transaction history?

*Situation:* At a fintech startup I worked at, the transaction history screen had visible scroll jitter on mid-range iPhones, and users were calling it out in App Store reviews.

*Task:* I needed to fix the jitter without redesigning the screen, which used dynamic row heights and small category icons per row.

*Action:* I used Instruments with the Core Animation template to identify which cells were triggering expensive layout passes during every scroll event. I pre-calculated and cached row heights on a background thread so layout was never recomputed mid-scroll. I replaced live image loading with a lightweight cache using URLSession and NSCache, cancelling in-flight requests when cells were recycled. I also replaced a gradient layer that was being redrawn on every layout pass with a static cached snapshot.

*Result:* Scroll performance became smooth and consistent on mid-range devices, confirmed through Instruments profiling. App Store reviews mentioning performance issues dropped noticeably in the following release cycle.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved test coverage for a module that had almost none.

*Situation:* I joined a team mid-project where the payment confirmation module had zero unit tests. Every release involved full manual regression on those screens.

*Task:* I was asked to add meaningful coverage before a major feature launch without delaying the launch date.

*Action:* I refactored the ViewModel to accept a protocol-based network dependency rather than a concrete class, which let me inject a mock in tests without touching production behaviour. I wrote tests covering the happy path, network failure, and timeout cases. I also added a snapshot test for the payment success screen to catch unintended UI regressions automatically.

*Result:* Two edge-case bugs surfaced during test authoring that would have reached production otherwise. The team adopted the protocol-injection pattern for all new modules, and the test suite ran automatically in CI from that release onward.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioural question. Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief, roughly two to three sentences each. Spend most of your time on Action, because that is what the interviewer is evaluating. Result should be specific: a measurable outcome, a business change, or a shift in team practice.

For technical questions, use a think-out-loud structure. Restate the question in your own words first. State your assumptions. Describe your approach before writing any code. After finishing, name one thing you would do differently with more time. This signals structured thinking, which Clear values as much as fluent syntax.

Quantify where you can, hedge where you cannot. If you do not have an exact number, say 'roughly' or 'around' rather than inventing a figure. Fintech interviewers tend to probe claims more carefully than those at general product companies.

Anchor system design answers in Clear's product context. The app serves users during high-traffic ITR filing deadlines. Mentioning graceful degradation, request queuing, and offline resilience, even when not directly asked, shows that you think about the product as well as the code.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Strong Swift fundamentals. Candidates report that interviewers probe deeply on value types vs reference types, ARC, and protocol-oriented design. Surface-level answers are quickly followed with 'why' questions, so prepare to justify every architectural choice you describe.

A security mindset by default. Clear stores and transmits sensitive financial data. Interviewers expect you to mention encryption, secure transport, and data minimisation without being prompted. Referencing Apple's security documentation or the OWASP Mobile Top 10 signals the right instinct.

Ownership over the full feature lifecycle. Clear typically looks for engineers who can take a feature from design review through to App Store release without constant direction. Give examples where you spotted a UX problem or a performance issue before it was formally assigned to you.

Ability to collaborate across teams. The iOS team works closely with backend, design, and compliance. Showing that you have navigated API contract disagreements or worked through accessibility reviews with other disciplines is a strong positive signal.

Genuine product curiosity. Interviewers commonly ask what you think of the Clear app or why you want to work in fintech. Have a real answer ready. Using the app before your interview is the minimum expected preparation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core iOS revision.
Revise Swift memory management, concurrency (async/await and GCD), and the UIKit app lifecycle. Write a couple of small projects from scratch to rebuild your coding speed and fluency. Review your most recent iOS project and be ready to walk through every architecture decision and the reasoning behind it.

Week 2: Security and fintech specifics.
Read Apple's Keychain Services documentation and the Secure Enclave overview. Try the Clear app and note how it handles session expiry, biometric login, and error states in the filing flow. Think about what the iOS layer specifically needs to do to support a tax product reliably at deadline-season scale.

Week 3: System design and behavioural preparation.
Practise one system design scenario per day: offline sync, push notification architecture, modular app structure. Write out your strongest three to four career stories in STAR format and time yourself telling each one in under two minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and active applications.
Do at least a couple of live mock interviews with a peer or through a platform offering iOS mock sessions. Review your answers for vague results and sharpen them with specific, concrete outcomes. Apply to Clear iOS roles and follow up with the recruiter after one week if you have not heard back.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Treating security as an afterthought. Candidates who say 'I would add encryption later' or who cannot name a specific iOS security API are filtered out early at fintech companies. Prepare security answers proactively and bring them up before being prompted.

2. Memorising syntax instead of explaining trade-offs. Interviewers at Clear typically care more about why you chose structs over classes, or async/await over callbacks, than whether you can recite API names from memory. Practise articulating your reasoning out loud.

3. Giving vague STAR results. 'The app got faster' is not a result. 'Scroll jitter disappeared on mid-range devices, confirmed in Instruments, and user reviews tagging performance dropped in the next cycle' is a result. Review every prepared answer for a specific, concrete outcome.

4. Not using the app. Arriving at the interview without having explored Clear signals low interest. Even a trial run through a dummy return gives you specific product context to reference in your answers.

5. Skipping clarifying questions on technical rounds. Jumping straight into code before confirming requirements is a common mistake. Interviewers want to see you ask one or two scoping questions before you start.

6. Under-preparing for concurrency. GCD, async/await, actors, and race conditions in UI updates come up in almost every senior iOS interview at a product-led company. Do not leave this topic until the last minute.

How many rounds does the Clear iOS interview typically have?

Candidates report a process of roughly 3 to 5 rounds. This typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical round with live coding or a take-home assignment, one or two deep technical interviews, and a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager. What salary can I expect for an iOS Engineer role at Clear?

Clear does not publicly list salary bands for iOS roles. Is the Clear iOS interview more focused on UIKit or SwiftUI?

Candidates report that both come up, but UIKit depth is tested more thoroughly because Clear's app relies heavily on UIKit for its complex multi-step form flows. Demonstrating SwiftUI knowledge and discussing when you would use each framework is a positive signal. Be prepared to explain how UIKit and SwiftUI interoperate, especially for incremental migration scenarios.

How much fintech domain knowledge do I need going into the interview?

Using the Clear app before your interview and forming a specific opinion on one or two product decisions is a practical way to demonstrate this.
