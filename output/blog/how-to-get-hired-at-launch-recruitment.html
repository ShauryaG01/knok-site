01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Launch Recruitment is a staffing and recruitment firm that connects businesses with talent across industries. Getting hired here means joining their internal team, typically as a recruitment consultant, senior consultant, business development specialist, or operations support professional. As of mid-2026, Launch Recruitment has 37 open roles across its business, which signals an active growth phase worth acting on quickly.

Working at a recruitment company is different from most corporate roles. Your ability to build relationships, match candidates to clients, and meet targets directly shapes your results and your income. It is a high-energy, people-first environment. If you enjoy conversations, problem-solving, and working towards clear goals, this kind of role can be genuinely rewarding.

Launch Recruitment typically hires across experience levels, from those entering the talent industry for the first time to experienced consultants who bring established client relationships. The process is structured but practical, focused on whether you can actually do the job rather than on lengthy theoretical assessments.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Launch Recruitment typically follows a sequence of stages, though the exact number of rounds can vary by role level and location. The description below reflects what candidates commonly report for internal hiring at recruitment firms.

Stage 1: Application and initial screening
After you apply, a member of the team reviews your CV for relevant experience, communication clarity, and any background in sales, HR, or client-facing roles. If your profile is a good match, expect a brief screening call by phone or video, where they confirm your interest, availability, and basic suitability for the role.

Stage 2: Competency interview
This is typically the core stage. A hiring manager or senior consultant will ask about your past experience using structured, situation-based questions. They want real examples: a time you had to persuade someone, a difficult conversation you handled well, or a goal you worked hard to hit. Candidates for consultant roles may also be asked to walk through how they would approach a new client brief or a candidate search.

Stage 3: Skills exercise or role-play (for consultant roles)
For recruitment-facing positions, some candidates are asked to complete a short practical exercise. This might be a mock candidate call, a cold outreach pitch, or a brief presentation on how you would approach filling a specific role. The goal is to see how you think on your feet, not to test industry jargon.

Stage 4: Final discussion or offer stage
Shortlisted candidates typically have a final conversation with a senior leader or director. This round is partly a culture fit check and partly an opportunity for you to ask questions about the team, targets, and growth path. Offer details, including base and commission structure, are usually discussed here.

The whole process from application to offer commonly takes a few weeks, though urgent roles can move faster.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Launch Recruitment, like most recruitment firms, is looking for a specific mix of personality, drive, and practical skill. Technical qualifications matter less here than they do in engineering or finance roles. What stands out is how you communicate, whether you are coachable, and whether you show genuine hunger to build something.

Communication clarity
Recruitment is built on conversations. Every interaction, whether with a candidate or a client, reflects on the firm. They want people who speak and write clearly, listen well, and can adapt their tone depending on who they are talking to.

Resilience and target orientation
Recruitment involves rejection. Candidates drop out, clients go silent, roles get closed without warning. Interviewers will probe whether you bounce back quickly and whether targets motivate rather than paralyse you. Be ready to talk about a time things did not go to plan and what you did next.

Relationship-building instinct
The best recruiters build trust over time. Launch Recruitment will want to see that you are someone who follows up, remembers details, and makes people feel heard. This often shows up in small ways during the interview itself, so treat every interaction with the team as part of the assessment.

Curiosity about people and industries
Recruiters who take a genuine interest in the sectors they cover, and who understand what companies are actually trying to build, consistently outperform those who treat it as a pure numbers game. Show that you are curious, not just transactional.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparing for a Launch Recruitment interview is less about cramming technical knowledge and more about sharpening how you present yourself and your stories.

Know your own CV inside out
Every line on your CV is fair game. If you list a sales role, be ready to talk about your method and your outcomes. If you mention a team project, be ready to explain your specific contribution. Vague answers about past experience are a red flag in a role where precision and clarity are core requirements.

Prepare situation-based examples
Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure your answers. Focus on examples that show persuasion, resilience, relationship-building, or target achievement. Recruitment interviews lean heavily on this style of questioning, so prepare at least four or five strong examples before you walk in.

Research Launch Recruitment itself
Look at the sectors they specialise in, the kinds of roles they fill, and any recent growth or news they have shared publicly. Being able to say 'I noticed you have been growing your tech and finance desks' shows you have done more than a surface-level search.

Practise your pitch
For consultant roles, you may be asked to do a short pitch or a mock candidate call. Practise speaking about a role or a candidate confidently and concisely. Focus on being natural rather than scripted, because scripted delivery is easy to spot and reads as low confidence.

Prepare sharp questions for them
Asking good questions signals that you are serious. Ask about how the team is structured, what success looks like in the first few months, how billing targets are set, and what career progression has looked like for people in similar roles.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Recruitment firms tend to have a distinct culture: fast-moving, social, and results-focused. Launch Recruitment reflects these traits, with an environment that rewards people who take initiative and hold themselves accountable.

Performance and recognition
In recruitment, your output is visible. Placements, client relationships, and billing are tracked closely, and high performers are typically recognised quickly through commission, title progression, and greater autonomy. This suits people who like direct, frequent feedback on how they are doing rather than a once-a-year review.

Team collaboration alongside individual drive
While individual targets matter, most recruitment teams also share leads, refer candidates across desks, and support each other through slower periods. The culture tends to be collegial rather than cutthroat, though the dynamic can vary by team and by the seniority of the desk.

Learning on the job
Most learning in recruitment happens through doing. New joiners typically shadow senior consultants, sit in on client calls, and get hands-on experience quickly. If you prefer structured classroom training over learning by doing, set that expectation early in your conversations with the hiring team so there are no surprises.

Straight talk
Recruitment professionals tend to be direct. Feedback is given openly, and the expectation is that you act on it without taking it personally. If you thrive in environments with honest, frequent feedback, this culture will suit you well.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need prior recruitment experience to apply at Launch Recruitment?

Not necessarily. Many recruitment firms hire graduates and career changers with strong sales, customer service, or HR backgrounds and train them in the specifics of recruiting. What matters more is your communication ability, your attitude towards targets, and your willingness to learn. If you have no direct recruitment experience, lean into transferable skills like persuasion, relationship management, or any role where you had to consistently meet performance goals.

What does a typical day look like as a consultant at Launch Recruitment?

A typical day involves a mix of outreach to candidates, calls with clients, CV review, and pipeline management. You might spend the morning sourcing profiles for an active role, have client check-in calls in the afternoon, and end the day following up on applications you have placed. The pace is high and no two days are identical, which many people in the industry find motivating. Team stand-ups and target reviews are usually built into the weekly rhythm as well.

How is pay structured at recruitment firms like Launch Recruitment?

Recruitment roles usually combine a fixed base salary with a commission structure tied to placements made. The exact split varies by company and seniority, and industry surveys suggest commission can form a significant share of total earnings for strong performers over time. Ask during your final interview exactly how targets are set, how commission is calculated, and what the ramp-up period looks like for new joiners before full commission eligibility kicks in.

How long does the hiring process typically take?

Based on what candidates commonly report for recruitment firm roles, the process from application to offer typically runs a few weeks. Urgent roles or positions with straightforward requirements can move faster. Following up after each stage is completely normal in this industry, so do not hesitate to send a polite check-in if you have not heard back within a reasonable time after your interview.

What are the biggest mistakes candidates make in recruitment interviews?

The most common mistakes include giving vague answers about past experience, failing to research the company before the interview, and not having specific examples ready for situation-based questions. Candidates also sometimes underestimate the importance of the questions they ask at the end of the interview. A sharp, informed question shows genuine interest and business awareness, while coming across as passive or uncertain about whether you want the role is a real risk in a culture that rewards people who are clear about their goals.

Launch Recruitment currently has 37 open roles. How do I figure out which one to apply for?

Start by matching the role to your experience level and the sector you are most knowledgeable about or excited by. Applying to a role in a sector you already understand gives you a natural edge in interviews because you can speak credibly about the kinds of candidates clients typically need. Read each job description carefully and tailor your CV to the specific requirements rather than sending a generic application. If you are genuinely unsure which role fits best, reaching out to someone on the team for a brief informational conversation is a legitimate move and demonstrates exactly the kind of proactive behaviour recruitment firms value.
