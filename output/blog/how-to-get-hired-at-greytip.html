01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Greytip Software is one of India's well-known HR tech companies, recognised mainly for greytHR, its cloud-based HR and payroll platform serving small and mid-sized businesses across India and the Middle East. The product is used by thousands of companies to run attendance, leave, and payroll for their employees, which means Greytip sits right at the core of how people get paid every month.

As of mid-2026, Greytip has 78 open roles across functions including engineering, sales, customer success, implementation, and product. Whether you are a fresher keen on HR tech or an experienced professional looking to move into a product company with real HR domain exposure, there is likely something on the list for you.

Most roles are based out of Bengaluru, with some hybrid and remote positions showing up occasionally depending on the team. The company is privately held and has been on a steady hiring path, so getting your application in now makes sense.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Greytip's hiring process commonly runs through four or five stages, though the exact number varies by role and team. The outline below reflects what candidates generally report; treat it as a guide rather than a fixed sequence.

Application and resume shortlist
Your resume goes through an initial review by the talent team. Roles in sales and customer success tend to move faster, while engineering and product roles may take a bit longer. Mentioning HRMS, payroll, or SaaS experience in your resume helps it stand out.

HR screening call
A recruiter will get in touch to verify your background, current salary expectation, notice period, and interest in the role. Keep your answers honest and concise. Be ready to explain why you want to work in HR tech specifically, not just any software company.

Technical or functional round
For engineering roles, this usually involves a coding exercise or a system design discussion. For customer success, implementation, or sales roles, expect a practical scenario around an HR or payroll problem. Candidates commonly report that Greytip's questions lean towards real-world situations rather than abstract puzzles.

Managerial or panel round
You will likely speak with a hiring manager or a small panel. This round focuses on your past experience, how you approach problems, and how you collaborate with customers or other teams. Product candidates can expect questions on product thinking and prioritisation.

Final round and offer discussion
For senior roles, there may be a short conversation with a senior leader. Salary negotiation and joining dates are typically discussed here or shortly after. Candidates commonly report the full process from first contact to offer takes around two to four weeks, though it can stretch if there are multiple panel members to coordinate.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Domain knowledge matters here
Greytip builds software for HR and payroll, so familiarity with concepts like attendance management, leave policies, statutory compliance (PF, ESI, TDS), and payroll cycles gives you a real edge, especially for non-engineering roles. You do not need to be an HR professional, but you should be able to hold a conversation about how a small company manages its payroll.

For engineering candidates
Greytip looks for solid fundamentals in Java or similar backend technologies, along with experience building scalable web applications. Experience with SaaS products, multi-tenant architecture, or any HR/payroll tool is a plus. Front-end and full-stack skills are valued for product-facing roles.

For customer-facing roles
Customer success and implementation roles require patience, strong communication, and the ability to explain complex payroll or compliance topics in plain terms to HR managers who are not tech-savvy. Regional language skills such as Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, or Hindi are often a practical advantage.

Attitude and ownership
Greytip is a mid-sized product company, which means individuals often wear multiple hats. They tend to value people who take ownership of problems rather than wait for someone else to solve them, and who can work with some ambiguity without needing everything spelled out.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know the product before you apply
Spend time with greytHR before your interview. Sign up for a free trial if one is available, or watch demo videos to understand the key modules: payroll, attendance, leave, and compliance. Being able to reference specific features shows genuine interest and curiosity, not just a rehearsed answer.

Brush up on HR and payroll basics
Even for engineering roles, understanding what the software actually does helps you give sharper answers. Know what CTC, gross salary, and take-home mean. Understand what statutory deductions like PF and professional tax are. For functional roles, go deeper: read about India's labour law updates and common payroll compliance challenges that small businesses face.

Prepare your 'why HR tech' answer
Interviewers will almost certainly ask why you want to join Greytip. Have a genuine, specific answer ready. Talking about the impact of getting payroll right for small businesses, or about the scale at which greytHR operates, works better than a generic 'I want to work in a product company.'

Practice scenario-based answers
For customer success and implementation roles, prepare two or three examples of times you handled a difficult client, explained a complex topic to a non-technical person, or resolved a problem under pressure. Use the situation-action-result format to keep your answers focused.

Research salary expectations
Before the HR screening call, have a clear sense of what you expect. Check publicly reported compensation data on platforms like Glassdoor or levels.fyi for similar roles in Bengaluru. Know your current CTC and your target, and be prepared to justify the difference.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

SME-first mindset
Greytip has built its business around serving small and mid-sized companies, a segment that is often overlooked by larger enterprise software vendors. This shapes how the team thinks: solutions need to be simple, affordable, and easy to implement without a lengthy consulting engagement. If you enjoy making complex things accessible, this culture will suit you.

Collaborative and relatively flat
For a company of its size, Greytip tends to keep hierarchies manageable. Individual contributors often get direct access to product and leadership teams, which means your ideas can travel faster than they might in a large IT company. At the same time, this means you are expected to be proactive rather than waiting for direction.

Compliance-driven responsibility
Payroll software carries real accountability. If the software fails, employees may not get paid on time, or statutory filings can go wrong. This creates a culture where accuracy, reliability, and customer trust are taken seriously. People who are detail-oriented and genuinely care about getting things right tend to thrive here.

Growth-oriented environment
Greytip has been expanding its product footprint and geographic reach over recent years. Employees who want to grow with a scaling product company, take on new responsibilities, and build a career in HR tech will find the environment encouraging. The company regularly promotes from within for senior individual contributor and management roles.
