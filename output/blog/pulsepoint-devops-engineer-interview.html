02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Pulsepoint DevOps interviews, based on what candidates report and what the role demands for a data-heavy, always-on ad platform.

  1. Walk me through a CI/CD pipeline you built or owned end to end. What did you automate, and where did things break?
  2. How have you managed Kubernetes clusters in production? Talk through upgrades, scaling policies, and handling failing pods under load.
  3. Pulsepoint processes health-related advertising data under compliance requirements. How have you handled secrets management and access controls in a regulated environment?
  4. Describe how you structured Terraform (or another IaC tool) to manage cloud resources across multiple environments: dev, staging, and production.
  5. What does your observability setup look like? Walk us through how you would design alerting for a service that requires very low latency.
  6. Tell me about a time an infrastructure incident happened on your watch. How did you detect it, contain it, and stop it from repeating?
  7. How do you handle database migrations in a zero-downtime deployment pipeline?
  8. Real-time bidding workloads spike heavily at certain hours. How would you design autoscaling for a service with that traffic pattern?
  9. What is your go-to scripting language for automation, and can you walk through a non-trivial script you wrote that saved your team real time?
  10. How do you manage cloud cost in an account that is growing fast? What specific levers have you pulled?
  11. Describe your experience with inter-service communication in a microservices setup, whether through a service mesh or another approach.
  12. How would you approach migrating a legacy monolith into containers without disrupting what is already live in production?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a CI/CD pipeline you built or owned end to end.

*Situation:* Our backend team was deploying manually to three environments, causing frequent configuration drift and late-night rollbacks that were burning out the team.

*Task:* I was asked to design and implement a fully automated CI/CD pipeline that would give every engineer a single, reliable deploy path.

*Action:* I set up GitHub Actions workflows that ran unit tests and integration tests on every pull request. On merge to main, the pipeline built a Docker image, pushed it to ECR, and triggered a rolling update to our EKS cluster using Helm. I added automated smoke tests post-deploy that rolled back automatically on failure, and integrated Trivy for container image scanning so security checks happened inside the pipeline rather than as a separate gate engineers would skip.

*Result:* Deploy frequency increased, manual steps dropped to zero, and the on-call team saw fewer rollback incidents. Engineers started deploying on Fridays, which had previously been off-limits.

---

Q: Describe how you handled an infrastructure incident on your watch.

*Situation:* A traffic spike caused our Redis cache layer to run out of memory, which cascaded into database overload during peak hours on a production service.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer responsible for restoring service quickly and then making sure the same cascade could not happen again.

*Action:* I identified the root cause using Datadog dashboards and correlated the spike with a product campaign launch that had not been flagged to the infrastructure team. I scaled up the Redis cluster and added a circuit breaker at the application layer as an immediate fix. In the following week, I introduced memory-based autoscaling alerts and a pre-launch checklist requiring product to notify infra before campaigns above a defined traffic threshold.

*Result:* Service was restored within the hour. Over the three months after the process change, we had no further surprise-cascade incidents tied to campaign launches.

---

Q: How do you manage cloud cost in a fast-growing account?

*Situation:* Our AWS bill had grown significantly quarter over quarter, and the engineering team had almost no visibility into which services were driving costs.

*Task:* Leadership asked me to bring cost visibility to the team and reduce waste without impacting reliability or developer velocity.

*Action:* I enabled AWS Cost Explorer tagging by team and service, then set up a weekly cost digest sent to each team lead. I identified several waste categories: oversized RDS instances running at low utilisation, dev environments left running overnight, and unattached EBS volumes accumulating over time. I wrote a Lambda function that stopped non-production instances after hours and set up S3 lifecycle policies on log buckets that had no retention rules.

*Result:* Each team had real numbers to act on for the first time. Industry surveys and publicly reported cloud cost initiatives commonly cite savings in the range of 20-30 percent from this type of cleanup, and our results were broadly consistent with that, though exact figures were kept internal.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR, but keep it tight. Pulsepoint interviewers are engineers, not HR panels, and they will follow up with deep technical questions. Keep your Situation and Task to one or two sentences each, and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

For system design or architecture questions, start by clarifying constraints: scale, latency targets, cost envelope. Then walk through your design choices and tradeoffs before naming the specific tools and services you would use. Engineers want to see how you think, not just which products you know.

For 'how do you approach X' questions, structure your answer as: what you look at first, what signals tell you something is wrong, what you do about it, and how you prevent recurrence. This pattern shows both technical fluency and operational maturity.

When you do not know something, say so and reason aloud. Interviewers at product companies like Pulsepoint value engineers who can think through unfamiliar problems over ones who claim wide expertise but cannot go deep.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Operational ownership, not just tool knowledge. Pulsepoint runs production systems that affect healthcare advertisers, so interviewers want evidence that you have been accountable for uptime, not just a contributor to infrastructure tasks. Be ready to discuss on-call experience and how you have improved systems after incidents.

Cloud-native fluency at depth. Expect technical follow-ups on Kubernetes, containers, and cloud services. Candidates report that surface-level answers get probed quickly. Know your tools well enough to discuss failure modes and edge cases, not just the happy path.

Security and compliance awareness. Because Pulsepoint operates in the health advertising space, interviewers care about how you handle sensitive data, secrets, and access controls. Even for roles that are not purely security-focused, showing that you build with compliance in mind stands out.

Cross-functional collaboration. DevOps at a product company means working closely with backend engineers, data teams, and product managers. Interviewers look for candidates who have navigated those relationships well, especially during incidents or migrations where priorities conflict.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical review.
Revise Kubernetes fundamentals: pod lifecycle, deployments, services, ingress, and horizontal pod autoscaling. Review Terraform state management, module structure, and remote backends. Refresh your knowledge of at least one major cloud provider's compute, networking, and managed database offerings.

Week 2: Scenario and system design practice.
Practice designing a CI/CD pipeline from scratch using a whiteboard or a blank doc. Think through a zero-downtime deployment for a stateful service. Prepare one strong incident story in STAR format, with concrete outcomes where you have them.

Week 3: Pulsepoint-specific preparation.
Read publicly available information about Pulsepoint's technology and the programmatic advertising space. Think about how real-time bidding systems differ from typical web apps in terms of latency, throughput, and failure tolerance. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask your interviewers about team structure, on-call culture, and current infrastructure challenges.

Before the interview:
Review your resume for any claims you cannot back up with specific detail. Confirm the interview format with your recruiter. Test your audio and screen-sharing setup if the session is remote.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about tools without talking about problems. Saying 'I use Kubernetes and Terraform' without explaining what problems they solved or what tradeoffs you made gives the interviewer nothing to work with. Always anchor your tool choices to a real situation and a real outcome.

Skipping the 'why' in design questions. Candidates often jump straight to a solution when asked to design a system. Pulsepoint interviewers want to see how you think, so walk through constraints and tradeoffs before naming the services you would use.

Treating incidents as embarrassing. Some candidates rush past incidents or downplay them. In reality, how you handled a production incident is one of the strongest signals of operational maturity. Own the story, including what went wrong, what you did, and what you changed afterward.

Ignoring the health and compliance angle. Pulsepoint is not a generic SaaS company. If every answer treats compliance requirements as irrelevant, you miss a key part of what their environment demands from a DevOps engineer.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team's current challenges, on-call culture, and what success looks like in the first few months in the role.

How many interview rounds does Pulsepoint typically have for DevOps roles?

Candidates report a process that typically involves a recruiter or HR screening call, a technical discussion with a senior engineer, and a final loop that may include system design and behavioural questions with multiple team members. The exact number of rounds varies based on the seniority of the role. Confirm the format with your recruiter at the start so you can prepare accordingly.

What cloud platform should I prepare for in a Pulsepoint DevOps interview?

Based on publicly available information, AWS is commonly cited as the primary cloud environment for companies in the programmatic advertising space, and Pulsepoint fits that profile. Prepare to discuss AWS compute, networking, managed databases, and cost management in depth. Candidates with multi-cloud experience can draw parallels, but grounding your answers in AWS specifics is the safer bet.

Will there be a coding or scripting test in the interview?

Candidates report that scripting ability is assessed, usually through a practical question involving Python or Bash rather than a competitive-programming-style algorithm problem. You may be asked to write or review a script that automates a common DevOps task, or to debug a short piece of infrastructure code. Brushing up on Python for automation tasks and Bash for system administration covers the practical preparation.

How important is compliance knowledge for this DevOps role?

Pulsepoint operates in the health advertising space, which brings data privacy and compliance considerations into day-to-day infrastructure work. Candidates who can speak to secrets management, least-privilege access policies, and audit logging will have a clear advantage. You do not need deep regulatory expertise, but showing that you build infrastructure with these constraints in mind signals the right mindset.
