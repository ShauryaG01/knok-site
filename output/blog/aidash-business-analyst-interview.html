02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates at AiDASH typically report a process that includes a recruiter screen, one or two domain or technical rounds, and a final conversation with a senior stakeholder. Questions tend to focus on requirements gathering, data interpretation, and client handling in regulated industries.

  1. How would you approach gathering requirements from a utility company migrating from manual vegetation inspection to AiDASH's satellite-based system?

  3. AiDASH products process satellite imagery through ML models. How would you explain a model's confidence score to a non-technical operations manager at a utility firm?
  4. Describe a time you identified a gap between what a client asked for and what they actually needed. How did you close that gap?

  8. Describe your experience writing user stories or functional specifications for a SaaS product. What does a good acceptance criterion look like to you?
  9. AiDASH serves clients in regulated industries. How would you handle a client requirement that conflicts with their own compliance constraints?
  10. How would you use SQL or a BI tool to build a report showing a utility client's monthly vegetation clearance progress?
  11. Tell us about a time you collaborated with a data science or engineering team to scope a technically complex feature under a tight deadline.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural questions. Here are three model answers tailored to AiDASH's context.

---

Q: How would you approach gathering requirements from a utility company migrating to a satellite-based monitoring system?

*Situation:* At my previous company we were onboarding a large infrastructure client that had relied on spreadsheets and periodic field visits to track asset health across hundreds of kilometres of transmission lines.

*Task:* I was responsible for capturing their existing workflows and converting them into product requirements the engineering team could implement within an agreed timeline.

*Action:* I ran separate discovery workshops with the operations manager and field supervisors because their priorities were very different. I mapped their current process as a simple flowchart, pinpointed three handoff points where data was regularly lost or delayed, and wrote a requirements document with explicit acceptance criteria for each item. I also flagged two legacy data formats the client depended on as integration risks before sprint planning started.

*Result:* The onboarding completed on schedule. The integration risk I raised led the product team to build a data import feature that later benefited several other clients. The client reported a meaningful reduction in their weekly reporting workload.

---

Q: How would you prioritise a backlog when multiple teams have competing requests?

*Situation:* At a previous role, the sales team wanted a reporting module promised to a new prospect, the customer success team needed a bug fix for an enterprise client, and the data team wanted infrastructure work to improve model refresh speed.

*Task:* I had to present a prioritised backlog recommendation to the product head by end of the week.

*Action:* I scored each item on business impact (revenue risk, retention risk, strategic fit) and estimated development effort, then prepared three prioritisation scenarios with trade-offs clearly explained. I also spoke with the enterprise client's account manager to check whether a temporary workaround could hold while the bug fix was scheduled.

*Result:* The bug fix moved to the top of the sprint, the reporting module was scoped down to a version the prospect accepted for the next sprint, and the infrastructure work was planned for the following quarter. The product head found the scenario-based presentation more useful than a single ranked list.

---

Q: A client says the risk scores on their dashboard do not match their ground inspections. How would you investigate?

*Situation:* A nearly identical issue came up when I was a BA at an analytics firm. A client flagged that our risk predictions for a specific corridor did not match what their crews found on the ground.

*Task:* I needed to determine whether the root cause was a data pipeline error, a model limitation, or a misunderstanding of what the score represented.

*Action:* I asked the client for specific examples with coordinates and dates. Working with the data engineer, I traced the satellite image acquisition timestamps against the client's inspection dates and found that two images had cloud cover above the acceptable threshold but had not been flagged in the pipeline. I also clarified that our score reflected a rolling average window rather than a point-in-time reading, which had not been explained clearly in the dashboard.

*Result:* The pipeline was updated to surface low-quality images more visibly. A plain-English tooltip explaining the scoring method was added to the dashboard. The client confirmed the discrepancy was resolved and gave a positive rating in their quarterly review.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioural questions at AiDASH. Keep each component concise: a sentence or two for Situation and Task, three to four sentences for Action, and a concrete or qualitative Result.

MoSCoW for prioritisation questions. When asked how you would prioritise a backlog or feature set, use Must-have, Should-have, Could-have, and Won't-have to show structured thinking. Pair it with a simple impact-vs-effort matrix to show you weigh development cost alongside business value.

CIRCLES for product sense questions. If asked to evaluate a product decision or design a new feature, walk through: Comprehend the situation, Identify the customer, Report customer needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise your recommendation. This signals product thinking that AiDASH interviewers value.

The 'so what' test for data questions. When presenting any analysis, always end with a business recommendation. Interviewers at AI-SaaS companies want to see that you move from insight to action, not just observation.

Domain bridging for technical gaps. For geospatial or ML questions where you lack deep expertise, use the phrase 'I would validate this with the data team, but my working hypothesis is...' This shows intellectual honesty without underselling your analytical capability.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity, not just domain knowledge. AiDASH works at the intersection of satellite technology, climate science, and utility operations. Interviewers typically look for candidates who are genuinely curious about how infrastructure companies operate and willing to learn specialised concepts like vegetation risk modelling or right-of-way compliance.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Business problems at a growth-stage AI company are often poorly defined. Interviewers want to see that you ask the right clarifying questions, break a vague problem into components, and communicate a structured approach before jumping to solutions.

Client empathy. AiDASH's clients are often large utilities or government infrastructure bodies with conservative cultures. Candidates who demonstrate patience, clear written communication, and the ability to explain AI-generated outputs in plain operational terms tend to score well.

Comfort with data and basic technical concepts. You do not need to write production code, but you should be comfortable reading SQL queries, understanding what a confidence interval means, and spotting data quality issues. Working directly with a BI tool or spreadsheet to validate a claim is a practical plus.

Ownership mindset. AiDASH is a growth-stage company. Interviewers look for candidates who proactively flag risks, follow up without being prompted, and treat client outcomes as personal goals rather than ticket items.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Understand AiDASH's product and market first. Read the company's public case studies, press releases, and any investor announcements. Understand what vegetation management means for electricity utilities, why it matters for safety and grid reliability, and how satellite imagery improves on traditional inspection. Note the specific problems their products solve and the client segments they serve.

Brush up on BA fundamentals. Review how to write user stories with clear acceptance criteria. Refresh your knowledge of Agile sprint ceremonies and backlog management. Practise drawing simple process flow diagrams from memory.

Prepare your STAR stories. Map your past experience to the questions listed above. Write out full STAR answers for the scenarios most relevant to your background and rehearse them aloud. Focus especially on stories involving cross-functional collaboration or difficult client situations.

Practise SQL and data interpretation. Be ready to write a basic SELECT query with GROUP BY and WHERE clauses. Know how to read a dashboard and identify anomalies. Be ready to explain what a trend or a percentage change means in business terms, not just mathematical terms.

Prepare thoughtful questions for the interviewer. Research AiDASH's recent news and customer announcements. Prepare a few specific questions: for example, how the BA team interfaces with the data science team day-to-day, or what a typical client onboarding engagement looks like from start to finish.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going straight to a solution without clarifying the problem. Interviewers often ask open-ended questions to see whether you will rush. Spend a moment restating your understanding of the problem before giving a response. This alone separates strong candidates from weaker ones.

Using jargon the interviewer did not use first. Terms like 'synergy', 'leverage', or 'agile transformation' signal template-reciting rather than genuine thinking. Use plain language and introduce technical terms only when you can define them in context.

Describing analysis without connecting it to a decision. Saying 'I built a dashboard' is incomplete. The interviewer wants to know what decision the dashboard informed and what happened as a result.

Underestimating domain preparation. Candidates who arrive without basic knowledge of what a utility company does or why vegetation management near power lines matters are at an immediate disadvantage. Even a focused session of research on electric utility operations sets you apart from most applicants.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'The client was happy' is a weak result. Anchor your result to a concrete outcome: faster delivery, fewer errors, a positive review, or a specific decision that followed. If precise figures are not available, describe the qualitative change clearly.

Asking no questions at the end. A BA role requires asking good questions for a living. Arriving with no questions signals low interest or poor preparation.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does AiDASH typically have for a Business Analyst role?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds: a recruiter or HR screen, a domain or case round with a product manager or senior BA, and often a final conversation with a business leader. Some candidates mention a short take-home case study between rounds. Round structures can vary depending on the seniority of the role, so ask the recruiter to outline the process at the start.

Does AiDASH give a case study or assignment during the BA interview?

Several candidates report receiving a short assignment, typically involving a client scenario, a data interpretation task, or a requirements prioritisation exercise. The assignment is usually followed by a discussion round where you walk the interviewer through your reasoning. Prepare to explain your thinking step by step, not just your final answer.

Do I need a background in climate tech or utilities to get a BA role at AiDASH?

A specialised background is not a strict requirement, but demonstrating genuine curiosity about the domain makes a noticeable difference. Candidates with experience in SaaS, infrastructure, logistics, or data-driven B2B environments have landed roles by pairing transferable skills with focused pre-interview research on utility operations and climate intelligence.

What salary can I expect for a BA role at AiDASH in India?

AiDASH has not publicly disclosed salary bands for India-based BA roles. For current benchmarks, check Glassdoor or LinkedIn Salary for Business Analyst listings in your city, filtering for SaaS or technology firms of a similar stage and size. Compensation at growth-stage companies often includes a base plus equity component, so ask the recruiter to clarify the full package early in the process.

Is SQL knowledge required for the AiDASH BA interview?

Based on candidate reports, expect at least a basic SQL question or a data interpretation exercise. You do not need to write complex stored procedures, but comfort with SELECT, GROUP BY, WHERE, and basic JOINs is strongly advisable. Being able to read a query and explain what it returns in plain English is as important as writing one from scratch.
