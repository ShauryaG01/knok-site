01 Overview

Overview

Solera is a global data and software company serving the automotive and insurance industries. Their platforms help insurers process vehicle claims, repair shops estimate damage costs, and fleet operators manage vehicle health at scale. For a Product Manager at Solera, this means owning complex B2B products that touch regulated data, enterprise client relationships, and deep system integrations.

As of the knok jobradar snapshot from July 2026, Solera has 1 open Product Manager role. Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product case exercise, and a final panel with engineering or senior product leadership. Solera values PMs who combine strong analytical thinking with the ability to operate inside a global enterprise structure.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are based on Solera's product domain and what candidates report from similar enterprise data and software company interviews. Prepare a concrete example or structured answer for each.

  1. Solera's platforms process data from vehicle claims and repair workflows. How would you prioritize new features for a product used by both insurers and repair shops, two audiences with different goals?
  2. How would you define success metrics for a claims automation product designed to reduce insurer settlement time?
  3. Solera has large enterprise clients who sometimes request features built only for them. How do you decide when to build for a specific client versus building something more broadly reusable?
  4. Tell us about a B2B product you managed that had deep integrations with external systems. How did you handle cross-team dependencies?
  5. How would you redesign the onboarding experience for a repair shop using a damage estimation tool for the first time?
  6. Solera's products involve insurance and vehicle data that falls under regulatory frameworks. Describe how you have managed compliance requirements as a PM.
  7. Walk us through a product or feature you chose to de-prioritize or sunset. What was your rationale and how did you communicate it internally?
  8. How do you build a product roadmap when customer research is slow to gather in an enterprise context?
  9. Describe a time you used data to challenge an assumption your team or leadership held about user behaviour.
  10. How would you approach competitive analysis if a rival automotive data platform started winning deals in a segment Solera currently leads?
  11. Solera operates globally. How do you adapt a product strategy or feature set for different regional markets?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioural and experience questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Here are three examples tailored to the types of questions Solera typically asks.

---

Q: Tell us about a B2B product you managed that had complex integrations. How did you handle cross-team dependencies?

*Situation:* I was PM for an invoicing module at a B2B SaaS company. It needed to sync with three different ERP systems used by our enterprise clients.

*Task:* We had to ship within a quarter while the engineering team was also managing a separate platform migration.

*Action:* I mapped all dependencies upfront in a shared doc that engineering, the integrations team, and client success could all track. I ran weekly syncs focused only on blockers, and negotiated a phased rollout with our two largest clients so we could test with real data before going fully live. I also kept a running decision log so any scope change was visible to all stakeholders without endless email threads.

*Result:* We shipped on time for the two priority clients and completed the full rollout in the following quarter. Client escalations during launch dropped compared to prior integrations because expectations had been set clearly in advance.

---

Q: Describe a time you used data to challenge an assumption your team held about user behaviour.

*Situation:* Our team believed that power users checked our analytics dashboard first thing in the morning. Based on that, we planned to run heavy data refresh jobs overnight.

*Task:* I was asked to validate this assumption before committing engineering resources to the refresh schedule.

*Action:* I pulled session logs and found that what the team commonly cited as 'morning usage' was not supported by the data for our user segment. Active sessions were clustered in mid-afternoon, after users finished client calls and before they wrapped their day. I presented a breakdown by user role and followed up with a short survey to confirm the pattern.

*Result:* We shifted the refresh window accordingly. This reduced infrastructure costs and improved dashboard load speed during peak hours. Support tickets about 'stale data' dropped noticeably in the weeks that followed.

---

Q: How would you prioritize features for a product that serves two audiences with different goals?

*Situation:* At a previous company, I owned a marketplace product used by both buyers and suppliers, similar to how Solera's tools serve both insurers and repair shops.

*Task:* Both sides had long feature backlogs and the engineering team had capacity for one major initiative per quarter.

*Action:* I built an impact-effort matrix and scored each request by three factors: revenue impact, churn risk if not built, and how many users on each side it affected. I ran separate discovery calls with power users from each group so I was not relying only on account manager summaries. Where a feature created value for both sides, I flagged it as shared value and moved it up the list.

*Result:* Over two quarters we shipped features that both sides rated highly in post-launch surveys. Renewal conversations became easier because both buyer and supplier stakeholders felt their needs were being tracked.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and feature prioritization questions: Start by clarifying the goal (what problem, for whom, and what does success look like). Only then move to solutions. For Solera specifically, always acknowledge the multi-stakeholder context and explain how you weigh the needs of insurers, repair shops, or fleet operators when they pull in different directions.

For metric and success definition questions: Start with the business outcome, then work backwards to leading indicators (daily active sessions, feature adoption) and lagging indicators (renewal rate, NPS). Avoid metrics that look good but do not connect to a business result.

For estimation and analytical questions: Show your structure out loud. State your assumptions, break the problem into components, and commit to a number. Solera works with large data volumes, so comfort with scale matters here.

For behavioural questions: Use STAR cleanly. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences each) and spend most of your answer on Action and Result. Quantify results where you can. Where you cannot, describe the qualitative outcome clearly and specifically.

For competitive or strategic questions: Cover four areas: your current market position, your differentiation, the signals telling you a competitor is gaining, and your recommended response. Solera operates in a market with established players, so demonstrating awareness of that landscape will help.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain awareness, even without insider experience. Solera works in vehicle claims, damage estimation, and fleet data. You do not need a background in insurance, but you should show you have researched how claims processing works and where the friction is for insurers and repair shops.

Comfort with enterprise complexity. Solera's clients are large companies with long sales cycles and specific requirements. Interviewers want to see that you understand how enterprise PM work differs from consumer work: longer feedback loops, procurement constraints, and the reality that one client conversation can shift your priorities for a quarter.

Data fluency. Solera is a data company at its core. You should be able to speak confidently about metrics, experimentation, and making decisions when data is incomplete. Candidates who rely only on instinct without grounding it in data tend to struggle in these interviews.

Cross-functional credibility. PMs at Solera work across engineering, design, legal, compliance, and client-facing teams. Interviewers will probe whether you can earn trust and drive alignment without direct authority.

Structured communication. Enterprise product roles reward clarity and conciseness. Practise giving answers that have a clear structure and a clear recommendation. Candidates who avoid committing to a position tend to get marked down.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand Solera's business and products.
Read Solera's public website and recent press releases. Focus on their core product lines covering claims processing, damage estimation, and fleet management. Write a short paragraph summarising what you believe their biggest PM challenge is. This becomes your anchor for every answer you give.

Week 2: Practise the domain questions.
Work through at least eight of the twelve questions listed above. For each, write out a two-minute spoken answer. Record yourself once and listen back for vague language or answers that never land on a clear recommendation.

Week 3: Build your STAR story bank.
Pick four or five strong experiences from your past: a time you used data to make or change a decision, a complex cross-functional project, a product you de-prioritised, and a difficult client or stakeholder situation. Write out a full STAR answer for each so you are not constructing them under pressure on the day.

Before the interview:
Prepare two or three questions for your interviewers. Good options: ask about the biggest unsolved problem the product team is currently working on, ask how success is measured for a new PM in the first few months, or ask how product and engineering collaborate on roadmap decisions at Solera.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Solera like a consumer tech company. Solera is enterprise and data-first. Candidates who bring only consumer app examples or who pitch 'move fast and break things' thinking often miss the mark. Lead with your B2B or platform product experience.

Listing metrics without explaining why they matter. Naming KPIs without connecting them to a business outcome is a common gap. Always tie your metric back to what the business is actually trying to achieve.

Vague STAR answers. 'I worked with the team to improve the product' tells an interviewer nothing useful. Be specific: what did you personally do, what was the actual result, and how do you know it worked?

Not knowing Solera's products. Candidates who have not looked into products like Audatex or Solera's fleet tools come across as unprepared. Even a basic understanding signals genuine interest and keeps you from giving generic answers.

Ignoring the multi-stakeholder complexity. Solera's products serve multiple user types simultaneously. Answers that only consider one user group suggest a narrow product sense.

Not asking any questions. Finishing a round with no questions is a missed signal. It can suggest you are not seriously evaluating the role or not curious about the team's real challenges.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Solera PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds. This usually means a recruiter or HR screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product case exercise (live or take-home depending on the team), and a final panel with engineering or senior product leadership. Round structure can vary by team and level, so confirm the details with your recruiter after the first call.

Does Solera give a take-home product case or a live case?

Candidates report both formats, depending on the team and role level. Some describe a take-home case with a day or two to prepare a written response or slide deck, while others report a live whiteboard-style session. Ask your recruiter after the first round so you can prepare the right way. Either way, practise structuring your thinking out loud before the interview.

Do I need automotive or insurance domain experience to get a PM role at Solera?

Domain experience helps but is not always a hard requirement. What matters more is showing you can learn a regulated, data-heavy domain quickly and that you understand enterprise customer dynamics. Read up on how vehicle claims processing works and come with a point of view on where the biggest product opportunities are. That preparation often compensates for not having direct industry experience.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Solera in India?

Solera has not publicly reported India-specific PM salary ranges. Based on knok data for the broader Indian market, mid-level PMs at the 3-6 year mark typically see ranges of 24-40 LPA and Senior PMs fall in the 40-60 LPA band. Your actual offer will depend on your experience level, the specific team, and your negotiation. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for self-reported data points that are more recent.

How important is data and analytics knowledge for a Solera PM interview?

It is quite important. Solera is fundamentally a data company and interviews tend to probe how you use data to make decisions, define metrics, and navigate situations where data is incomplete or conflicting. You do not need to write SQL during the interview, but you should be able to speak confidently about metrics frameworks, data-driven tradeoffs, and how you have used quantitative signals in past product decisions.

Where is the Solera PM role based, and is remote work an option?

Solera has offices in multiple cities globally, and India-based PM roles can vary by team in terms of location and remote flexibility. Check the specific job listing for location details and ask your recruiter about the team's working model during the first call. It is a reasonable question and most hiring teams have a clear answer ready.
