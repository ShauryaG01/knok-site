01 Overview

Overview

Khatabook is one of India's best-known SMB fintech companies, built to help small business owners manage credit, track payments, and run their books digitally. The platform focuses on Bharat users: kirana store owners, small traders, and micro-entrepreneurs across Tier 1, 2, and 3 cities. With 61 open roles as of July 2026, Khatabook is actively growing its product team.

The PM interview process typically runs 3-5 rounds. Candidates report an initial HR or recruiter screen, followed by a product sense round, an analytical or case-study round, a cross-functional or leadership round, and sometimes a final founder-level discussion. Confirm the exact structure with your recruiter, as it can vary by level and team.

What sets Khatabook PM interviews apart is the focus on the Bharat user. Interviewers want to see that you understand users who may be first-generation smartphone users, have limited digital literacy, and operate in low-connectivity environments. Simple, high-impact thinking matters more than complex, ambitious roadmaps.

Salary ranges for PM roles, based on industry surveys and publicly reported figures for growth-stage fintechs, broadly align with these bands: Associate PM at 12-20 LPA, mid-level PM (3-6 years of experience) at 24-40 LPA, and Senior PM at 40-60 LPA.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you improve Khatabook's core ledger feature for a kirana store owner who is not tech-savvy?

*Situation:* At a previous company, I owned a B2B reporting dashboard that small retailers were supposed to use daily, but adoption was very low among users with limited digital literacy.

*Task:* My goal was to increase daily usage among this segment without removing functionality that power users relied on.

*Action:* I started with in-person interviews at three small stores to understand how owners tracked credit today. Most used a physical khata. I redesigned the digital flow to mirror that mental model: one button for 'gave credit,' one button for 'received payment.' I removed all financial jargon, added voice input in the local language, and built a daily SMS summary so owners could review transactions without opening the app.

*Result:* Adoption in the target segment improved meaningfully within the first quarter, and support tickets related to the feature dropped noticeably. The lesson I would apply at Khatabook: start with the user's existing behaviour and language, not the ideal digital behaviour.

---

Q: A core engagement metric has dropped sharply week-over-week. How do you investigate?

*Situation:* At my last company, daily transaction volume on our payments product fell sharply over a single weekend.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause quickly because the drop was affecting merchant trust.

*Action:* I followed a structured diagnostic. First, I confirmed the data was accurate by checking for tracking bugs. Then I segmented the drop by platform (Android versus iOS), user cohort (new versus returning), and geography. The drop was concentrated in one state, only on Android. I pulled the release changelog and found a crash introduced in a build deployed Friday evening. I looped in engineering immediately and we rolled back the release.

*Result:* The metric recovered within two days. The bigger win was that we added a pre-release smoke-test checklist that caught similar issues in the following sprints.

---

Q: Engineering says a feature will take three months, but the business needs it in four weeks. How do you handle this?

*Situation:* I was PM on a lending product when a regulatory deadline required a new KYC flow to go live in four weeks. Engineering's estimate was eight weeks.

*Task:* My job was to close the gap without burning the team or shipping something unsafe for compliance.

*Action:* I ran a scoping session with engineering to separate what was 'required for compliance' from what was 'nice for UX.' We agreed on a minimal version that met the legal requirement by integrating an existing vendor API, with the full custom flow deferred to the next cycle. I documented the trade-offs clearly for leadership so the decision was visible and deliberate.

*Result:* We shipped the compliant version in five weeks. The deferred features shipped the following quarter with no compliance issues. The key was treating the deadline as a design constraint, not a pressure tactic.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and improvement questions, use a user-first structure. Start by clarifying the user segment and their core job to be done, then identify pain points, brainstorm solutions, prioritise using an effort-versus-impact lens, and define success metrics. At Khatabook, always anchor your user segment to the SMB or Bharat context before jumping to solutions.

For prioritisation questions, RICE scoring is well-understood by most interviewers. Break it down: Reach (how many users are affected), Impact (how much does it move the needle per user), Confidence (how sure are you of the estimate), and Effort (engineering weeks). Be explicit about your assumptions rather than presenting RICE scores as precise math.

For metric-drop questions, use a layered diagnostic tree:
- Is the data correct? (check for tracking or instrumentation bugs)
- Is the drop broad or concentrated? (segment by platform, geography, user cohort, and feature)
- What changed recently? (code releases, marketing campaigns, external events)
- What is the fix and who owns it?

Candidates report that Khatabook interviewers appreciate when you think out loud through the diagnostic tree rather than jumping straight to a hypothesis.

For build-versus-partner questions on new verticals, frame your answer around three questions: Does this capability differentiate Khatabook in the market? Can a partner deliver it faster at acceptable quality? What is the cost of being wrong? This keeps the discussion grounded and avoids analysis paralysis.

For cross-functional conflict questions, lead with alignment on shared outcomes before discussing timelines. Interviewers want to see that you collaborate first and escalate only when necessary.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep empathy for the Bharat user. Khatabook's core users are often first-generation smartphone users running small businesses with thin margins. Interviewers probe whether you genuinely understand this user or are pattern-matching from urban consumer product experience. If your examples all come from metro, English-first products, prepare to struggle. Have at least one example involving low-literacy, low-connectivity, or regional-language contexts ready.

Simplicity as a design principle. Based on publicly reported interviews with Khatabook's founders, the company's product philosophy centres on doing one thing well for the underserved business owner. Interviewers typically push back on feature-rich proposals. If your answer involves five screens and a dashboard, expect a follow-up: 'What if we could only ship one thing?'

Comfort with ambiguity. Khatabook operates in a fast-moving market. Candidates report that interviewers value people who can make decisions with imperfect data, run quick experiments, and move without waiting for certainty.

Precise metric thinking. You should be able to name a North Star metric for any feature you discuss, explain why you chose it, and describe how you would track leading indicators. Vague answers like 'we would measure engagement' get probed hard in interviews.

Ownership beyond formal scope. Khatabook PMs are expected to drive outcomes, not manage processes. Interviewers typically ask for examples of moments where you pushed beyond your job description to get something done.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the user.
Download the Khatabook app and use it every day for a week. Try every core flow: adding a customer, recording a credit entry, sending a payment reminder, reviewing a report. Note what feels simple, what feels confusing, and what you would change. Read publicly available founder interviews to understand the company's philosophy on the Bharat market.

Week 2: Build your case-study muscle.
Practice answering the 12 questions listed in this guide out loud, not just in your head. Time yourself. A solid product sense answer typically runs 6-8 minutes in an interview setting. Use RICE for prioritisation questions and the diagnostic tree for metric questions. Record yourself and watch for filler words and vague statements.

Week 3: Mock interviews and story preparation.
Run at least two full mock interviews with someone who can give honest feedback. Prepare 4-5 STAR stories from your own experience that you can adapt across different questions. Make sure at least one story covers working with non-metro users, one covers a data-driven decision under uncertainty, and one covers navigating a cross-functional conflict.

Before each round, check Khatabook's latest app store release notes and any recent product announcements. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for your interviewer about the team's current challenges. Candidates report that asking informed questions about the product roadmap leaves a strong impression.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Khatabook users like urban tech users. The most common feedback candidates share after rejections is that their solutions assumed high digital literacy, stable internet, and comfort with complex interfaces. Always ground your answer in who Khatabook's actual users are before proposing anything.

Jumping to solutions too quickly. Interviewers want to see your reasoning process, not just your conclusion. Candidates who skip straight to 'I would build X' without clarifying the problem, the user, and the success metric tend to score lower on product sense. Slow down, state your assumptions, and ask a clarifying question before designing.

Using jargon without substance. Saying 'I would run an A/B test' or 'I would use data to decide' without specifying what you would test, what data you would examine, and what threshold would trigger a decision comes across as surface-level. Be specific every time.

Over-engineering for edge cases. Khatabook is known for keeping the product simple. Candidates who try to handle every edge case in version one signal that they have not absorbed the company's product values. Lead with the core use case, then acknowledge edge cases and explicitly defer them.

Not having used the app. Interviewers often ask 'what would you change about our product?' A generic answer reveals immediately that you have not done basic preparation. Use the app before every round, not just the first one.

Weak metric definitions. Stating that you would 'track success' without naming a specific metric and defending why it is the right one is a common miss. Know the difference between a vanity metric (total downloads) and an actionable metric (daily transactions per active merchant), and be ready to defend your choice under questioning.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Khatabook PM interview typically have?

Candidates report 3-5 rounds, though this varies by level and team. A common sequence includes an HR screen, a product sense or case-study round, an analytical round, and a leadership or culture-fit discussion. Senior roles sometimes include a final conversation with a founder or senior leader. Always confirm the exact structure with your recruiter before preparing for each round.

What kind of case studies does Khatabook ask in PM interviews?

Candidates report that case studies are usually grounded in Khatabook's own product context: improving an existing feature, diagnosing a metric drop, or designing for a new SMB user segment. Purely abstract prompts are less common. Knowing the Khatabook app well and anchoring every answer in the SMB or Bharat context gives you a meaningful edge over candidates who approach it generically.

Do I need a fintech background to get a PM role at Khatabook?

Candidates report that a fintech background helps but is not mandatory. What matters more is demonstrated empathy for the SMB and Bharat user, clear product thinking, and comfort working with data under ambiguity. If your background is in consumer or B2B SaaS, prepare examples that show you have worked with non-metro users, low-literacy contexts, or products that had to be extremely simple to succeed.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Khatabook?

Specific Khatabook salary data is limited and not verified at scale. Based on industry surveys and publicly reported ranges for growth-stage fintechs, PM roles at the 3-6 year experience level are commonly cited in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PM roles at 40-60 LPA. Actual offers depend on your level, negotiation, and the specific role. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for the most current candidate-reported numbers.

How should I prepare for the 'improve our product' question?

Use the Khatabook app for at least a week before your interview. Pick one flow that you find genuinely interesting or frustrating, think through why the current design exists, who it serves, and what you would change. Avoid vague answers like 'make it more intuitive.' Instead, name a specific user segment, a specific pain point, and a specific change, then define the metric you would use to measure success.

Khatabook has 61 open roles right now. Does that mean hiring standards are lower?

A high number of open roles signals active team growth, not lower standards. Khatabook is expanding across product, engineering, and business functions as of mid-2026, which means PM hiring is genuinely active. That said, competition for PM roles at well-known fintechs remains strong. Use the volume as motivation to apply promptly and prepare thoroughly, not as a reason to cut corners on preparation.
