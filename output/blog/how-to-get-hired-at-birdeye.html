01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

BirdEye is a SaaS platform that helps businesses collect reviews, manage their online reputation, and run customer experience programs. The company primarily serves small and mid-sized businesses across North America, with a strong product and engineering presence in Gurugram, India.

As of mid-2026, BirdEye has 36 open roles spanning software engineering (frontend, backend, full-stack), QA, data, product management, sales, and customer success. Whether you are a software engineer with a few years of experience or a customer success professional with a SaaS background, there is likely a relevant opening to explore.

The hiring process is structured and moves at a reasonable pace. Most candidates complete the full cycle within a few weeks, though this varies by team and role. If you are targeting BirdEye, solid preparation makes a bigger difference than luck.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Here is how the process typically looks, based on publicly reported candidate experiences:

Stage 1: Application and resume screening
Your resume is reviewed by a recruiter. Tailor it to show relevant tech stack, SaaS experience, and measurable outcomes. A strong LinkedIn profile with a clear summary helps you get noticed faster.

Stage 2: Recruiter call
A brief call to verify your background, notice period, location preference, and salary expectations. Be clear and honest here. This is also your chance to ask about the team structure and role scope.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment
For engineering roles, expect an online coding test covering data structures, algorithms, and sometimes a small system design task. For sales or customer success roles, this might be a case study or written exercise to assess how you think about customer problems.

Stage 4: Technical interview rounds
Engineering candidates typically go through one or two rounds with senior engineers. Expect questions on your core stack, live problem-solving, and debugging scenarios. System design questions are common at mid and senior levels.

Stage 5: Hiring manager round
A deeper conversation about how you work, how you handle ambiguity, and how your past experience fits the team's current needs. Behavioral questions come up here, so be ready with specific examples from your work history.

Stage 6: HR and offer discussion
Final alignment on compensation, joining date, and reference checks if applicable. Come prepared with a clear compensation number in mind based on your market research.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

BirdEye hires people who are comfortable in a fast-moving SaaS environment. Here is what stands out across roles:

For engineering roles: Strong fundamentals in your primary stack. React, Node.js, Python, Java, and cloud platforms (AWS in particular) are commonly used across their teams. Clean coding habits, comfort with code reviews, and the ability to ship independently without hand-holding.

For customer success and sales roles: Understanding of how small and mid-sized businesses think and operate. Empathy for the customer, strong written and verbal English communication, and experience with CRM tools. Prior SaaS experience is a clear advantage.

For all roles: An ownership mindset. BirdEye teams are distributed across India and the US, so you need to manage your work proactively and communicate across time zones without waiting to be followed up. Initiative is noticed and rewarded here.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the product before your first call.
See what BirdEye actually does: sign up for a demo, watch their product videos, or browse their website. Know what reputation management means for a dentist, a car dealership, or a hotel chain. Interviewers notice when a candidate has done this homework.

For engineering candidates: Refresh your data structures and algorithms. Publicly reported feedback from candidates points to LeetCode-style questions, mostly at the medium difficulty level. For senior roles, brush up on system design: API design, database indexing, caching strategies, and how to scale a multi-tenant SaaS product.

For non-engineering candidates: Research common SaaS customer success and sales frameworks. Be ready to walk through a past situation where you handled a difficult customer, turned around a struggling account, or hit a target under pressure.

Prepare strong behavioral answers. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and anchor your answers in specific outcomes. 'I improved performance' is weaker than 'I reduced API response time noticeably and the product team highlighted it in the quarterly review.'

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

BirdEye operates as a product-led, customer-obsessed company. A few things define what it is actually like to work there:

Fast and iterative. Teams ship frequently. Priorities can shift as the product evolves. If you thrive in environments where you own outcomes and adapt quickly, this suits you well. If you prefer slow, heavily planned release cycles, expect some adjustment.

India office is a real engineering hub. The Gurugram team is not a support function. Engineers and product managers there own meaningful product areas. Expect collaboration with US counterparts, which means some late-evening overlap in India standard time.

Ownership is the default expectation. Employees are expected to spot problems and bring solutions, not just flag issues upward. In interviews, showing that you have taken initiative without being told to will help you stand out.

Growth opportunities exist for the proactive. Publicly reported feedback on platforms like Glassdoor and AmbitionBox describes a workplace where career growth is available for those who ask for it and demonstrate readiness. It is not handed to you, but the path is visible.

BirdEye's open roles lean toward candidates with some prior experience, particularly in SaaS environments. Entry-level positions do appear, especially in customer support and junior engineering tracks, but they are less common than mid-level openings. If you are a fresher, focus your application on projects that demonstrate product thinking, SaaS exposure, and ownership of end-to-end delivery.

What is the best way to stand out in the BirdEye application process?
