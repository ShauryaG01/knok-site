01 Overview

Overview

Infilect is a Bangalore-based retail AI startup that builds computer vision tools to help brands and retailers monitor shelves, track planogram compliance, and analyse in-store data. QA Engineers here are expected to go beyond traditional web or mobile testing: you will validate AI model outputs, test image-processing pipelines, and work closely with data science and product teams.

As of July 2026, Infilect has 8 open QA Engineer roles, signalling active hiring across experience levels. Candidates report a process of roughly 3-4 rounds: an initial HR or recruiter call, a technical screen, a hands-on coding or testing exercise, and a final discussion with a senior engineer or hiring manager. Round structure can vary by team, so confirm the details with your recruiter after the first call.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Infilect QA Engineer interviews, based on candidate reports and the company's AI-first product context. Expect a mix of conceptual, tool-based, and scenario-driven questions.

  1. How would you design a test plan for a computer vision model that checks shelf compliance in a retail store?
  2. Walk us through your approach to REST API testing. Which tools have you used and how do you structure your test suites?
  3. How do you validate the output of an AI or ML model when there is no single correct answer, such as a confidence score or a bounding box location?
  4. Describe your experience with test automation frameworks. Which framework did you choose for your last project and why?
  5. How do you handle flaky tests? Walk us through how you investigate and fix them.
  6. Tell us about a critical bug you found late in the release cycle. How did you handle it and what was the outcome?
  7. How do you decide which test cases to automate first when you join a project with little or no existing automation?
  8. How does QA fit into a CI/CD pipeline? Have you integrated your test suite with a CI tool?
  9. How would you test an image-upload feature end-to-end, from the mobile app all the way to the backend processing pipeline?
  10. What metrics do you use to measure the quality and coverage of a QA process?
  11. Have you tested mobile applications? What tools did you use and how did you manage device coverage?
  12. If a product manager asks you to sign off on a release in half the usual time, how do you decide what to test and what to skip?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural questions. Here are three worked examples relevant to Infilect's context.

Q: Tell us about a time you had to test an AI or ML feature with non-deterministic outputs.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we shipped an OCR feature that extracted product names from shelf images. The extracted text was never perfectly identical across runs because of model updates and image quality variation.

*Task:* I needed to define a test strategy that could flag real regressions without failing on minor, acceptable variation.

*Action:* I worked with the data science team to agree on an acceptable accuracy threshold. I then built a labelled test dataset of representative shelf images and wrote scripts that compared model output against ground truth, reporting pass or fail based on whether accuracy stayed above the agreed threshold. I also added a review queue for edge cases that fell just below the threshold.

*Result:* We caught two model regressions before release and significantly reduced false-alert noise. The data science team adopted the same framework for future model evaluations.

---

Q: Describe a time you found a critical bug just before a release.

*Situation:* Two days before a major retail client demo, I was doing a final smoke test on our planogram compliance dashboard.

*Task:* My job was to confirm the release was stable, but I spotted that compliance scores were showing clearly incorrect values across all stores.

*Action:* I immediately raised it with the backend engineer and the product manager. We traced the issue to a recent config change that had overwritten the scoring algorithm's environment variable. I documented the reproduction steps, confirmed the fix in staging within a few hours, and ran a focused regression suite on the affected module.

*Result:* The bug was resolved before the demo and the client presentation went smoothly. The team then added an environment variable audit step to the pre-release checklist.

---

Q: Walk us through how you decided what to automate first on a project with very little existing coverage.

*Situation:* I joined a team where the entire QA process was manual and there was no automation at all. We had a product with a mobile app, a backend API, and a reporting dashboard.

*Task:* I had to build automation from scratch while also keeping up with weekly release testing.

*Action:* I mapped all test cases by two factors: how often they were run and how risky the area was if it broke. The API layer scored highest on both counts, so I started there using Postman collections with Newman for CI integration. I set up a basic smoke suite in the first two weeks, then gradually expanded to regression coverage.

*Result:* Within three months, the smoke suite ran on every pull request. Manual regression time dropped noticeably and we caught three API contract breaks before they reached staging.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical questions about test strategy, use a structure of: Scope first, then Risk assessment, then Tool or method choice, then How you would measure success. For example, if asked how you would test an image-processing API, start by clarifying what counts as a correct output, then identify the highest-risk paths (bad input, large file, model timeout), then name the tools you would use, then describe how you would know the tests are effective.

For 'how do you handle X' questions (flaky tests, tight deadlines, unclear requirements), use a three-part structure: Diagnose the root cause, take a specific action, and measure or confirm the result. Avoid vague answers like 'I would communicate with the team'. Instead, say exactly who you would speak to, what you would ask, and what you would do with the answer.

For system design or test plan questions, follow this sequence:

  1. Understand the feature and its users
  2. List the functional and non-functional requirements to test
  3. Identify the riskiest areas first
  4. Decide what to automate vs. test manually
  5. Define what 'done' looks like (exit criteria)

For questions about AI or ML validation, be ready to explain the difference between deterministic testing (fixed inputs give fixed outputs) and threshold-based testing (outputs are scored against an acceptable range). Infilect interviewers value candidates who understand that AI outputs require statistical thinking, not just pass/fail assertions.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Infilect QA interviewers are typically looking for a few things beyond standard QA knowledge.

Comfort with ambiguity. Because AI model outputs are probabilistic, you will rarely have a binary pass/fail for every test. Interviewers want to see that you can define quality thresholds, work with data science teams to agree on acceptance criteria, and build tests that are meaningful rather than just mechanical.

Automation maturity. The company moves quickly and manual regression does not scale. Expect questions about your hands-on experience with tools like Selenium, Appium, Pytest, or Postman with Newman. Be ready to talk about how you structured your automation, not just which tools you used.

Product and domain curiosity. Candidates who have looked at Infilect's product (retail shelf intelligence, planogram compliance, in-store analytics) and can connect their QA experience to that domain stand out. You do not need retail industry experience, but showing genuine curiosity about how the product works signals cultural fit.

Clear communication. QA sits between engineering, product, and clients. Interviewers look for people who can explain a bug or a risk clearly to a non-technical stakeholder, and who can push back on unrealistic release timelines with data rather than just 'no'.

Ownership mindset. Candidates who say 'I raised the bug and then moved on' score lower than those who say 'I tracked the bug through the fix, wrote a regression test, and updated the checklist'.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations and company research
Read Infilect's website and any public case studies to understand their product. Review the job description carefully and map your experience to each requirement. Refresh your knowledge of REST API testing, HTTP methods, status codes, and JSON payloads. Practise writing test cases for an API endpoint from scratch.

Week 2: Automation and tools
Pick one automation framework you are comfortable with (Selenium, Pytest, Appium, or similar) and prepare two or three strong examples of how you have used it. Be ready to write or read code live. If you have used CI tools like Jenkins, GitHub Actions, or GitLab CI, prepare a short explanation of how your test suite was integrated.

Week 3: AI and ML testing concepts
This step is specific to Infilect. Study the basics of how image recognition and object detection models work. Understand concepts like precision, recall, confidence thresholds, and what makes a good test dataset. You do not need to be a data scientist, but you should be able to explain how you would validate an AI feature's output.

Week 4: Interview practice
Practise answering five or six behavioural questions using the STAR format out loud, not just in your head. Do a mock API test or coding exercise under a time limit. Review the common mistakes section and prepare two or three genuine questions to ask the interviewer about team structure, release cadence, and tooling.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague answers to 'how do you test X' questions. Saying 'I would create test cases and run them' is not enough. Interviewers want a specific approach: what types of tests, in what order, using which tools, with what exit criteria.

Ignoring the AI context. Many candidates prepare for a standard web or mobile QA interview and are caught off-guard by questions about testing non-deterministic outputs. Spending even a few hours on basic ML quality concepts before your interview will put you ahead.

Over-claiming automation experience. If you have used a tool but cannot explain how you structured the test suite, maintained it, or dealt with failures, do not lead with it. Interviewers often probe one or two levels deeper than your resume claim.

Not asking clarifying questions. On scenario questions, jumping straight to an answer without asking 'what is the user journey here?' or 'what does a passing test look like?' signals shallow thinking. Pause, ask, then answer.

Forgetting to mention collaboration. QA at a startup like Infilect involves constant coordination with developers, product managers, and sometimes clients. If your answers never mention working with others, that is a gap.

Not preparing questions for the interviewer. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team's current testing challenges, the CI/CD setup, or how QA metrics are tracked.
