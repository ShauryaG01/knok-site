01 Overview

Overview

Zocket is an AI-powered ad creation and campaign management platform that helps small and medium businesses launch digital ads quickly, without needing a design team or agency. The product lets users generate ad creatives, publish to social and search platforms, and track campaign performance from a single dashboard. Zocket currently has 10 open Product Manager roles, signalling active expansion of its product team.

Candidates typically report a process spanning three to four rounds: an initial recruiter or HR screen, a product sense or case discussion, a metrics and analytical round, and a final leadership or culture conversation. Some candidates report a take-home product case in addition to or instead of the live case round. All rounds are typically conducted virtually. Confirm the exact structure with your recruiter after the first call.

Salary bands for PM roles in India vary by seniority. Associate PMs typically see 12-20 LPA, PMs with three to six years of experience see 24-40 LPA, Senior PMs land in the 40-60 LPA range, and Group or Principal PMs can reach 55-90+ LPA. For Zocket-specific offer data reported by candidates, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Zocket interviews concentrate on AI product thinking, empathy for small business users, and growth metrics. Candidates commonly report the following questions appearing across rounds:

  1. How would you improve Zocket's AI ad generation feature to increase activation for first-time users?
  2. Zocket competes with AI creative tools and general design platforms. How would you differentiate Zocket's product to win more SMB customers?
  3. What is the north star metric for Zocket's platform, and why did you choose it over alternatives like ad spend processed or total ads created?
  4. A small business owner tells you that Zocket's AI-generated ads 'don't look professional enough.' How do you investigate and respond as a PM?
  5. Design an onboarding flow for a kirana store owner using Zocket for the very first time, assuming zero marketing knowledge.
  6. Zocket wants to expand from social media ads into Google Search ads. Walk us through how you would approach this as a PM.
  7. You shipped a feature that significantly reduced ad creation time, but users now report lower click-through rates on their ads. What do you do?
  8. How would you prioritise improving the AI model quality for creative generation versus adding integrations with more ad platforms?
  9. Walk us through how you would design and interpret an A/B test for Zocket's ad copy generation suggestions.
  10. How would you build a simple performance dashboard for SMB owners who have no marketing or data background?
  11. What metrics would you track to determine whether Zocket's paid plan is delivering real value to customers and reducing churn?
  12. How would you approach increasing retention among free-tier users who create one ad and never return to the platform?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for behavioural and product execution questions. The three examples below show the level of specificity Zocket interviewers typically look for.

Q: Tell me about a time you used data to challenge an assumption about your users.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the product team assumed that power users of our content tool wanted more advanced editing controls.
*Task:* I was leading the roadmap for the editor experience and needed to decide whether to invest in advanced controls or simplify the interface.
*Action:* I pulled usage data and found that most active users never opened the advanced settings panel. I then ran five user interviews with the segment we had labelled 'power users.' They told me they valued speed and reliability over control. I reframed the roadmap around one-click defaults instead of expanding the settings library and presented the data to leadership to get alignment.
*Result:* The simplified editor led to a meaningful increase in weekly active usage among that segment. Support tickets about 'confusing options' dropped noticeably in the following quarter. The team removed three planned features from the backlog.

Q: Describe a product improvement you made by deeply understanding a non-technical user.

*Situation:* I worked on a billing and invoicing tool used by small shop owners with limited smartphone experience. Activation rates were low and the support team was overwhelmed with setup calls.
*Task:* I was asked to find out why new users were not completing onboarding.
*Action:* I visited three small retail shops and watched owners use the app without prompting. I found that asking for a GST number in step one caused immediate drop-off, because most owners did not have it handy when signing up. I moved that field to a later optional step and replaced the opening screen with a single prominent button: 'Create your first invoice.' I worked with the designer and shipped the change in one sprint.
*Result:* Onboarding completion improved noticeably in the first month after launch. Support call volume for setup issues dropped, and the team used the freed capacity on a new feature.

Q: How have you handled a situation where engineering pushed back on your prioritisation?

*Situation:* I had prioritised a real-time competitor ad-spend tracker as a high-priority feature for our platform.
*Task:* I needed to deliver this within the quarter to hit an OKR target.
*Action:* Engineering flagged that the required data pipeline would take two months to build correctly, which was outside our window. I met with the tech lead to understand the constraint. We mapped the underlying user need: users wanted to know if they were overspending relative to competitors. I proposed a lighter version, a weekly digest email with benchmark ranges sourced from publicly available data, which engineering could ship in three weeks. I updated the OKR target and walked stakeholders through the reasoning.
*Result:* The digest launched on time, received positive responses from pilot users, and gave us real usage data to justify investing in the full pipeline in the next quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Having a clear structure for each question type saves time and signals PM maturity to interviewers.

For product improvement questions (very common at Zocket): clarify the goal, name the target user segment, map the user journey to find the biggest friction or drop-off, generate solutions, prioritise by impact versus effort, and define a success metric. Do not jump to solutions before naming the user and what they are actually trying to accomplish.

For metric questions: open with your north star metric and explain why it reflects real user value rather than just platform activity. Then name two or three input metrics that drive the north star. For Zocket, a strong answer connects ad creation actions to actual outcomes for the SMB customer, such as whether the business got new enquiries or sales from the ad.

For prioritisation questions: use a simple impact-versus-effort grid. State your constraints out loud: team size, current quarter, technical dependencies. Zocket interviewers typically want to see you balance near-term retention against longer-term platform capability building.

For design questions: use the Jobs-to-be-Done frame. Ask what job the user is hiring this product to do. For a kirana store owner using Zocket, the job is 'get more customers this week without spending hours on design or learning ad platforms.' Every design choice should trace back to that job.

For A/B test questions: walk through your hypothesis, the primary metric you will move, the guardrail metrics you will protect, the minimum effect size that matters, and how you will call the result. Interviewers want to see rigour and clear judgment, not unnecessary complexity.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Zocket PMs work across AI, design, engineering, and growth teams on a product used by business owners who are not technical. Based on what candidates typically report, interviewers look for four qualities:

Deep SMB empathy. Can you think like a local business owner who is focused on footfall and margins, not marketing funnels? Candidates who reference actual conversations with small business owners consistently stand out in Zocket interviews.

Comfort with AI product tradeoffs. Zocket's core is generative AI for ad creatives. You should be able to speak to the tension between output quality, speed, cost, and user trust. You do not need to write code. You do need to understand where AI currently falls short and how a PM manages that gap with the user.

Metrics fluency without hiding behind frameworks. Candidates who name the right metric AND make a clear judgment about what to do with it score well. Candidates who present frameworks without making a call do not.

Bias toward shipping. Zocket is a growth-stage company that moves quickly. Answers that propose a perfect solution over several months lose points. Answers that propose a focused MVP, name its risks, and explain what you will learn from it gain them.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week approach covers the core ground most candidates need.

Week one: understand the product and the user.
Sign up for Zocket's free plan and create at least two ads from start to finish. Note every friction point and every moment of delight. Look for recent coverage of Zocket in the Indian startup press to understand their current focus. Find three to five small business owners through friends, contacts, or local business communities online, and ask them how they currently handle digital advertising. This user context makes your answers specific and credible.

Week two: practice structured answers.
Choose five questions from the list above and write out complete STAR answers for each. Record yourself answering two of them and review the recording. Aim for two to three minutes per answer. Practice one product design question using the Jobs-to-be-Done frame. Practice one metrics question by starting with a north star and then naming the input metrics that drive it.

Before the interview:
Prepare two questions for the interviewer. Good options: 'What does the PM team currently track as the most important signal of product health?' and 'What is the biggest unsolved problem on the roadmap right now?' These show genuine curiosity and give you useful signal about the role and team.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Zocket like a large enterprise product company. Zocket is a growth-stage startup. Answers that assume large dedicated research teams, lengthy discovery cycles, or multi-layer approval processes will feel out of sync with how the company actually works. Show that you can move fast with limited resources.

Talking about ads from an advertiser perspective, not an SMB owner perspective. Many candidates with performance marketing backgrounds default to thinking like media buyers. Zocket's user is a business owner who wants results without learning ad jargon. Keep the SMB perspective in view throughout your answers.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Name the specific event, explain the threshold that would cause you to act, and connect it to revenue or retention. Zocket interviewers care about outcomes, not activity.

Skipping constraints. When asked to design or prioritise, candidates often propose ambitious solutions without mentioning team size, time, or technical limits. Naming constraints shows commercial awareness and seniority.

Over-crediting AI. Some candidates, wanting to appear aligned with Zocket's product direction, treat AI as a solution to every problem. Interviewers know that AI creative tools produce inconsistent output and that user trust in AI is fragile. Acknowledging these limits and explaining how you would manage them builds more credibility than enthusiasm alone.

Not preparing questions. Candidates who have nothing to ask at the end of a round miss the chance to show genuine interest and to learn something useful about the team.

How many rounds does the Zocket PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds. These commonly include a recruiter or HR screen, a product sense or case discussion, a metrics or analytical round, and a final conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder. Some candidates also report a take-home case assignment as part of the process. The exact structure can vary by role level, so confirm with your recruiter after the first call.

Does Zocket give a take-home assignment for PM roles?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home product case, typically covering a product improvement scenario or a go-to-market problem. These are usually expected to take a few hours and are followed by a live walkthrough discussion in a later round. Ask your recruiter early whether the specific role includes a take-home, so you can factor it into your preparation plan.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Zocket?

Zocket-specific offer data from candidates is limited. Market data puts PMs with three to six years of experience at 24-40 LPA and Senior PMs at 40-60 LPA. Do I need an advertising or marketing background to be a PM at Zocket?

A direct advertising background is helpful but not required, based on what candidates typically report. Stronger signals are empathy for small business owners, comfort with growth metrics, and the ability to reason through AI product tradeoffs. If you lack advertising experience, spend time using Zocket's free product and speaking with a few SMB owners before the interview to build relevant context quickly.

How should I think about AI when answering Zocket PM questions?

Zocket's core product is built on generative AI for ad creatives, so expect questions about AI product decisions. You do not need a machine learning background, but you should be comfortable discussing how output quality, user trust, and feedback loops interact.
