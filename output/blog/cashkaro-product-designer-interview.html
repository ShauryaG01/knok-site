01 Overview

Overview

CashKaro is India's largest cashback and coupons platform, connecting shoppers with partner retailers like Amazon, Flipkart, and Myntra. The product design team owns the full user journey: discovering deals, clicking through to partner sites, tracking pending cashback, and withdrawing earnings. Because cashback involves delayed money and real stakes for users, the UX work is heavily focused on building trust and reducing anxiety at every step.

As of July 2026, CashKaro has 46 open roles, with Product Designer being one of its active hiring areas. Candidates report the interview process typically covers 3-4 rounds: a recruiter screen, a portfolio walkthrough, a design challenge or take-home assignment, and a final round with product leadership. The full process commonly takes 2-4 weeks.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in CashKaro designer interviews, based on what candidates report and the nature of the product:

  1. Walk me through a project where you improved a conversion funnel. CashKaro depends on users clicking through to partner sites and completing purchases. They want designers who understand conversion, not just visual polish.
  1. How would you redesign the cashback tracking screen? This is a trust moment. Users get anxious when they cannot see their pending cashback. How would you reduce that anxiety?
  1. How do you design for users who are skeptical about whether a cashback offer is real? First-time users on cashback platforms often doubt the product. How do you build credibility through design choices?
  1. Describe how you would approach designing for both Hindi-speaking and English-speaking users. CashKaro targets Tier 1 and Tier 2 Indian cities. Localization and language flexibility are real product constraints, not afterthoughts.
  1. How would you design a referral program that feels rewarding rather than spammy? Referrals are a key growth lever. They want designers who balance business goals with genuine user value.
  1. How do you decide what to measure to know if a design change worked? They want to see you connect design decisions to outcomes, not just to aesthetics.
  1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product manager or engineer about a design decision. How did you handle it? Cross-functional collaboration is central to this role.
  1. How would you simplify the coupon redemption flow for a first-time user? Redemption drop-off is a real problem in cashback products. They want practical, user-centred thinking.
  1. How do you handle designing under tight deadlines or with incomplete information? CashKaro moves at startup pace. They want evidence you can ship without perfect conditions.
  1. How would you improve the CashKaro mobile app's onboarding experience? Expect them to ask you to critique their own product. Use the app thoroughly before any interview round.
  1. Describe a time your design failed or did not perform as expected. What did you learn? They want intellectual honesty and a growth mindset, not a highlight reel.
  1. How do you balance aesthetics and functionality when working with engineering constraints? Practical design that ships matters more to this team than concepts that cannot be built.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these as a starting point and adapt them to your own experience.

---

Q: Walk me through a project where you improved a conversion funnel.

*Situation:* I was working on an e-commerce app where the add-to-cart to checkout conversion was dropping off at the address entry step.

*Task:* My goal was to identify why users were abandoning at that point and redesign the flow to reduce that drop-off before the next release cycle.

*Action:* I ran session recording analysis and found users were confused by the multi-column form layout on small screens. I simplified it to a single-column design, added inline validation with clear error messages, and introduced address auto-complete. I then set up an A/B test with the product team to validate the change before a full rollout.

*Result:* The redesigned flow reduced drop-off at that step, confirmed through our internal tracking. The PM used the case study for the next quarter planning. I came away with the habit of checking session recordings before jumping to solutions.

---

Q: How do you design for users who are skeptical about whether a cashback offer is real?

*Situation:* At a previous role, we noticed users were clicking on cashback offers but not completing the purchase. Exit surveys pointed to trust issues: users were not sure the cashback would actually arrive.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the offer detail page to improve purchase completion among skeptical first-time users.

*Action:* I mapped what users were worried about: Will the cashback actually be tracked? When will it arrive? What happens if it does not? I added a clear cashback timeline, a 'how it works in 3 steps' explainer, and a count of how many users had redeemed the same offer recently. I tested prototypes with 5 users from our existing base before handing off to engineering.

*Result:* Internal metrics showed improvement in the test cohort, and the trust-layer pattern was adopted across other offer pages. Platforms of a similar type commonly cite first-time user trust as a top design challenge, and this project sharpened my thinking on exactly that problem.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a PM about a design decision. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* A PM wanted to add a full-screen interstitial promoting the referral program every time a user withdrew their cashback, reasoning it was a high-intent moment.

*Task:* I felt this would frustrate users at precisely the moment they felt most satisfied. I needed to make my case without dismissing the business goal behind the ask.

*Action:* I proposed treating it as a test. Instead of blocking the withdrawal success screen, I suggested a non-intrusive referral nudge placed after the success message, with a clear dismiss option. I built a quick prototype and framed both versions as hypotheses in the team review, not as 'my design vs. your idea.'

*Result:* The team agreed to test both approaches. The non-intrusive version performed better on referral taps and had lower app-uninstall signals in the week after launch. The PM and I now use it as a shared example of how framing a design debate as a hypothesis leads to better outcomes than debating opinions.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Three frameworks that work well for CashKaro-style design interviews:

Portfolio Walk: Problem, Decision, Outcome
When presenting past work, structure every project around three things: what was the user problem, what design decisions did you make and why, and what happened after you shipped. CashKaro interviewers care about your reasoning, not just how the final screens look. Spend no more than a third of your time on the visuals themselves.

Metrics-First Framing
Before describing any design, state what success looks like in plain terms. For example: 'The goal was to reduce drop-off at the cashback withdrawal step.' This signals that you think like a product designer, not just a visual designer. CashKaro is a data-driven company, and connecting your design choices to outcomes will consistently set you apart from candidates who only talk about the craft.

Constraint-Led Ideation
When asked to redesign a feature or solve a problem in the interview, name your constraints before you start: platform (Android-first?), user literacy levels, network conditions, and localization needs. This signals design maturity. A solution that only works for a Bangalore power-user on fast internet is not a CashKaro solution. Lead with constraints, then show how your design works within them.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report from CashKaro design interviews, here is what the team typically looks for:

Product thinking over pixel-pushing. CashKaro's design team is lean relative to its user base. Every designer needs to think like a mini-PM. Show that you connect design decisions to business outcomes, not just to how things look.

Comfort with trust and anxiety UX. Cashback products live or die on user trust. If you have designed for situations where users are uncertain (financial products, first-time transactions, delayed gratification), bring those stories to the front of your portfolio.

Collaboration without ego. The role requires constant negotiation: with engineers about what can ship, with PMs about what to build, and with data teams about what is working. They want designers who advocate for users without being difficult to work with.

Speed and pragmatism. CashKaro moves at startup pace. They want evidence you can make good-enough decisions quickly, iterate based on feedback, and ship rather than perfect indefinitely.

Familiarity with their own product. Candidates who have clearly used CashKaro before the interview and who come with a specific observation consistently stand out. Do not just say you 'explored the app.' Come with one concrete friction point and an opinion on how you would address it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Follow this plan in the 1-2 weeks before your CashKaro design interview:

  1. Use the product deeply. Download the CashKaro app and complete a full cashback cycle if possible. Note every point of friction and every moment that works well. Come with one specific critique and one thing you genuinely appreciate. Generic praise will not impress anyone on this team.
  1. Prepare 2-3 strong portfolio projects. Choose projects that show product thinking, not just visual craft. If you have worked on conversion funnels, trust-building flows, or mobile-first products for Indian users, lead with those in your walkthrough.
  1. Practice the STAR format out loud. Write answers to the questions listed above using real examples from your career. Record yourself and listen back. Rambling is the most common failure mode in design interviews.
  1. Brush up on design metrics. Be ready to talk about conversion rate, task completion rate, and retention in plain terms. You do not need to be an analyst, but you should know how to check whether your design worked.
  1. Prepare for the take-home. Candidates report CashKaro typically includes a design assignment. Prioritise showing your thinking process through annotations and a clear problem statement rather than delivering the most polished output possible.
  1. Research CashKaro's competitive context. Understand how it differs from other rewards and cashback products in India. This context helps you ask sharper questions and give grounded answers during the final round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

These are the mistakes that most often cost candidates the offer at CashKaro designer interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. Showing up without using the app. Nothing kills credibility faster than saying 'I have not personally used CashKaro but I looked at some screenshots.' Use the product. Have real opinions.
  1. Focusing only on aesthetics. Talking about colour choices and visual language without connecting them to user goals or business outcomes reads as junior. Lead with the problem, not the palette.
  1. Ignoring Indian user contexts. Designs that assume high-end Android devices, fast internet, or English fluency will get challenged. Acknowledge constraints like low-bandwidth access, Hindi-speaking users, and Tier 2 city audiences proactively rather than waiting to be asked.
  1. Vague answers to behavioural questions. 'I always collaborate well with engineers' is not an answer. A specific story with a real conflict and a real resolution is what interviewers are looking for.
  1. Not preparing questions to ask. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest or low preparation. Bring 2-3 thoughtful questions about the team, the design process, or the product direction.
  1. Over-engineering the take-home. Candidates sometimes submit a sprawling prototype for a single flow. CashKaro wants to see clear thinking and good judgment. Match the scope of your solution to the scope of the brief.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the CashKaro Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically has 3-4 rounds: a recruiter or HR screen, a portfolio review, a design challenge or take-home assignment, and a final round with a product lead or senior designer. Some candidates report skipping the take-home if the portfolio is strong enough. The full process commonly takes 2-4 weeks from first contact to offer.

Does CashKaro give a design assignment, and what should I expect?

Yes, candidates typically report receiving a design assignment as part of the process. It is usually a product design problem related to cashback flows, user trust, or onboarding. You will typically have a day to a few days to complete it. Focus on showing your thinking process through annotations and a clear problem framing rather than producing the most polished visuals.

What kind of portfolio projects does CashKaro's design team respond well to?

Projects that show product thinking over visual polish tend to land best. Conversion funnel improvements, trust-building flows, onboarding redesigns, and mobile-first work for Indian users are all highly relevant. Be ready to walk through the problem you solved, the decisions you made and why, and the measurable outcome. Generic UI redesigns with no performance context are less compelling to this team.

Should I expect salary negotiation at CashKaro?

Salary negotiation is common and expected. Publicly reported ranges on platforms like Glassdoor suggest Product Designer compensation varies significantly by experience level. The broader market data shows the mid-level band (3-5 years experience) sits in the 14-24 LPA range. Come to negotiation with a specific number anchored to your impact and experience, rather than a vague range.

Is the CashKaro Product Designer role remote or in-office?

Candidates report a mix of hybrid and in-office arrangements, with the Gurgaon office being the primary base for many roles. The specifics vary by team and role level. Check the job listing for the current working arrangement and ask directly during the recruiter screen if flexibility matters to you. Do not wait until the offer stage to raise it.

How important is it to have used CashKaro before the interview?

Very important. Interviewers frequently ask candidates to critique or improve the CashKaro product, and showing up without having used it is a significant disadvantage. Candidates who come with specific observations, including one friction point and one thing that works well, consistently stand out from those who give only generic feedback. Spend at least an hour using the app before any interview round.
