01 Overview

Overview

Sardine is a fraud prevention and compliance platform used by fintechs, banks, and payment companies to detect fraud in real time using device intelligence, behavioural signals, and machine learning. Their Data Analyst roles sit at the intersection of risk intelligence and product analytics, so interviews test both your SQL depth and your ability to reason about fraud patterns, false positive trade-offs, and business impact.

With 35 open roles at sardine right now (knok jobradar, July 2026), this is an active hiring period. The interview process typically runs three to four rounds: an HR or recruiter screen, a take-home analytics challenge, one or two technical rounds with the data or risk team, and a final hiring manager conversation. Candidates report that the take-home often involves a transaction dataset with fraud labels where you are asked to write queries, surface patterns, and present conclusions.

Salary bands for Data Analyst roles in India run 5-10 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 10-18 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 18-30 LPA for senior profiles (6-9 years), based on knok jobradar data.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you discovered a data anomaly. What steps did you take?

*Situation:* I was maintaining a weekly fraud reporting pipeline for a digital lending product. During a routine check, I noticed the flagged transaction count for one merchant had more than tripled compared to the prior week, yet the approval rate had not changed.

*Task:* My job was to determine whether this was a real fraud pattern, a pipeline error, or a misfiring rule, and bring a clear recommendation to the risk team before the weekly review.

*Action:* I first ruled out a pipeline issue by tracing the raw source data against the aggregated table. I then segmented the flagged transactions by device type, time of day, and merchant category. Almost all of them came from a single device model running an OS version that had just been updated. I cross-referenced the rule logs and confirmed a detection threshold had been changed the previous day.

*Result:* It was a false-positive surge from the rule update, not a new fraud wave. I flagged it to the risk team, the threshold was recalibrated the same day, and I documented the check as a standard post-update audit step going forward.

---

Q: How would you measure whether a new fraud detection rule is working when there is no control group?

*Situation:* My previous company launched a new velocity-check rule to catch card-testing fraud. Because it rolled out to all users simultaneously, there was no holdout group.

*Task:* I had to estimate the rule's impact and give the team a clear recommendation: keep it, tune it, or roll it back.

*Action:* I used a pre/post comparison anchored to matching weekday windows before and after the launch date, to control for weekly seasonality. I tracked three signals: the flagged-transaction rate, the false-positive rate on legitimate transactions, and the chargeback rate on approved transactions. I also compared the trend to a cohort of merchants with historically similar fraud profiles.

*Result:* The chargeback rate on approved transactions dropped noticeably in the two weeks after launch, while the false-positive rate stayed flat. The team kept the rule, and I set up a fortnightly monitoring report to catch any future drift.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you explained a complex data finding to a non-technical stakeholder.

*Situation:* I had found that a particular device segment accounted for a disproportionate share of fraudulent signups, but the finding involved conditional probability and segmentation that was hard to communicate quickly.

*Task:* I needed the product head to approve a change to the onboarding flow without getting lost in statistical detail.

*Action:* I led with the business implication first: 'one device segment is driving most of our fraud-related losses, and here is the fix.' I replaced the confusion matrix with a single bar chart showing fraud rate by device segment. I also pre-prepared a slide addressing the obvious concern, showing that the legitimate user volume in that segment was small.

*Result:* The product head approved the onboarding change in the same meeting. The fraud rate for that channel dropped in the following month, and the team adopted the impact-first format for future risk reviews.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For fraud investigation questions, use the PSAR structure: start with the Problem (what signal triggered the investigation), describe the Signals you examined, walk through your Analysis (segmentation, root cause), and close with your Recommendation. This maps naturally to how a risk team like sardine's operates.

For SQL questions, narrate your logic before or while writing the query. Say out loud: 'I will first filter users with zero prior activity, then join to transactions on the same day, then count by user.' Interviewers care as much about your reasoning as the syntax.

For stakeholder communication questions, use BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front): state the finding and its business implication in the first sentence, then provide the supporting data. At a company where fraud decisions are time-sensitive, burying the conclusion is a red flag.

For ambiguity questions (missing data, no control group), show your thinking in layers: acknowledge the limitation, explain what you would do with the data you have, and state what additional data would make the analysis stronger. This signals maturity without being evasive.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Sardine interviewers look for a specific set of signals, shaped by the nature of their product.

Strong SQL and data manipulation skills. Fraud analysis is query-heavy. Expect at least one hands-on SQL problem involving window functions, CTEs, or aggregations over time-series transaction data.

Risk domain intuition. You do not need prior fraud experience, but you should understand the trade-off between catching more fraud (higher recall) and blocking fewer good customers (lower false positives). Show that you think about both sides of that equation.

Clear, structured communication. Sardine serves enterprise clients who need actionable reports. Interviewers want to see that you can tell a story with data, not just produce a table.

Comfort with ambiguity. Real fraud data is messy. Candidates who freeze when data is incomplete or who only feel safe with textbook problems tend to struggle. Show that you can make reasonable assumptions and move forward.

Genuine curiosity about the product. Read up on device intelligence, behavioural biometrics, and how real-time fraud scoring works. Candidates who understand what sardine actually builds stand out from those who treat it as a generic analytics role.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: SQL and data fundamentals. Practice window functions (ROW_NUMBER, LAG, LEAD, running totals), CTEs for multi-step logic, and time-series aggregations. Kaggle has free fraud transaction datasets that make ideal practice material. Focus on queries that calculate rolling rates, identify first and last events per user, and flag velocity patterns.

Week 2: Fraud and risk domain. Read about the false positive vs. false negative trade-off in fraud detection, KYC funnel metrics (completion rate, drop-off by step, approval rate), and what device fingerprinting does at a high level. You do not need to be a fraud specialist, just conversant enough to discuss trade-offs confidently.

Take-home preparation. Candidates report sardine often sends a dataset with a problem statement. Practice presenting findings as a short narrative: three to five slides or a clean notebook that leads with the business implication, not the methodology. Clarity matters more than complexity here.

Mock interviews. Do at least two mock SQL interviews out loud before the real one. Narrating your query logic while writing is a skill that needs practice, not just SQL knowledge.

Company research. Read sardine's public blog and product pages to understand the fraud types they tackle (card testing, account takeover, synthetic identity). Work at least one sardine-specific example into your answers to show genuine interest.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Solving SQL problems silently. Many candidates write a query without explaining their thinking. Interviewers want to follow your reasoning, so narrate as you go.

Treating false positives as acceptable collateral. At a fraud prevention company, blocking a legitimate customer is a real cost. Candidates who focus only on catching fraud without discussing the impact on good users miss a core part of sardine's product philosophy.

Over-engineering the take-home. Some candidates submit notebooks with complex ML models when the prompt only asked for SQL and a short summary. Match the depth of your response to what was actually asked.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved the fraud detection process' without a concrete action or measurable outcome is a missed opportunity. Sardine interviewers are quantitative, so specifics always land better than generalities.

Not asking clarifying questions. If a problem statement is ambiguous (and fraud problems often are), asking one smart clarifying question signals experience. Diving in without checking assumptions can send you down the wrong path.

Skipping the business context. Every analytical finding should connect back to a decision or action. Candidates who present data without a recommendation often do not advance past the technical round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the sardine Data Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report three to four rounds in most cases: an initial HR or recruiter screen, a take-home analytics challenge, one or two technical rounds with the data or risk team, and a final conversation with the hiring manager. The take-home is typically the most important filter. Round structures can vary by team, so confirm the process with your recruiter after the first call.

What SQL topics should I focus on for a sardine Data Analyst interview?

Focus on window functions (ROW_NUMBER, LAG, LEAD, running totals), CTEs for multi-step logic, and aggregations over time-series data. Fraud-specific patterns like velocity checks (how many events per user in a time window) and first-event detection come up often. Practice writing queries that are readable and well-structured, not just syntactically correct.

Do I need prior experience in fraud or fintech to get this role?

Prior fraud experience helps but is not a strict requirement, especially for entry and mid-level roles. What matters more is that you understand the false positive vs. false negative trade-off, can reason about anomalies in transaction data, and show genuine curiosity about the domain. Reading sardine's public product content before the interview goes a long way toward demonstrating that interest.

What salary can I expect for a Data Analyst role at sardine in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Data Analyst salaries in India broadly run 5-10 LPA for entry-level profiles (0-2 years), 10-18 LPA for mid-level (3-5 years), and 18-30 LPA for senior roles (6-9 years). For sardine-specific numbers, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi, keeping in mind that sample sizes for any single company can be limited. Actual offers also depend on your experience level and how you negotiate.

How long does the sardine hiring process take end to end?

Candidates typically report a two to four week timeline from the first recruiter call to an offer, though this varies by team and how quickly you return the take-home. Submitting the take-home promptly and following up politely after each round helps keep momentum. If you have a competing offer deadline, mention it to the recruiter early so they can try to align timelines.

Is there a coding round in the sardine Data Analyst interview?

The coding component is typically SQL-focused rather than algorithmic. Some candidates report being asked to write Python for data manipulation using pandas as part of the take-home or a technical round. Full software engineering problems like algorithm-style questions are not commonly reported for this role. Confirm with your recruiter what tools and languages are expected before your round.
