01 Overview

Overview

superk is building hyperlocal grocery and FMCG distribution for Tier-2 and Tier-3 India, working at the intersection of offline kirana networks and digital commerce. As of mid-2026, the company has 19 open Product Manager positions, signaling active team expansion across multiple product areas.

PM interviews at superk typically span three to four conversations. Candidates report a recruiter screening, a product case round, and a final discussion with a senior leader or founder. The process tends to move quickly for candidates who can show a clear understanding of price-sensitive consumers, supply chain realities, and mobile-first design for users who may be new to app-based shopping.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and the product domain superk operates in. Expect a mix of product design, analytical, and behavioural questions.

  1. How would you design a feature to increase repeat purchase rate for grocery customers in a Tier-2 city?
  2. Walk us through a product you built or owned end-to-end. How did you decide what to prioritise first?
  3. superk competes with both local kirana stores and large e-commerce platforms. How would you position the app to win a customer who currently buys everything from a kirana?
  4. How would you measure success for a new same-day delivery feature? What is your north star metric and what supporting metrics would you track?
  5. Describe a time you had to make a product decision with incomplete or conflicting data. What did you do?
  6. How would you improve the onboarding experience for a first-time grocery app user in a small town where internet is slow and screen literacy is low?
  7. A key supplier says their products are not getting enough visibility on the platform and is threatening to reduce stock. How do you handle this as a PM?
  8. How would you design a loyalty or rewards programme that works for customers spending modest amounts per order?
  9. Tell me about a time you worked with an engineering team under a tight deadline. What did you cut, and why?
  10. How would you grow average order value without raising prices?
  11. Describe a product feature or initiative that failed. What did you learn, and what would you do differently?
  12. How would you build a B2B product for kirana store owners who wholesale from superk?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you worked with an engineering team under a tight deadline. What did you cut, and why?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were building a reorder reminder feature for a grocery app. Three days before the planned launch, the backend team flagged that personalised reminders would need two more weeks of work due to a data pipeline issue.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to delay the launch, ship a stripped-down version, or find a workaround that still delivered real value to users.

*Action:* I ran a quick priority exercise with the team. The core value was reminding users before they ran out of staples. Personalisation was a nice-to-have at this stage. I proposed launching with category-level reminders (for example, 'You usually reorder atta every three weeks') instead of item-level ones, cutting the backend complexity entirely. I got alignment from engineering, design, and the business stakeholder within a day.

*Result:* We launched on time. The feature drove a measurable lift in weekly active users on our internal dashboard, and we shipped personalised reminders the following sprint once the pipeline fix was in place.

---

Q: Describe a product feature or initiative that failed. What did you learn?

*Situation:* I led a social sharing feature for a consumer app where users could share their purchase lists with family members. The team was convinced it would drive referrals.

*Task:* I owned the full product lifecycle from discovery through launch and post-launch measurement.

*Action:* We spent six weeks building it based on qualitative interviews with urban users. I had assumed that shared grocery lists were a universal behaviour in joint households and did not test this assumption broadly enough.

*Result:* Adoption was very low. Post-launch interviews revealed that in most households one person makes all grocery decisions and sharing felt unnecessary. I had over-indexed on a small, vocal segment. I now always test the 'who else in the household is involved' assumption before building collaboration features for everyday consumer apps.

---

Q: How would you improve the onboarding experience for a first-time grocery app user in a small town?

*Situation:* At a previous company, we noticed that users from Tier-3 cities dropped off at a much higher rate during onboarding compared to metro users.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the onboarding flow for this segment with a focus on reducing drop-off at account creation and first-order placement.

*Action:* I ran usability sessions (remote, over phone calls) with five users from smaller towns. Three core problems came up consistently: the app asked for too many details upfront, users were uncomfortable entering card details, and the product category structure felt unfamiliar. I redesigned the flow to allow browsing before sign-up, defaulted to cash-on-delivery, and replaced text category names with visual icons and local language labels.

*Result:* In a pilot with a small cohort, first-order completion improved noticeably. I presented the findings to the growth team and the changes were adopted in the main app.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product design questions respond well to a structured approach: start with the user, then the problem, then solutions, then metrics. Avoid jumping to features in the first sentence. Interviewers at product-focused startups want to see that you slow down and ask 'who is this for and what are they struggling with' before you start building.

RICE for prioritisation. When asked how you would prioritise a backlog or choose between two features, walk through Reach (how many users does this affect), Impact (how much does it move the metric), Confidence (how certain are you about your estimate), and Effort (how much engineering work is needed). Being explicit about your confidence level shows intellectual honesty and is valued at startups.

Metrics questions benefit from a funnel structure. Start at acquisition, move through activation, engagement, retention, and then revenue. For superk, a sensible funnel looks like: app install, first order placed, second order placed within a given window (the real retention signal for grocery), and monthly order frequency.

Behavioural questions call for the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation brief (two to three sentences). Spend most of your answer on Action, because that is what interviewers are evaluating. Quantify results where you can, and if exact numbers are not available, describe the directional outcome clearly.

Root cause and analytical questions benefit from a structured breakdown. State your hypothesis first, then list the dimensions you would slice data by (geography, device, user cohort, time of day), then explain what you would do with each finding. Avoid locking onto a single cause too quickly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

superk is building for a user profile that is different from the urban, English-first consumer most PM candidates have worked with. Interviewers are looking for genuine empathy with price-sensitive, mobile-first users in smaller cities, not just a stated awareness of the segment.

Customer understanding. Can you describe a Tier-2 grocery shopper's decision-making process without relying on assumptions from your own lifestyle? Candidates who have done real field research or usability testing with this segment stand out clearly.

Comfort with ambiguity. Early-stage and growth-stage startups rarely have clean, complete data. Interviewers want to see that you can make reasonable decisions with partial information and that you know when to gather more data versus when to ship and learn.

Cross-functional awareness. PM roles at superk involve working closely with supply chain, operations, and last-mile logistics teams, not just engineering and design. Showing that you understand operational constraints (delivery slot limits or cold chain requirements for perishables, for example) will differentiate you from candidates who think only in product terms.

Ownership mentality. Candidates report that superk interviewers respond positively to people who treat their product areas as their own business. Frame your answers around outcomes you personally drove, not just features your team shipped.

Communication clarity. You will often need to explain complex trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders. Interviewers assess whether your answers are crisp, structured, and free of unnecessary jargon.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product and user.
Download the superk app and use it as if you are a first-time buyer in a small town. Note what works, what confuses you, and what feels missing. Read any publicly available news about the company from 2024 to 2026 to understand where the team is focusing its energy.

Week 2: Practice product design and metrics questions.
Take two to three questions from the list above and write out full structured answers. Time yourself. Most PM case questions should be answered in a focused, concise way in a live setting. Record yourself if possible and listen back for filler words and clarity of structure.

Week 3: Prepare your behavioural stories.
Write out five to six experiences from your career using the STAR format. Cover at least one failure, one cross-functional conflict, one prioritisation decision under pressure, and one example of customer research directly driving a product decision. Practice telling each story in under three minutes.

Before the interview.
Prepare three to four thoughtful questions for your interviewer. Good questions signal product thinking: ask about the metrics the team is most focused on right now, what the biggest unsolved product problem is, or how the team balances speed with quality.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to solutions too fast. The most common PM interview mistake is proposing a feature within the first thirty seconds of a design question. Take a moment to ask a clarifying question or state your assumptions out loud. Interviewers almost always reward this pause.

Ignoring operational and supply chain constraints. For a company like superk, a feature that sounds great on paper (two-hour delivery for every category, for example) may be impossible given cold chain or last-mile realities. Show that you think about feasibility alongside desirability.

Using metro-user assumptions for Tier-2 audiences. Candidates often describe features designed for urban, high-disposable-income users. If the question is about superk's core market, explicitly state that your assumptions are different: smaller basket sizes, cash-on-delivery preference, lower smartphone storage, and limited data connectivity.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Name the specific event (second order placed within two weeks, weekly active buyer rate) and explain why that metric captures the real business outcome you care about.

Not quantifying outcomes in STAR answers. Even without exact numbers, you can say 'drop-off reduced noticeably in our pilot cohort' or 'the feature was adopted by the majority of daily active users within two weeks.' Vague results like 'it went well' leave interviewers unconvinced.

Treating every round as a case screen. superk PM interviews typically include leadership and values conversations. Do not switch off your storytelling instincts once the case round is done.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a superk PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process of three to four conversations, typically starting with a recruiter screening, followed by a product case discussion, and ending with a senior leader or founder round. Some candidates report an additional take-home assignment or a second case round depending on the level. The exact structure varies by team, so it is worth confirming the format with your recruiter after the first call.

What salary can I expect as a PM at superk?

Compensation depends on your level and years of experience. Based on knok jobradar data for PM roles in India, Associate PMs are typically in the 12-20 LPA range, PMs with 3-6 years of experience are in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PMs are typically at 40-60 LPA. Group or Principal PM roles are commonly cited at 55-90+ LPA. Actual offers vary based on your specific background, competing offers, and the team's budget.

Does superk ask SQL or data questions in PM interviews?

Candidates report that superk PM interviews focus more on product thinking and case discussions than on SQL or technical coding tests. However, you should be comfortable interpreting data, defining metrics clearly, and walking through how you would analyse a product problem. Being able to describe what data you would pull and why (without necessarily writing the query) is usually sufficient for most PM roles.

How should I prepare for superk's product case questions?

Start by deeply understanding the superk product and its target user in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities. Practice using a structured approach: define the user, identify the core problem, propose two to three solutions, and state how you would measure success. Avoid jumping to features before you have clearly framed the problem. Practice out loud, ideally with a mock interview partner, so your answers are crisp and well-paced.

Is prior grocery or e-commerce experience required to get a PM role at superk?

Domain experience is helpful but not required. Candidates report that superk values strong product thinking and genuine user empathy over a specific industry background. If you do not have direct grocery or FMCG experience, show that you understand the category constraints (perishables, basket size, purchase frequency) and the user profile (Tier-2 consumer, price sensitivity, digital literacy). Doing real research on the product before your interview goes a long way.

How important is startup experience for a PM role at superk?

superk operates in a fast-moving environment where PMs are expected to own outcomes with limited resources and shifting priorities. Candidates who have worked at startups or in entrepreneurial roles within larger companies tend to align well with this expectation. If your background is primarily from a large company, come prepared with examples of times you worked with ambiguity, made decisions without full data, or drove a project from idea to launch without a large support structure behind you.
