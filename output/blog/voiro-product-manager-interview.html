01 Overview

Overview

Their platform helps sales teams manage advertising inventory, track revenue, and generate insights, placing the PM role at the intersection of data, B2B SaaS, and the media industry.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioural questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you had to say no to a feature request from an important client.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a large enterprise client requested a fully custom reporting module that the engineering team estimated would take three months to build.

*Task:* I needed to evaluate the request fairly, weigh it against committed roadmap items, and communicate the decision clearly without damaging the relationship.

*Action:* I first spoke with the client's operations lead to understand the root problem behind the request. I found that most of their core need could be addressed by extending our existing export feature, a change that would take two weeks. I mapped the remaining gaps and presented this alternative with a clear timeline, showing exactly which parts of their need it would solve and what would be deferred to a future release.

*Result:* The client accepted the alternative. The enhanced export feature was later adopted by several other clients, turning it into a high-leverage improvement. The client renewed their contract that cycle.

---

Q: How would you prioritise features for an ad revenue dashboard serving both sales and finance teams?

*Situation:* Two internal stakeholder groups at my previous company, a sales director and a CFO's team, both needed new features in the same quarter but had conflicting priorities.

*Task:* I had to create a prioritised plan that served both groups without delaying a committed product milestone.

*Action:* I ran separate discovery sessions with each team and mapped their requests to underlying jobs-to-be-done. I scored features on an impact-versus-effort grid and found significant overlap: both teams needed reliable revenue forecasting data, just presented in different views. I proposed a shared forecasting engine with two distinct interfaces, which reduced total engineering effort considerably.

*Result:* We shipped the shared engine in six weeks. Both teams reported higher satisfaction in the next internal survey, and we avoided building two separate overlapping dashboards.

---

Q: Describe a product decision you made with limited or ambiguous data.

*Situation:* My team was deciding whether to build a mobile app for our B2B analytics platform. Usage data was thin because we had never formally surveyed clients about their mobile workflows.

*Task:* I needed to make a clear go-or-no-go recommendation to the product head even without strong quantitative backing.

*Action:* I ran interviews with eight client teams over two weeks, focusing on where they consumed data outside their desks. I also referenced publicly available industry surveys on mobile adoption in media sales roles. Based on these signals, I recommended a phased approach: ship a mobile-first daily revenue snapshot in four weeks, then review engagement data before committing to further investment.

*Result:* Within three months, mobile engagement grew steadily among sales reps who worked on the go. That data gave us confidence to invest further in the mobile experience.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and strategy questions, a simple structure works well: clarify the goal, identify the user segments affected, explore options, evaluate trade-offs, and commit to a recommendation with measurable success metrics. Voiro is a data-heavy company, so interviewers respond well when you name specific metrics rather than speaking in vague outcomes.

For prioritisation questions, an impact-versus-effort grid is effective and easy to explain out loud. Be ready to define what 'impact' means in a B2B context: revenue effect, client retention risk, and engineering complexity all belong in that conversation.

For behavioural questions, use STAR and keep the Situation and Task to two or three sentences. The Action section is where you differentiate yourself: describe your personal decisions and the trade-offs you weighed, not just what the team eventually shipped. Voiro interviewers typically care about how you navigated ambiguity or stakeholder tension.

For diagnostic or root-cause questions (such as 'yield forecasting is inaccurate'), use a structured approach: clarify how the metric is defined and measured, segment the problem by client type, time period, or ad category, hypothesise two or three causes, then propose both an immediate fix and a longer-term solution.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity over domain expertise. Voiro serves a specialised vertical, and interviewers do not expect every candidate to arrive with deep ad-tech knowledge. What they look for is a clear pattern of learning complex domains quickly. If you have worked in a niche B2B space before, highlight how you got fluent in that domain and how long it took.

Comfort with data. The Voiro platform is analytics-heavy. Saying 'I would look at the data' is not enough. Interviewers want to hear which metrics you would look at, how you would segment them, and what you would do when the data is incomplete or contradictory. Bring specific examples from past roles.

B2B client empathy. Voiro's customers are publishers, broadcasters, and media companies. These are not individual consumers. Interviewers want to see that you understand long sales cycles, multi-stakeholder buying decisions, and the cost of breaking existing client workflows. Show that you treat client feedback as a signal to investigate, not as a direct feature spec.

Structured thinking under pressure. In product design rounds, candidates who jump straight to solutions without framing the problem tend to score lower. Take a moment to structure your thinking out loud. Interviewers often care as much about your process as your final answer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your domain foundation.
Learn the basics of the publisher-side ad-tech ecosystem: how digital advertising is bought and sold, what yield management means, and what terms like CPM, fill rate, programmatic, SSP, and DSP refer to. Read whatever is publicly available about Voiro's platform, including their blog and any customer stories. Understand the problem they solve for media companies and who the typical buyer is.

Week 2: Prepare your STAR stories.
Build five or six strong behavioural examples from your past work. Cover at least one story for each theme: a product you launched end to end, a feature you killed or deprioritised, a conflict with a stakeholder, a data-driven decision made with incomplete information, and a time you learned a new industry quickly. Practice telling each story in under three minutes.

Week 3: Practice product design out loud.
Record yourself answering product design questions or do mock interviews with a peer. Focus on structuring your answer before diving into solutions. Prepare thoughtful questions for the panel: ask about current roadmap challenges, how they measure PM success, and how product and engineering collaborate day to day. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the domain homework. Candidates who walk in without understanding basic ad-tech concepts struggle to engage with scenario questions. You do not need prior ad-tech experience, but you should be able to discuss what a publisher's revenue operations challenge looks like and where Voiro fits in solving it.

Treating client feedback as a roadmap. In B2B companies, it is tempting to say 'I listen closely to clients and build what they ask.' Voiro interviewers want to see that you can synthesise feedback across clients, identify patterns, and make independent product decisions rather than acting as a feature transcription service.

Vague Action sections in STAR answers. Many candidates describe the situation in detail and jump straight to the result without explaining what they personally decided or did. The Action section is where you differentiate yourself. Be specific about the trade-offs you weighed and the calls you made, not just the team's output.

Ignoring metrics in design answers. When asked to improve a feature or design a new one, always tie your proposal to measurable outcomes. Saying 'this will improve revenue visibility for sales teams' is weaker than naming the specific metric you would track and how you would know the feature is working.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Voiro PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds. These commonly include an initial HR or recruiter screen, one or two product thinking rounds with senior PMs or the head of product, and a final conversation with leadership. The exact structure can vary by role and team, so ask your recruiter to confirm the process early on.

Do I need prior ad-tech experience to apply for a PM role at Voiro?

Prior ad-tech experience is not typically listed as a hard requirement, and candidates without it do receive offers. What matters more is your ability to learn a specialised domain quickly and to think through complex B2B product problems with structure. Be ready to show how you have picked up new domains in past roles and how quickly you got productive.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Voiro?

Voiro does not publicly publish salary ranges. Based on knok job radar data for 2026, PM roles in the broader market range from 24-40 LPA for mid-level roles and 40-60 LPA at the senior level, though actual offers vary by experience and role scope. How long does the full hiring process take at Voiro?

Candidates commonly report a process lasting two to four weeks from first contact to offer, though timelines vary based on team availability and role urgency. If you have a competing offer with a deadline, communicate that to your recruiter early. Companies typically move faster when candidates are transparent about their situation.

What is the best way to prepare for Voiro's product design round?

Focus on structuring your thinking before jumping to solutions. Practice framing the user, the problem, and the success metric before proposing features. Doing two or three timed mock sessions out loud, even with a peer, makes a noticeable difference in how structured you sound under pressure.

Does Voiro hire associate or entry-level PMs?

Voiro typically hires PMs with some prior product or adjacent experience. An associate PM role is possible for the right candidate, but most open roles require at least a couple of years in product management or a closely related function such as business analysis, growth, or strategy consulting.
