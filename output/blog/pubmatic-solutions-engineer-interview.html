01 Overview

Overview

PubMatic is a sell-side programmatic advertising platform that helps publishers manage and monetize their ad inventory at scale. A Solutions Engineer (SE) at PubMatic is a client-facing technical role: you help publishers and buyers integrate with PubMatic's SSP, OpenWrap header bidding wrapper, and Identity Hub products, debug technical issues in live ad serving pipelines, and advise on yield optimization strategies.

As of July 2026, PubMatic had 62 open roles listed on knok jobradar, making it one of the more active ad-tech hirers in India. Solutions Engineer openings concentrate in Bangalore and Mumbai. The interview process typically combines a screening call, a technical round covering ad tech fundamentals and SQL, and a client-simulation or case round. Candidates report that interviewers care most about your depth in programmatic concepts (RTB, OpenRTB, header bidding), your structured approach to troubleshooting, and how clearly you can explain technical ideas to a non-technical publisher.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in PubMatic SE interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. Walk us through how Real-Time Bidding works end to end, from a user landing on a publisher page to an ad being served.
  2. What is the OpenRTB protocol? Describe the key fields in a bid request and a bid response.
  3. A publisher calls you saying their fill rate has dropped sharply overnight. How do you troubleshoot it?
  4. Write a SQL query to find the top five publishers by total revenue for a given week.
  5. Explain header bidding. How is it different from a waterfall or passback setup?
  6. A DSP partner reports their bid responses are being rejected with a high error rate. What is your step-by-step approach?
  7. PubMatic's OpenWrap is a header bidding wrapper. How would you explain its value to a publisher's business team with no technical background?
  8. How do you handle multiple open customer escalations at the same time when all of them feel urgent?
  9. A publisher's engineering team cannot reproduce a cookie-sync failure that their users are seeing. Walk us through how you investigate.
  10. What metrics would you monitor to assess the overall health of a publisher's programmatic setup?
  11. Describe a time you had to translate a complex technical concept for a non-technical stakeholder. What was the outcome?
  12. How do you keep up with changes in programmatic advertising, such as cookie deprecation, privacy regulations, and new identity solutions?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how Real-Time Bidding works end to end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a newly onboarded publisher asked why their eCPMs varied so much across different times of day. I used this as a chance to walk them through the full RTB flow, and it became my go-to explanation structure.

*Task:* I needed to explain a multi-step, millisecond-level process clearly enough that both their engineering lead and their ad ops manager could follow along together.

*Action:* I broke the flow into four stages: (1) the user's browser fires an ad request when the page loads; (2) the publisher's SSP packages a bid request using the OpenRTB spec, containing ad size, floor price, and available user signals; (3) the bid request goes to connected DSPs who evaluate it and return a bid response within the timeout window; (4) the SSP runs the auction, the winning creative is returned to the browser, and the impression is logged. I then tied the eCPM variance back to demand-side competition patterns during peak and off-peak hours.

*Result:* The publisher's ad ops manager said it was the clearest explanation they had heard. They adopted my four-stage framing in their own internal training deck.

---

Q: A publisher's fill rate dropped sharply overnight. How do you troubleshoot it?

*Situation:* I received an escalation from a large publisher: their fill rate had fallen sharply starting at midnight, with no planned changes on their side.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly and communicate clearly to both the publisher and our internal engineering team.

*Action:* I followed a top-down funnel approach. First, I checked whether the drop was global (all ad units, all geos) or isolated (one placement, one region), which pointed toward either a platform issue or a configuration issue. I then pulled bid request and impression logs for that time window and compared request volume versus bid response count versus win rate. The data showed bid request volume was normal but win rate had collapsed. I checked floor price settings and found that an automated rule had triggered a sharp floor increase at midnight, pushing most DSP bids below the floor. I flagged this to the publisher immediately and suggested temporarily reverting the floor rule while we reviewed the automation logic.

*Result:* Fill rate recovered within a short window of reverting the rule. I wrote up a post-mortem and recommended the publisher add alerting on large automated floor price changes going forward.

---

Q: Describe a time you translated a complex technical concept for a non-technical stakeholder.

*Situation:* I was supporting a publisher whose CFO wanted to understand why switching from waterfall to header bidding would improve their yield, before approving the migration budget.

*Task:* I had to explain header bidding without assuming any programmatic knowledge, and connect it directly to a business outcome the CFO cared about: revenue.

*Action:* I used a simple auction analogy. Waterfall, I explained, is like selling a house by approaching buyers one by one in a fixed order, only moving to the next buyer if the previous one says no. Header bidding is like calling all interested buyers at the same time and taking the highest offer. I showed a simplified before-and-after comparison (keeping the publisher's actual data confidential) and explained that more simultaneous bidders generally means more competition and a higher winning price per impression.

*Result:* The CFO approved the migration. The publisher's team later told me the 'house auction' analogy was what made the concept click for their leadership.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For troubleshooting questions (fill rate drops, bid rejections, latency spikes):
Use a top-down funnel. Start broad (is this global or isolated?) and narrow down (which stage of the pipeline failed?). Name the data sources you would check: bid request logs, win rate reports, creative audit logs, floor price settings, integration specs. State your communication plan with the client as you go.

For 'explain a concept' questions (RTB, header bidding, OpenRTB):
Lead with the business problem the technology solves, then describe the mechanism step by step, then connect back to the publisher's or buyer's outcome. Avoid jargon in your first sentence. If the interviewer is technical, add protocol-level detail in a second pass.

For SQL and data questions:
Think aloud: name the table(s) you would use, the columns you need, any joins or aggregations, and edge cases (nulls, duplicate rows). Write clean, readable SQL with aliases. For a question like 'find the top five publishers by revenue,' sketch it as: SELECT publisher\_id, SUM(revenue) AS total\_revenue FROM impressions GROUP BY publisher\_id ORDER BY total\_revenue DESC LIMIT 5.

For behavioral questions (prioritization, stakeholder management):
Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences combined). Spend most of your time on Action: what you specifically did, not what the team did. End with a concrete Result where possible.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

PubMatic SE interviewers consistently look for a few things, based on what candidates report:

Deep ad tech fluency. You should be able to explain RTB, OpenRTB bid request and response structure, header bidding, cookie syncing, and viewability without prompting. Surface-level definitions are not enough. Interviewers typically probe 'what happens when X goes wrong.'

Structured troubleshooting. Client-facing SEs handle escalations constantly. Interviewers want to see a systematic method: isolate the scope, identify the stage of failure, check data before drawing conclusions, communicate clearly throughout.

Client communication quality. You will be tested, sometimes through a role-play, on how you handle an anxious publisher on a call. Calm, clear, honest communication matters as much as the technical answer.

SQL and log analysis. Expect at least one practical data question. Know how to write GROUP BY, JOIN, and window function queries. PubMatic's scale means you may work with large impression log datasets.

Ownership mindset. Interviewers notice the difference between 'I escalated to engineering' and 'I investigated the issue, identified the root cause, escalated with a clear hypothesis, and followed up until it was resolved.' Take ownership in how you narrate your stories.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Ad tech fundamentals
Review RTB end-to-end flow, the OpenRTB spec (bid request, bid response, win notice, loss notice), header bidding versus waterfall, and cookie syncing. Read PubMatic's public documentation on OpenWrap. Understand what an SSP does versus a DSP versus a DMP.

Week 2: SQL and data practice
Practice queries involving GROUP BY, HAVING, JOINs, and window functions (RANK, ROW\_NUMBER) on ad-tech style datasets (impressions, clicks, revenue by publisher or placement). Practice writing clean, readable SQL under time pressure.

Week 3: Behavioral and case prep
Prepare three to five STAR stories covering: a complex technical issue you debugged, a time you managed a difficult client, a time you explained a technical concept to a non-technical person, and a time you had to prioritize competing demands. Rehearse a 'troubleshoot a fill rate drop' scenario out loud.

Week 4: PubMatic-specific research
Read PubMatic's recent press releases and earnings commentary for current product focus areas (Identity Hub, Activate, supply-path optimization). Understand their publisher and buyer value propositions. Prepare two or three questions that show you have done this research.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Defining RTB without going deep enough. Saying 'an ad auction happens in milliseconds' is a starting point, not an answer. Interviewers expect you to walk through the actual flow: bid request construction, DSP evaluation, auction logic, win/loss notification. Stopping at a surface definition signals you have read a summary, not worked in the field.

Jumping to solutions before clarifying scope. Candidates often skip the first and most important question: 'Is this drop global or limited to specific placements or geos?' That one question narrows the problem space dramatically and signals structured thinking. Interviewers consistently reward candidates who ask it and penalise those who skip straight to guessing root causes.

Writing SQL that works but is hard to read. Use aliases, format your query across multiple lines, and handle obvious edge cases (filtering out test traffic or null revenue rows). A working query written as one unreadable line suggests you do not write production-grade SQL.

Centering your STAR stories on the team, not yourself. 'We resolved the issue' tells the interviewer nothing about your specific contribution. Use 'I' and be specific: what did you personally investigate, decide, or communicate?

Not knowing PubMatic's own products. SE interviews at product companies typically include a question about how you would explain the company's product. Read the OpenWrap documentation and understand the difference between PubMatic's SSP, OpenWrap, and Identity Hub before your first round.

Overselling or underexplaining in client-facing scenarios. If a role-play puts you in front of a 'panicking publisher,' avoid both extremes: do not promise a fix timeline you cannot guarantee, and do not go so deep into technical detail that the client feels lost. Calm, structured, honest updates are what interviewers reward.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the PubMatic Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds: an initial HR or recruiter screening, a technical round covering ad tech concepts and SQL, a client-simulation or case round, and a final managerial or cultural-fit conversation. Some candidates report an additional take-home or system design component. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level.

Is coding a major part of the PubMatic SE interview?

SE roles at PubMatic are not pure software engineering roles, so heavy algorithmic coding (competitive programming style problems) is not typically the focus. Candidates report that SQL is tested seriously, and you may be asked to write or review simple scripts or API calls. Understanding integration concepts (REST APIs, JSON payloads, JavaScript tags) matters more than writing complex algorithms.

What ad tech topics should I prioritize for the technical round?

Focus on RTB end-to-end flow, the OpenRTB bid request and bid response structure, header bidding versus waterfall, cookie syncing, and viewability measurement. PubMatic is an SSP, so understand the sell-side perspective deeply. Knowing how OpenWrap works and what supply-path optimization means will set you apart from candidates with only generic ad tech knowledge.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer role at PubMatic in India?

PubMatic does not publish fixed salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and industry surveys for Solutions Engineer roles at programmatic ad tech companies in Bangalore do include some figures, but sample sizes for this specific role are small, so treat any numbers with caution. Your best data point is to ask the recruiter directly during the screening call and benchmark against publicly reported figures on Glassdoor or levels.fyi.

Are there Solutions Engineer openings in cities other than Bangalore?

As of July 2026, knok jobradar showed PubMatic with 62 open roles across India. Among Solutions Engineer postings across all companies on the platform, Bangalore led with 55 openings, followed by Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20), with smaller counts in Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). For city-specific PubMatic openings, check current listings directly, as distribution shifts regularly.

How important is prior ad tech experience for this role?

Prior ad tech experience is a strong advantage but not always mandatory, especially at the associate level. Candidates report that interviewers will test your understanding of programmatic concepts regardless of your background. If you are coming from a different technical domain, invest heavily in learning RTB, OpenRTB, and header bidding before applying, and frame your troubleshooting and client-facing experience clearly in your resume and answers.
