01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Squarespace is a New York-based website-building and e-commerce platform, popular with small business owners, freelancers, and creators who want a polished online presence without hiring a developer. The company is well regarded for its design-first culture and close collaboration between engineering and product teams.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 25 open roles at Squarespace. Openings span software engineering, data, product management, design, and customer operations. Some roles are US-only, but Squarespace has historically listed remote-eligible positions as well, so it is worth reading each listing carefully before applying.

The hiring process is structured and thorough. Squarespace takes time to assess both technical skill and cultural fit, so expect multiple stages spread over a few weeks from first contact to offer.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Squarespace's hiring process typically runs through the following stages. Timelines and exact stages can vary by role and team, so treat this as a general guide rather than a guaranteed sequence.

Stage 1: Application and resume review
Your resume is screened against the job description, often with an ATS before a recruiter looks at it. Customising your resume to match the role's exact language improves your chances of being noticed.

Stage 2: Recruiter call
A short introductory call covering your background, current situation, and interest in Squarespace. Salary expectations often come up here, so be ready with your range.

Stage 3: Technical or skills screen
For engineering roles, this is commonly a timed coding challenge or a take-home problem. Design candidates often do a portfolio walkthrough. Product managers may receive a short case study or an analytical question to work through.

Stage 4: Technical interview panel
Engineers typically face two to three rounds covering data structures, algorithms, and system design for senior roles. Expect questions about trade-offs, not just solutions. The panel usually includes engineers from the team you would be joining.

Stage 5: Behavioural interviews
Squarespace uses structured behavioural questions to understand how you collaborate, handle setbacks, and take ownership. You will likely speak with a cross-functional mix of interviewers across these rounds.

Stage 6: Offer
After the panel, the team aligns internally. If all goes well, the recruiter reaches out with a verbal offer before formal paperwork follows. Negotiating at this stage is normal and expected.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Strong fundamentals, clearly communicated. For engineering roles, Squarespace expects solid grounding in data structures, algorithms, and system design. Senior candidates should be ready to discuss architecture decisions and trade-offs, not just write working code.

Craft and attention to detail. Squarespace's product is built on visual polish and reliable performance. They look for people who hold themselves to the same standard, whether that means clean code, a tight design, or a well-structured document.

Ownership and independence. Interviewers want to see that you can take a problem end-to-end. They look for people who identify what needs to be done and follow through without needing to be guided through each step.

Clear written communication. With distributed teams, the ability to express ideas clearly in writing, whether in a Slack thread, a code comment, or a design doc, matters as much as being sharp in a live meeting.

Genuine interest in the product. Squarespace builds tools for small business owners and creators. Candidates who show real empathy for that user and curiosity about the product tend to stand out in interviews.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product before your first call. Sign up for a Squarespace trial and build a sample site. Form a real opinion: what works well, what feels clunky, what you would improve. A concrete product observation in your interview signals genuine interest and makes your answers memorable.

Practise coding with a focus on fundamentals. For engineering roles, work through problems covering arrays, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. For system design, be ready to reason through something like a URL shortener, a notification system, or a search index. Focus on explaining your trade-offs out loud, not just arriving at an answer.

Prepare four to five strong work stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure examples of ownership, handling ambiguity, cross-functional collaboration, and delivering under pressure. A solid set of stories covers most behavioural questions you will face.

Research the company's recent direction. Read Squarespace's product blog and look at recent feature launches. Any publicly reported news about the company's priorities is worth knowing. Showing you understand where the company is headed makes a strong impression on interviewers.

Prepare smart questions to ask. Ask about team structure, how success is measured in the first six months on the job, or what the biggest challenge the team is currently working through. Questions like these show you are thinking about the role seriously.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Squarespace is known for a design-first culture where product and engineering work closely together. The company generally values quality over speed, preferring to ship something well-considered rather than rushing to release. This shows up in interviews too: they want to see your thinking process, not just a fast answer.

The company runs a hybrid and remote-friendly setup across offices in the US and Ireland. Because teams are spread out, written communication is treated as a real skill. Being able to express ideas clearly in a document or a message thread matters a great deal day-to-day.

Squarespace's mission centres on helping people build a meaningful online presence. Many employees genuinely care about that mission, and it shapes how teams prioritise work. If you have a personal connection to small business owners, freelancers, or creators, mentioning it in your interview makes your interest feel authentic rather than rehearsed.

The culture is relatively flat for a company of Squarespace's size. Individual contributors are expected to take real ownership and bring their own ideas to the table, not just execute tasks handed down to them.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Squarespace hire candidates based in India?

Squarespace's primary offices are in the US and Ireland, and many of its roles are tied to those locations. That said, some roles are listed as remote-eligible, which may be open to international candidates depending on legal and time zone requirements. Always read the location field in each listing carefully, and ask the recruiter about remote eligibility before investing time in the full process.

How long does the hiring process at Squarespace usually take?

The full process from application to offer commonly spans a few weeks, though this varies by role, team, and interview slot availability. If you have not heard back after one to two weeks at any stage, a polite follow-up with your recruiter is perfectly reasonable. Having a competing offer in hand can sometimes accelerate timelines on their end.

What kind of coding questions should I expect at Squarespace?

Engineering interviews at Squarespace commonly cover data structures and algorithms, with a focus on arrays, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. For senior roles, system design is a significant part of the process. Interviewers generally care as much about how you reason through a problem as whether you arrive at the optimal solution, so talking through your thinking out loud is important.

Do non-engineering roles also involve a technical screen?

Non-engineering roles at Squarespace typically do not involve coding rounds. Product managers may face case studies or analytical exercises, while design candidates usually do a portfolio walkthrough. Support and operations roles generally focus on situational and behavioural interviews. The job description will usually give you a clear sense of what the assessment format looks like.

What salary can I expect from Squarespace?

Squarespace does not publicly publish salary bands for all roles. For US-based positions, Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry publicly reported ranges shared by employees, which can give you a rough benchmark. Compensation for remote or India-based positions may differ from those figures. It is best to share your expectations with the recruiter early in the process to avoid a mismatch at the offer stage.

How do I write a strong answer to 'Why Squarespace?' in the interview?

The strongest answers are specific: mention a product feature you use or find well-designed, reference something about the company's mission that resonates with you, or connect your own background to the small business and creator community Squarespace serves. Generic answers about 'great design' are common and easy to overlook. A concrete product observation or a personal connection to the user base makes your answer stand out from the crowd.
