01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Island builds the Island Enterprise Browser, a security product that gives IT and security teams full control over how employees access the web at work. Think of it as a browser built for enterprises, with data protection, threat defence, and visibility baked directly into the browsing experience. The company has found strong traction in regulated industries like finance, healthcare, and legal, where data leakage and compliance are constant concerns.

As of July 2026, Island has 69 open roles across engineering, product, sales, customer success, and corporate functions. The company is still in a high-growth phase, so hiring is active and the bar is real, but so is the opportunity to build something in a genuinely new product category. Most roles are US-based, but Island does post remote positions, and Indian professionals with strong backgrounds in cybersecurity, enterprise SaaS, or sales engineering have paths into the company.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Island hiring process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact structure varies by team and role level. Here is what candidates commonly describe:

Recruiter screen. A brief introductory call to confirm your background, interest in the role, and basic fit. The recruiter will ask about your current situation and what draws you to enterprise security or browser technology.

Hiring manager interview. A deeper conversation about your experience and how it maps to the role. For engineering roles this often includes a discussion of past projects and technical decisions you made. For sales or customer success roles, expect questions about deal cycles, customer relationships, and your experience working with enterprise buyers.

Technical or skills assessment. Engineering candidates typically receive a take-home exercise or a live coding session focused on the skills listed in the job description. Security engineers may be asked to walk through threat models or discuss past incident response work. Sales engineering candidates often do a mock demo or whiteboard exercise.

Panel interviews. A set of back-to-back conversations with team members and cross-functional stakeholders. Expect a mix of technical depth, behavioural questions, and a culture conversation. This is where Island assesses whether you will thrive in a fast-moving environment.

Reference checks and offer. Island typically runs reference checks before extending an offer. Once cleared, offers move fairly quickly.

Candidates commonly report a timeline of three to six weeks from first contact to offer, though this can shift based on the urgency of the role.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Island is building a product category that did not exist a few years ago, so they value people who can operate with ambiguity and learn fast. A few themes come through consistently in job descriptions and candidate feedback:

Security mindset. Even in non-security roles, Island wants people who understand why enterprises care about data protection, compliance, and threat prevention. If you can speak the language of a CISO, you will stand out.

Enterprise software experience. Island's customers are large organisations with complex procurement, IT, and security teams. Experience working with or selling to enterprise buyers matters across engineering, sales, and customer success alike.

Problem-first thinking. The company values people who start from the customer problem and work backwards to the solution, rather than those attached to a particular technology or approach.

Ownership and pace. Island is still a growth-stage company. They look for people comfortable owning outcomes, not just tasks, and who can move quickly without waiting to be told what to do next.

Clear communication. Given that the product serves enterprise buyers, written and verbal communication is important across all functions, especially roles that involve customer interaction.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the product before you apply. Island's enterprise browser is the core of what you will be working on or selling. Read publicly available material on the enterprise browser category, understand what problems it solves for IT and security teams, and be ready to explain why the product matters in your own words.

Brush up on enterprise security concepts. Even for engineering or customer success roles, interviewers will expect you to understand concepts like data loss prevention (DLP), secure web gateways, endpoint management, and identity access management at a working level.

Prepare specific examples from past work. Island interviews are heavily behavioural at the panel stage. Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure answers about past projects, decisions, or challenges. Concrete examples beat general claims every time.

Research the competitive landscape. Being able to discuss how Island sits in the enterprise security market and what makes its approach different shows genuine preparation. Publicly reported industry analysis on secure browsing and zero-trust access gives good context here.

For engineering roles, revisit technical fundamentals. Commonly cited preparation advice for browser or security engineering includes reviewing networking basics, browser internals (how rendering and JavaScript engines work), and secure coding practices.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Island operates with the urgency of a company still proving a new product category while also carrying the confidence that comes from real enterprise traction. A few things define the day-to-day culture:

High ownership, low hierarchy. Employees are expected to take initiative and make decisions without waiting for sign-off at every step. This suits people who find traditional corporate structures frustrating.

Customer obsession. Because Island serves large enterprises, understanding what the customer needs and delivering on it consistently is a core value. This shows up in how teams prioritise features, handle escalations, and communicate internally.

Cross-functional collaboration. Engineering, sales, and customer success work closely together. Given the company's size, silos are not really tolerated.

High standards for quality. Island competes in a space where security failures have real consequences. The culture reflects that, with a preference for careful review and doing things right over doing them fast.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Island hire outside the US?

Island's headquarters and most roles are based in the US, but the company does post remote positions. Indian professionals in cybersecurity, enterprise SaaS engineering, or sales engineering should check the job description carefully for location requirements. If a role is listed as remote without a geographic restriction, it is worth applying. For customer-facing roles, time zone overlap with US business hours is often expected.

What kinds of roles does Island typically hire for?

As of July 2026, Island has 69 open roles across software engineering (frontend, backend, security), sales, sales engineering, customer success, product management, and corporate functions like HR and finance. Engineering and go-to-market roles tend to be the most active hiring areas given the company's current growth stage.

How long does the Island interview process take?

Candidates commonly report a timeline of three to six weeks from first contact to offer. This covers the recruiter screen, hiring manager conversation, technical assessment, and panel interviews. Timelines can be shorter for urgent roles and longer if the panel stage involves many stakeholders across the organisation.

What salary can I expect at Island?

Compensation data for Island is limited and should be verified on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before you negotiate. Island is a growth-stage company and typically offers a mix of base salary and equity. Equity upside can be meaningful at this stage, so make sure you understand the vesting schedule and equity type in any offer you receive.

Are Island interviews technical even for non-engineering roles?

Yes, to a degree. Sales and customer success roles at Island involve technical product discussions, because the buyers are IT and security teams who ask detailed questions. Sales engineering and solutions consultant roles in particular involve mock demos and architecture discussions. Understanding zero-trust security concepts and enterprise browser technology will help you across most role types.

How do I stand out in the Island hiring process?

Tailor your resume to show experience with enterprise security, SaaS products, or regulated-industry customers. In your outreach to the hiring team, mention a specific customer problem that Island's browser solves, which signals genuine research rather than a generic application. Candidates who can connect their past work directly to Island's product mission tend to move faster through the process.
