01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Ama Assn, short for the American Medical Association, is one of the most recognised healthcare organisations in the United States, known for shaping health policy, publishing JAMA (the Journal of the American Medical Association), and advocating for physicians and patients across the country. If healthcare policy, medical research, or digital health excites you, this is a place where your work connects directly to real-world outcomes.

As of mid-2026, Ama Assn has 36 open roles across areas including technology, health policy, data analytics, communications, digital health, finance, and operations. The organisation is headquartered in Chicago, with a number of remote and hybrid positions that can make certain roles accessible to candidates outside the US or based in Indian tech hubs.

Roles here are competitive. The AMA attracts candidates who are genuinely motivated by mission, so quality of application matters a great deal. A strong resume is a starting point, but the organisation also expects you to show a clear sense of why healthcare advocacy matters to you personally.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Most candidates who are invited to interview at Ama Assn go through a process with several distinct stages. Timelines vary by role and team, so treat the sequence below as a general guide rather than a fixed rule.

Application and HR screen: After you submit your application online, a recruiter typically reaches out if your profile is a strong match. The initial call is brief, covering your background, interest in the role, and basic logistics. This is also your first opportunity to show that you understand the AMA's mission.

Hiring manager conversation: If the recruiter screen goes well, you move to a conversation with the hiring manager. Expect questions about your specific experience, how you approach problems, and what draws you to this particular role. For technical positions, some managers include light technical questions at this stage.

Panel or team interview: For most mid-level and senior roles, a panel round follows. You may speak with a small group including peers, cross-functional partners, or a senior leader. The 'tell me about a time when' format is widely used at this stage, so preparing a handful of strong examples from your career is important.

Skills assessment or work sample: Roles in content, policy, data, or technology sometimes include a take-home assignment or a short in-interview exercise. This is meant to evaluate practical ability, not just interview presence. Assignments are generally focused and reasonable in scope.

Final conversation and offer: A closing conversation with a senior leader or HR often precedes the offer. This tends to be a culture and alignment check more than a hard evaluation. If you have not heard back within a week of your last round, a polite follow-up to your recruiter is perfectly acceptable.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Healthcare or adjacent knowledge: You do not need a medical degree, but familiarity with the US healthcare system, what physicians face day-to-day, or how health policy is made gives you a clear advantage. Even prior experience in a health-adjacent field or volunteer work in healthcare settings signals the right orientation.

Clear, structured communication: The AMA produces policy statements, research publications, and advocacy materials that reach doctors, legislators, and the public. Even in technical roles, the ability to write clearly and explain complex ideas in plain language is consistently valued.

Analytical thinking: Roles in data, technology, and policy all expect you to back your thinking with evidence. Glassdoor reviews and publicly reported employee feedback suggest the organisation values people who can frame a problem clearly, not just execute tasks they are given.

Cross-functional collaboration: The AMA works in interdisciplinary teams where policy, digital, and communications functions regularly overlap. Showing that you have worked across departments or with diverse stakeholders makes a strong impression.

Mission motivation: Interviewers at the AMA typically probe for why healthcare and why the AMA specifically. A genuine, specific answer referencing something real about the organisation's work will stand out far more than a general statement about wanting to help people.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the AMA's current priorities: Read the AMA's recent policy positions and understand what the organisation has been publicly advocating for in 2025 and 2026. Knowing the healthcare debates the AMA is engaged in gives you natural talking points throughout every round, not just the 'why us' question.

Build a set of behavioural examples: The AMA's interview process leans heavily on the Situation, Task, Action, Result (STAR) format. Prepare a handful of strong examples from your past work that you can adapt to questions about leadership, handling conflict, cross-team collaboration, and solving problems under pressure.

Tailor your application to the specific role: Generic resumes and cover letters do not perform well here. Highlight any healthcare, policy, or social impact experience you have, even if it is tangential. Your cover letter should speak both to your qualifications and to why the AMA's mission resonates with you personally.

Practise your 'why AMA' answer: This question comes up in almost every round. Vague answers like 'I want to make an impact in healthcare' will not stand out. Reference something specific, such as a policy initiative the AMA is leading, a challenge in the health system it is addressing, or a publication that shaped your thinking.

Follow up professionally: A brief, courteous note to your recruiter or interviewer after each round is a small step that is noticed in mission-driven organisations. It reinforces your interest and leaves a positive impression.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Ama Assn has a professional, purpose-driven culture that moves at a deliberate pace. It is not a startup environment. Decision-making involves multiple stakeholders, thoroughness is prized over speed, and the work is shaped by the organisation's long-standing mission to improve healthcare in America.

Mission is central: Employees across departments frequently describe a shared sense of purpose that goes beyond job titles. The AMA's work connects directly to how health policy is written, how physicians are trained, and how patients are treated. This shared orientation is a real part of daily culture, not just a line in the employee handbook.

Commitment to health equity: The AMA has made public commitments around health equity and diversity. Internally, this shows up in conversations about representation in policy, programming, and hiring. Candidates who bring experience with underserved communities or equity-focused work tend to resonate well with interviewers.

Reasonable work-life balance: Industry surveys and publicly reported employee reviews suggest the AMA offers a more sustainable pace than consulting or high-growth tech roles. Extended hours are not the norm, though teams do put in additional effort during major policy cycles or publication deadlines.

High employee tenure: Many AMA employees stay for several years, which reflects genuine satisfaction with the work and culture. It also means that when a role opens up, the bar is high. You are being compared, at least implicitly, to the kind of dedicated, long-term contributor the organisation is used to working with.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the hiring process at Ama Assn typically take?

The full process from application to offer varies by team and seniority. Based on commonly reported candidate experiences, the process runs across several weeks from first contact to offer. Following up politely with your recruiter after each stage is a good practice, as timelines can stretch during busy periods or when multiple rounds are involved.

Is a cover letter important when applying to Ama Assn?

At a mission-driven organisation like the AMA, a well-written cover letter carries more weight than it might at a purely commercial employer. Use it to connect your specific background to the role and to articulate why the AMA's mission matters to you personally. A generic cover letter that could apply to any employer is unlikely to strengthen your application here.

Do I need a healthcare background to be considered for a role at Ama Assn?

Not for all roles. Technology, finance, communications, and operations positions are open to strong candidates from adjacent industries. However, even for non-clinical roles, showing some familiarity with the US healthcare system or a genuine interest in health policy will help your candidacy. The AMA consistently hires for mission alignment alongside technical skill.

What types of roles is Ama Assn currently hiring for?

As of mid-2026, Ama Assn has 36 open roles spanning a range of functions. These typically include technology and software development, data analytics, health policy and research, communications and content, digital health programmes, finance, and operations. Checking the AMA's official careers page directly gives you the most current picture of available positions.

How should I answer 'why do you want to work at the AMA' in my interview?

Be specific rather than general. Reference a recent policy initiative, a challenge in the healthcare system that the AMA is actively addressing, or a piece of research or advocacy from the organisation that influenced your thinking. Generic answers about 'wanting to make an impact in healthcare' are common and forgettable. Showing that you have actually engaged with the AMA's work is what separates strong candidates from the rest.

Does Ama Assn offer remote or hybrid work options for candidates outside the US?

The AMA has expanded its remote and hybrid offerings in recent years, and a portion of its current open roles include remote or hybrid arrangements. Specific work-model details vary by team and position. It is worth clarifying the expected setup with your recruiter early in the process so there are no surprises on either side.
