01 Overview

Overview

Alaan is a B2B spend management and corporate card platform built for finance teams across the Middle East and South Asia. As a Product Designer at Alaan, you would work on products that help finance managers control company spending, automate expense reporting, and get real-time visibility into budgets. The company currently has 22 open roles, signalling active growth.

Across India, knok jobradar tracked 393 Product Designer openings as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 62 and Delhi at 33. Alaan sits in the fintech-for-business space, so interviewers look for designers who can handle complex workflows, dense data tables, and the practical needs of non-technical finance users.

Candidates report a process that typically includes a portfolio review, one or two design exercises, and stakeholder-style discussions. Rounds are not always named the same way, so treat the outline below as a guide, not a guarantee.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you had to simplify a complex data-heavy screen without losing information.

*Situation:* I was designing the transaction history screen for an internal expense tool used by a mid-sized company's finance team. The existing table had many columns and users were scrolling horizontally to find key fields.

*Task:* My goal was to reduce cognitive load and help finance managers find the most relevant information faster, without hiding data they occasionally needed.

*Action:* I ran several contextual interviews with finance team members to find out which columns they checked first every day. I grouped columns into a primary view showing the top fields and a secondary expandable panel for the rest. I then tested two prototypes with the same users in follow-up sessions and iterated on label wording based on their feedback.

*Result:* The redesigned screen reduced the average number of clicks users needed to locate a specific transaction. The finance lead reported that weekly reconciliation felt noticeably faster, and the engineering team appreciated that the component structure was cleaner to build.

---

Q: Describe a situation where engineering pushed back on your design. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* I had proposed an animated card-flip interaction for a spend summary widget. The front-end engineer flagged that the animation would add significant complexity given the existing tech stack.

*Task:* I needed to deliver a solution that met the user need (quick access to card details) without creating unnecessary engineering debt.

*Action:* I set up a short working session with the engineer to understand the constraints. Together we explored a slide-out drawer pattern that achieved the same goal. I updated the prototype, ran a quick preference test with two internal users, and confirmed the drawer pattern tested just as well.

*Result:* The feature shipped on schedule. The engineer later said the collaboration made the handoff much smoother, and the pattern became a reusable component in the design system.

---

Q: How do you conduct user research when your users are busy finance professionals who are hard to reach?

*Situation:* At a previous role I was redesigning an approval workflow and needed input from CFOs and finance controllers, who had very limited availability.

*Task:* I had to gather enough qualitative insight to make confident design decisions without demanding too much of participants' time.

*Action:* I prepared a focused discussion guide covering only the top decisions I needed to resolve. I offered async options, including a short screen-recording task where participants narrated their current workflow. I also used existing support tickets and sales call recordings as secondary research to fill gaps.

*Result:* I collected enough signal to identify the two biggest friction points in the approval flow. The redesign addressed both, and the product manager noted that user objections during UAT were lower than on previous launches.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For portfolio walkthroughs, use a 'problem, process, outcome' spine: state the user problem in one sentence, describe the research and iterations you went through, then share a measurable or observable outcome. Alaan interviewers typically want to hear how you handled constraints (technical, time, or business) as much as the final design.

For hypothetical redesign questions (like 'how would you improve our expense flow'), use a structured discovery approach: start by clarifying who the primary user is, what job they are trying to do, and what success looks like. Then describe how you would research before proposing solutions. Avoid jumping straight to wireframe ideas. Candidates report that showing your thinking process matters more than the solution itself.

For conflict or collaboration questions, follow a straightforward four-part structure: context, your specific action, the other person's response, and the outcome for the product. Stay factual and avoid framing colleagues as obstacles.

For metrics and success questions, pick one or two behavioural signals (task completion, error rate, support tickets) and one business signal (retention, activation). Explain why those metrics connect to the design change you made.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Alaan's product serves finance managers who need precision, not just aesthetics. Interviewers are typically looking for designers who can think in workflows, not just screens. They want to see that you understand the difference between a consumer app and a B2B tool where errors have real financial consequences.

Domain fluency matters. You do not need prior fintech experience, but you should be comfortable talking about concepts like approval hierarchies, spend limits, real-time transaction data, and audit trails. Spending time with Alaan's public product screenshots or demo videos before the interview shows initiative.

Storytelling over polish. A common observation from candidates is that Alaan interviewers ask follow-up questions that probe your reasoning, not just your output. They want to know why you made each choice, what you ruled out, and what you would do differently. A slightly rough prototype with a clear rationale lands better than a polished mock with vague justification.

Cross-functional awareness. Because Alaan is a growth-stage company, designers often work closely with engineers and product managers. Interviewers look for evidence that you can communicate constraints, negotiate scope, and adapt designs based on technical feedback without losing the core user value.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Research and portfolio curation

Learn what Alaan does: read their website, look for any public product demos or case studies, and understand their core user (finance managers, CFOs, controllers). Then select two or three portfolio projects that involve complex workflows, data-heavy interfaces, or B2B users. For each project, prepare a tight narrative covering the problem, your process, and a concrete outcome.

Week 2: Practice and mock runs

Write out answers to the questions listed above. Pay attention to answers that rely on numbers: use only figures you can attribute to a source (Glassdoor, publicly reported data, your own project metrics). Practice saying 'I do not have that data yet, but here is how I would find it' rather than guessing. Ask a friend or peer to listen to your portfolio walkthrough and flag any moments where the 'why' behind a decision is unclear.

Week 3: Design exercise prep and final checks

Candidates report that some rounds include a take-home or live design exercise. Practise whiteboard-style walkthroughs where you narrate your thinking out loud: user, problem, constraints, options considered, recommendation. Prepare a few questions to ask your interviewers about the design team's current challenges and how success is measured on the product side. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to solutions in hypothetical questions. When asked 'how would you redesign X', many candidates skip straight to layouts. Interviewers at growth-stage fintechs want to see you ask clarifying questions first.

Presenting only the final design. Showing only polished screens without explaining the iterations and dead ends you explored leaves interviewers guessing about your process. Walk through what you tried and why you changed direction.

Using consumer-app language for a B2B product. Phrases like 'delight' and 'aesthetic experience' land differently in a fintech context. Frame your value in terms of accuracy, efficiency, trust, and compliance.

Ignoring the business context. Finance tools exist to support real money decisions. Candidates who do not connect their design choices to business outcomes (faster approvals, fewer errors, reduced support volume) miss a key part of what Alaan evaluates.

Weak metrics. Saying 'users liked it' is not enough. Even if your project had no formal analytics, you can describe proxy signals like reduced support tickets, faster task times observed in testing, or positive feedback from a UAT session.

Underselling collaboration. Product design at a startup involves constant negotiation with engineering and product. If your case studies only mention your own contributions, interviewers may wonder how you work in a team.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Alaan Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a portfolio review, a design exercise, and one or more stakeholder discussions. The exact number of rounds varies and Alaan may adjust the process as the company grows. Treat any outline you read online as a general guide and confirm the structure with your recruiter when you get the invite.

Does Alaan give a take-home design assignment?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home exercise focused on a B2B workflow or data screen, while others describe a live whiteboard session. The format is not fixed. Practise both so you are comfortable narrating your thinking out loud as well as preparing a more polished async submission.

What salary can I expect as a Product Designer at Alaan?

Salary depends on your experience level. Based on knok jobradar data for Product Designer roles in India, mid-level designers (3-5 years) typically see ranges of 14-24 LPA, while senior designers (6-9 years) see 26-40 LPA. Alaan-specific compensation is not publicly reported, so use these bands as a reference and check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for more recent company-specific data before negotiating.

Do I need fintech experience to get this role?

Not necessarily. Alaan values designers who can handle complex workflows and data-heavy interfaces. If your background is in enterprise SaaS, HR tech, or any tool used by non-technical business users, that experience transfers well. Show that you understand how finance professionals think about accuracy, approval chains, and audit trails, and you will stand out even without a fintech title on your CV.

How competitive is the Product Designer job market right now?

Knok jobradar tracked 393 Product Designer openings across India as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 62 roles and Delhi at 33. Alaan alone had 22 open roles at that time, suggesting active hiring. B2B and fintech product design roles tend to attract fewer applicants than consumer roles, so strong portfolio storytelling gives you a real edge.

What is the best way to prepare for a live design exercise?

Practise narrating your process out loud before the session. Start every exercise by restating the user and the problem, then list the constraints you are assuming. Interviewers typically care more about how you think than whether your final wireframe is pixel-perfect. Candidates report that asking one or two clarifying questions at the start signals maturity and is viewed positively.
