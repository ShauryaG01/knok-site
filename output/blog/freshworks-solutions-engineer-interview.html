01 Overview

Overview

Freshworks is a Chennai-founded SaaS company whose product suite covers customer support (Freshdesk), CRM (Freshsales), IT service management (Freshservice), and team collaboration (Freshchat). The Solutions Engineer (SE) role is a pre-sales technical position: you run live product demos, handle technical objections from prospects, build proof-of-concept integrations, and partner with account executives to close deals.

As of July 2026, there are 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings across India. Bangalore leads with 55 SE openings, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). Freshworks itself has 145 open roles across job platforms, making it one of the more active hirers in the SaaS pre-sales space right now.

The interview process typically spans 3-4 rounds: an initial HR screen, a technical or product knowledge discussion, a live demo or role-play exercise, and a final conversation with a senior leader. Candidates report that the demo or role-play round carries the most weight, so practising a live Freshworks product walkthrough before your interview is non-negotiable.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Freshworks SE interviews are heavily scenario-based. Candidates report a strong emphasis on product knowledge, objection handling, and the ability to run a structured discovery call. These are the questions that come up most often:

  1. Walk us through how Freshdesk handles multi-channel ticket routing. How would you explain this to a prospect who currently uses only email support?
  2. A prospect says their IT team is already on ServiceNow and is skeptical about switching to Freshservice. How do you handle that objection?
  3. Tell us about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical decision-maker.
  4. How would you set up a live demo of Freshsales for a mid-market retail company that wants to track leads coming in from WhatsApp?
  5. Freshworks competes with Zendesk, Salesforce, and HubSpot. How would you position Freshworks against one of these in a competitive deal?
  6. Describe a proof of concept you built (or would build) to demonstrate Freshdesk's API integration with a third-party ERP system.
  7. A customer's pilot is going badly because data migration is behind schedule and the account executive is under pressure to close. What do you do?
  8. How do you stay current with Freshworks product updates, and how would you incorporate a new feature into an existing demo flow?
  9. Tell me about a deal where you had to significantly customise your technical pitch because of the prospect's unique use case.
  10. How do you structure a discovery call to uncover the technical requirements of a company evaluating Freshservice?
  11. Freshworks has Freddy AI built into its products. How would you demo Freddy's capabilities to a CTO who has had a poor experience with AI chatbots in the past?
  12. What metrics would you track to measure the success of a Freshdesk implementation during a pilot period?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical decision-maker.

*Situation:* I was supporting a sales cycle for a mid-sized logistics company. The Head of Operations wanted to understand how our API integration with their warehouse management system would work, but she had no technical background.

*Task:* I needed to explain REST APIs, webhooks, and data-sync logic in a way that built her confidence without losing her patience.

*Action:* I dropped the technical jargon and used an analogy she could picture: 'Think of the API like a phone call between our software and yours. When a ticket is raised in Freshdesk, it immediately calls your warehouse system and passes the update, so your team never has to log into two systems.' I then opened a live sandbox demo and triggered a webhook while she watched the data appear in both systems in real time.

*Result:* She signed off on the pilot that same week. She later told the account executive that 'the demo made everything click.' The deal closed within that quarter.

---

Q: A customer's pilot is going badly because data migration is behind schedule. The account executive is pushing to close. What do you do?

*Situation:* During a Freshservice pilot at a mid-sized manufacturing firm, the IT team's legacy ITSM data was only partially migrated by week three of the planned pilot period.

*Task:* I needed to keep the pilot on track technically while giving the account executive an honest picture of the commercial risk.

*Action:* I called an internal sync first. I told the AE clearly: pushing for a signature while the customer's team was frustrated would damage the relationship post-sale. I then called the customer's IT lead, acknowledged the delay without assigning blame, and proposed a short extension with a specific migration checklist and brief daily check-ins. I also set up a demo environment using the already-migrated data so stakeholders could see product value while migration continued in the background.

*Result:* The extension was approved and migration completed early in the extension window. The customer signed, and they expanded to Freshdesk six months later. The AE told me that slowing down the close had protected the account long-term.

---

Q: How would you demo Freddy AI to a CTO who has had a poor experience with AI chatbots before?

*Situation:* I was in a technical evaluation meeting with the CTO of a mid-market e-commerce company. He opened by saying he had wasted months on a chatbot from another vendor that gave wrong answers to customers.

*Task:* I had to rebuild his confidence in AI-assisted support without overpromising or dismissing his concern.

*Action:* I started by validating his experience: 'That is a real problem with generic chatbots that are not trained on your specific knowledge base.' I then showed him exactly how Freddy ingests a company's own help articles and past ticket data, and how it surfaces suggestions to human agents rather than replacing them outright in the first phase. I walked him through the confidence-score display so agents can see when Freddy is certain versus when it is unsure, and demonstrated the fallback rule: anything below a set threshold routes automatically to a live agent.

*Result:* He moved from skeptic to champion. He asked to include Freddy in the pilot scope specifically so his team could stress-test the fallback logic. The company went live on Freshdesk with Freddy enabled three months later.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Every 'Tell me about a time' question at Freshworks expects this structure. Keep the Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined), spend most of your answer on the Action, and always close with a concrete Result. Vague endings like 'the customer was happy' do not satisfy Freshworks interviewers.

Discovery-first for demo and scenario questions. Before describing any demo, state what you would first need to learn from the prospect. A reliable sequence: current pain (what is broken?), current tools (what do they use today?), success definition (what does a good outcome look like?), and technical constraints (integrations, security, compliance). Interviewers want to see that you do not demo blindly.

Three-part objection handling. When asked about objections such as competitor comparisons or feature gaps, structure your answer as: (1) Acknowledge the concern genuinely, (2) Reframe with evidence or a targeted demo moment, (3) Propose a concrete next step. Freshworks interviewers specifically watch for whether you stay calm and consultative under pushback rather than arguing with the prospect.

Business outcome first for metrics questions. Lead with the result the customer cares about, then name the metric. For example: 'Success for the customer is faster resolution for their end users. The metric I would track is first-response time and CSAT score inside the Freshdesk analytics dashboard, not just raw ticket volume.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report from Freshworks SE rounds, these are the qualities that move the needle:

Product fluency. Interviewers want to see that you have actually used Freshdesk, Freshsales, or Freshservice, not just read the marketing page. Free trial accounts are available. Candidates who reference specific UI workflows or configuration steps stand out immediately from those who speak only in generalities.

Structured communication. The SE role requires translating technical concepts for business buyers. Interviewers watch for whether you instinctively simplify without dumbing down. If your answer sounds like a product datasheet read aloud, that is a red flag.

Sales instinct alongside technical depth. This is not a pure engineering role. Interviewers want to see that you understand the commercial context: deal stages, why a delayed pilot can kill a renewal, and how to work alongside an account executive under pressure. Purely technical candidates who cannot connect features to business outcomes typically do not clear the demo round.

Composure under pressure. Role-play rounds at Freshworks often include a planted objection or a difficult prospect persona. The expectation is not a perfect answer but a calm, logical response that keeps the conversation moving forward.

Curiosity before pitching. The strongest candidates ask discovery questions before diving into a demo. Interviewers consistently notice when you assume the prospect's problem rather than diagnosing it first.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product immersion
Sign up for free trials of Freshdesk and Freshservice. Spend real time inside each product. Map out the core workflows: ticket creation, routing rules, SLA policies, and reporting dashboards. Read the Freshworks developer documentation on APIs and webhooks so you can speak confidently to integration scenarios without handwaving.

Week 2: Competitive and business context
Study how Freshworks positions itself against Zendesk, ServiceNow, HubSpot, and Salesforce. Focus on use-case fit rather than feature checklists. Read recent Freshworks blog posts and watch any publicly available demo videos to understand how the company presents its own products. Review the company's publicly reported milestones and recent product launches so you can speak to its direction.

Week 3: Mock interviews and demo practice
Practise a 10-minute Freshdesk demo out loud, ideally with a friend playing a skeptical prospect. Record yourself once and review for jargon, filler words, and moments where you assumed the prospect's problem instead of asking about it. Prepare STAR stories for at least five scenarios: explaining a technical concept, handling a competitive objection, managing a difficult pilot, customising a pitch, and collaborating with an account executive.

Before each round: Review the specific team segment (SMB, mid-market, or enterprise) from the job description. Tailor your demo story to a prospect that fits that segment. Candidates report that interviewers appreciate when you reference the right customer profile for the team you are joining.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Demoing without discovering. The most common mistake is launching into a product walkthrough before asking a single question about the prospect's situation. Interviewers treat this as a signal that you sell features, not solutions.

Treating the SE interview like a coding round. Freshworks SE interviews are not engineering assessments. Candidates who go deep on system architecture and skip the business-outcome narrative typically do not clear the demo round.

Being vague about results in STAR answers. 'The customer was happy' is not a result. Describe what actually happened: deal closed, pilot extended and won, account expanded. If you cannot share confidential figures, describe the qualitative outcome clearly and specifically.

Not knowing the Freshworks product portfolio. Candidates who confuse Freshdesk with Freshsales, or who cannot explain what Freshservice is for, signal that they did not prepare. With free trials readily available, there is no excuse for this.

Overloading the demo with features. A demo that tries to cover everything is worse than one that covers three features tied directly to a specific pain point. Freshworks interviewers reward focus and relevance, not comprehensiveness.

Folding under objection pressure. In role-play rounds, some candidates apologise or abandon their position the moment the prospect pushes back. The expectation is that you acknowledge, reframe, and move forward. Capitulating signals that you cannot hold a commercial conversation.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Freshworks Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report 3-4 rounds in most cases. The process typically starts with an HR screening call, moves into a technical or product knowledge discussion, and then includes a live demo or role-play round. A final conversation with a senior leader or hiring manager typically follows. Round sequences can vary by team and geography, so ask your recruiter upfront what to expect for the specific opening you applied to.

Do I need to know how to code for the Freshworks SE interview?

You do not need to write production code, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation and explaining how webhooks and REST APIs work in plain language. Candidates report occasional questions about how they would approach a basic integration between Freshdesk and a third-party tool. Knowing the Freshworks API well enough to describe a real use case is more important than writing code on the spot.

How should I prepare for the demo round?

Sign up for a free Freshdesk or Freshservice trial and practise a 10-minute demo out loud at least three times before your interview. Structure it as: open with a discovery question, show three to four features tied to a specific use case, handle one planted objection, and close with a clear next step. Candidates who rehearse out loud consistently outperform those who only read about the product or watch videos.

What is the salary range for a Solutions Engineer at Freshworks?

Freshworks has not published official salary bands publicly, and current job data does not include figures for this specific role. Glassdoor and industry surveys for SE roles in Bangalore commonly cite a wide range depending on years of experience and whether the team covers SMB or enterprise accounts. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for the most recent community-reported numbers before you negotiate an offer.

Is the Freshworks SE role more sales-focused or more technical?

It sits firmly in between, but candidates report that Freshworks leans toward sales instinct in their evaluation criteria. You need enough technical depth to run a credible demo and handle integration questions, but the role also requires you to understand deal stages, partner with account executives, and translate features into business outcomes. Engineers who are uncomfortable with commercial conversations typically find the role a stretch.
