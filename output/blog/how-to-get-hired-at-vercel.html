01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Vercel is a US-based cloud platform best known as the home of Next.js and as a deployment tool trusted by frontend teams worldwide. The company is fully remote-first and hires globally, which gives Indian engineers, developers, and go-to-market professionals a genuine shot at landing a role without relocating.

As of July 2026, Vercel has 73 open roles listed on its careers page, spread across engineering, product, design, sales, customer success, and developer relations. That is a healthy volume and signals active growth rather than a cautious hiring cycle.

Vercel's products sit squarely in the developer tooling space, so the bar for technical depth is high even in non-engineering functions. If you use their platform regularly, you already have a head start: you can speak about their product from a real user's perspective, which matters a lot to this team. Competition is global and stiff, but a strong portfolio, open-source contributions, and a clear understanding of the Vercel platform can set your application apart.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Vercel's hiring process varies by team and level, but candidates commonly report a sequence similar to the following.

Application and resume review. Your resume is screened by a recruiter, often within one to two weeks. Tailoring your resume to reflect hands-on experience with Vercel, Next.js, or modern frontend infrastructure helps you get past this stage.

Recruiter call. A short conversation covering your background, motivations, and logistics such as timezone and availability. This is also your chance to ask about team structure, role scope, and the pace of hiring.

Technical screen. For engineering roles, this is typically a one-hour call with a senior engineer covering JavaScript, TypeScript, React, and system design concepts relevant to frontend infrastructure. For non-engineering roles, this stage may be a skills-based conversation or a short written exercise.

Take-home or async task. Vercel is known for assigning a short practical task rather than live competitive coding. This might involve building a small feature, debugging a sample project, or writing a technical document. Treat this stage seriously: it is usually the heaviest signal in the process and the place where candidates most often differentiate themselves.

Final panel. A set of back-to-back video calls with potential teammates, cross-functional partners, and sometimes a senior leader. Expect a mix of technical depth, behavioural questions, and culture-fit conversations.

Offer. If all rounds go well, the recruiter returns with a written offer. Compensation for remote hires is typically benchmarked against global market rates, according to publicly reported accounts from past candidates.

The full process commonly takes three to six weeks from application to offer, though timelines vary by team urgency.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

For engineering roles, Vercel looks for strong command of JavaScript and TypeScript, solid React and Next.js knowledge, and a practical understanding of how modern web infrastructure works: CDNs, edge computing, serverless functions, and build pipelines. Hands-on familiarity with the Vercel platform itself is a meaningful signal.

Open-source contributions carry real weight. Vercel maintains Next.js and several other widely used projects. A history of meaningful contributions to public repositories shows both technical skill and the kind of collaborative, written communication style the company values. Even small but thoughtful contributions to documentation or bug fixes count.

For developer relations and customer success roles, Vercel wants people who can talk to developers as peers. If you have supported, trained, or sold to engineering teams, that experience translates directly into what they need.

Across all roles, the company consistently looks for:

  • Clear, concise written communication, since most work happens async across timezones
  • Customer obsession: a genuine interest in what developers struggle with and why
  • Ownership: the ability to ship things end-to-end without waiting for permission
  • Comfort with ambiguity in a fast-moving product environment
05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Build something real on Vercel before you apply. Deploy a non-trivial project on the platform. Use the Next.js App Router, experiment with Edge Functions or Middleware, and understand how deployments, preview environments, and environment variables work. This gives you concrete, specific examples to draw on in every round.

Study their changelog and engineering blog. Vercel ships frequently and documents it publicly. Reading recent releases tells you what problems the team is solving right now and signals to interviewers that you follow the space closely, not just casually.

Practise system design for frontend infrastructure. For senior engineering roles, expect questions like 'how would you design a caching layer for server components?' or 'what are the tradeoffs between edge rendering and server rendering for a high-traffic page?' Review these tradeoffs deliberately.

Sharpen your async writing. Vercel is remote-first and text-heavy. Before your interviews, practise writing clear, structured explanations of technical decisions. The take-home task often evaluates your written clarity as much as your code.

Prepare your open-source story. If you have contributed to Next.js, Turborepo, or any related project, be ready to walk through your contributions in detail. If you have not, even small contributions made before applying add credibility and show initiative.

Research their customers and competitors. Understanding why a team chooses Vercel over alternatives like Netlify, AWS Amplify, or self-hosted setups helps you speak the language of the business confidently in non-technical rounds.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Vercel operates as a remote-first company with employees spread across North America, Europe, and increasingly Asia. The culture is described by current and former employees in publicly reported reviews on Glassdoor and similar platforms as fast-paced, engineer-friendly, and strongly product-driven.

Developer obsession is the most consistent theme. Vercel's entire business depends on developers choosing their platform, so the team thinks deeply about developer experience in everything they build and every decision they make. If you are the kind of person who gets genuinely bothered by poor tooling or unnecessary friction in a developer workflow, you will find that instinct is welcomed here.

Async-first communication is real, not just a talking point. Most decisions happen in writing. Long-form documents, RFCs, and detailed pull request descriptions carry more weight than being online at the same hour as your manager. Indian professionals comfortable with structured written communication adapt well to this environment.

Shipping velocity is celebrated. The company is known for frequent releases and a strong bias toward getting things in front of users quickly, then iterating. This suits people who can move fast and accept that some things will be refined after launch rather than before.

Open-source alignment runs deep. Contributing to the broader web ecosystem is treated as part of the job, not a side activity. Employees are encouraged to engage publicly with the developer community, which creates visibility and career opportunities beyond the company itself.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Vercel hire engineers based in India?

Yes, Vercel is fully remote-first and hires globally. Indian engineers can apply for most engineering, developer relations, and go-to-market roles without relocating. Compensation for remote hires is typically benchmarked to competitive rates, and some roles use global benchmarks, according to publicly reported accounts from past candidates. Always check the specific job listing for any location restrictions before applying.

Do I need to know Next.js to get hired at Vercel?

For engineering roles, strong familiarity with Next.js is a significant advantage since Vercel maintains the framework and their platform is built around it. That said, deep knowledge of React, TypeScript, and modern frontend infrastructure can compensate if your Next.js exposure is limited. For non-engineering roles such as sales, support, or design, Next.js knowledge is helpful context but is rarely a hard requirement.

How important is open-source experience for a Vercel application?

It is genuinely valued, not just a nice-to-have. Vercel maintains Next.js, Turborepo, and other widely used open-source tools, so open-source participation is part of the company's identity. Contributions to relevant projects, especially Vercel's own, can set your application apart from equally skilled candidates. Even small but meaningful contributions such as fixing a confirmed bug or improving documentation count and show both technical engagement and written communication skill.

What is the take-home task like and how much time should I spend on it?

Candidates commonly report a short, practical assignment rather than abstract algorithmic puzzles. This might involve building a small feature in Next.js, debugging a provided project, or writing a technical proposal or explanation. The task is typically designed to reflect real work you would do on the job. Focus on code quality, clear documentation, and thoughtful tradeoffs rather than just getting something functional: the written explanation of your decisions is often as important as the code itself.

How long does the full hiring process take at Vercel?

The process commonly takes three to six weeks from application to offer, though this varies by role, team, and how urgently they need to fill the position. Engineering roles tend to have more rounds and take longer. If you have not heard back within two weeks of applying, a polite follow-up note to the recruiter is reasonable. Keep your broader job search active during this time since timelines can slip even when the process is going well.

Are there non-engineering roles at Vercel that suit Indian professionals?

Yes, and there are currently 73 open roles across functions including product management, design, developer relations, sales, customer success, and marketing. Developer relations and customer success roles in particular suit Indian professionals with a technical background and strong communication skills, since Vercel's users are developers and the company needs team members who can engage that audience credibly. Roles in sales engineering and solutions architecture are also worth exploring if you sit between technical and client-facing work.
