01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Karbon is a B2B fintech company building corporate credit cards and spend management tools for Indian businesses. The company has grown its product significantly and is actively hiring across multiple functions.

As of mid-2026, Karbon has 32 open roles across engineering, product, growth, sales, and operations. This signals a healthy growth phase, which means the company is bringing in fresh talent at multiple levels. Whether you are a fresher eyeing your first fintech role or a mid-career professional looking to move, there is likely a relevant opening worth applying to.

The hiring process is structured and typically spans several weeks. Karbon values candidates who understand the B2B fintech space and can demonstrate clear problem-solving ability. Preparation makes a real difference here.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Karbon's interview process typically follows these stages, though the exact structure may vary by role and team:

Stage 1: Application and Screening
Your resume is reviewed by the recruiter. For tech roles, a quick profile check often happens first. Make sure your resume clearly highlights relevant experience in fintech, SaaS, or related domains.

Stage 2: Recruiter Call
A short call with an HR or talent team member. Expect questions about your background, why you are interested in Karbon, your current notice period, and salary expectations. This is also your chance to ask about the team size, the role's scope, and next steps.

Stage 3: Technical or Skills Round
For engineering roles, this is usually a coding or system design round. For product roles, expect a product thinking exercise or a case study. For sales and operations, you may be given a scenario to walk through. This round tests how you approach problems, not just whether you know the right answer.

Stage 4: Panel or Manager Interviews
This typically includes one or two rounds with the hiring manager and cross-functional team members. Expect a mix of behavioural questions ('Tell me about a time you had to handle a difficult stakeholder') and role-specific depth questions. Candidates who can speak to outcomes and not just actions tend to do well here.

Stage 5: Leadership or Culture Round
Some roles include a final round with a senior leader or founder. This is less about technical depth and more about your thinking, values, and long-term fit. Be ready to talk about why fintech excites you and what kind of problems you want to solve.

Stage 6: Offer and Onboarding
If selected, you will receive a verbal offer followed by a written offer letter. Background verification is standard. Negotiate respectfully if needed, and clarify your joining date and onboarding process upfront.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Karbon looks for a specific profile, and understanding it helps you tailor your application and interviews.

Problem-solving with context
They want people who do not just solve problems but understand the business context behind them. For engineers, this means thinking about product impact alongside code quality. For product managers, it means connecting user pain points to business outcomes.

Ownership mindset
Karbon is a growth-stage company, which means teams are lean and individuals carry significant responsibility. They value candidates who say 'I will figure it out' more than those who wait for instructions. In interviews, show examples where you took initiative and saw something through to completion.

Fintech or domain curiosity
You do not need to have worked in fintech before, but you should be genuinely curious about it. Interviewers notice when a candidate has read about Karbon's product, understands how corporate credit works, or has opinions on the SME lending space in India. This curiosity signals long-term engagement.

Clear communication
Across all functions, Karbon values people who can communicate their thinking clearly, whether in writing or in conversation. In interviews, walk through your reasoning out loud. In case studies or assignments, structure your answer before diving into details.

Collaboration over ego
Interviewers often probe for how you have worked with people who disagreed with you, or how you handled a situation where you did not have all the answers. They want team players who can still make decisions under ambiguity.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the company deeply before you apply.
Go beyond the homepage. Understand Karbon's product (corporate credit cards, expense management), their target customers (Indian SMEs and corporates), and how they differentiate from competitors. Being able to speak about this in your interview immediately sets you apart.

Tailor your resume to the role.
If you are applying for an engineering role, highlight systems you have built and their scale and impact. If you are applying for product, show metrics you moved. Generic resumes get filtered out early. Use the job description as a checklist and match your language to it.

Practise structured answers.
For behavioural questions, use a simple structure: situation, what you did, and what happened as a result. Practise saying these answers out loud, not just in your head. Common themes include handling ambiguity, working with cross-functional teams, and driving outcomes with limited resources.

Prepare for a case study or assignment.
Many roles at Karbon include a take-home or live case study. For product roles, practise product critique and feature prioritisation exercises. For sales, practise pitching B2B products. For engineers, practise coding questions on standard platforms and revise system design fundamentals if you are applying for mid or senior levels.

Prepare smart questions for each stage.
Have a few thoughtful questions ready at every round. Ask about the team's current priorities, what success looks like in the first few months, or what challenges the team is navigating. This signals genuine interest and helps you evaluate fit from your side too.

Track your application and follow up.
If you applied through a job portal, follow up politely after a week if you have not heard back. A brief, professional note reiterating your interest often helps move things along. 06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Karbon operates with the energy of a growth-stage fintech. The culture tends to be fast-paced, with teams that value outcomes over process and directness over hierarchy.

Builders are celebrated here.
The company rewards people who ship, iterate, and improve. If you thrive in environments where you can see the results of your work quickly and where your ideas can actually influence the product roadmap, Karbon is likely a good fit.

Transparency and directness.
Feedback is typically direct and given frequently. This can feel intense if you come from a large corporate background, but most employees who prefer it find it refreshing. If you are used to environments where feedback is softened or delayed, be prepared to adapt.

Customer-first thinking.
Karbon's customers are Indian businesses trying to manage their spend more efficiently. The team genuinely cares about solving real problems for these customers, and that orientation tends to show up in how decisions are made across functions.

Collaboration across functions.
Engineering, product, sales, and customer success teams work closely together at Karbon. If you enjoy cross-functional work and want visibility into how different parts of a business operate, you will find plenty of opportunity here.

Growth opportunities.
Because the company is scaling, high performers often find that their responsibilities grow faster than they would at a large established company. This also means some processes are still being built, which can be an advantage if you like shaping how things are done.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does Karbon's hiring process take from application to offer?

The process commonly takes several weeks to a couple of months, depending on the role and how quickly rounds are scheduled. Engineering and senior roles tend to take longer because there are more interview stages. To speed things up, respond to recruiter messages quickly and have your availability for interview slots ready in advance.

Does Karbon hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

Karbon hires across levels. With 32 open roles currently, there are positions suited to both early-career candidates and experienced professionals. Entry-level roles are more commonly found in sales, operations, and customer success. Engineering and product roles typically require some prior experience, though strong internship or project experience can sometimes substitute.

What is the best way to get a referral at Karbon?

Look for Karbon employees on LinkedIn and connect with a personalised message explaining why you are interested in the company and which role you are targeting. Do not lead with 'please refer me.' Instead, start a genuine conversation about the team and the role. A referral from someone who knows your work carries significantly more weight than a cold application.

What kind of questions are asked in Karbon's technical interviews?

For engineering roles, expect coding problems covering data structures and algorithms, plus system design questions for mid-to-senior candidates. For product roles, expect product thinking exercises such as 'how would you improve this feature' or 'how would you prioritise this roadmap.' Karbon interviewers tend to focus on how you think, so talking through your reasoning matters as much as reaching the right answer.

How should I negotiate salary at Karbon?

Come prepared with a number grounded in your current CTC, any competing offers you hold, and publicly reported or Glassdoor salary data for similar roles at comparable fintech companies. State your expectation clearly and give a reason for it. Karbon, like most growth-stage companies, has some flexibility, especially for candidates with strong competing offers or specialised skills.

Can I apply to multiple roles at Karbon at the same time?

Yes, you can apply to more than one role if you genuinely qualify for each. However, limit yourself to two or three roles that closely match your skills, rather than applying to everything available. Applying broadly with an unfocused resume can signal a lack of clarity. If a recruiter reaches out, be transparent about which role excites you most and why.
