01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Aidoc builds AI-powered clinical decision support tools that help radiologists and hospital teams identify critical findings faster. Their platform analyzes medical images in real time, flags urgent scans, and pushes alerts to the right clinician before a life-threatening condition gets missed. Think of them as building the 'co-pilot' that sits alongside every radiologist on every shift.

As of July 2026, Aidoc has 17 open roles across engineering, product, clinical operations, sales, and customer success. The company has been growing steadily since 2024 as hospitals across the US, Europe, and the Middle East accelerate their adoption of clinical AI. For Indian professionals, software engineering and data science roles are the most common entry points, though product management and clinical operations positions do appear regularly.

Aidoc is headquartered in Tel Aviv, with a strong US presence. Many engineering and data science roles offer remote or hybrid flexibility, which opens the door for Indian candidates more than a traditional on-site company would.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Aidoc's hiring process typically runs across four to five stages. The exact steps vary by role and team, but here is what candidates commonly describe.

Recruiter screen. A short call with HR to confirm your background, gauge your interest in clinical AI, and do a quick alignment on expectations. Be ready to explain why healthcare AI appeals to you specifically, not just as a tech trend but in terms of real patient impact.

Hiring manager conversation. A deeper call with the team lead or manager. They will ask about specific past projects, how you handle ambiguity in fast-moving environments, and whether your technical depth matches what the role requires.

Technical assessment. For engineering and data science roles, expect either a take-home assignment or a live coding session. Problems often involve real-world data pipelines, model evaluation, or system design with reliability constraints. Product and clinical operations candidates typically receive a case study instead.

Panel interviews. Two to four conversations with team members, cross-functional partners, and sometimes a clinical expert. This round tests both your technical skill and your ability to communicate with non-technical colleagues, which Aidoc weighs seriously.

Closing call or offer. Some candidates have a short final conversation with a senior leader before the offer is extended.

The full process from first contact to offer typically spans two to five weeks, depending on scheduling and how quickly both sides move.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Aidoc is building software for a high-stakes clinical environment, so they need people who can combine strong technical skill with the careful, reliable thinking that healthcare demands.

Strong fundamentals over brand-name pedigree. For engineering roles, they care about clean code, solid data structures, and a genuine understanding of how systems fail. For ML and AI roles, comfort with Python and deep learning frameworks is expected, along with the ability to work on complex, messy real-world data.

Healthcare domain awareness. You do not need a medical degree, but candidates who understand basic radiology workflows, why false positives carry real costs in clinical settings, and how regulations affect product decisions stand out clearly from those who bring only technical backgrounds.

Cross-functional communication. Aidoc's product touches engineers, clinical scientists, hospital IT teams, and enterprise sales partners. Interviewers will probe whether you can explain technical decisions clearly to people who are not engineers, and give concrete examples of doing so.

Ownership mindset. The company expects individuals to take end-to-end responsibility for their work, from design through deployment and monitoring. Waiting for someone else to define the problem is not a trait that thrives here.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A few focused steps will put you ahead of most applicants.

Learn the clinical context first. You do not need to study medicine, but spend a few hours understanding what a radiology workflow looks like, what conditions like pulmonary embolism or intracranial hemorrhage are, and why early detection changes outcomes. Aidoc publishes blog posts and case studies that give you this context quickly and for free.

Refresh ML fundamentals with a healthcare lens. For AI and data science roles, practice explaining model performance metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, and AUC in plain terms. Be ready to discuss the trade-off between false positives and false negatives when the patient is the one at risk.

Prepare system design answers around reliability. Clinical software carries a higher reliability bar than most consumer apps. Interviewers will probe how you approach testing, graceful degradation, and safe deployment. Have concrete examples from your past work ready to discuss.

Tailor your resume to clinical AI keywords. Terms like 'medical imaging', 'DICOM', 'HL7 FHIR', 'clinical decision support', and 'regulatory compliance' help your application get noticed. Even if your background is not in healthcare, map your past work honestly to these themes wherever you can.

Practice explaining technical decisions to non-technical people. Prepare two or three stories where you translated a complex technical choice into plain language for a manager, a customer, or a clinical partner. Aidoc interviewers will ask for exactly these stories.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Aidoc is mission-driven in the most literal sense. The software they build affects patient outcomes, which gives the culture a real sense of urgency and shared purpose that is hard to manufacture at companies building less consequential products.

Impact is visible and close. Unlike building an ad ranking algorithm or a recommendation feed, the problems Aidoc solves have direct clinical stakes. Teams talk about the patients their product helps in a day-to-day operational way, not just in marketing language.

Collaboration is the default mode. Engineers work closely with clinical scientists, product managers, and hospital implementation partners. If you prefer deep solo work with minimal cross-team interaction, this culture will feel like friction. If you enjoy pulling different disciplines together toward a shared goal, you will feel at home.

Speed and rigour are both non-negotiable. Aidoc moves fast for a healthcare software company, but they do not cut corners on safety or regulatory compliance. Holding both values at the same time is a skill the company explicitly looks for in candidates.

Direct feedback is normal. The hierarchy is relatively flat, especially on the technical side. Feedback flows up and down, and people are expected to give honest input even when it is uncomfortable.

For Indian professionals, Aidoc's openness to remote and hybrid arrangements for certain roles makes the company genuinely accessible, not just theoretically so.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Can Indian professionals apply to Aidoc, and do they support remote work?

Yes, Indian professionals can apply to Aidoc. Many engineering and data science roles offer remote or hybrid flexibility, which means geography is not automatically a barrier. That said, roles requiring direct hospital engagement or US regulatory work are more likely to need a locally based candidate. Check each job description carefully for location requirements before applying.

What salary can I expect if I join Aidoc?

Aidoc does not publicly post salary bands, so there is limited verified data for Indian candidates specifically. For roles based in Israel or the US, publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest compensation is competitive with other well-funded health-tech companies. For remote roles accessible to India-based candidates, the pay structure may differ, so it is worth asking the recruiter directly during the first call rather than guessing.

Do I need a medical or clinical background to work at Aidoc?

No, a clinical background is not required for most roles. Engineering, data science, and product management positions are open to candidates with strong technical skills and a willingness to learn the healthcare domain. Showing genuine curiosity about how clinical AI works and why healthcare software has higher stakes than consumer apps will help your candidacy. A handful of roles, such as clinical implementation or medical affairs, do expect direct clinical experience.

How long does the full Aidoc interview process take?

From initial recruiter contact to a final offer, the process commonly takes two to five weeks. Scheduling delays, availability of panel interviewers, and internal approvals can all stretch this, so build some buffer into your timeline if you are managing other offers in parallel. Following up with the recruiter after each stage is completely normal and often helps things move faster.

What is the most common reason candidates do not make it through the Aidoc process?

Based on patterns in clinical AI hiring generally, candidates most often stumble at two points: the technical assessment, where gaps in coding or ML fundamentals become visible, and the cross-functional communication round, where purely technical candidates struggle to explain decisions in plain language. Preparing concrete stories that bridge both technical depth and clear communication will help you clear both hurdles.

Can I apply to more than one role at Aidoc at the same time?

Yes, applying to multiple roles is generally accepted at most tech companies, including those in the health-tech space. With 17 open roles at Aidoc right now, there may be more than one position that fits your background. The practical advice is to tailor your application for each role rather than sending the same resume everywhere, and to mention your broader interest to the recruiter on your first call so they can help point you to the best fit.
