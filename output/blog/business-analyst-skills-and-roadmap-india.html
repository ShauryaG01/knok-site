01 Role Overview

Role Overview

A Business Analyst (BA) is the bridge between business teams and technology teams. You gather requirements, document processes, analyse data, and make sure the solution that gets built actually solves the right problem. In India the role spans IT services, product companies, BFSI, e-commerce, consulting, and startups, so demand is wide and consistent across sectors.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Technical skills hiring managers check

SQL is the single most tested skill in BA interviews in India. You should be comfortable writing queries, joining tables, and interpreting results. Excel (or Google Sheets) comes right after: pivot tables, VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP, and basic data cleaning are expected. A BI tool like Power BI or Tableau is increasingly asked for, especially in product and analytics-heavy roles.

Beyond data tools, you need to write clear Business Requirement Documents (BRDs) and Functional Requirement Documents (FRDs). User story writing for Agile teams is now standard even in services companies. Knowing how to draw process flow diagrams using tools like Lucidchart or draw.io rounds off the technical picture.

Soft skills that get you hired and promoted

Communication is the core skill. You will be translating between two groups who speak different languages: business stakeholders who care about outcomes, and developers who care about specifications. Stakeholder management, meeting facilitation, and written clarity are all part of this. Critical thinking and the ability to ask 'why' before jumping to solutions separate strong BAs from average ones.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

Step 1: Get comfortable with SQL and Excel first

Take a free SQL course (Mode Analytics SQL Tutorial and W3Schools SQL are solid starting points). Practise in short, regular sessions rather than occasional long marathons. For Excel, focus on pivot tables and VLOOKUP because these appear in almost every BA screening test.

Step 2: Pick one BI tool and learn it properly

Power BI is the safer bet for Indian job listings because Microsoft's ecosystem dominates here. Download the free desktop version, connect it to a public dataset (Kaggle has many), and build a dashboard. Tableau Public is a free alternative if you prefer it.

Step 3: Learn Agile and practise writing user stories

Complete the free Scrum.org overview or the Atlassian Agile guide. Then write user stories for a product you use daily, an app, a website, anything familiar. This exact exercise often appears as a take-home task in BA interviews.

Step 4: Build one end-to-end project

Pick a real dataset (government open data, Kaggle, or your college's data), define a business problem, analyse it in Excel or SQL, visualise it in Power BI, and write a one-page BRD summarising your findings and recommendations. This project will do more for your resume than any certification alone.

Step 5: Get certified strategically

For freshers, the IIBA Entry Certificate in Business Analysis (ECBA) is the lowest-barrier entry. It requires no prior work experience and tests core BA fundamentals. Once you have two years of experience, the CCBA becomes achievable. The CBAP is for senior BAs with significant experience. Agile-focused roles value the Professional Scrum Product Owner (PSPO I) from Scrum.org, which is an affordable online exam.

Step 6: Polish your LinkedIn and Naukri profiles

Add your SQL and Power BI projects as portfolio links. Write a headline that clearly includes 'Business Analyst' and the industry you are targeting. Recruiters in India search Naukri heavily, so a fully complete Naukri profile with relevant skill tags is not optional.

Step 7: Apply with focus, not volume

Target roles that match your current skill level. A focused set of well-matched applications consistently outperforms sending hundreds of generic ones. Customise your resume for each role's key skills and highlight domain experience where it is relevant.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

Starting from scratch: fresher or career switcher

Month 1: Complete a SQL course and master the core Excel functions. Set up a Kaggle account and explore one dataset to get comfortable with real data.

Month 2: Start Power BI or Tableau. Build your first simple dashboard. Read a free BABOK Guide summary to understand the BA knowledge framework.

Month 3: Write your first set of user stories. Do a mock Agile sprint on paper for a product you use daily. Begin preparing for the ECBA exam if you want a fresher-friendly certification.

Month 4: Complete your end-to-end project covering data, analysis, and a one-page BRD. Add it to your resume and LinkedIn. Start applying to junior BA and business operations roles.

Month 5-6: Focus on interview preparation. Common Indian BA rounds include an aptitude and SQL test, a case study or gap analysis round, and an HR round. Mock interviews with peers are more effective than solo preparation.

One to two years of experience

Year 1, months 1-6: Close skill gaps, typically SQL depth or stakeholder communication. Pursue the CCBA if you are eligible. Begin targeting mid-level BA roles in product or analytics teams.

Year 1, months 7-12: Lead at least one requirement-gathering cycle end to end at your current company. Document it clearly because this story becomes your primary interview material for the next role.

Three or more years in: the specialisation stage

At this stage, domain or track specialisation matters more than additional certifications. Pick a domain (BFSI, healthcare, e-commerce, ERP) or a track (product management, data analytics, project management). Glassdoor and Naukri salary data commonly shows that senior BAs with domain specialisation command meaningfully higher compensation than generalists at the same level of experience.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

Naukri and LinkedIn: use both, differently

In India, Naukri.com is where many mid-market IT services and BFSI companies post BA roles. Keep your Naukri profile fully complete and update it every few weeks so it stays fresh in recruiter searches. LinkedIn is stronger for product companies, MNCs, and startups. A well-written LinkedIn profile with recommendations from colleagues or managers carries real weight with these employers.

Communities worth joining

The IIBA India chapter holds regular events and webinars. Search for 'IIBA India' on LinkedIn to find the local chapter in your city. The 'Business Analyst India' group on LinkedIn has active discussions covering interview prep and certifications. Reddit's r/businessanalysis has a global community with many Indian members sharing real hiring experiences and tips.

College background matters less than you think

Tier-1 engineering or MBA colleges help for structured intake programs at large IT companies. But a large share of BA openings, especially in startups and mid-size companies, care more about your SQL score on the screening test and your ability to walk through a case study clearly. A strong project portfolio and sharp communication can offset a tier-2 college background in most of these roles.

Domain knowledge is your fastest accelerator

Indian hiring managers frequently prefer candidates who already understand their domain. If you have a background in banking, target BFSI BA roles. If you come from healthcare operations, healthcare IT BA roles are a natural fit. Do not erase your domain experience when switching to BA: frame it as a strength, because many competing candidates have no domain depth at all.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What salary can a Business Analyst expect in India in 2026?

Salary varies widely by city, company type, and years of experience. Publicly reported data on Naukri and Glassdoor commonly shows entry-level BAs earning in the 4-7 LPA range, mid-level BAs with three to five years of experience in the 8-15 LPA range, and senior BAs or those with domain specialisation going higher. Product companies and MNCs typically pay more than traditional IT services companies for the same title.

Is an MBA necessary to become a Business Analyst in India?

No, an MBA is not required. Many practising BAs come from engineering, commerce, or science backgrounds without a postgraduate degree. An MBA from a strong institute helps in consulting-track roles and can accelerate seniority, but the majority of BA openings across IT services, product companies, and BFSI do not list it as mandatory. Certifications like ECBA, CBAP, or PSPO combined with a demonstrated skills portfolio can substitute effectively.

Which is better for a BA career: IT services or a product company?

Both paths have real merit depending on your goals. IT services companies give you structured training, exposure to multiple domains across projects, and a stable career ladder, which is valuable early on. Product companies offer deeper involvement in a single product, faster ownership, and generally higher pay. Many BAs start in services to build a broad foundation and then move to product companies once they have two to three years of experience and a sharper skills profile.

What does a typical BA interview in India look like?

Most Indian companies run two to four rounds: an online SQL and aptitude test, a case study or user story assignment, a technical or managerial interview, and a final HR round. Practising SQL on platforms like HackerRank and doing mock case studies with peers covers the bulk of what you need. Prepare to walk through your projects clearly, because that is the core of the technical interview round.

How important is SQL for getting a Business Analyst job in India?

SQL is very important. It appears consistently in Indian BA job postings, and many companies include a SQL screening test even for roles that are not heavily data-focused. You do not need to write complex stored procedures: being confident with SELECT, WHERE, JOIN, GROUP BY, and aggregate functions is enough to clear most screening rounds. This level of SQL is well within reach with consistent practice and is one of the highest-return skills investments you can make as a BA candidate.

What is the difference between a Business Analyst and a Product Manager in India?

Generally, a BA focuses on requirements, documentation, and bridging business with technology, while a Product Manager owns the product vision, roadmap, and feature prioritisation. In IT services companies, the BA role is more common and clearly distinct. In product companies and startups, the two roles often overlap and sometimes the same person handles both. Many BAs transition into product management by gradually taking on roadmap ownership, and the core skills (user stories, stakeholder management, data analysis) transfer well.
