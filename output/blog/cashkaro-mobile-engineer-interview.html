01 Overview

Overview

CashKaro is India's largest cashback and coupons platform, linking millions of shoppers to deals across thousands of e-commerce partners. The Mobile Engineer role is central to the product, covering the Android and iOS apps that users open daily to find offers, track cashback, and earn referral rewards.

As of July 2026, CashKaro has 46 open roles tracked on knok jobradar, making it one of the more active hirers in the consumer fintech space right now. Of the 79 Mobile Engineer openings across India that knok tracks, 21 are in Bangalore, the clear hub for this profile. Delhi and Pune each show 3 roles, Mumbai has 2, and Chennai has 1.

The interview process typically spans a few rounds. Candidates report a take-home or live coding task, followed by a technical deep-dive covering mobile architecture and performance, and a closing culture or leadership conversation. The process moves fairly quickly by startup standards, and offer decisions typically follow the final round within days.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from publicly reported candidate experiences and the core skills a cashback app demands at scale.

  1. Walk through how CashKaro's cashback tracking works at a mobile level. How would you build the deep-link and attribution flow on Android or iOS?

  10. How would you run an A/B test on a new onboarding flow without a full app release cycle?
  11. How do you keep the app binary size small while shipping rich UI features?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural and most technical questions at CashKaro.

Q: Describe how you handled a critical production crash.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a routine upgrade to our image-loading library caused a spike in crashes on older Android devices shortly after a release.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and needed to identify, reproduce, and fix the issue quickly before more users were affected.

*Action:* I pulled crash logs from Firebase Crashlytics, filtered by device model and OS version, and traced the pattern to an OutOfMemoryError in the image cache. I rewrote the cache size calculation to scale with available device RAM rather than a fixed value, tested on low-RAM emulator profiles, and pushed an emergency patch through our fast-track release pipeline.

*Result:* The crash rate returned to baseline within a few hours of the patch rolling out, and we added a device-tier memory check to our release checklist going forward.

---

Q: How have you improved scroll performance in a content-heavy feed?

*Situation:* A deals feed I was maintaining felt janky on mid-range devices, with users reporting visible dropped frames when scrolling through image-heavy rows.

*Task:* I needed to bring rendering smoothness up to our team's target without redesigning the entire feed architecture.

*Action:* I used Android Studio's GPU rendering profiler to find the bottleneck: each cell was inflating a complex view hierarchy on the main thread. I refactored to RecyclerView with a ViewHolder pool, moved image decoding off the main thread via Glide's background executor, and reduced overdraw by flattening nested layouts.

*Result:* Frame rendering time on mid-range test devices dropped well below our team's internal target, and QA confirmed the feed felt smooth across all tested device tiers.

---

Q: Tell me about a feature you built end-to-end on mobile.

*Situation:* The product team needed a referral link feature to drive new installs, and I was the only mobile engineer on the squad for that sprint.

*Task:* I had to design, build, and ship the full flow: link generation, deep-link handling on install, reward attribution, and a shareable UI sheet.

*Action:* I used Firebase Dynamic Links for deep linking, built a share sheet using the platform's native share APIs, and wired the attribution callback to our backend via a REST call on first launch. I also wrote unit tests for the attribution logic to guard against edge cases.

*Result:* The feature shipped on schedule and contributed to a measurable lift in new installs in its first month, per analytics shared in the sprint retrospective.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For architecture questions: State your preferred pattern (MVVM, Clean Architecture, BLoC for Flutter), explain why it suits a large codebase, and give a specific example from your own work. CashKaro's app is data-heavy, so emphasise how your pattern keeps the UI layer thin and the data layer testable.

For performance questions: Always name a profiling tool (Android Profiler, Crashlytics, Systrace), describe a metric you measured, and explain what you changed and why it worked. Vague answers like 'I optimised it' do not land well in a startup interview.

For system design questions: Think out loud. Cover the client-server contract first, then local caching, then edge cases like offline mode and retry logic. CashKaro's cashback flows involve third-party redirects, so expect questions about handling incomplete attribution events gracefully.

For behavioural questions: Use STAR format, keep the Situation brief (a few sentences), spend most of your time on Action, and quantify the Result even loosely (for example, 'noticeably faster' or 'no recurrence in the next release cycle'). Avoid saying 'we' throughout without also explaining your personal contribution.

For 'why CashKaro' questions: Mention the scale of their cashback attribution challenge, the diversity of the Android device fleet they must support, and the referral-growth mechanic as an interesting engineering surface. Authentic curiosity about the product goes further than rehearsed lines.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Product empathy: CashKaro is a consumer product that competes on user experience. Interviewers want to see that you have actually used the app or a comparable cashback product and that you think about the user's journey, not just the code.

Ownership over polish: Startup interviews reward candidates who say 'I shipped it' over those who say 'the team shipped it.' Be ready to describe a feature you owned from design to production, including the decisions you made when requirements were unclear.

Android breadth: The majority of Indian shoppers use Android, often on mid-range or older hardware. Expect pointed questions about memory management, background processing limits, and ANR prevention. Flutter or React Native experience is a plus, but native Android depth is valued highly.

Data-flow thinking: CashKaro's core value proposition is accurate cashback attribution. Interviewers probe whether you understand deep links, redirect chains, and the risk of attribution loss when a user switches apps mid-flow.

Speed and pragmatism: This is a startup. Candidates who can articulate trade-offs (for example, 'I chose this library because we needed to ship quickly, but here is what I would revisit with more time') come across better than those who insist on the theoretically perfect solution.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations

  1. Revise RecyclerView internals, ViewHolder patterns, and Jetpack Compose if you have not used them recently.
  2. Practice deep-link implementation on Android: App Links, Firebase Dynamic Links, and intent filters.
  3. Read CashKaro's Play Store listing and recent app reviews to understand real user pain points.

Week 2: Performance and systems

  1. Run through Android Profiler (CPU, Memory, Network) on a sample project and practice narrating what you observe.
  2. Study push notification flows: FCM token lifecycle, notification channels, and foreground versus background delivery.
  3. Build a small caching layer using Room or a similar ORM so you can speak from direct experience.

Week 3: Interview practice

  1. Do at least a couple of mock technical interviews focused on mobile system design (design a cashback app, design a coupon feed).
  2. Write out STAR stories for several past projects covering performance, ownership, and cross-team collaboration.
  3. Prepare a few sharp questions to ask the interviewer about CashKaro's tech stack, release cadence, and team structure.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping app-specific context: Candidates who give generic mobile answers without tying them back to cashback, attribution, or deal discovery miss the point. CashKaro has a very specific technical surface, and showing you have thought about it sets you apart.

Over-engineering on system design: Proposing a complex distributed architecture for a feature that could be a simple REST call with local caching raises eyebrows at a startup. Match your solution's complexity to the stated constraints.

Weak ownership language: Saying 'our team built...' without clarifying your role makes interviewers doubt your direct contribution. Use 'I designed,' 'I reviewed,' or 'I was responsible for' wherever accurate.

Ignoring device diversity: Answering Android performance questions as if all users have flagship phones signals a lack of experience shipping to a real Indian user base. Always anchor examples to low-RAM or mid-range device behaviour.

Not asking questions: Candidates who skip the 'do you have any questions?' stage leave a weak impression. Asking about the CI/CD pipeline, crash-rate tooling, or how the mobile team is structured shows genuine interest and readiness to contribute.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the CashKaro Mobile Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a few rounds in most cases. The process typically includes an initial screening call, a take-home or live coding task focused on Android or Flutter, a technical deep-dive on architecture and performance, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or product lead. The exact number can vary based on seniority level and the specific team hiring.

Does CashKaro ask DSA (data structures and algorithms) questions for the Mobile Engineer role?

Publicly reported experiences suggest the focus leans more toward practical mobile coding than competitive DSA. You may encounter a coding task involving collections, sorting, or basic traversal in the context of a mobile feature, but whiteboard-style hard algorithmic questions are not commonly cited for this role. Brushing up on arrays, hash maps, and basic tree traversal is a reasonable precaution.

Is React Native or Flutter experience required, or is native Android enough?

Candidates report that native Android depth in Kotlin or Java is valued most highly, given the breadth of devices CashKaro must support. Flutter or React Native experience is a clear plus for cross-platform roles, and some positions are cross-platform by design. Check the specific job description to know which stack is primary before you apply.

What salary can I expect for a Mobile Engineer role at CashKaro?

CashKaro does not publish fixed salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and industry surveys suggest mobile engineer compensation in Bangalore varies considerably with experience, team, and negotiation outcomes. Research current benchmarks on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before your final round so you enter the conversation with a well-informed ask.

How should I prepare for CashKaro's take-home assignment?

Candidates report that take-home tasks typically ask you to build a small feature, such as a paginated feed, a deep-link flow, or a local caching layer, within a set time window. Focus on clean architecture, readable code, and a short README explaining your key decisions. A working, well-structured solution consistently lands better than an ambitious but incomplete one.

Is Bangalore the only city where CashKaro hires Mobile Engineers?

Bangalore has the highest concentration of Mobile Engineer openings tracked by knok, with 21 of the 79 open roles across India based there. CashKaro currently has 46 open roles in total, though not all are for Mobile Engineers specifically. Check the current listings for remote or hybrid availability, as options change frequently with each hiring cycle.
