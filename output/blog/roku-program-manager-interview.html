01 Overview

Overview

Roku builds the operating system and platform that powers streaming devices and smart TVs used by millions worldwide. The Technical Program Manager (TPM) role at Roku is a high-ownership position: you drive large, cross-functional engineering programs, coordinate between hardware, software, and business teams, and keep delivery timelines on track.

As of July 2026, Roku carries 253 open roles on knok jobradar, reflecting active hiring across functions. The TPM interview typically spans several rounds. Candidates report seeing a mix of behavioral questions rooted in leadership principles (Roku has strong ex-Amazon leadership across many teams), technical depth checks on your understanding of engineering systems, and program management scenarios where you must show structured thinking under ambiguity.

Roku values speed, data-driven decisions, and direct communication. Preparation with real examples from your past work, structured using the STAR format, gives you the strongest shot at moving forward.

Most Asked Questions

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about the most complex cross-functional program you managed.

*Situation:* I was managing the launch of a new firmware update coordinated across a hardware team in Bangalore, a cloud backend team in Hyderabad, and a QA team in Pune.

*Task:* My job was to get all three teams to a synchronized release date without any one team becoming a blocker for the others.

*Action:* I set up a single shared tracker with clearly owned milestones, held a weekly sync with all three leads, and built a dependency map so everyone could see what was at risk. When the hardware team hit a chip availability delay, I flagged it to leadership immediately with three mitigation options and a revised timeline.

*Result:* We shipped two weeks later than the original date, but with zero critical bugs in the first week post-launch. Leadership cited the early escalation as the reason the delay stayed contained.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to push back on a deadline.

*Situation:* A VP wanted a new streaming feature live before a major product event, but the engineering lead privately told me the team needed three more weeks.

*Task:* I had to reconcile the VP's expectation with engineering reality, in writing, with data.

*Action:* I prepared a scope-versus-timeline document showing three options: full scope with the extended date, a reduced MVP scope hitting the event date, or a phased rollout. I presented it to the VP directly, framing each option by its business impact.

*Result:* The VP chose the phased rollout. The core feature shipped on time, the full version followed four weeks later, and the engineering team avoided crunch mode.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a critical dependency slipped.

*Situation:* A third-party certification we needed for a device launch was delayed by the testing lab by three weeks.

*Task:* I needed to protect the launch date or give leadership enough warning to adjust marketing spend.

*Action:* I mapped which parts of the launch were certification-dependent versus independent, found that retail packaging and device production could proceed, and escalated only the certification risk with a clear go-or-no-go date attached.

*Result:* Leadership pushed the on-sale date by ten days instead of the full three weeks, recovered partial marketing spend, and the device launched with all certification complete.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Roku interviewers are comfortable with STAR but also respond well to structured problem-solving frameworks. Here are the most useful ones to keep in mind.

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result): Use this for every behavioral question. Keep the Situation brief (two sentences at most), spend most of your time on Action, and always close with a concrete Result.

DACI (Driver, Approver, Contributor, Informed): When asked about stakeholder alignment or decision-making, mention how you clarify who is the decision-maker versus who has input. Roku values clear ownership.

Risk register format: For questions about how you track programs, describe your approach as: identify the risk, score it by likelihood and impact, assign an owner, set a review date. This shows systematic thinking.

'Options, not problems' framing: Whenever you escalate in a story, show that you brought options along with the problem. This is a strong signal at Roku, where TPMs are expected to be solution-oriented rather than just reporters of bad news.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Roku TPM interviewers are typically looking for three things above everything else.

Technical credibility without being an engineer. You do not need to write code, but you must show you understand system architecture well enough to spot risks, ask the right questions, and know when an engineering estimate is unrealistic.

Bias for action under ambiguity. Roku moves fast. Interviewers want to see that when you face incomplete information, you do not wait. You make a decision, document your assumptions, and adjust when new data arrives.

Cross-functional influence. TPMs at Roku work with hardware, software, content, and business teams. Show that you have navigated competing priorities across teams you did not manage, and that you achieved alignment without relying on authority.

Candidates report that Roku interviewers probe for specificity. Vague answers like 'I kept the team motivated' land poorly. Answers with clear metrics, named stakeholders (by role, not by name), and concrete timelines land well.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your story bank. Write out ten stories from your past work, one per major program management scenario (missed deadline, stakeholder conflict, technical risk, scope change, team escalation, post-mortem, and others). Map each story to a potential Roku question before your first round.

Week 2: Build technical depth. Review the basics of streaming architecture, CDN delivery, device firmware lifecycles, and A/B testing at scale. Roku's products sit at the intersection of hardware, cloud, and content. You do not need deep code knowledge, but understanding how these systems connect will help you ask smart questions in the interview.

Week 3: Do your company research. Read Roku's recent earnings releases and product announcements. Understand their revenue model (advertising platform, not hardware margin). Think about what program management challenges arise from coordinating hardware releases with a software platform and an advertising business.

Week 4: Run mock interviews. Practice out loud with a peer or record yourself. Time your STAR answers (aim for two to three minutes each). Review your story bank and tighten the weakest stories.

On the day, listen carefully before answering. Candidates report that Roku interviewers ask follow-up questions specifically to test whether you actually own your examples or just memorized them. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Staying too high-level. Many candidates describe programs in terms of goals and outcomes but skip the specific actions they personally took. Roku interviewers want to know what you did, not just what the team achieved.

Claiming credit for the whole team. Use 'I' for decisions and actions, 'we' for team effort. Interviewers are trained to notice when a candidate inflates their individual contribution.

Not knowing Roku's business. Treating Roku as a generic tech company is a red flag. Know that Roku makes money primarily through its advertising platform, not device sales. A TPM who understands the business context earns more credibility in every answer.

Over-engineering the framework. Candidates who spend too long explaining their methodology and too little time on actual results lose the interviewer's attention. Frameworks should support your story, not replace it.

Failing to show ownership when things went wrong. When describing a setback, own what you could have done earlier or differently. Candidates who only point to external dependencies without reflecting on what they learned typically do not move forward in the process.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Roku TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves a recruiter screen, one or two technical or program management phone rounds, and a final panel that may include engineering leads, product managers, and senior TPMs. Round names and counts vary by team, so treat any specific number as a rough guide. Confirm the exact format with your recruiter before each stage.

Does Roku use Amazon-style leadership principles in its interviews?

Roku is not Amazon, but it has a significant number of ex-Amazon employees in leadership, and candidates report that behavioral questions often feel similar in structure. Preparing with STAR stories aligned to ownership, bias for action, and delivering results will serve you well. You do not need to memorize any specific company's official list, but those underlying themes are a strong guide for Roku interviews.

What technical knowledge does a Roku TPM need to demonstrate?

You do not need to write code, but candidates report being asked to explain concepts like CDN architecture, API dependencies, release pipelines, and A/B testing in conversational terms. The bar is whether you can hold a credible technical conversation with engineers, not whether you can implement solutions yourself. Reviewing system design basics at a high level is sufficient preparation for most TPM roles at Roku.

How competitive is the Roku TPM role for candidates in India?

Based on knok jobradar data from July 2026, there are 313 TPM openings listed across Indian cities, with Bangalore showing the highest count at 41. Roku carries 253 open roles across all functions, reflecting active hiring. Competition for TPM roles at well-known global tech companies is always high, but the volume of openings suggests meaningful opportunity for strong candidates with cross-functional program experience.

Should I ask questions at the end of a Roku interview?

Yes, and your questions matter. Interviewers notice candidates who ask thoughtful questions about the team's current program challenges, how success is measured for TPMs, or how hardware and software releases are coordinated internally. Avoid asking about compensation or benefits in technical rounds, and save those topics for the recruiter conversation.

What salary should I expect for a Roku TPM role in India?

Roku does not publicly publish India-specific salary bands for TPM roles. Publicly reported ranges on platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi vary widely by experience level and team. Research current ranges on those platforms before your offer stage, and factor in total compensation including equity, not just the base salary figure.
