03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you increase AMC renewal rates for Aquaguard?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we ran a B2C subscription product where renewal rates had been falling for consecutive quarters.

*Task:* I was asked to diagnose the problem and propose a fix within the same quarter.

*Action:* I pulled customer lifecycle data and found that most non-renewals happened among customers who had not logged any service requests in the preceding months, suggesting low perceived value. I worked with the CRM team to set up a proactive re-engagement flow: a reminder with usage tips sent well before the renewal date, followed by a personalized offer closer to the deadline. I also flagged to the field-service team that these customers needed a proactive check-in call.

*Result:* Renewal rates improved in the next cycle. I would apply the same diagnosis-first approach at Eureka Forbes, starting with data before jumping to solutions.

---

Q: How would you launch a digital feature to Eureka Forbes's largely offline customer base?

*Situation:* At a previous role, I needed to move a large segment of field-sales customers onto a new app-based service portal.

*Task:* I owned the go-to-market plan, with the goal of getting a meaningful share of the base onboarded within the target timeframe.

*Action:* I partnered with the field-service team, since technicians have the most direct contact with customers. We created a simple one-page guide for technicians to walk customers through app setup during every service visit. I also made app registration part of the AMC renewal flow so there was a natural incentive to sign up.

*Result:* Onboarding numbers grew steadily quarter on quarter. The key learning was that offline distribution teams are your best digital adoption channel when you align their incentives correctly.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to prioritize between competing stakeholder demands.

*Situation:* At my last role, the sales head wanted a bulk-discount feature for enterprise accounts, while engineering had bandwidth for only one major feature that quarter and preferred building an API improvement.

*Task:* I needed to make a call, get alignment, and keep both teams motivated.

*Action:* I built a simple RICE scorecard and shared it openly with both stakeholders. The API improvement scored higher on reach and confidence. I also found a lighter version of the discount feature, a manual workaround using existing tooling, that sales could use short-term without requiring engineering effort.

*Result:* Engineering shipped the API improvement on time, and sales used the workaround successfully for the following months until the full feature was built in the next planning cycle. Both teams felt heard because the reasoning was transparent.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions (like 'improve the Aquaguard app'): use the CIRCLES method. Start with Customers, then Identify pain points, Report priorities, Cut through trade-offs, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise. Always anchor on the customer job-to-be-done before jumping to features.

For metrics questions (like 'how do you measure success for an IoT feature'): use the HEART framework covering Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, and Task success. Pick 2-3 metrics that are directly actionable, not vanity numbers. For Eureka Forbes, retention and task success (filter changed on time, no water quality complaint) are usually the most meaningful.

For prioritization questions (like 'sales vs engineering conflict'): use RICE covering Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort. Present the scorecard openly. Interviewers want to see that you can make a data-backed call and communicate it without politics.

For go-to-market questions (like 'launching a feature to offline customers'): structure your answer around segments, channels, and incentives. Eureka Forbes's field-service network is both a constraint and a powerful last-mile channel. Any launch plan that ignores the technician touchpoint will come across as naive.

For behavioural questions: use STAR strictly. Situation (2-3 sentences), Task (1-2 sentences), Action (the bulk of your answer, with specific choices you made), Result (quantify where you can, hedge where you cannot).

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Eureka Forbes PM interviewers typically look for a few things that are easy to miss if you prepare only with generic PM interview guides.

Comfort with hardware constraints. Aquaguard and Euroclean are physical products with supply chains, service cycles, and installation timelines. Candidates who treat every problem as a pure software problem stand out negatively. Show that you understand concepts like SKU management, service SLAs, and field-force coordination.

Empathy for non-digital customers. A large share of Eureka Forbes's customer base interacts primarily through a phone call or a technician visit, not an app. Interviewers probe whether you can design for these users, not just for smartphone-first segments.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Questions are often open-ended. Interviewers want to see you clarify scope, state your assumptions, and walk through a logical framework before giving answers. Jumping straight to solutions is a red flag.

Stakeholder management. With a large direct-sales force, regional managers, and an engineering team, PM work at Eureka Forbes is heavily cross-functional. Come with at least two strong stories about managing competing priorities or influencing without authority.

Customer obsession grounded in data. Opinions are fine as a starting point, but strong candidates always say 'I would validate this by looking at...' or 'I would run a small test to confirm...' before committing to a direction.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Know the company and product.
Use Aquaguard or Euroclean products, or read detailed user reviews on e-commerce platforms. Understand the AMC model, the filter replacement cycle, and how the field-service network operates. Read any publicly available news from 2024-2026 about Eureka Forbes's digital initiatives.

Step 2: Build your story bank.
For each of the 11 questions listed above, write a short STAR answer. Prioritize stories involving hardware, offline channels, or large cross-functional teams. If you have never worked with a hardware team, prepare an honest answer about how you would ramp up and what you would learn first.

Step 3: Practice metrics and frameworks.
Pick any Eureka Forbes product and practice defining the north star metric, three supporting metrics, and one counter-metric. Time yourself. Interviewers are not looking for perfect answers; they are looking for a clear, structured thought process.

Step 4: Check recent candidate reports.
Search Glassdoor and LinkedIn for recent interview experience posts from Eureka Forbes PM candidates. Candidates report that the process typically includes a case study or take-home assignment, so have your frameworks ready before you need them.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Eureka Forbes like a tech startup. Candidates who pitch AI-powered recommendation engines without addressing field-service and supply-chain realities often get knocked out early. Ground every idea in operational feasibility.

Ignoring the offline customer. Many PM candidates default to app-centric solutions. Eureka Forbes's customer base includes a large segment that interacts via phone or in-person service visits. If your answer does not include these users, it is incomplete.

Skipping clarifying questions. Jumping straight into solutions without clarifying scope signals shallow thinking. Always ask: 'Are we optimizing for new customer acquisition or retention?' or 'What is the primary constraint, cost or speed?'

Generic STAR answers. Saying you improved a metric without explaining your specific decisions and trade-offs does not impress interviewers. They want to understand your reasoning, not just the outcome.

Not knowing the product portfolio. Candidates who do not know that Eureka Forbes has a home security line, or who confuse Aquaguard features with a competitor's product, signal low preparation and low genuine interest in the company.

Over-engineering the solution. Proposing a multi-phase roadmap for what is really a quick validation experiment is a red flag. Show that you know when to start small, validate, and then scale.
