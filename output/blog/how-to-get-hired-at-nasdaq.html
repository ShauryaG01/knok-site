01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Nasdaq is a global technology company that powers financial markets, and its India operations are a core part of that mission. Landing a role here means joining a company where technology and finance intersect. Competition is real, but Nasdaq hires consistently across experience levels, from fresh graduates joining rotational programmes to senior engineers and domain specialists.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process typically runs across several stages, though exact steps vary by role and team.

Stage 1: Online application and resume screen
Submit your application through Nasdaq's careers portal. Recruiters look for relevant experience in finance, technology, or data. Tailor your resume to the specific job description before applying.

Stage 2: Recruiter call
A talent acquisition partner reaches out to discuss your background, role fit, location, compensation expectations, and availability. Be ready to explain why you want to work at Nasdaq specifically and what you know about their business.

Stage 3: Technical or functional assessment
Depending on the role, you may receive an online coding test (for engineering roles) or a case study (for finance, product, or operations roles). Engineering assessments commonly test data structures, algorithms, and system design.

Stage 4: Technical interview rounds
For tech roles, expect one or two rounds with engineers or architects. These cover coding problems, system design, and discussions about past projects. For non-tech roles, expect functional deep-dives with your potential manager or team lead.

Stage 5: Behavioural interview
A structured interview exploring how you handle collaboration, pressure, and ethical situations. Nasdaq takes integrity seriously given its role in regulated financial markets.

Stage 6: Offer and background verification
Successful candidates receive a verbal offer followed by a written offer letter. Background verification covers education, employment history, and criminal records. Timelines after the offer depend on your current notice period and how quickly the verification checks complete.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Nasdaq looks for a specific combination of technical depth and professional maturity. Here is what comes up consistently across roles.

Domain knowledge in fintech or capital markets. Experience with trading systems, market data, risk platforms, or financial regulations gives you a significant edge. Adjacent experience in banking technology or insurance platforms is also valued.

Strong data and engineering skills. Python, Java, C++, and cloud platforms such as AWS and Azure are frequently listed in job descriptions. For data roles, SQL and experience handling large datasets matter a lot.

Regulatory and compliance awareness. Because Nasdaq operates globally regulated markets, candidates who understand compliance frameworks, audit requirements, or data governance stand out.

Communication and stakeholder management. Many Nasdaq India roles involve working with global counterparts. Clear written and spoken English is a baseline expectation across almost every function.

Problem-solving under pressure. Market infrastructure cannot go down. Interviewers want evidence that you have handled high-stakes technical situations and made sound decisions under real constraints.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the business first. Nasdaq is not just a stock exchange. It also sells technology, analytics, and regulatory tools to other exchanges and financial firms worldwide. Knowing this context helps you connect your experience to their actual products during interviews.

Research the specific team. Nasdaq India works across market technology, anti-financial crime solutions, and corporate platforms. The interview experience differs significantly between a core exchange engineering team and a SaaS product team, so know which you are targeting.

Practise system design for scale. For engineering roles, expect questions about distributed systems, low-latency architecture, and data pipelines. Use publicly available resources on system design to sharpen these skills before your interview.

Prepare STAR-format answers. Behavioural questions are a consistent part of the process. Structure your answers around Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Focus on concrete outcomes rather than general statements.

Know your compensation range. Salary data for Nasdaq India roles appears on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for various positions and seniority levels. Research before the recruiter call so you can give a confident range.

Apply through the right channel. Direct applications through Nasdaq's careers portal get seen. Employee referrals move faster. If you know someone at Nasdaq, a referral can meaningfully improve your chances at the initial screen.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Nasdaq's culture is shaped by its position at the intersection of technology and financial regulation. A few themes come through clearly from publicly reported employee feedback.

Integrity is non-negotiable. Operating regulated markets means every employee is held to high ethical standards. Nasdaq expects you to raise concerns, follow processes, and prioritise long-term trust over short-term shortcuts.

Global collaboration is the norm. India teams work closely with counterparts in the US, Europe, and other regions. Early morning or late evening calls are common, and cross-cultural communication skills matter for day-to-day work.

Learning is encouraged. Nasdaq publicly reports investment in employee development, including access to internal mobility, certifications, and leadership tracks. Employees who proactively grow their skills tend to advance faster within the organisation.

The work is detail-oriented and high-stakes. Financial markets move on milliseconds. The culture rewards precision, documentation, and careful decision-making rather than speed at all costs.

Diversity and inclusion are active priorities. Nasdaq has published commitments around gender diversity and inclusive hiring. India offices participate in these initiatives and it shows up in how teams are structured.
