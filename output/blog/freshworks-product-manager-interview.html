01 Overview

Overview

Freshworks is a Chennai-born, NASDAQ-listed SaaS company building products for customer support (Freshdesk), IT service management (Freshservice), and CRM (Freshsales, Freshchat). PM roles here require B2B product instinct, SMB customer empathy, and comfort collaborating with global engineering and design teams.

Freshworks currently lists 145 open PM roles across its product lines (knok jobradar, July 2026). The interview process typically spans a few rounds, though candidates report variation by team and seniority. Rounds commonly include a recruiter screening call, a hiring manager discussion on past experience, a product case study (often take-home), cross-functional rounds with engineering or design leads, and a final leadership interview.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report facing a mix of product design, analytical, and behavioural questions across Freshworks PM rounds. Here are 12 questions that come up most often:

  1. Walk me through a Freshworks product (pick Freshdesk or Freshservice) and tell me one thing you would improve and why.
  2. How would you prioritise a backlog when engineering bandwidth is limited and three stakeholders are each pushing for different features?
  3. Tell me about a product you owned end-to-end. What was your biggest learning from the launch?
  4. Freshworks serves SMB customers globally. How do you design for a customer who has minimal IT support and a tight budget?
  5. How do you decide whether to build a feature in-house or integrate with a third-party tool?
  6. A key metric for Freshdesk shows a sudden drop week over week. Walk me through how you would investigate and respond.
  7. How would you design an onboarding flow for a first-time user of Freshservice who has never used an ITSM tool?
  8. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a customer request. How did you handle it?
  9. How do you measure the success of a feature six months after launch?
  10. Freshworks competes with Zendesk, ServiceNow, and Salesforce. Where should Freshworks double down, and why?
  11. Describe a situation where data changed your mind about a product decision.
  12. How would you approach building a roadmap for a new AI-powered feature inside Freshchat?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a Freshworks product and tell me one thing you would improve and why.

*Situation:* I used Freshdesk's free trial when preparing for B2B PM roles, spending focused time on the support agent experience.

*Task:* Identify a meaningful improvement, not a cosmetic one, that fits Freshworks' SMB customer profile.

*Action:* I noticed that the 'canned responses' feature is powerful but buried. Agents must type a shortcut to trigger it, and new agents often miss it entirely. I would surface canned response suggestions automatically as the agent types, using the ticket subject and first message as context, similar to how predictive text works in messaging apps. This reduces handle time and benefits SMB customers whose agents are generalists without deep product training. I would measure success by tracking average handle time per ticket and canned response adoption rate in the first month after launch.

*Result:* This is a low-engineering, high-impact change that aligns directly with Freshworks' goal of helping SMB teams do more with fewer resources.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to say no to a customer request.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a large enterprise customer requested a deeply custom reporting dashboard that would require multiple sprints to build.

*Task:* Decline without losing the relationship or the deal.

*Action:* I first acknowledged the underlying pain: they needed faster executive reporting. Then I showed them how our existing export-to-Sheets feature, combined with a simple dashboard template, could solve most of their need in one week. I told them honestly: 'We cannot build a bespoke dashboard right now without pushing back features that serve many other customers, but here is what we can do immediately.' I documented the request, added it to a roadmap candidate list, and followed up quarterly with the account team.

*Result:* The customer stayed and used the workaround. Six months later, their request became part of a broader reporting feature we built for the segment. The relationship stayed intact because I was direct and gave them a real alternative.

---

Q: Describe a situation where data changed your mind about a product decision.

*Situation:* I was confident that adding a 'team inbox' view in our support tool would improve agent collaboration. Qualitative customer feedback seemed to support it.

*Task:* Validate the assumption before committing a full sprint to it.

*Action:* We ran a quick prototype with a small set of beta users and tracked whether they actually used the team inbox view versus their individual queue. The usage data was surprising: agents opened the team inbox but immediately switched back to their personal queue. Exit interviews revealed that seeing all unassigned tickets made agents feel anxious, not empowered. The 'shared visibility' we thought would help was creating stress instead.

*Result:* We pivoted to a 'soft assignment' model where tickets are auto-assigned but agents see an overflow queue only when their own queue is empty. Adoption was strong, and we caught the mismatch early enough to ship on time.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions (improving a product or designing a new flow):

Start with the user, not the feature. Name a specific persona tied to Freshworks' SMB market (a support agent at a small e-commerce company, for example), describe their biggest pain, propose a solution, and say how you would measure success. Freshworks interviewers respond well when you anchor on SMB constraints: limited IT budget, non-technical admin, need for fast time-to-value.

For prioritisation questions:

Use a simple impact-versus-effort framing. Name your criteria (customer impact, strategic fit, engineering cost, time-to-revenue), apply them explicitly to the options given, and arrive at a ranked list. Mention that you would validate your ranking with data or customer conversations before locking the roadmap.

For metric and analytical questions (sudden drop scenarios):

Use a structured breakdown: first confirm the metric is real (rule out a data pipeline issue), then segment by cohort, geography, and product area to isolate the drop, form hypotheses ranked by likelihood, and identify the fastest way to test each one. End with what you would communicate to stakeholders and on what timeline.

For behavioural questions:

Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two sentences each). Spend most of your time on Action and use 'I' not 'we.' End with a quantified or clearly observable Result, and optionally add one sentence on what you would do differently.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Customer empathy, especially for SMB users. Freshworks' core market is small and mid-size businesses. Interviewers listen for whether you understand the constraints these customers face: limited budgets, generalist teams, low tolerance for complex onboarding. Generic 'user-centric' talk does not impress here; specific SMB scenarios do.

Product depth on Freshworks itself. Candidates who have used Freshdesk or Freshservice before the interview stand out clearly. Superficial answers about 'improving the UX' without knowing the actual product surface are a common reason candidates do not advance past early rounds.

Data-driven reasoning. Every product decision you discuss should have a metric attached. How would you measure success? What signals told you a hypothesis was wrong? Freshworks teams work in a data-rich environment and expect PMs to be comfortable with funnel analysis, cohort behaviour, and A/B testing.

Cross-functional maturity. PM interviews at Freshworks often include engineers or designers in the panel. They look for PMs who respect technical constraints, write clear specs, and can build alignment without authority.

Communication clarity. Answers should be structured and concise. Practice giving your answer in under two minutes, then stopping. Interviewers will redirect candidates who ramble, and it counts against you.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Know the product.

Sign up for Freshdesk and Freshservice free trials. Use them as an SMB customer would: raise a ticket, assign it, resolve it, run a report on it. Note at least three areas you would improve and why. Read Freshworks' recent blog posts and investor materials to understand where the company is investing right now (AI features, enterprise expansion, platform integrations).

Step 2: Practice the core question types.

Spend focused time on product design, metric breakdown, and prioritisation questions. For each, use the frameworks described in the answer frameworks section. Record yourself answering out loud and listen back. Most candidates are surprised at how much they ramble when they hear themselves.

Step 3: Behavioural prep and mock interviews.

Write out five STAR stories covering: a successful launch, a failure or course correction, a stakeholder conflict, a data-driven decision, and a time you said no to a request. Practice each story until it is concise and crisp. Do at least two mock interviews with a peer or coach who will give honest feedback.

Before each round:

Review the job description for the specific team (growth, platform, core product). Prepare two or three sharp questions about what the team is currently building or struggling with. Candidates who ask specific, informed questions are remembered after the panel.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Freshworks like a generic tech company. Freshworks has a clear SMB focus, a Chennai-Bangalore footprint, and a specific competitive position against Zendesk and ServiceNow. Answers that could apply to any SaaS company do not land well. Tie your examples and suggestions back to Freshworks' customer profile.

Not knowing the product before the interview. Freshworks offers free trials for Freshdesk and Freshservice. Interviewers can tell within the first few minutes whether you have actually used the product. Walking in cold is one of the most common reasons candidates do not advance.

Over-engineering product design answers. SMB customers need simplicity. If your improvement idea requires three new user settings and a redesigned navigation, it is probably wrong for this audience. Simpler, faster to adopt, and cheaper to support wins.

Using 'we' instead of 'I' in behavioural answers. Interviewers need to understand what you specifically did. 'We launched the feature' tells them nothing. 'I wrote the spec, ran alignment sessions with three engineering leads, and made the call to cut the mobile version from v1' tells them exactly what they need.

Skipping the measurement step. For every product or analytical question, if you do not say how you would measure success or validate your hypothesis, the answer feels incomplete. Always end with a metric.

Asking generic questions at the end. 'What is the culture like?' signals low preparation. Prepare specific questions based on what you know about the team's product or the challenges the company is publicly facing.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Is Freshworks a good company for PMs in India right now?

Freshworks is one of the more active SaaS employers for PMs in India, with 145 open PM roles listed as of July 2026 (knok jobradar). It offers exposure to global product work, a real SMB customer base, and engineering teams across Bangalore and Chennai. The company has been investing in AI capabilities across its product line, which creates PM opportunities in a high-visibility area. For PMs who want B2B SaaS experience with global scope from an India base, Freshworks is a strong option.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Freshworks?

Knok jobradar estimates ranges of 12-20 LPA for Associate PM roles, 24-40 LPA for PM roles with 3-6 years of experience, and 40-60 LPA for Senior PM roles. Group or Principal PM roles are commonly cited in the 55-90+ LPA range. Actual offers depend on your specific experience, the team you are joining, and how well you negotiate.

How long does the Freshworks PM interview process typically take?

Candidates report the full process taking anywhere from a few weeks to a couple of months from the first recruiter call to an offer, though it can move faster for senior roles where the team has urgent headcount. The process typically involves a few rounds spanning recruiter, hiring manager, case study, and leadership stages. Following up with the recruiter after each stage is normal practice and is generally well received.

Do I need to know Freshworks products before applying?

You do not need to know them before applying, but you absolutely need to know them before any interview round. Freshworks offers free trials for Freshdesk and Freshservice. Spend a few hours using both products as an SMB admin would, not just as a casual visitor. Interviewers frequently ask you to critique or improve a specific product, and answers from candidates who have never logged in are easy to identify.

What kind of case study does Freshworks typically assign?

Candidates report receiving product design cases (design or improve a feature for an SMB customer), metric breakdown cases (a KPI dropped, investigate and respond), or go-to-market cases (how would you launch this feature to a new segment). Cases are typically take-home with a short window followed by a presentation to a panel. Interviewers focus more on the quality of your reasoning and the clarity of your assumptions than on whether your final answer is correct.

How do I stand out among other PM candidates at Freshworks?

Use the product before your interview and come with a specific improvement backed by a user insight and a clear success metric. Anchor your answers to SMB customer constraints rather than generic best practices. Ask sharp, specific questions about what the team is currently building or struggling with. Candidates who show genuine curiosity about the product and the customer consistently stand out over those who deliver polished but generic answers.
