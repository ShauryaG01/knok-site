01 Overview

Overview

Esper is a cloud-based device management platform built for enterprises running Android devices at scale, from retail kiosks and logistics handhelds to healthcare tablets and digital signage. A Solutions Engineer at Esper sits at the intersection of technical consulting and customer success. You will demo the platform, scope integrations, guide pilot deployments, and help customers move confidently from a handful of test devices to a full production rollout.

Esper currently has 14 open roles across its India operations. Candidates report that the process typically runs three to four rounds: an initial HR or recruiter screen, a technical discussion on Android fundamentals and MDM concepts, a live customer-scenario or case-study exercise, and a final round with senior leadership. There are no officially published round names, but this sequence is what most applicants describe.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on what candidates report and the nature of Esper's platform, these are the questions you are most likely to encounter:

  1. Walk us through how Android MDM works end to end. Where does Esper fit in the ecosystem?
  2. A customer's retail kiosks are going offline intermittently during peak hours. How do you diagnose the issue and guide them toward a resolution?
  3. How would you explain Esper's provisioning options (QR code, NFC, zero-touch enrollment) to a non-technical operations head?
  4. Tell me about a time you handled a technically complex customer escalation. What was your process?
  5. A prospect says they are already using a competing MDM solution. How do you run that conversation without speaking poorly of the competitor?
  6. How would you scope and structure a pilot deployment for a company that is new to device management?
  7. Walk me through how you would build a device policy for a delivery fleet: GPS must always be on, but app installs should be restricted.
  8. A customer's development team wants to automate device enrollment using Esper's APIs. How do you guide them through that integration?
  9. How do you balance technical depth versus business outcomes when your audience includes both a CTO and a cost-focused operations head?
  10. Describe a situation where you had to align multiple stakeholders with competing priorities on a customer project.
  11. You have twenty minutes to demo Esper to a skeptical security team. How do you structure that session?
  12. What metrics would you track and present to show a customer that their Esper deployment is delivering real business value?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you handled a technically complex customer escalation.

*Situation:* A retail chain was seeing random disconnections across their Android POS devices during peak business hours. They were a few weeks away from a major store expansion and were getting anxious.

*Task:* I needed to diagnose the root cause, coordinate with engineering internally, and keep the customer's IT head informed and calm throughout.

*Action:* I set up a brief daily sync with the customer's IT lead while pulling device logs and cross-referencing them with our platform's event history. I found that a firmware update from the OEM was conflicting with our device agent. I escalated to engineering with a clear reproduction case and worked with the customer to roll back the firmware on a small test batch first before applying the fix across all devices.

*Result:* The issue was resolved within two business days. The customer proceeded with their store expansion on schedule. I also documented the incident internally so the support team could resolve similar cases faster in the future.

---

Q: How would you scope a pilot for a company that is new to MDM?

*Situation:* A mid-size logistics company wanted to digitise their delivery operations but had never used a device management platform before and had a small IT team.

*Task:* I needed to design a pilot that built confidence without overwhelming them.

*Action:* I proposed starting with a small batch of devices across two locations for a fixed period. I defined success criteria upfront with the customer: device uptime, enrollment time per device, and number of support tickets raised. I ran a hands-on onboarding session for their IT admin and scheduled regular check-ins throughout the pilot.

*Result:* By the end of the pilot, their IT admin was independently managing devices. The business head had concrete data to present to their CFO, and the full rollout was approved shortly after.

---

Q: How do you balance technical depth with business outcomes in a customer conversation?

*Situation:* I was working with a customer whose CTO wanted API-level control over everything, while the operations head cared only about reducing device downtime and support costs.

*Task:* I had to run a single discovery call that satisfied both stakeholders without losing either one.

*Action:* I opened with the business question: 'What does success look like for your operations team in the next quarter?' That anchored the room. I then invited the CTO to go deeper on technical architecture and made the connection explicit: here is how the API capabilities enable the outcomes your ops team just described. I drew a simple integration diagram so both people were looking at the same picture.

*Result:* Both stakeholders left the call aligned. The CTO felt heard on the technical side, and the ops head had a clear picture of what would change for her team. We moved to a paid pilot shortly after.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Most Esper Solutions Engineer questions fall into three categories. Knowing which framework to reach for makes your answers feel structured without sounding rehearsed.

STAR (for experience questions): Situation, Task, Action, Result. Use this for any question beginning with 'tell me about a time' or 'describe a situation.' Keep the Situation and Task brief. Put most of your time on the Action, where the interviewer sees your actual judgment. Always close with a specific, observable Result.

Problem-Solution-Impact (for technical scenarios): When asked to troubleshoot a device issue or design a deployment, name the problem clearly, walk through your reasoning for the solution you chose, then state the business impact. Esper interviewers are looking for structured thinking, not a single perfect answer.

Listen-Clarify-Confirm (for demo and customer questions): Before jumping into how you would structure a demo or handle a customer scenario, ask one clarifying question about the customer's priority or context. This mirrors how a strong Solutions Engineer actually operates and signals real maturity. Interviewers notice when candidates skip this step and jump straight to pitching.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Esper interviewers are evaluating you on three dimensions simultaneously.

Technical credibility: Can you explain Android MDM concepts clearly, including provisioning methods, device policies, app management, and API integration? You do not need to know every Esper feature before joining, but you should know the device management category deeply. Candidates who cannot explain zero-touch enrollment or basic device policy concepts typically do not progress past the technical round.

Customer empathy: Solutions Engineers work directly with enterprise IT teams and business stakeholders. Interviewers want to see that you adapt your communication style based on who is in the room. Speaking only in technical terms to a business audience is a common red flag they screen for.

Ownership and follow-through: Esper is a fast-growing company. Interviewers look for candidates who close loops, document things properly, and proactively update customers without being chased. Your STAR stories should demonstrate this through specific actions, not general claims like 'I am a proactive person.'

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

First: build product and category knowledge
Read everything publicly available about Esper's platform. Understand Android Enterprise, zero-touch enrollment, and how MDM differs from MAM. Watch any public demo videos or webinars Esper has published. You should be able to explain in plain language how a device moves from factory reset to fully managed, and what can go wrong at each step.

Next: prepare your STAR stories
Map your past experience to the twelve questions listed above. Write out at least five STAR stories in full. Focus on moments where you translated technical complexity into business language, recovered a troubled deployment, or handled a demanding enterprise customer. Practice speaking these out loud, not just reading them silently.

Before interview day
Research Esper's customer segments: retail, logistics, healthcare, and field services are common use cases. Read recent press coverage or blog posts the company has published. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team, the sales cycle, and how Solutions Engineers are measured. Interviewers almost always invite questions at the end, and strong candidates always have them ready.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Leading with technology instead of outcomes: Candidates who open every answer with technical details and skip the business impact tend to struggle in the customer-scenario rounds. Always connect your technical actions to what changed for the customer's business.

Vague STAR stories: 'I handled a complex project and the customer was happy' tells the interviewer nothing. Be specific about what decision you made, what you actually did, and what changed as a result. The more concrete the detail, the more credible you sound.

Skipping clarifying questions in scenario exercises: If the interviewer presents a customer problem and you immediately launch into a solution, it signals that you would do the same thing with real customers. Pause, ask one or two clarifying questions, and show that you diagnose before you prescribe.

Not having questions ready at the end: Many candidates say 'I think you have covered everything.' Strong candidates come with prepared, specific questions about the role, the team structure, and how success is measured in the first few months on the job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Esper Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report three to four rounds as the most common pattern. This typically includes a recruiter or HR screen, a technical discussion on Android and MDM concepts, a customer-scenario or case-study exercise, and a final round with senior leadership or the hiring manager. Esper does not publish an official process, so the exact sequence can vary by team and location.

Do I need to know Esper's product in detail before the interview?

You do not need to know every feature, but you should know the MDM category well. Understand Android Enterprise fundamentals, zero-touch enrollment, device policies, and the difference between MDM and MAM. If Esper has public demo videos or webinars available, watch them. Interviewers consistently report being able to tell which candidates did the research and which did not.

What background do successful Esper Solutions Engineer candidates typically come from?

Candidates report backgrounds in pre-sales, technical account management, and implementation consulting, often from enterprise software or SaaS companies. Experience with Android devices, IoT, or enterprise mobility management is a strong advantage. Esper values customer-facing communication skills as heavily as technical depth, so a blend of both is what the role requires.

How important is coding or API knowledge for this role?

You will not typically be asked to write code in the interview, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding REST basics, and guiding a customer's development team through an integration. Esper's platform has a strong API layer, and customers frequently ask Solutions Engineers to help them automate enrollment or device management workflows.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer at Esper?

Esper does not publicly share salary bands. For market context, Glassdoor and industry surveys commonly cite Solutions Engineer compensation at enterprise SaaS companies in Bangalore spanning a wide range depending on seniority, domain expertise, and company stage. Your final offer will depend on your experience level, current CTC, and how well you negotiate.

Is there a take-home assignment or technical test in the process?

Some candidates report a take-home case study or a live whiteboard scenario, typically involving scoping a customer deployment or diagnosing a device management problem. There is no universally consistent test format, so prepare for both possibilities. Treat any written assignment as you would a real customer deliverable: clear, concise, and tied to business outcomes rather than technical detail alone.
