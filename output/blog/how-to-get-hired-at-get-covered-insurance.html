03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Based on patterns commonly seen at insurance-sector companies of this size, here is what the process at Get Covered Insurance typically looks like, stage by stage.

Stage 1: Application screening
Your resume goes through an initial review, often by a recruiter or HR coordinator. They check for relevant experience in insurance, customer service, sales, or the technical skills listed in the job description. A tailored resume that mirrors the language in the job post moves faster through this stage.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR call
If your profile is shortlisted, expect a brief call covering your background, why you are interested in the company, and basic role-fit questions. This is also your chance to ask about team structure, timelines, and the next steps.

Stage 3: Hiring manager or panel interview
This is the core interview, often a video or in-person conversation with the direct hiring manager and sometimes a peer from the team. For sales or customer-facing roles, expect scenario-based questions around handling customer objections, explaining policy details, or dealing with complaints. For technology roles, expect problem-solving questions relevant to the stack.

Stage 4: Final round or leadership discussion
For senior or specialist roles, a final round with a senior leader is common. This round focuses less on technical skills and more on how you think, communicate, and align with the company direction.

Stage 5: Offer and background check
Once the panel signs off, HR shares a verbal offer followed by a formal letter. Background verification typically covers previous employment and educational credentials.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Insurance companies like Get Covered Insurance generally look for a specific blend of skills and traits, regardless of the exact role.

Domain awareness: You do not need to be an actuary to apply, but a working understanding of how insurance products work, what customers worry about, and how claims or policies get processed is a clear advantage. Even reading up on the basics before your interview signals genuine interest.

Communication that builds trust: Insurance is a relationship business. Interviewers pay close attention to how clearly and honestly you explain things. Whether you are in sales, support, or operations, the ability to simplify complex information for a customer is valued highly.

Attention to detail: Policy documents, compliance requirements, and customer records all demand accuracy. Highlight examples from your past where precision prevented a problem or caught an error.

Comfort with targets: Many roles, especially in sales and renewals, come with measurable goals. Be ready to talk about how you have managed targets in the past, including times when you fell short and what you learned.

Adaptability: The insurance industry is changing fast, with more digital tools, comparison platforms, and regulatory updates. Showing that you keep up with changes and adapt your approach accordingly makes you stand out.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Good preparation for a Get Covered Insurance interview covers four areas: company knowledge, role fit, story practice, and logistics.

Know the company before day one: Read about what Get Covered Insurance does, which insurance products or segments they focus on, and any recent news about the company. Being able to speak to why their specific model interests you, rather than a generic 'I want to work in insurance', shows you have done your homework.

Match your resume to the role: Go through the job description line by line. For every requirement, prepare a concrete example from your work history that demonstrates it. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to keep your answers structured and evidence-based.

Prepare questions to ask: Interviewers remember candidates who ask thoughtful questions. Ask about what success looks like in the role in the first few months, how the team is structured, or what challenges the team is currently working through. Avoid asking only about salary or leave in early rounds.

Practise out loud: Reading notes is not enough. Practise answering common questions out loud, ideally with someone who can give you feedback on clarity and pacing. Insurance interviews often include situational questions, so rehearsing how you walk through a scenario helps a lot.

Sort the basics: Confirm the interview format (video or in-person), test your tech if it is online, keep your documents ready, and send a short, specific thank-you note promptly after the interview. A note that references something specific from the conversation is far more effective than a generic one.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Insurance companies at the scale of Get Covered Insurance tend to build their culture around a few consistent themes: customer trust, accountability, and continuous learning.

Customer trust first: The core product is a promise to help people when things go wrong. This shapes how teams think about their work. Employees who genuinely care about getting the right outcome for the customer, not just closing a ticket or making a sale, tend to thrive in these environments.

Accountability without blame: Most insurance operations involve shared workflows, from underwriting to claims to customer service. A healthy culture here means individuals own their part of the chain clearly, and mistakes are treated as problems to fix rather than reasons to point fingers.

Learning as part of the job: Insurance regulations, products, and customer expectations shift regularly. Companies in this space tend to encourage ongoing learning, whether through formal certifications or simply staying current with industry changes. If you have pursued any insurance or finance certifications, mention them.

Collaboration across functions: Sales, operations, technology, and compliance all need to work closely together in an insurance business. Candidates who have worked in cross-functional settings, or who can demonstrate they communicate well across different teams, fit in faster.

Frequently asked

How many jobs are currently open at Get Covered Insurance?

As of mid-2026, Get Covered Insurance has 8 open roles listed across the business. The mix of roles changes as the company grows, so it is worth checking regularly if your preferred function is not live yet. Applying early in a hiring cycle tends to give your application more attention.

Do I need prior insurance experience to apply?

Not always. Many roles in sales, customer service, and operations are open to candidates from adjacent fields like banking, fintech, or retail who bring strong communication and customer-handling skills. Technical and compliance roles are more likely to require specific domain knowledge. Read the job description closely to judge which side your target role falls on.

How long does the hiring process usually take?

For most roles at insurance companies of this size, the process from application to offer takes a few weeks. Senior or specialist roles can take longer if multiple panel members need to align on a decision. Following up politely with HR after each stage is reasonable if you have not heard back within a week or so.

What kind of questions come up in the interview?

Expect a mix of behavioural questions ('tell me about a time when...') and situational ones ('how would you handle a customer who...') for most roles. Technical roles add problem-solving or case-study elements. Prepare specific examples from your past work rather than speaking in generalities. Insurance interviewers value candidates who can back up their claims with real outcomes.

Is it worth applying even if I do not match every requirement?

Yes, especially if you meet the core requirements and are close on the rest. Job descriptions are often a wish list rather than a strict checklist. Focus your cover letter or application on the areas where you do match strongly, and be upfront about where you are still learning. Companies value candidates who show self-awareness and a genuine plan to grow.
