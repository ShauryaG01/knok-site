02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently based on what candidates report from First Advantage Data Analyst interviews.

  1. Write a SQL query to find duplicate records in a screening results table. Explain your deduplication logic and how you handle ties.
  2. How would you calculate turnaround time (TAT) for background check requests? Walk through the SQL or formula you would use, including how you handle weekends or public holidays.
  3. A client reports that their dashboard numbers do not match the raw data export you sent. How do you investigate and resolve the discrepancy?
  4. Describe a time you cleaned a messy or incomplete dataset. What was the biggest data quality issue and how did you fix it?
  5. How would you build a report showing SLA breach rates by client, region, and check type? What columns, filters, and aggregations would you use?
  6. Write a query to rank clients by total checks processed in the last 30 days, excluding internal test accounts.
  7. What operational metrics would you track to measure the health of a team processing background verification requests?
  8. A sudden spike appears in your daily 'checks failed' metric. Walk us through how you would root-cause it.
  9. How have you handled stakeholder requests for ad-hoc reports when your underlying data was incomplete or delayed?
  10. Explain the difference between a LEFT JOIN and an INNER JOIN. Give a real example from your past work where choosing the wrong join caused a problem.
  11. Have you worked with case-management or workflow data? How did you structure your analysis and what were the key dimensions?
  12. How do you ensure data accuracy before presenting findings to a business or client-facing team?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A client reports that their dashboard numbers do not match the raw data you sent. How do you investigate?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a key enterprise client flagged a discrepancy between their weekly completion report and the numbers in their internal HR system.

*Task:* I had to identify the root cause and deliver a corrected report before the client call scheduled four hours later.

*Action:* I compared row counts at each stage of the pipeline: raw extract, post-filter, and post-aggregation. I found that our status filter was dropping records where the 'status' field had a trailing space, a value the client system treated as valid. I fixed the filter logic, re-ran the report, and documented the data-quality rule so the team could add a trim-and-validate step going forward.

*Result:* The numbers aligned, the corrected report reached the client before the call, and the pipeline improvement prevented the same issue from recurring.

---

Q: Describe a time you built a report that improved an operational decision.

*Situation:* Our operations team had no visibility into which background check types were causing the most SLA breaches each week.

*Task:* The team lead asked me to build a tracker they could use in daily standups.

*Action:* I pulled data from our case-management system, grouped checks by type and client tier, and built a summary table with breach rates. I highlighted anything above the agreed threshold in red and added a rolling four-week trend so the team could see direction, not just a snapshot.

*Result:* The team reassigned resources toward high-breach check types, and the overall breach rate fell noticeably the following month. The report became a standard weekly artefact that the team lead still uses.

---

Q: Explain the difference between a LEFT JOIN and an INNER JOIN with a real example.

*Situation:* During a reconciliation project, a colleague's query was silently dropping client records and neither of us caught it immediately.

*Task:* I needed to explain the join difference clearly and fix the query before the report went to the client.

*Action:* I showed that an INNER JOIN returns only rows matching in both tables, so any client with no checks in the period simply disappeared from the output. Switching to a LEFT JOIN on the client master kept all clients in the result, showing zero checks for inactive ones instead of omitting them entirely.

*Result:* The corrected report included all clients as expected, the client flagged no anomalies, and the colleague understood the distinction well enough to catch similar issues independently after that.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For SQL questions: Restate what the query needs to return before writing anything. Then build it step by step out loud: table selection, join logic, filters, aggregation, and ordering. Mention edge cases such as nulls, duplicate keys, or timezone offsets in date columns before you finish. Candidates report that First Advantage interviewers respond well when you explain the 'why' alongside the 'how'.

For data-discrepancy questions: Use a layered approach. Start at the data source, trace through each transformation step, and isolate exactly where counts or values diverge. Name the tools you would use at each stage: a SQL audit query, a row-count check, a pandas merge, or a pivot table comparison.

For metrics and dashboard questions: Lead with the business question the metric is meant to answer. Then define the numerator and denominator precisely. Then describe how you would visualise it and who the audience is. This signals that you think about metrics end-to-end, not just technically.

For behavioural questions: Use a compact STAR structure. Keep 'Situation' and 'Task' to two sentences combined. Spend the bulk of your answer on 'Action' (what you personally did, not the team) and close with a specific, concrete 'Result'. If you have no figure to cite, describe the qualitative outcome clearly rather than leaving the result vague.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report, First Advantage interviewers look for a few qualities beyond pure technical skill.

Ownership of data quality. Screening data feeds compliance decisions and client trust. Interviewers want to see that you validate outputs, flag anomalies, and do not simply pass numbers downstream without checking them.

Clear communication with non-technical stakeholders. Many Data Analyst roles here sit between operations teams and clients. Explaining a data issue in plain terms matters as much as fixing it technically.

Hands-on SQL fluency. Expect to write queries without assistance. Window functions, aggregations, date logic, and joins come up consistently. Python is useful but typically not the primary filter.

Process thinking. Interviewers often ask how you would build something repeatable, not just how you solved a one-off problem. Show that you think in terms of pipelines and reusable reports, not one-time fixes.

Domain curiosity. You do not need prior background-check industry experience, but showing genuine interest in how the business works, such as SLA structures, check types, and client reporting cycles, leaves a strong impression.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: SQL foundation. Practice GROUP BY, all JOIN types, window functions (RANK, ROW_NUMBER, LAG, LEAD), CTEs, and subqueries. Focus specifically on problems involving deduplication, running totals, date-range filters, and comparing two tables for discrepancies. These map directly to the data First Advantage analysts work with day to day.

Week 2: Domain and tools. Read publicly available material on background screening workflows so you understand what TAT, SLA breach, and check status mean in context. If you use Power BI, Tableau, or Excel pivot tables, prepare one concrete example for each tool you mention on your resume. Be ready to describe your dashboard or report from memory, including the business question it answered.

Week 3: Mock interviews and behavioural stories. Write out three to five STAR stories covering data quality issues, stakeholder miscommunications you navigated, and processes you improved. Practice saying them out loud, not just writing them. Ask a peer to play interviewer and give follow-up questions mid-answer.

Day before the interview: Re-read the job description and map your stories to the listed responsibilities. Prepare two or three genuine questions to ask the panel. Strong options include: 'What does a typical analyst request look like for this team?' and 'How does the team currently handle data discrepancies flagged by clients?'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to code without clarifying the problem. Candidates who start writing SQL immediately, without confirming the table structure or edge cases, often solve the wrong problem. Take a moment to ask one clarifying question before you begin.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved the report' is not enough. Interviewers want to know what specifically changed, why you made that choice, and what concretely got better. If you have no figure to cite, describe the qualitative outcome clearly.

Ignoring data quality in technical answers. A SQL query that does not account for nulls, duplicate keys, or inconsistent status-field values signals inexperience to a team working with real-world, messy screening data every day.

Over-claiming tool expertise. If Power BI or Python is on your resume, expect a practical question on it. Only claim tools you can demonstrate under mild pressure.

Not asking any questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing can come across as uninterested. One or two genuine questions about the team's data stack or how analyst impact is measured go a long way.

Preparing only for technical questions. First Advantage interviews typically include behavioural questions. Candidates who prepare only SQL and arrive unprepared for 'tell me about a time...' questions often struggle in the final round.

How many rounds does the First Advantage Data Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report two to three rounds in most cases. The first is typically a technical screen covering SQL and analytical thinking. A second round may involve a case study or a walk-through of past work. A final conversation with a hiring manager or team lead is common. Round structures vary by team, and First Advantage does not officially publish a fixed process.

Is Python mandatory for the First Advantage Data Analyst interview?

Based on candidate accounts, SQL is the core technical requirement and comes up in almost every process. Python is a useful plus, and some roles ask about pandas or basic scripting for automation. If Python is listed in the job description, prepare a practical example from your own work, but strong SQL skills matter more overall.

Candidates generally report that deep domain knowledge is not required before joining. Interviewers focus more on your analytical approach and data skills than on industry-specific knowledge.
