The company works at the intersection of data connectivity and developer tooling, so you will find engineers, product managers, and business folks collaborating closely on problems that directly affect how software teams ship.

If you have a background in backend engineering, integrations, developer tools, or B2B sales, your profile is likely relevant to at least one of the 8 open roles currently live. 03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Knit's interview process is not publicly documented in detail, so what follows is based on patterns commonly seen at similar early-stage SaaS startups in India. Treat each stage as a guide, not a guarantee, and confirm the actual steps with your recruiter once you apply.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen
Your resume goes through an initial filter, either automated or reviewed by the hiring team directly. At a startup with 8 open roles, the team likely reviews applications personally rather than relying entirely on a parser. A clear, specific resume that mirrors the job description will move faster at this stage.

Stage 2: Introductory call
A recruiter or hiring manager typically reaches out for a brief call to get acquainted. This is mostly a conversation: why knit, what you have built, what you are looking for next. It is also your first chance to ask what the role actually involves day to day, so come prepared with a question or two.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment
For engineering roles, candidates commonly report a take-home assignment or a live coding round focused on backend concepts, API design, or system design. For product or business roles, this stage might involve a case study or a short written exercise. Startups at this stage tend to keep assessments practical rather than algorithmic-puzzle-heavy.

Stage 4: Deep-dive interview
This is usually a longer round with the hiring manager or a senior team member. Expect questions about past projects, how you handled ambiguity, and how you think through trade-offs. For technical roles, system design discussions are common here. For product or business roles, expect scenario-based questions tied to real problems the company faces.

Stage 5: Founder or leadership round
At a company of knit's size, a final conversation with a founder or senior leader is standard. This round is less about hard skills and more about alignment: do you understand what the company is building, and do you genuinely want to help build it? Come with thoughtful questions about the product roadmap and the team's current challenges.

Offer and negotiation
If all rounds go well, an offer typically follows within a few days. Salary ranges for startup roles at this stage are not always publicly listed, but Glassdoor and levels.fyi have community-reported data for similar roles you can use as a benchmark going into that conversation.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Technical depth with practical breadth
Knit builds infrastructure-level tooling, so engineers who understand how APIs, webhooks, and data pipelines actually work under the hood stand out. Knowing REST, GraphQL, or event-driven architectures is more relevant here than any single language preference. The ability to reason about reliability, data consistency, and developer ergonomics carries weight.

Product thinking, even in technical roles
Candidates who can explain why a feature matters to the end user (not just how to build it) tend to do well. Knit's customers are often developers themselves, so empathy for the developer experience is a genuine plus. If you have ever redesigned an internal API because the previous one was painful to use, that instinct is exactly what they are looking for.

Ownership mentality
Small teams mean there is no one to hand off ambiguous problems to. Interviewers at startups like this commonly look for examples where you identified a problem, drove a solution, and saw it through without waiting to be told what to do next. Past examples of this carry more weight than any certification.

Clear, direct communication
Written and verbal communication matters, especially at distributed or semi-distributed teams. Being able to explain a technical trade-off to a non-technical stakeholder is a skill that surfaces repeatedly in early-stage hiring. Clear Slack messages and concise pull request descriptions are part of the job.

Genuine startup fit
This is harder to define but easy to spot in an interview. Candidates who are comfortable with incomplete information, rapid iteration, and changing priorities tend to thrive. If you come from a large company and expect well-defined processes everywhere, the adjustment can be real. Being honest about this in the interview is better than discovering the mismatch after you join.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product before your first call
Before any interview, sign up for knit's platform (or request a demo) and understand what problem it solves, who the target customer is, and where it sits relative to competitors. Interviewers at startups notice very quickly whether a candidate has actually engaged with the product or just skimmed the homepage. Being able to say 'I tried to set up an integration and noticed X' is a strong opening.

Tailor your resume to the specific role
With 8 open roles at knit right now, pick the one that genuinely fits your background and update your resume to match its language. If the job description says 'API integrations' and your resume says 'system interfaces,' close that gap before you apply. Keyword alignment matters more at a small company where a human is reading every application.

Prepare a few project-based stories
Knit interviews tend to lean toward 'tell me about a time you built X' rather than abstract puzzles. Have a few specific examples ready from your past work covering: a technically hard problem you solved, a time you dealt with ambiguity or changing requirements, and a product or business outcome you directly contributed to.

Refresh your integration and API knowledge
For engineering roles, review REST API design principles, webhook patterns, OAuth flows, and system design topics like rate limiting, retry logic, and idempotency. These map directly to what knit builds, and they come up frequently in technical discussions at companies like this.

Prepare questions that show business curiosity
Come with questions that reflect genuine interest: what does success look like in this role in the first few months, what is the biggest technical challenge the team is currently working through, how does knit think about expanding its integration coverage. These signal that you are thinking about joining a business, not just taking a job.

Follow up after each round
A brief, professional note to your interviewer after each stage is a small gesture that many candidates skip entirely. At a small company where everyone knows everyone, it is noticed more than you might expect, and it reinforces the impression that you are genuinely interested.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Knit operates as a product-first, developer-focused team. Based on how the company presents itself publicly, a few cultural patterns are visible.

Speed and iteration over perfect planning
Startups building developer tooling tend to move fast and ship often. The expectation is that you will learn on the job, absorb feedback quickly, and adjust course. Waiting for perfect information before acting is not the norm here. People who thrive tend to be comfortable making reasonable bets and correcting quickly.

Deep empathy for the developer customer
Knit's customers are often developers themselves, which shapes how the team thinks about every product decision. There is a strong emphasis on making the experience frictionless, well-documented, and reliable. If you have ever been frustrated by a poorly designed API and wanted to fix it, that instinct fits well here.

Flat structure with direct access
At a company of this size, hierarchy is minimal. You are likely to work directly with founders or senior leaders on a regular basis. This is energising for people who want visibility and autonomy. It can feel unstructured to those used to larger organisations with defined escalation paths.

Transparency over polish
Early-stage startups tend to value people who surface problems early and bring others in, rather than quietly struggling alone. The culture at companies like knit typically rewards raising your hand and being honest about blockers over appearing to have everything under control.

Active growth phase
With 8 open roles to fill, knit is in a clear growth phase. The team is looking for people who are excited about building something at an early stage, not just joining a stable organisation. If you want to see your fingerprints on the product roadmap and the team culture, this is the kind of place where that is genuinely possible.
