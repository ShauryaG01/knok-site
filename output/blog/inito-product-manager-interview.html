01 Overview

Overview

Inito builds at-home fertility monitors and hormone-tracking apps used by women trying to conceive. The company is Bangalore-based and sits at the intersection of consumer hardware, mobile software, and reproductive health. As of mid-2026, Inito has 10 open product roles, signalling an active growth phase.

The PM interview at Inito typically unfolds over several rounds, candidates report, covering product sense, analytical thinking, and behavioural scenarios. Interviewers tend to probe your understanding of users navigating a personal and often emotionally loaded journey, so empathy and data both matter. Expect questions that test how you think about health outcomes, retention in a niche consumer app, and bridging hardware with software experiences.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through the Inito app as a user. What would you improve first and why?

*Situation:* I spent time using the Inito app as a first-time tester to prepare for this conversation. *Task:* I wanted to identify the single highest-impact improvement backed by real user pain. *Action:* I mapped the full user flow from device pairing to hormone result interpretation. The step where users receive their LH/progesterone result felt like the most confusing point. The app showed a number with a small colour indicator but offered no plain-language explanation. I benchmarked how other femtech apps handle result communication and outlined a 'result card' concept: one headline ('Your LH is rising, ovulation is likely approaching'), a plain explainer, and a next-best-action prompt. I also noted that this card could double as a shareable insight for users who consult their doctor. *Result:* The proposed change focused on a single screen but touched activation, retention, and doctor-sharing, making it a high-leverage, low-scope improvement worth validating with a quick usability test.

---

Q: Tell me about a product you shipped that failed or underperformed.

*Situation:* At my previous company, I was PM for a notification feature meant to re-engage dormant users. *Task:* The goal was to lift monthly retention by nudging users back into the core workflow. *Action:* I shipped a push notification sequence based on time-since-last-login. Post-launch, opt-out rates climbed faster than I expected. I dug into the data and found that messages were arriving at times users found intrusive, and the copy was generic rather than action-specific. I ran a rapid A/B test on message content and send-time logic, shifting to context-triggered notifications tied to in-app actions. *Result:* Opt-outs dropped and re-engagement improved over the following month. More importantly, I added a 'notification fatigue' check to my launch checklist for every feature after that.

---

Q: How would you grow Inito's user base in Tier 2 and Tier 3 Indian cities?

*Situation:* I was asked a similar growth question in a case interview at my last role and approached it systematically. *Task:* The challenge is that awareness, trust, and distribution all look different outside metro markets. *Action:* I would start with a barrier audit covering language (app localisation in Hindi and regional languages), price sensitivity (EMI options or a rental model for the device), and trust (partnerships with local gynaecologists and ASHA workers who already have community credibility). I would pick one pilot city, set a defined acquisition and activation target, and measure cost-per-activated-user rather than just installs. Learnings from the pilot would feed the next city rollout. *Result:* This framework kept the plan specific and measurable rather than a list of generic growth tactics, which is what interviewers at early-to-mid-stage health startups typically want to see.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions: Use a 'user, problem, solution, metric' spine. Start by naming a specific user segment, not just 'women trying to conceive' but, say, 'a professional in her second month of tracking, anxious about irregular cycles'. Name the pain, propose a solution, then state exactly how you would measure success.

Prioritisation questions: Use a simple impact-versus-effort grid or a RICE-style mental model (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort). For Inito, always anchor 'impact' to health outcomes or emotional relief, not just engagement metrics.

Analytical and metric drop questions: Follow a structured diagnostic: rule out data or tracking errors first, then check external factors (app update, competitor launch, seasonal pattern), then internal factors (feature change, pricing change, onboarding tweak). State your hypothesis before your fix.

Behavioural questions: Use STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep the Situation brief (a couple of sentences), spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Design and feature questions: Frame your answer with: who is the user, what is the core job-to-be-done, what does success look like, and what are the top risks. Interviewers at health-tech companies especially want to see that you think about trust, privacy, and clinical accuracy, not just UX polish.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Inito interviewers, candidates report, are looking for three things above all else.

Genuine empathy for users in a sensitive health context. Fertility tracking is emotional and high-stakes. Interviewers notice quickly if a candidate treats it as a generic consumer app problem.

The ability to work across hardware and software. Inito's product is a physical device paired with a mobile app. PMs who can speak to firmware constraints, Bluetooth sync edge cases, and hardware-release cycles alongside app sprints stand out noticeably.

Data comfort without data dependence. You should be able to reason from first principles when data is thin, as it often is in a niche category, but also know how to instrument an experiment and read results critically.

Bonus signals that candidates report impressing interviewers: knowledge of the Indian women's health market, awareness of regulatory considerations around medical devices, and a track record of shipping in resource-constrained startup environments.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product cold. Use the Inito app (or watch detailed demo walkthroughs) and read every piece of publicly available coverage, including product reviews and user forum threads. Map the full user journey from device purchase to ongoing cycle tracking.

Weeks 1-2: Build your story bank. Write out several STAR stories covering: a product you built end-to-end, a prioritisation call you made under pressure, a metric you moved, a failure and what you learned, and a cross-functional conflict you navigated.

Week 2: Practise live cases. Run several mock product case sessions, ideally with a peer who can push back. Aim for clear, structured answers that stay focused rather than rambling, and practise stopping at a crisp conclusion.

Before each round: Review Inito's recent announcements, fundraising news, and any new feature releases. Interviewers notice when a candidate's preparation ends at 2024.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating fertility tracking like a generic app. Candidates who jump straight to 'add a social sharing feature' or 'add gamification' without acknowledging the emotional sensitivity of the category lose points fast.
  1. Skipping the 'why' behind prioritisation. Saying 'I would build feature X' without tying it to a user need, a business metric, or a constraint is incomplete. Always show your reasoning.
  1. Ignoring the hardware layer. Candidates who only think about the app and ignore the physical device miss a core part of Inito's product complexity.
  1. Over-engineering the answer. Inito is a focused startup. Proposing a long roadmap packed with many features when asked for a single improvement signals poor scope judgement.
  1. Generic STAR stories. Stories that could apply to any product role at any company, without health, hardware, or Indian market specificity, feel interchangeable. Where possible, connect your story to themes Inito cares about.
  1. Not asking clarifying questions in a case. Jumping into an answer without confirming the goal, the user, or the constraint is a red flag in product interviews at any company.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Inito PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process spanning several rounds, typically covering an initial screen, one or more product and case rounds, and a final leadership or culture conversation. The exact number can vary by role level and team. It is worth asking your recruiter for the current process at the start of your engagement.

Does Inito ask for a take-home assignment?

Some candidates report receiving a written case or product brief as part of the process. These typically ask you to analyse the product, propose an improvement, or outline a prioritisation approach. Treat it like a live case: be specific, show your reasoning, and keep it concise.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Inito?

Inito is a mid-stage startup, so compensation blends base pay with equity. Industry surveys and Glassdoor suggest PM base salaries in India range from 12-20 LPA at the associate level to 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience. Exact figures depend on level, skills, and negotiation, so always get the full picture including ESOP vesting terms.

Is prior health-tech experience required?

Candidates report that Inito values product fundamentals and genuine user empathy over a strict health-tech background. That said, showing that you have researched the space, understand the regulatory context for medical devices, and can speak to the emotional weight of the category will give you a clear edge. Candidates who demo the product before the interview consistently stand out.

How important is SQL or analytics proficiency?

Inito, like most data-driven startups, expects PMs to be comfortable pulling and interpreting data. You do not need to be an analyst, but candidates report that being able to describe how you would query a funnel or set up a metric is expected. Brush up on funnel analysis, cohort thinking, and basic A/B test interpretation before your rounds.

What is the best way to stand out in the Inito PM interview?

Three things candidates consistently highlight: deep product knowledge (use the app, read real user reviews), concrete past examples tied to measurable outcomes, and a genuine perspective on where the Indian women's health market is headed. Interviewers at purpose-driven startups respond well to candidates who care about the problem, not just the role.
