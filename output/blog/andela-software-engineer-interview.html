02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these questions coming up most often in Andela's Software Engineer interviews:

  1. Tell me about yourself and your software engineering background.
  2. How do you manage your work when your client is in a very different time zone?
  3. Walk me through a system you designed or significantly contributed to, end to end.
  4. How do you debug a production issue you have never seen before?
  5. Describe a time you disagreed with a technical decision your team or lead made. What did you do?
  6. How do you keep your technical skills current while working full-time on a client project?
  7. Give an example where you improved the performance or reliability of a production system.
  8. How do you communicate progress and blockers to a client who is not technical?
  9. What does a good code review look like to you, both as the reviewer and as the author?
  10. How do you handle unclear or frequently changing requirements from a client?
  11. Describe your experience in agile teams, especially in a fully remote or async-first setup.
  12. What is your approach to writing code that other engineers will maintain long after you have left the project?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these three examples as templates and adapt the details to your own experience.

---

Q: Give an example where you improved the performance of a production system.

*Situation:* My team was running a product listing API that regularly timed out for users on slower mobile connections.

*Task:* I was asked to find the bottleneck and bring response time down without breaking any existing functionality.

*Action:* I profiled the endpoint and found N+1 database queries for category and seller data on every request. I rewrote the query to use a single JOIN, added a cache layer for the most-requested category trees, and indexed two columns used in WHERE clauses that had no index.

*Result:* Response time dropped significantly and cart abandonment on that page fell in the following weeks, which our analytics dashboard confirmed.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision.

*Situation:* My tech lead wanted to spin up a new microservice for a feature I felt was too small to justify the operational overhead.

*Task:* I needed to raise my concern constructively without blocking the team or creating conflict.

*Action:* I prepared a short written comparison of both approaches covering deployment complexity, latency, and maintenance cost. I shared it in our async Slack channel before the planning call so everyone could read it first, rather than springing it in the meeting.

*Result:* The team agreed to ship the feature as a module inside the existing service. Six months later, when requirements grew, we migrated it cleanly. My lead said the documented comparison made the migration much easier.

---

Q: How do you handle unclear or frequently changing requirements?

*Situation:* On a contract project, a US-based client kept changing the scope of a dashboard feature mid-sprint.

*Task:* I needed to deliver working software on schedule while managing expectation drift without damaging the relationship.

*Action:* I set up a short async check-in at the start of each week where I shared a one-paragraph summary of what I understood the current goal to be, and asked for a confirm or correction before I wrote any code. I also logged each scope change as a separate ticket so the client could see the timeline impact.

*Result:* Scope changes dropped considerably in the following sprints, and the client told my Andela account manager that I was one of the clearest communicators they had worked with.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For coding challenges: State your assumptions out loud before you start. Walk through your reasoning step by step and explain trade-offs between solutions. Andela's clients value engineers who can justify decisions, not just produce an answer quickly.

For system design questions: Open with clarifying questions about scale, read/write ratio, and latency requirements. Sketch a high-level design first, then drill into the components the interviewer seems most interested in. Do not jump straight into schemas or code.

For behavioural questions: Use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep the Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on the Action. Results do not have to be big wins. A clear explanation of what you learned or changed next time is equally strong.

For remote-work questions: Andela's clients specifically care about async communication. Show that you document decisions, over-communicate blockers early, and do not wait for a scheduled meeting to share a critical update.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Andela's model is built on long-term client placements, so interviewers are assessing more than coding ability alone.

Independence: Can you make reasonable decisions without hand-holding? The client is often asleep when you are working and cannot review every step.

Communication: Do you write clear async updates, raise blockers early, and adjust your language depending on whether you are talking to an engineer or a product manager?

Code quality: Candidates report that reviewers specifically look for readable, maintainable code. Clean naming, sensible abstractions, and basic test coverage matter as much as a correct output.

Distributed team readiness: Many of Andela's clients are in the US or Europe. Interviewers want evidence that you have worked in, or are fully prepared for, a globally distributed team with significant time zone differences.

Ownership: Can you take a problem end to end, from understanding the requirement through to deploying, monitoring, and iterating on the solution?

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations and profile
Refresh core data structures and algorithms: arrays, strings, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. Review the system design concepts you use most at work, such as caching, database indexing, and load balancing. Update your Andela profile with specific impact statements rather than just a list of technologies.

Week 2: Practice and communication drills
Do one timed coding challenge per day on a practice platform. Record yourself answering two behavioural questions each day and watch the playback for filler words and vague language. Practice async writing: draft a Slack-style message explaining a blocker, then a follow-up message with the resolution and what you changed.

Week 3: Company-specific preparation
Read Andela's public blog and LinkedIn to understand how they talk about their product and mission. Prepare two or three stories showing you working effectively in a remote or async-first environment. Have a specific answer ready for 'Why Andela?' that speaks to their distributed model, not a generic 'I like the culture' response.

On assessment day
Test your audio, video, and internet connection before the call. For take-home assessments, submit before the deadline and include a short README explaining your design decisions and any trade-offs you made intentionally.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a standard product company interview. Andela's process specifically screens for remote-work readiness. Preparing only DSA and system design means you may clear technical rounds but stumble in the culture and values conversation.

Vague behavioural answers. Saying 'I am a good communicator' is not enough. You need a specific story with a concrete outcome or a clear lesson.

Jumping straight to a solution in technical rounds. Not asking clarifying questions signals that you may misread requirements from a real client, which is a direct red flag for Andela's model.

Ignoring async communication skills. Candidates report that Andela specifically asks how you handle situations where you cannot get an immediate answer. Prepare examples of making independent progress while waiting for input.

Underselling remote experience. If you have worked remotely, mention specific tools and habits (written status updates, Loom videos, Notion docs) rather than simply saying 'I work from home.'

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Andela Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process with roughly three to four stages: a profile screen, a technical assessment, a technical interview with an engineer, and a values or culture conversation. The exact number of rounds can vary by role and by the specific client involved. Typically the full process wraps up within two to four weeks.

Does Andela ask data structures and algorithms questions?

Candidates report that Andela's technical assessments do include coding challenges covering fundamentals like arrays, strings, and trees. The difficulty is generally described as practical rather than competitive-programming level. Clean, readable code and a clear explanation of your reasoning tend to matter as much as finding the most optimal solution.

What salary can I expect from an Andela Software Engineer role?

Andela often quotes compensation in USD rates because engineers work with international clients. For reference, industry surveys put locally benchmarked Software Engineer salaries in India at 6-12 LPA for entry level (0-2 years) up to 40-65+ LPA for lead or staff roles. Andela's actual offers may differ depending on the client, the seniority of the role, and the engagement model, so always clarify the full package before you accept.

How important is remote-work experience when interviewing at Andela?

Very important. Andela's entire business model depends on engineers who can work independently in async, distributed teams. Candidates who demonstrate specific habits like structured written updates, proactive blocker communication, and time-zone-aware scheduling report a noticeably smoother experience in later interview rounds compared to those who only prepare on the technical side.

Are there currently open Software Engineer roles at Andela?

As of July 2026, Andela has 17 open Software Engineer roles listed. Role availability changes frequently as new client engagements open and close, so check Andela's careers page or a job aggregator for the most current listings.

What technologies does Andela look for in Software Engineers?

Because Andela places engineers across many different clients, the required tech stack varies widely by role. Candidates report that backend skills (Node.js, Python, Java), frontend skills (React, TypeScript), and cloud platforms (AWS, GCP) come up most often in publicly listed job descriptions. Focus on core engineering fundamentals and your strongest stack rather than trying to learn every technology before applying.
