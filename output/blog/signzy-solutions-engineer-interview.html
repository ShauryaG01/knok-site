01 Overview

Overview

Signzy is a fintech company that builds AI-powered digital onboarding and KYC solutions for banks, NBFCs, insurance companies, and other regulated financial institutions. A Solutions Engineer here is the person who makes sure clients actually get the product working: you run technical demos, help clients integrate Signzy's APIs, troubleshoot live issues, and act as the link between the sales team and the engineering team.

As of mid-2026, knok jobradar shows 44 open roles at Signzy and 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings across India. The strongest hiring markets are Bangalore (55 listings), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20), with smaller demand in Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). Signzy's own office is Bangalore-first, so most client-facing roles will typically be based there.

The role rewards people who are comfortable switching between reading an API error log in the morning and presenting a product walkthrough to a bank's compliance head in the afternoon. If you have a background in pre-sales engineering, integration engineering, or technical account management in fintech or SaaS, this is a strong match.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Signzy interviewers typically focus on three areas: your technical depth with APIs and integrations, your ability to communicate with non-technical stakeholders, and your awareness of the regulated banking environment Signzy operates in. Candidates report a mix of behavioral and technical questions across rounds.

  1. Walk me through a time you explained a complex API integration to a non-technical client or stakeholder.
  2. A client is going live tomorrow and reports that the video KYC flow is breaking on their mobile app. How do you troubleshoot it?
  3. What do you know about RBI's digital KYC regulations, and how do they affect how a product like Signzy's must be built?
  4. Describe your experience with REST APIs. How have you debugged an integration failure in production?
  5. How would you handle a situation where a client's legacy core banking system cannot connect directly to our API?
  6. Signzy serves both CTOs and compliance officers. How do you adjust your communication style for each audience?
  7. How would you build a technical demo of Signzy's onboarding API for a prospect who has no in-house engineering team?
  8. Give an example of working across sales, product, and engineering at the same time on a single deal or project.
  9. Three enterprise clients each raise a critical issue on the same afternoon. How do you prioritize?
  10. How would you position Signzy against a competing KYC vendor in a client pitch?
  11. Have you worked with webhook-based systems? Describe a challenge and how you resolved it.
  12. What metrics would you track to confirm that a client's onboarding integration is healthy and performing well?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you explained a complex API integration to a non-technical stakeholder.

*Situation:* I was supporting the onboarding of a mid-sized NBFC onto our loan origination platform. Final sign-off needed to come from the business head, who had no engineering background.

*Task:* I had to explain how our API would connect to their legacy core banking system and what data would pass between the two, without losing her attention or credibility.

*Action:* I built a one-page flowchart using plain business language. Instead of 'POST request,' I wrote 'your system sends us the customer's PAN and we send back a verified status.' I then ran a live demo in a test environment so she could see the actual screen her customers would experience.

*Result:* She approved the integration in the same meeting and instructed the technical team to proceed. The client went live within the agreed timeline with no rework on the demo material.

---

Q: Three enterprise clients each raise a critical issue on the same afternoon. How do you prioritize?

*Situation:* While managing a portfolio of banking clients, I received three urgent escalations on the same afternoon: one had a live customer flow blocked, one had a staging issue ahead of a Monday go-live, and one had a reporting discrepancy.

*Task:* I needed to respond to all three without letting any client feel ignored, and without making the live-customer issue worse by splitting my attention.

*Action:* I triaged by business impact first: the blocked live flow went to engineering immediately. I sent each client a short, honest update stating what I knew and when they would hear from me next. I then worked the staging issue myself while monitoring the engineering fix on the live case.

*Result:* The highest-impact issue was resolved before end of business that day. All three clients received updates throughout and none escalated to account management. I later built a triage checklist for the team so future situations could be handled faster.

---

Q: Describe a time you handled a client whose system was incompatible with an API you were implementing.

*Situation:* A bank I was supporting had an on-premise core system that could not make outbound HTTPS calls directly, which was required for our KYC API.

*Task:* I needed to find a solution that did not require the bank to re-architect its entire infrastructure before go-live.

*Action:* I worked with our engineering team to design a lightweight middleware adapter the bank could deploy internally. I documented the setup, ran a pilot in a sandboxed environment with their IT team, and validated it against all test cases before presenting it to leadership.

*Result:* The bank went live using the adapter. The solution was repeatable: we packaged it as a standard offering for other clients with similar legacy constraints, removing that objection from future deals.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use STAR: Situation (one or two sentences of context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, step by step), Result (a concrete outcome the interviewer can picture).

For live troubleshooting questions, use a four-step structure:

  1. Clarify: confirm what the client is seeing and on which environment.
  2. Diagnose: check logs, API response codes, and recent changes.
  3. Fix or escalate: apply a known fix or loop in engineering with a clear summary.
  4. Verify and close: confirm with the client and document the root cause.

For product or competitive questions, structure your answer around three points: the customer's problem, how Signzy's approach solves it, and one specific differentiator (for example, RBI-compliant video KYC flow or the breadth of bank integrations already live).

For regulation questions, be honest about what you know and frame gaps as things you actively research. Saying 'I stay updated via RBI circulars and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act updates' shows the right mindset even if you are not a compliance expert.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without pure engineering. Interviewers are not looking for a software engineer who can build Signzy's product from scratch. They want someone who can read an API reference, interpret an error log, and explain the fix to both a developer and a bank manager.

Customer empathy in a regulated industry. Banking and NBFC clients operate under RBI oversight. An interviewer will notice if you understand why a compliance officer reacts differently to an API outage than a CTO does. Show that you respect the stakes your clients are under.

Ownership over escalation. The best candidates stay with a problem until it is closed and follow up proactively. Interviewers at Signzy typically probe for this by asking how you communicated with clients during an incident, not just how you fixed it.

Clear, jargon-free communication. Candidates who slip into pure technical language when describing past work often struggle in this role. Practice narrating your work the way you would explain it to a relationship manager at a bank, not a fellow engineer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Days 1 and 2: Know the product. Read Signzy's public developer documentation and any case studies available on their website. Understand the main product lines: digital onboarding, video KYC, and bureau integrations. Note the types of clients they serve (banks, NBFCs, insurance firms).

Days 3 and 4: Know the regulation. Read a plain-English summary of RBI's KYC Master Direction and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. You do not need to be a compliance expert, but you should be able to explain why banks cannot store raw Aadhaar numbers and what 'V-CIP' means.

Day 5: Prepare your stories. Write down three to five examples from your own experience covering: a complex integration you drove, a client crisis you handled, and a time you worked across teams. Practice each one out loud using the STAR format.

Day 6: Mock technical questions. Ask a friend or colleague to role-play as a non-technical client. Walk them through an API flow you have worked on. This surfaces jargon habits you may not notice when rehearsing alone.

Before each interview round: Review what you know about the specific panel if a recruiter shares it. Candidates report that Signzy rounds typically move from a screening call to a technical discussion to a panel or leadership round, though this can vary. Send a brief, specific note to your recruiter on the morning of the interview to confirm the format and any tools you will need access to.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Speaking only to engineers on the panel. Solutions Engineer interviews often include a business or sales stakeholder. If you answer every question at a code level, you will lose half the room.

Vague answers to 'tell me about a time' questions. Saying 'I handled many integrations' is not an answer. The interviewer wants one specific story with a clear outcome.

No knowledge of RBI or KYC regulations. Signzy's entire business is built around compliance requirements. Candidates who have never heard of the RBI KYC Master Direction or V-CIP signal that they have not researched the domain.

Treating troubleshooting as a solo activity. Strong answers show when you looped in engineering, what information you gave them, and how you kept the client informed. Pure solo-hero answers miss the collaborative nature of the role.

Asking no questions at the end. A Solutions Engineer who is curious about the client's world should also be curious about Signzy's own clients, roadmap, and team. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the role's day-to-day and the types of integrations the team is currently running.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What background do most Solutions Engineers at Signzy come from?

Candidates report that Signzy typically hires from pre-sales, integration engineering, and technical account management backgrounds, often in fintech, SaaS, or banking technology. A strong grasp of REST APIs and client-facing communication matters more than a pure software engineering background. Experience with KYC, onboarding, or bureau APIs is a strong plus but not always required.

How many rounds does the Signzy interview process typically have?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screening call, followed by a technical discussion covering APIs and troubleshooting scenarios, and then a final panel or leadership round. The exact structure can vary by team and hiring manager. Confirm the format with your recruiter before each round so you can prepare accordingly.

Do I need to know how to write code for this role?

You are not expected to build product features, but you should be comfortable reading API documentation, interpreting JSON responses, and spotting common integration errors like authentication failures or malformed payloads. Being able to write a simple test call using a tool like Postman or curl is typically expected. Deep coding skills in a specific language are usually not a hard requirement for this role.

What regulation topics should I study before the interview?

Focus on RBI's KYC Master Direction (which governs how banks verify customer identity), the concept of Video Customer Identification Process (V-CIP), and the basics of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. You do not need to memorize circular numbers, but you should understand why these rules shape how a product like Signzy's must be designed and what constraints they place on data handling.

Is the role more technical or more sales-oriented?

It sits in between. You are not closing deals (that is the account executive's job), but you are expected to run product demos, answer detailed technical questions from a client's IT team, and sometimes write or review integration code. The blend varies by team: some Solutions Engineers are closer to pre-sales, others closer to implementation. Ask your recruiter which way this specific opening leans.
