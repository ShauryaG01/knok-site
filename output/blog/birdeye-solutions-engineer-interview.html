01 Overview

Overview

BirdEye is a reputation and customer experience platform used by businesses across healthcare, automotive, home services, and retail to manage reviews, listings, and customer messaging. A Solutions Engineer (SE) at BirdEye sits at the crossroads of sales and technical consulting: you demo the platform to prospects, field integration questions, handle objections, and help close deals alongside account executives.

BirdEye currently has 36 open roles across its teams, which signals active hiring. Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two business or technical rounds, and a live demo or presentation round. The exact structure can vary by team and hiring manager, so confirm details with your recruiter after you apply.

The role rewards people who can translate complex API and integration concepts into plain business value, run live product demos confidently, and build rapport quickly with technical buyers.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you ran a technical demo that did not go as planned.

*Situation:* I was demoing an integration between our platform and a prospect's POS system during a call with their CTO and VP of Operations.

*Task:* The live environment threw an authentication error mid-demo, and both decision-makers were watching.

*Action:* I calmly acknowledged the issue, said I wanted to show them the correct flow rather than work around it, and switched to a pre-recorded walkthrough I keep ready for exactly this scenario. I narrated the steps live so the call still felt interactive. After the call, I filed a bug report and sent the prospect a clean sandbox recording with a written summary.

*Result:* The prospect commented that my composure gave them confidence in how our support team would handle real incidents. The deal moved to next stage within the week.

---

Q: How do you explain a webhook integration to a non-technical business owner?

*Situation:* A regional restaurant chain owner asked me why their reservation system data was not syncing automatically with our platform.

*Task:* I needed to explain webhooks without losing them in technical jargon.

*Action:* I used an analogy: 'Think of a webhook like a doorbell. Instead of your team manually checking whether a new reservation came in, the reservation system rings our doorbell the moment something happens, and we answer instantly.' I then showed a simple flow diagram and confirmed what their developer would need to configure on their end.

*Result:* The owner felt confident enough to loop in their in-house developer, who completed the setup in a single session. The client onboarded ahead of schedule.

---

Q: Tell me about a deal you helped close by solving a technical blocker.

*Situation:* A healthcare group was close to signing but their IT team raised concerns about how patient data would be handled inside our messaging module.

*Task:* I needed to address HIPAA-related questions clearly and quickly before the deal stalled.

*Action:* I pulled together our security documentation, scheduled a focused call with their IT lead, and walked through our BAA process, data encryption standards, and access controls. I prepared a one-page summary the IT lead could share internally with their compliance team.

*Result:* The IT team gave sign-off within a few days. The deal closed, and the account became one of our top references in the healthcare vertical.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For objection questions, use a three-step structure: acknowledge the concern genuinely, bridge to a concrete example or data point, then invite the prospect to see the gap themselves. Avoid jumping straight to product features.

For 'how would you demo X' questions, lead with discovery first. Interviewers want to see that you would ask questions before showing slides. A strong answer names a few discovery questions and then explains how the answers shape the demo flow.

For prioritization questions, be explicit about your criteria. Candidates who say 'I would talk to my AE' without adding a tiebreaker framework come across as passive. Name the factors you weigh: deal stage, prospect responsiveness, technical complexity.

For cross-vertical tailoring questions, show you have read BirdEye's industry pages. A dental chain cares about appointment reminders and review volume, while an auto dealer focuses on sales reviews and inventory-level messaging. Specifics beat generalities every time.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

BirdEye SE interviewers typically look for three qualities above all else.

Commercial curiosity. Can you connect a product feature to a business outcome? Candidates who demo features without tying them to revenue, retention, or cost savings rarely advance.

Live composure. The demo round is a test of how you handle uncertainty in front of a buyer. Practise narrating a product walkthrough while answering questions simultaneously. Hesitation is fine; freezing is not.

Vertical fluency. BirdEye serves several industries. Candidates who can speak a healthcare buyer's language in one breath and an automotive dealer's language in the next signal that they can own a territory independently.

Candidates also report that interviewers pay close attention to how you handle 'I don't know.' Saying 'I don't have that answer right now, but here is how I would find it' scores better than guessing.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product cold. Sign up for a BirdEye trial or watch their public demo videos. Map every core feature (reviews, messaging, listings, surveys) to a business problem in at least two industries. Write out one-sentence value statements for each.

Week 2: Practice your demo. Build a focused product walkthrough that opens with a discovery question, moves through a few features tied to a specific persona, and closes with a clear next step. Practise it out loud, not just in your head, and rehearse handling interruptions mid-flow.

Week 3: Sharpen objections and integrations. List the common objections a prospect might raise (price, switching cost, data security, CRM compatibility, team adoption) and prepare a focused response for each. Review BirdEye's integration documentation so you can speak to common CRM and POS connections confidently.

Before the interview: Confirm the exact rounds and formats with your recruiter. Research BirdEye's recent press, product announcements, and the industries they are expanding into. Prepare a few questions that show you have thought about the territory you would own.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping discovery. Many candidates launch into a demo without asking what the prospect actually needs. BirdEye interviewers will notice if you treat every persona the same.

Feature-dumping. Listing every product capability without connecting it to a specific business pain is the most common way to lose a demo round. Less is more when each feature earns its place.

Underselling composure. Candidates sometimes rush past the 'bug during a live demo' or 'difficult objection' questions. These are your chance to show the quality SE roles value most: calm under pressure.

Not asking about success criteria. Failing to ask 'what does success look like in the first few months' signals low commercial awareness. Interviewers expect an SE candidate to treat the interview itself like a discovery call.

Ignoring vertical context. A generic answer about 'helping businesses manage reviews' will not stand out. Name BirdEye's specific verticals and show you understand why each one has distinct needs.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the BirdEye Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically spans a recruiter screen, one or two business or technical rounds, and a final demo or presentation stage. The exact structure varies by team and hiring manager, so confirm with your recruiter after you apply. Some candidates report a panel component that brings together sales and product stakeholders.

Is there a take-home assignment or live demo round?

Most candidates report a live demo or presentation component where you walk through the BirdEye platform for a fictional prospect persona. Typically you receive a brief in advance and are expected to tailor the demo to a specific industry. Practise narrating the walkthrough out loud while handling interruptions, since interviewers often play the role of a sceptical buyer.

What salary can a Solutions Engineer expect at BirdEye?

BirdEye does not publish a fixed salary band for this role publicly. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list compensation for SE roles at comparable SaaS companies, which you can use as a reference point when negotiating. The final package typically includes a base plus a variable commission component tied to deals closed, so ask for a full breakdown during the offer stage.

Does BirdEye hire Solutions Engineers remotely or only in specific cities?

BirdEye has posted SE roles in multiple locations. Among the 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings tracked on knok jobradar as of July 2026, Bangalore leads with 55 listings, followed by Mumbai with 23 and Delhi with 20. Remote and hybrid arrangements exist at many companies in this space, but confirm the specific work model with your recruiter for BirdEye roles.

What is the difference between a Solutions Engineer and a Sales Engineer at BirdEye?

At most companies the titles are used interchangeably, and BirdEye is no exception based on publicly available job descriptions. Both roles involve pre-sales technical support, product demos, and helping account executives close deals. If you see both titles at BirdEye, ask your recruiter whether there is a functional difference in territory, deal size, or product focus.

How important is coding or API knowledge for this role?

BirdEye's SE role is primarily pre-sales, so deep coding ability is not typically required. You do need to be comfortable discussing REST APIs, webhooks, and CRM integrations at a conceptual level and to answer questions from a prospect's IT team with confidence. Candidates report that being able to read API documentation and explain integration flows in plain language matters more than writing code yourself.
