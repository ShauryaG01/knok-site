01 Overview

Overview

Acceldata builds a data observability and reliability platform used by large enterprises to monitor, debug, and optimise their data pipelines. A DevOps Engineer here works at the crossroads of cloud infrastructure, Kubernetes orchestration, and a live SaaS data product, keeping systems reliable while engineering teams ship features at pace.

As of mid-2026, Acceldata has 45 open DevOps roles in India, part of a wider market of 811 DevOps openings tracked across the country. Bangalore leads with 187 postings, followed by Delhi (40), Pune (37), and Hyderabad (28). Salary bands run from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years) to 45-70+ LPA for lead or staff engineers.

Candidates typically report a multi-round process that includes a hands-on infrastructure or coding task, a system design round focused on cloud-native or data platform scenarios, and one or two final conversations with engineering leads. Acceldata interviewers tend to favour practical, scenario-based questions over abstract theory, grounded in real challenges like pipeline reliability, multi-tenancy, and observability at scale.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to keep answers focused and concrete. Here are three examples tailored to common Acceldata DevOps interview themes.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you improved reliability in a production data pipeline environment.

*Situation:* At my previous company, our Spark-based data pipeline was failing regularly in production, causing customer-facing reports to go stale for hours at a time.

*Task:* I was asked to reduce the failure rate and improve mean time to recovery without a complete rewrite of the pipeline.

*Action:* I instrumented the pipeline with Prometheus metrics and built Grafana dashboards to track job durations, retry counts, and error types. I identified that most failures came from resource contention during peak ingestion windows. I adjusted Kubernetes resource requests and limits, added horizontal pod autoscaling, and implemented circuit breakers for downstream API calls.

*Result:* Pipeline failures dropped significantly over the following month and the team could detect issues within minutes instead of hours. This observability-first thinking is core to what Acceldata's own platform delivers to its customers.

---

Q: Describe a CI/CD pipeline you built or significantly improved.

*Situation:* Our team was manually deploying microservices to staging and production using ad-hoc scripts, which led to inconsistent environments and occasional broken deployments.

*Task:* I was responsible for designing a standardised CI/CD pipeline that all service teams could adopt.

*Action:* I built a GitLab CI pipeline template that ran unit tests, built Docker images, pushed to a private registry, and triggered Helm-based deployments to Kubernetes staging environments automatically on merge. I added a manual approval gate for production, integrated Trivy for container vulnerability scanning, and wrote documentation so teams could onboard independently.

*Result:* Deployment frequency increased, rollback time dropped from over an hour to a few minutes, and all new services were onboarded to the pipeline within a quarter.

---

Q: How have you handled a high-severity production incident?

*Situation:* One evening, our data platform's metadata store became unresponsive, taking down real-time observability for all customers in one region.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer, I needed to restore service quickly and communicate clearly with stakeholders throughout.

*Action:* I checked our runbook, confirmed the issue was cascading connection pool exhaustion, and restarted the affected pods in a rolling fashion to avoid full downtime. I posted status updates in our incident Slack channel at regular intervals, looped in the database team for a longer-term fix, and drafted a preliminary post-mortem within a day.

*Result:* Service was restored quickly and the post-mortem led to permanent connection pool tuning plus a new alert that catches this condition before it cascades.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions (such as Kubernetes multi-tenancy or metadata ingestion infrastructure), candidates report that Acceldata interviewers value a structured walk-through: start with requirements and constraints, move to component choices with trade-offs, then explain how you would monitor and operate the system in production. Jumping straight to a tool name without context tends to land poorly.

For incident and debugging questions, use a clear sequence: how you detected the problem, how you isolated the root cause, what you changed, and how you prevented recurrence. Naming specific tools (PagerDuty, Grafana, Loki, kubectl logs) rather than speaking vaguely signals genuine hands-on experience.

For 'how would you approach X' questions, the strongest answers follow a scope-then-execute pattern. First confirm the constraints that matter most (cost, latency, compliance, team size), then describe your approach in concrete steps. This shows both strategic thinking and practical depth.

For behavioural questions, use the STAR structure shown in the sample answers above. Keep Situation brief (two sentences at most), spend the most time on Action, and make Result as specific as you can without inventing numbers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have gone through Acceldata DevOps interviews report that the engineering team consistently looks for a few traits.

Deep Kubernetes and cloud-native fluency. Because Acceldata's product runs on cloud-native infrastructure, interviewers go well beyond surface-level kubectl commands. Expect questions on networking, scheduling, resource management, and real debugging scenarios.

An observability mindset. Acceldata sells data observability to its customers, so they want DevOps Engineers who think in metrics, logs, and alerts by default, not as an afterthought bolted on at the end.

Ownership and on-call maturity. Multiple candidates report that interviewers specifically dig into incidents and post-mortems. They want to see end-to-end ownership of production issues rather than handoffs.

Comfort with the data platform layer. Experience with tools in the data ecosystem (Spark, Kafka, Airflow, or similar) is a clear differentiator. You do not need to be a data engineer, but understanding how data pipelines fail, and why, matters in this context.

Clear, structured communication. In design rounds, the ability to think out loud, ask clarifying questions, and explain trade-offs matters as much as arriving at the right final answer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core infrastructure depth
Revise Kubernetes internals covering scheduling, networking, RBAC, and autoscaling. Practise writing Helm charts and Kubernetes manifests from scratch. Brush up on Terraform or Pulumi for infrastructure-as-code scenarios, since candidates report these come up regularly.

Week 2: Data platform and observability tools
Read about common data pipeline architectures (batch vs. streaming, metadata management). Get comfortable with Prometheus, Grafana, and a log aggregation tool like Loki or the ELK stack. Understand how observability tooling works at the platform level, not just for application monitoring.

Week 3: CI/CD and cloud cost
Review a CI/CD pipeline you have built and prepare to walk through every stage in detail. Prepare one concrete story about cost optimisation with a clear outcome. Revisit incident post-mortems you have been part of and be ready to describe your personal contribution clearly.

Week 4: Mock interviews and company research
Read Acceldata's public engineering blog and product documentation to understand their architecture and terminology. Practise system design questions out loud with a friend or record yourself. Prepare thoughtful questions for the panel about on-call culture, deployment cadence, and current tooling choices.

Candidates report that Acceldata's process typically includes at least one live or hands-on infrastructure task, so practise in a real terminal environment, not just on paper. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Kubernetes as only a deployment tool. Candidates who can describe basic deployments but stumble on pod disruption budgets, topology spread constraints, or network policies leave a weak impression. Interviewers at Acceldata probe deeper than kubectl apply.

Skipping the 'why' in system design. Naming tools without explaining trade-offs (why Kafka over RabbitMQ, why a service mesh vs. not) signals surface knowledge. At a product company like Acceldata, interviewers push hard on reasoning.

Generic incident stories. Saying 'I escalated to my manager' or 'the team handled it' weakens your answer immediately. Interviewers want to hear specifically what you diagnosed, what you changed, and what you put in place to prevent recurrence.

Ignoring the data platform context. Candidates from pure cloud infrastructure backgrounds sometimes struggle when questions touch on Spark resource management, pipeline scheduling, or metadata store behaviour. Even a basic familiarity with the data layer gives you a meaningful edge.

Not asking questions at the end. Acceldata engineers typically welcome genuine questions about their stack, on-call load, and product roadmap. Skipping this part can leave the impression that you are not genuinely invested in the role.
