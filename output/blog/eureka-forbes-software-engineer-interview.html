02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from publicly reported Eureka Forbes interview experiences and reflect what candidates say they were asked most often.

  1. Walk me through your experience with backend or full-stack development, and a project you are most proud of.
  2. How would you design a system to monitor the health of thousands of IoT-connected water purifiers deployed across India?
  3. Describe a time you identified and fixed a performance bottleneck in a production system.
  4. How do you approach debugging a live issue when logs are sparse or unclear?
  5. What data structures would you use to build a real-time alerting system for field engineers?
  6. Tell me about your experience with REST APIs or third-party service integrations.
  7. Describe a project where you collaborated closely with a non-technical team, such as sales, operations, or customer service.
  8. How would you break down and deliver a new customer-facing feature when requirements are not fully defined?
  9. SQL vs NoSQL: explain the difference and walk me through when you would choose one over the other.
  10. What is your experience with cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, or GCP) or on-premise deployments?
  11. How do you ensure code quality in a team that ships frequently?
  12. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision made by your team or manager, and how you handled it.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you identified and fixed a performance bottleneck in a production system.

*Situation:* Our checkout service was loading slowly during peak hours and customer complaints were rising.
*Task:* I was assigned to investigate and resolve the issue within the sprint.
*Action:* I profiled the API calls and found one database query doing a full table scan because an index was missing. I added the index, rewrote one N+1 query to use a join, and added response caching for product data that rarely changes.
*Result:* Page load time dropped significantly according to our monitoring tool, and checkout drop-offs reduced in the following week. The fix was deployed with zero downtime.

---

Q: Describe a project where you worked closely with a non-technical team.

*Situation:* The field-service operations team was tracking technician visits in a spreadsheet, which caused frequent errors and delays in customer callbacks.
*Task:* My team was asked to build a scheduling and status-update tool for field engineers to use on their phones.
*Action:* I ran several short sessions with the operations leads to understand their workflow before writing any code. I then built a lightweight mobile-friendly web app with status updates, technician assignment, and a basic dashboard for supervisors.
*Result:* The operations team adopted it within two weeks. Scheduling errors dropped significantly by their own account, and they stopped relying on the spreadsheet entirely.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision and how you handled it.

*Situation:* My tech lead wanted to use a third-party vendor library for a core data-processing pipeline. I felt it introduced unnecessary dependency risk.
*Task:* I needed to raise my concern without derailing the timeline or the team dynamic.
*Action:* I prepared a short written comparison: the vendor library versus a lightweight in-house solution, covering maintenance risk, licensing cost, and the complexity of building it ourselves. I shared it with the lead before the next planning meeting so he had time to review without feeling put on the spot.
*Result:* We used the vendor library for a non-critical part of the pipeline and built the core component in-house. The lead said the written comparison made the conversation much easier, and the pipeline has been stable since.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most reliable structure for any behavioral question. Keep your Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did), and always close with a measurable or observable Result.

For system design questions, candidates report that Eureka Forbes interviewers appreciate a structured walk-through: start with clarifying questions (scale, users, constraints), then cover high-level components, data flow, storage choices, and finally failure modes. Thinking out loud is valued more than arriving at a perfect answer in silence.

For coding questions, explain your approach before you start typing. If you see a brute-force solution first, say so, then explain how you would optimize it. Interviewers at product and enterprise companies like Eureka Forbes typically care about whether you can communicate your reasoning as much as whether you get the optimal solution.

For questions about working with non-technical stakeholders, frame your answer around listening and translating: how you understood their problem in their own language, and how you turned that into a technical deliverable. This matters more at a consumer-goods company than at a pure SaaS startup.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that Eureka Forbes interviewers consistently look for a few core qualities.

Practical problem-solving over algorithm performance. The engineering work at Eureka Forbes spans IoT device management, customer platforms, and field-service tools. Interviewers want to see that you can build reliable, maintainable software for real operational needs, not just pass whiteboard puzzles.

Clear communication. You will likely work with product, operations, and service teams who are not engineers. Interviewers look for candidates who can explain technical choices in plain language and who ask clarifying questions rather than assuming.

Ownership. Candidates who talk about taking end-to-end responsibility, following up on a bug after it was fixed, or going back to a stakeholder to confirm the outcome, consistently receive positive interview feedback.

Adaptability. Eureka Forbes is actively modernizing its tech stack. Interviewers respond well to candidates who have learned new tools on the job and can describe how they approached that learning.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations and company context

Read up on Eureka Forbes products, especially Aquaguard and their service network, so you can ground your system design answers in their actual domain. Revise core data structures (arrays, trees, graphs, hash maps) and practice several medium-difficulty coding problems focused on arrays, strings, and SQL queries.

Week 2: System design and behavioral prep

Practice designing systems relevant to Eureka Forbes: a device health monitoring system, a customer service ticketing tool, or a field-engineer scheduling app. For behavioral questions, prepare several stories from your past work using the STAR format. Cover at least one performance fix, one cross-team collaboration, one technical disagreement, and one project you owned end-to-end.

Week 3: Mock interviews and polish

Do at least two mock interviews out loud, either with a friend or by recording yourself. Review your resume and be ready to walk through every project listed. Prepare a few questions to ask the interviewer about the team's tech stack, deployment practices, or current engineering priorities.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping clarifying questions on system design. Candidates report that jumping straight into the architecture without asking about scale, users, or constraints is one of the most common ways to lose points. Take a few minutes to ask and confirm scope before you start drawing components.

Giving generic behavioral answers. Saying 'I am a good team player' without a specific story is the fastest way to lose the interviewer's attention. Every behavioral answer needs a concrete situation with a clear action you personally took.

Ignoring the consumer-goods context. Eureka Forbes is not a fintech or a pure SaaS company. Candidates who frame every answer around high-frequency trading systems or massive concurrent-user scenarios without adjusting to the actual domain can come across as disconnected from the role.

Not following up after coding. If you finish a coding solution, always ask yourself out loud: 'What is the time and space complexity? What edge cases should I handle?' Interviewers note that candidates who stop after getting a working solution miss an easy opportunity to show depth.

Underselling cross-functional work. At a company like Eureka Forbes, the ability to work with operations and service teams is as valuable as technical skill. Do not skip or rush through experiences where you collaborated with non-engineers.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Eureka Forbes Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically runs 2-4 rounds. This usually includes an HR or recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final discussion with the hiring manager or team lead. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and seniority level.

Does Eureka Forbes ask competitive programming questions?

Publicly reported experiences suggest Eureka Forbes focuses more on practical coding and system design than on competitive algorithm puzzles. You should be comfortable with common data structures and SQL, but highly theoretical problems are unlikely. The emphasis is typically on writing clean, working code and explaining your reasoning clearly.

What is the salary range for a Software Engineer at Eureka Forbes?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, typical market ranges for Software Engineers in India are 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). These are general market ranges, not Eureka Forbes-specific figures. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and negotiation.

How important is domain knowledge about Eureka Forbes products?

You do not need to be an expert in water purifiers or vacuum cleaners, but knowing the basics of the Aquaguard product line and the company's field-service model will help you give more grounded system design answers. Candidates who connect their technical ideas to real operational scenarios at the company tend to leave a stronger impression.

Are the interview rounds conducted online or in-person?

Candidates report that initial rounds are typically conducted online via video call. Final rounds may be in-person depending on the team and location. Confirm the format with the recruiter before each round so you can prepare your setup or travel accordingly.

How long does the hiring process take from first round to offer?

Based on publicly reported experiences, the process from first contact to offer can range from two to four weeks. The timeline depends on how quickly rounds are scheduled and the number of candidates in the pipeline. Following up politely with the recruiter after each round is a reasonable way to stay informed on your status.
