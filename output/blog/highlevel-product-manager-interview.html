01 Overview

Overview

HighLevel (also known as GoHighLevel) is a white-label B2B SaaS platform that lets marketing agencies run CRM, email, SMS, funnels, and reputation management under their own brand name. The company has a distinctive two-sided model: agencies pay HighLevel directly, then resell these tools to their own SMB clients. This shapes almost every product decision the PM team makes.

HighLevel currently lists 17 open Product Manager roles, making it one of the more active PM hirers in the SaaS space right now. Across India, knok job radar tracked 2,009 PM openings as of July 2026, with Bangalore leading at 271 roles, Delhi at 177, and Mumbai at 56.

The interview process typically spans 3-5 rounds, candidates report. Expect a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product case study (take-home or live), and a cross-functional panel. HighLevel is a fast-moving, remote-first company, so expect questions that test your ability to ship quickly, manage agency workflows, and communicate clearly with distributed teams.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Product Thinking and Prioritization

  1. HighLevel serves thousands of agencies with very different workflows. How would you decide which features to build next?
  2. HighLevel is a multi-product suite (CRM, funnels, SMS, reputation). How do you decide what goes into the core platform versus a paid add-on?
  3. How would you measure the success of HighLevel's AI-powered conversation and automation features?
  4. A major agency segment is churning faster than expected. Walk me through how you would diagnose and address this.

Execution and Cross-Functional Work

  1. Tell me about a feature you owned end-to-end. What was the hardest tradeoff you made?
  2. How do you handle scope changes mid-sprint when a third-party integration breaks or an API changes?
  3. Walk me through how you write a product requirements document for a complex integration, such as a payment gateway or SMS provider.
  4. Describe a time you had to say no to a technically feasible customer request.

Strategy and Customer Understanding

  1. HighLevel sells to agencies who resell to SMBs. How does that two-layer model affect how you prioritize features?
  2. How would you improve HighLevel's onboarding experience for a brand-new agency with no technical background?
  3. A competitor launches a feature that several of your top accounts are asking for. What do you do?
  4. Tell me about a time you used data to change a product direction you or your team believed in.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you used data to change a product direction.

*Situation:* I was PM for a B2B SaaS product where the team was convinced a redesigned dashboard was the highest-priority feature for the quarter.

*Task:* My job was to validate this assumption before committing several weeks of engineering time to it.

*Action:* I pulled usage logs and found that only a small share of active users visited the existing dashboard more than once a week. I ran structured interviews with several agency owners and learned they were far more frustrated by the manual effort required for client reporting. I prepared a prioritization document using RICE scoring and recommended pivoting to an automated reporting module instead.

*Result:* We shipped the reporting module in the next cycle. Renewal rates in that cohort improved, and the feature became one of the top-mentioned positives in publicly reported customer feedback surveys the following quarter.

---

Q: Describe a time you said no to a customer request.

*Situation:* A large agency asked us to build a fully custom white-label mobile app just for their brand.

*Task:* I needed to evaluate whether this was worth building and communicate a clear decision to the account without losing the relationship.

*Action:* I estimated the engineering effort, checked how many other clients had made similar requests (only two in the past year), and mapped the request against roadmap priorities. I wrote a 'no for now' note explaining that resources were focused on features serving the broader agency base, and I proposed an alternative: a faster, improved mobile-responsive web experience.

*Result:* The client accepted the alternative and renewed their contract. The decision protected the team's focus on higher-impact work.

---

Q: How did you manage a mid-sprint scope change?

*Situation:* Three days into a two-week sprint, we learned that a third-party SMS provider was deprecating an API we depended on.

*Task:* I had to decide quickly what to cut, what to protect, and how to communicate the change to stakeholders.

*Action:* I held a quick sync with engineering, listed every in-progress task, and ranked them by customer impact and time to complete. We paused a low-urgency analytics widget and moved two engineers to the SMS migration. I sent a short update to the sales team explaining the delay and the reason behind it.

*Result:* We completed the SMS migration before the deprecation deadline with zero customer downtime. The analytics widget shipped in the following sprint.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for every behavioral question. HighLevel interviewers typically ask about real past experiences, so structure every story with a clear setup, your specific role, the steps you took, and a concrete outcome. Practice keeping each answer under three minutes when spoken aloud.

RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) is the go-to for prioritization questions. When asked how you would choose between features for an agency platform, walk through each dimension out loud. This signals structured thinking rather than gut feel.

The 'two-customer lens' is specific to HighLevel. For any product design or strategy question, clarify early whether you are optimizing for the agency (the paying customer) or the agency's SMB client (the end user). Interviewers at two-sided SaaS companies notice immediately when candidates treat these as the same person.

Five Whys works well for root-cause questions like 'a segment is churning.' Start at the symptom and work backwards. Pair it with a data-first approach: state which metrics you would look at before forming a hypothesis.

PRD structure is often tested at HighLevel because strong written communication is a core PM competency there. A solid PRD covers: problem statement, target user, success metrics, scope, and open questions. Keep it concise.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Agency-first thinking. HighLevel's primary customers are marketing agencies, not the SMBs who use the tools agencies build for their clients. Candidates who instinctively think about the agency's workflow, their reseller business model, and their relationship with end clients stand out clearly from those who think product-first without the customer layer.

Speed and pragmatism. HighLevel is known for shipping fast. Interviewers typically look for PMs who can make a good-enough decision quickly, communicate it cleanly, and iterate rather than waiting for perfect information before acting.

Strong written communication. As a remote-first company operating across time zones, HighLevel relies on async documentation. Candidates who can write a clear PRD, a crisp 'no for now' decision note, or a concise escalation memo are valued more than those who are only strong in live conversation.

Data fluency without over-indexing. Interviewers want PMs who reach for data first but can also act when data is thin or ambiguous. Avoid presenting yourself as someone who needs a perfect dataset before making any move.

Cross-functional credibility. You will work with engineers, designers, and go-to-market teams. Interviewers want to see that you can influence without authority, push back on unrealistic timelines, and protect your team from scope creep.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product and customer

Sign up for a HighLevel trial or watch agency walkthrough videos to map the core modules: CRM, funnels, SMS, email, reputation management, and booking. Try to understand how an agency would use each module to serve their SMB clients. Write down three things you would improve and the reasoning behind each.

Week 2: Build your story bank

Prepare 6-8 STAR stories covering: prioritization decisions, customer pushback you handled well, data-driven pivots, cross-functional conflicts, and scope changes. Each story should fit under three minutes when spoken aloud. Time yourself and cut anything that is not directly relevant to the point.

Week 3: Practice live case studies

Work through one product case study per day using the RICE framework for prioritization questions and the two-customer lens for HighLevel-specific design prompts. Do at least two mock sessions with a friend or practice partner so you are comfortable thinking out loud under light pressure.

Before each round

Read HighLevel's recent product release notes and browse agency community forums to see what real users are asking for. Prepare at least two specific, thoughtful questions per round. This signals genuine interest in the product, not just interview preparation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating agencies and SMBs as the same customer. This is the most common error for candidates new to two-sided SaaS models. Always clarify who you are optimizing for before diving into any product answer.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'we improved retention' without explaining what you specifically did or what the result was loses credibility fast. Use concrete actions and real outcomes, even if you cannot share exact figures.

Over-engineering prioritization answers. Some candidates build elaborate scoring models in case interviews when a clear, spoken RICE walkthrough would be far more effective. Show your thinking process, not a spreadsheet.

Ignoring async communication signals. For a remote-first company, how you write matters as much as how you speak. Candidates who only optimize for verbal fluency miss this dimension entirely.

Not asking questions. HighLevel interviewers typically notice whether a candidate engages with genuine curiosity about the product or the team. Prepare at least two specific, thoughtful questions per round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the HighLevel PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-5 rounds. This usually includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product case study (take-home or live), and a cross-functional panel. Round names and order can vary, so confirm the structure with your recruiter at the start.

Does HighLevel give a take-home case study or a live case?

Candidates report seeing both formats. Some are given a take-home case to submit before the panel, while others work through a product problem live with the interviewer. Prepare for both so you are not caught off guard. For take-home cases, crisp written structure matters as much as the quality of the idea itself.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at HighLevel in India?

HighLevel does not publish band-level salary data publicly. Based on India PM market data from knok job radar, typical ranges are: Associate PM at 12-20 LPA, PM with 3-6 years of experience at 24-40 LPA, and Senior PM at 40-60 LPA. Actual offers may vary based on your level, location, and negotiation.

Is HighLevel a good fit for PMs from a non-agency background?

It can be, but you will need to close the knowledge gap before interviews. HighLevel's platform is built around agency workflows, so candidates who do not understand how agencies bill clients, manage sub-accounts, or white-label tools will struggle with product design questions. Spend time with the product and agency community content before your first round.

How important is technical knowledge for a HighLevel PM role?

Candidates report that HighLevel does not expect PMs to write code, but they do expect comfort with integrations such as SMS APIs, payment gateways, and webhooks, along with an ability to scope technical work realistically. Being able to discuss third-party API constraints or data model tradeoffs in plain language is a clear differentiator in interviews.
