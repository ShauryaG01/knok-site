01 Overview

Overview

Freshdesk is the flagship customer support SaaS product from Freshworks, a publicly listed company with engineering hubs in Chennai, Hyderabad, and Bangalore. Engineering Manager roles here span product squads covering ticketing, automation, telephony, and AI-powered features under the Freddy AI umbrella. As of knok jobradar data from mid-2026, there are 975 Engineering Manager openings across India, with Bangalore leading at 182 open roles. Freshdesk alone currently lists 23 active EM-track openings.

Candidates typically go through several rounds: a recruiter screen, one or more technical or system design discussions, a people management round, a cross-functional or values-based round, and a final discussion with senior leadership. The exact sequence and number of rounds varies by team and seniority level. Candidates report the full process usually spanning several weeks from first contact to offer.

For compensation context, knok jobradar data puts Manager-level EM roles in the 35-60 LPA band, Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range, and Director-equivalent roles at 90-150+ LPA. Freshworks typically includes ESOPs in the total package, which candidates report as a meaningful component at senior levels.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report facing in Freshdesk EM interviews. Prepare concrete examples for each.

  1. Freshdesk serves millions of SMB customers globally. How do you make sure your team ships features that are simple and reliable for a small business, not over-engineered for enterprise use?
  1. How have you managed a team working on features directly tied to trial-to-paid conversion or growth metrics? How did you balance speed with engineering quality?
  1. Tell me about a time you improved on-call reliability for a customer-facing SaaS product. What did you change, and how did you know it was working?
  1. Freshworks has engineering across Chennai, Hyderabad, Bangalore, and San Mateo. How do you manage a distributed team and keep alignment without flooding everyone with meetings?
  1. Describe a situation where you migrated a high-traffic, customer-critical feature with near-zero downtime. How did you plan, communicate, and execute it?
  1. Freshdesk is investing heavily in AI through Freddy AI. Have you managed a team shipping ML-backed product features? What made that process different from standard feature development?
  1. How do you handle a situation where your product manager and your engineering team strongly disagree on the technical approach for a critical feature?
  1. Tell me about a hard call you made on technical debt: either prioritising it over a feature request, or deferring it despite real risk. How did you decide and what happened?
  1. How do you manage an engineer who is technically excellent but struggles with communication in cross-functional settings, such as with product or customer success teams?
  1. Freshworks has a strong 'customer obsession' value. Give an example where your team had to change or delay a technical decision directly because of customer feedback.
  1. How do you track team health and engineering output beyond velocity or story points? What signals tell you things are going well versus heading for trouble?
  1. How have you supported senior engineers who are growing toward Staff or Principal roles? What did you do differently for them compared to mid-level engineers?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioural questions. Here are three worked examples tailored to what Freshdesk interviewers typically probe.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you improved on-call reliability for a customer-facing SaaS product.

*Situation:* My team owned the notifications service for a B2B SaaS product. We were getting paged multiple times each week, engineers were burning out, and customer escalations were rising.

*Task:* I needed to reduce alert fatigue and improve mean time to recovery without adding headcount, as we were mid-cycle on a major feature delivery.

*Action:* Over two sprints I ran a focused audit where every alert was classified as either actionable-and-customer-impacting or noisy. We removed or downgraded alerts that fired often but had no direct customer impact. I introduced a tiered on-call rotation with a backup engineer covering critical hours, worked with the team to write runbooks for the most common recurring incidents, and made blameless post-mortems a monthly ritual.

*Result:* Pages dropped sharply within two months, and the team's next pulse survey showed a clear improvement in work-life satisfaction. The process was later adopted by two other squads in the org.

---

Q: How have you handled a situation where your product manager and your engineering team disagreed on a technical approach?

*Situation:* We were building a new bulk-action feature for a high-usage workflow. The PM wanted a synchronous API so results appeared in real time. My engineers felt this would cause timeouts at scale and hurt reliability for our largest customers.

*Task:* I needed a decision quickly because the feature was on a public roadmap with a committed date.

*Action:* I set up a structured discussion where each side presented the customer impact of their approach, not just technical or product arguments. I asked the PM to share data on typical bulk action sizes, and asked engineering to prototype both approaches in a single sprint and share latency results. The data showed the synchronous approach would fail at the upper end of our customer usage distribution. We landed on an async approach with a real-time progress indicator, which addressed the PM's core need (visible feedback) without the reliability risk.

*Result:* The feature shipped on time, had no incidents in the first month, and the PM later told me the progress indicator was better received by customers than the original synchronous design.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you supported a senior engineer growing toward a Staff-level role.

*Situation:* I had a technically excellent senior engineer whose impact was confined to her own squad. She solved problems brilliantly but rarely shared context across teams, wrote RFCs, or brought other engineers along.

*Task:* My goal was to help her see that Staff-level impact means multiplying the organisation, not just individual output.

*Action:* I gave her a cross-team initiative as the technical lead, owning both the design and the communication plan. We met weekly to work on writing proposals that non-technical stakeholders could engage with, and I explicitly debriefed after every cross-team meeting on what landed and what did not. I also connected her with a Staff engineer from another org who had recently made the same transition.

*Result:* Within two quarters she was being referenced in cross-team planning meetings without me nominating her. She earned the Staff promotion in the next cycle and told me the structured communication practice was the thing that changed her trajectory.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'tell me about a time...' questions, use STAR: Situation (two to three sentences of context), Task (what was your specific responsibility), Action (what YOU did, not the team generically), Result (measurable or observable outcome). Freshdesk interviewers typically follow up with 'what would you do differently,' so prepare an honest reflection for each story.

For system design or technical strategy questions, lead with customer impact before architecture. Freshdesk is a product company where every technical decision has a customer-facing consequence. Frame your answer as: the customer problem is X, the constraint is Y, so the technical choice is Z.

For conflict or disagreement questions, show that you separate the person from the position. Describe how you structured the conversation to bring data in rather than opinions. Freshdesk values cross-functional collaboration, so avoid any story where you simply overruled someone without building alignment.

For 'how do you manage...' questions about people, be specific about the steps you took and the timeline. Vague answers like 'I coached them' do not land. Say what you coached, how often you met, what changed, and what the outcome was.

For values-based questions around customer obsession or ownership, pick a story where you or your team did something that was harder or slower for engineering but clearly better for the customer. These resonate strongly at Freshworks, where the product ethos is rooted in serving underserved SMB customers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Freshdesk EM interviewers are typically looking for four things.

Product sense paired with technical credibility. They want someone who can challenge a spec on customer impact grounds in a product review, then turn around and review a pull request meaningfully. Pure managers who cannot engage technically do not typically clear the bar here.

Evidence of SaaS-specific challenges handled well. Candidates who have worked on multi-tenant systems, managed SLAs for SMB customers, or shipped AI-backed features in production get a clear advantage. Frame your experience in SaaS terms even if your previous product was not customer support software.

Multiplier thinking. Freshworks has scaled rapidly and promotes the idea that a good EM should make the engineers around them better. Expect probing questions about how you have grown people, spread context across teams, and improved processes beyond your own squad.

Low ego, high ownership. Candidates report that interviewers notice quickly when someone deflects blame, uses 'we' to absorb credit in positive stories, or hides personal failures behind 'the team.' Own your mistakes directly and your contributions clearly.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and company.
Use Freshdesk hands-on if you have not already, on a free trial or as a paying user. File a support ticket, try the automation rules, explore the Freddy AI features. You will be asked about customer empathy and product intuition, and hands-on experience makes your answers specific rather than generic. Also review Freshworks public announcements and investor communications about their AI direction for 2025-2026.

Week 2: Build your story bank.
Write down five to seven work stories covering: a technical migration, a people conflict, a team health problem you fixed, a cross-functional disagreement, a hiring or growth decision, and one time engineering had to serve the customer at a cost to itself. Map each story to the STAR format and identify which Freshdesk interview questions each story answers.

Week 3: Practice out loud.
Record yourself answering questions or run mock sessions with a peer. Freshdesk rounds are typically conversational with follow-up questions, so practising against a written transcript is not enough. Keep behavioural answers focused and under three minutes, and system design answers structured from customer impact down to implementation.

Before each round, re-read the job description for the specific team if you have it, and prepare two or three thoughtful questions for each interviewer. Asking good questions signals genuine interest and is itself evaluated.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about the team instead of yourself. When asked what YOU did, answering with 'we built...' or 'the team delivered...' signals low self-awareness to the interviewer. Use 'I' for your decisions and actions, even when the outcome was collaborative.

No signals in your results. Saying 'the project was a success' is weak. Even without exact metrics, describe observable outcomes: customer escalations dropped, the on-call rotation stopped burning people out, the engineer got promoted in the next cycle.

Ignoring Freshdesk's SMB focus. Answers that assume enterprise-scale complexity or large dedicated SRE teams can feel off-context here. Freshdesk's core customers are small and mid-sized businesses with simpler needs, lower price tolerance, and heavy reliance on support. Show you understand that context.

Over-preparing for coding and neglecting leadership depth. Candidates sometimes over-rotate on system design and arrive underprepared on people management stories. Freshdesk typically weights leadership and cross-functional collaboration heavily at the EM level.

Not asking questions. Candidates report that rounds where they asked no questions felt flat, even when their answers were strong. Thoughtful questions show genuine interest in the team and the role.

Hedging every answer. Saying 'it depends' without committing to a position reads as indecisive. Give your actual opinion first, then acknowledge the trade-offs clearly.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Freshdesk Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates typically report going through four to six rounds total, including a recruiter screen, a technical or system design discussion, a people management round, a cross-functional or values round, and a final discussion with senior leadership. The exact number depends on the team, the level, and how earlier rounds go. Some candidates also report an additional hiring manager call before the formal rounds begin.

Is there a coding round for Engineering Manager roles at Freshdesk?

Most candidates report there is no live coding round at the EM level. However, expect at least one round where your technical depth is probed through system design, architecture trade-offs, or discussion of past technical decisions. Being able to engage meaningfully with code and architecture choices, even if you are not writing code day-to-day, is important at Freshdesk.

What salary can I expect for an Engineering Manager role at Freshdesk?

Knok jobradar data puts Manager-level EM roles in the 35-60 LPA band and Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range. Director-equivalent roles are in the 90-150+ LPA range. Freshworks typically includes ESOPs in the total package, so always negotiate the full offer, not just base salary.

How important is SaaS or customer support domain experience for this role?

Having shipped SaaS products, especially multi-tenant systems or customer-facing tools, gives you a clear advantage. Freshdesk interviewers care that you understand SMB customer constraints, SLA-driven development, and the cost of downtime on a support platform. Candidates from adjacent domains like CRM, ITSM, or productivity tools have cleared the bar by framing their experience in customer-impact terms.

How should I prepare for the people management round?

Prepare specific stories about: a low-performer you managed through a formal or informal improvement process, a high-performer you helped grow into a larger role, a team conflict you resolved, and a hard resourcing or hiring call you made. Vague answers do not land. Interviewers will probe for what exactly you said, how often you met, and what changed as a direct result of your actions.

Does Freshdesk hire Engineering Managers externally or mainly promote internally?

Freshdesk actively hires EMs externally. With 23 open roles currently listed, a significant share of those are filled through external hiring. Candidates from product SaaS companies, including startups and larger tech firms, regularly clear the process. Internal promotions also happen, but external hiring at the EM level is a consistent pattern candidates report.
