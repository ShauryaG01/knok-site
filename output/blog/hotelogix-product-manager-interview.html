01 Overview

Overview

Hotelogix is a cloud-based hospitality technology company that builds Property Management System (PMS) software for independent hotels, resorts, and serviced apartments worldwide. Their product stack includes a core PMS, a channel manager connecting to OTAs like MakeMyTrip and Booking.com, and an online booking engine for direct hotel bookings. As of mid-2026, knok jobradar tracked 44 open roles at Hotelogix, reflecting active product team growth.

PM roles here sit at the intersection of B2B SaaS and travel. You will typically own features used daily by hotel front-desk staff, revenue managers, or operations heads. These users are not technical and rely on software that just works. Candidates report that interviews test both core product fundamentals and awareness of hospitality workflows such as check-in, rate management, and OTA distribution.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you prioritize features for a small hotel owner switching from paper records to Hotelogix PMS?

*Situation:* In a previous B2B SaaS role, I was responsible for onboarding a segment of first-time software users, similar to independent hotel owners moving off manual records.

*Task:* I needed to decide which features to surface first so users saw value quickly without feeling overwhelmed by a complex product.

*Action:* I ran discovery interviews with hotel owners and found their biggest daily fear was double-booking a room, which caused guest complaints and reputation damage. I scoped a focused onboarding flow around three core capabilities: a visual room calendar, creating a reservation, and generating a basic invoice. I worked with design to tuck advanced features behind a 'more options' link until the user completed their first few bookings.

*Result:* Users who completed the core flow early in onboarding had noticeably stronger longer-term retention. The approach became the standard onboarding path, and the customer success team reported fewer 'how do I do this basic task' support tickets.

---

Q: A hotel reports that occupancy data in Hotelogix does not match actual bookings. How do you investigate?

*Situation:* At a previous company, a key client flagged a data discrepancy between what our platform showed and their actual sales figures. It was creating a trust issue with the account.

*Task:* I had to identify whether the problem was a data pipeline issue, a sync failure with a third-party integration, or user error, and keep the client informed throughout.

*Action:* I mapped the full data flow from end to end: booking created in OTA, synced to channel manager, updated in PMS, then reflected in reports. I asked engineering to pull the raw event log for that hotel over the prior week. We found that one OTA had changed their API response format and our parser was not handling the new structure. I escalated for a hotfix, updated the client daily, and proactively flagged two other hotels using the same OTA so we could check their data before they reported issues.

*Result:* The discrepancy was resolved quickly. We caught similar issues at the other hotels before they became complaints. I then proposed an automated nightly reconciliation check, which engineering shipped in the following sprint.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you said no to a customer request.

*Situation:* A large hotel chain asked our team to build a fully custom reporting module tailored to their internal finance format, with a deadline tied to their quarterly board meeting.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to take it on as a one-off build or push back, knowing the custom work would delay several features on our core roadmap.

*Action:* I met with the account manager and the client to understand the underlying need. What they actually needed was data exported in a specific column order for their Excel template. I offered two alternatives: a configurable export format we were already building for another customer segment, and a short-term data extract via our existing API that their IT team could format. I was honest that a fully custom module was not something we could commit to without delaying work that affected all our clients.

*Result:* The client accepted the API-based workaround for their board meeting and agreed to wait for the configurable export. The relationship stayed intact. When we shipped the configurable export a few months later, it fully met their needs without any custom engineering.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions respond well to a structure that starts with the user, moves to the problem, then evaluates solutions. For Hotelogix, always name the specific user type: hotel owner, front-desk agent, revenue manager, or OTA channel coordinator. Each has different goals and pain points.

For prioritization questions, use a straightforward impact-versus-effort framing. Define impact in terms of what matters most to a hotel: reducing overbooking, increasing direct bookings, saving staff time, or cutting support calls. Candidates report that Hotelogix interviewers appreciate operational thinking, not just abstract frameworks.

For metrics questions, separate leading from lagging indicators. For a booking engine, leading indicators might include session-to-booking conversion and page load time. Lagging indicators include revenue per available room growth for the hotel. Avoid vague answers like 'engagement' without defining exactly what you are measuring and how.

For root cause and case study questions, use a structured breakdown: define the expected behavior, map the actual flow step by step, isolate where the gap is, and propose a fix with a way to verify it worked. This approach suits the occupancy data mismatch question type that Hotelogix commonly tests.

For stakeholder or cross-functional questions, show that you distinguish between listening to understand and listening to agree. Interviewers want PMs who can say no with data. Prepare one clear example of a time you disagreed with a stakeholder and handled it constructively.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity matters more than domain expertise. You do not need years of hospitality experience to interview well at Hotelogix. What interviewers want to see is that you have thought about how hotels actually operate: how they manage room inventory, how OTA commissions affect pricing decisions, and what happens at the front desk during peak check-in hours.

Customer empathy for non-technical users. A hotel owner in a tier-2 Indian city running a small property is not a tech-savvy startup founder. Candidates who design with that user in mind, prioritizing simple interfaces, minimal training requirements, and strong error prevention, tend to stand out.

Comfort with integrations and data flows. Hotelogix is deeply connected to OTAs, payment gateways, and booking platforms. Interviewers typically probe whether you understand how API failures, sync delays, or format changes affect the end user, even if you are not writing the code yourself.

Commercial awareness. PMS is a competitive market. Interviewers want to know you understand the business model: subscription revenue, customer acquisition cost, and churn risk when a competitor offers a lower price. Tie your product decisions to business outcomes.

Clear, structured communication. Candidates report that Hotelogix interviewers listen carefully for structure. Answer in clear steps. Use a user-first, problem-next, solution-last order consistently and you will stand out from candidates who jump straight to features.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: Learn the product and domain.
Sign up for the Hotelogix free trial or watch walkthrough videos of the PMS, channel manager, and booking engine. Understand what a hotel does from check-in to check-out and where each software module fits. Read publicly available articles on OTA distribution, RevPAR, and rate management.

Week two: Practice product questions through the Hotelogix lens.
Take generic product sense questions and reframe them around hospitality. Instead of 'design a feature for a marketplace,' think 'design a feature for a hotel revenue manager.' Practice your answers out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself and listen for vague phrases that need to be made more specific.

Week three: Prepare your STAR stories.
Select experiences from your past that cover: a complex cross-functional project, a time you used data to make a decision, a time you said no to a request, and a product you shipped end to end. Make sure at least two stories involve B2B or enterprise customers, since Hotelogix sells to businesses.

Before each round:
Check for recent Hotelogix news, product launches, or integration announcements. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for the interviewer that show you have done your homework. Candidates report that asking about the PM team's current focus area lands well.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Treating the hotel owner like an end consumer.
Hotelogix is B2B. The buyer is a business owner making an operational purchasing decision. Answers that focus on delight or gamification often miss the mark. Focus on reducing operational friction, saving time, and cutting costs.

2. Skipping the user definition.
Hotelogix has multiple user types: the hotel owner, the front-desk agent, the revenue manager, the IT administrator. Candidates who jump to a solution without naming which user they are solving for come across as shallow. Always say who you are designing for before you say what you are building.

3. Proposing features without mentioning trade-offs.
Candidates who only list the benefits of their proposed solution, without acknowledging what it costs to build, what it might break, or who it might not help, tend to get pushed back in interviews. Show that you think in trade-offs.

4. Not knowing basic hospitality terms.
RevPAR, ADR (Average Daily Rate), occupancy rate, OTA, and channel manager are terms that come up naturally in Hotelogix interviews. Using them correctly in context signals domain readiness. Stumbling on them signals you have not done your homework.

5. Giving vague metrics.
Saying 'I would track user engagement' without defining what that means for a hotel front desk is a red flag. Be specific about what you are counting, over what time period, and what a good result looks like.

6. Underestimating integrations.
Hotelogix's value partly comes from connecting to dozens of OTAs and third-party tools. Candidates who ignore this layer when discussing features or metrics leave a gap in their understanding of the product's real complexity.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Hotelogix PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves two to four rounds. These commonly include an initial screening call with HR, a product sense or case-study round with a PM or product leader, and a final discussion with a senior stakeholder. The exact structure varies by level and team, so it is worth asking the recruiter what to expect when you are first contacted.

Do I need hospitality industry experience to apply for a PM role at Hotelogix?

Not necessarily. Hotelogix hires PMs from B2B SaaS, fintech, and other verticals. What matters more is whether you can quickly learn how hotels operate and show empathy for non-technical business users. Spending a week understanding the PMS product and basic hospitality concepts like OTA distribution and RevPAR goes a long way. Candidates without hospitality backgrounds have cleared interviews by demonstrating strong product thinking and genuine domain curiosity.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Hotelogix?

Based on knok jobradar data from July 2026, PM salaries range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate level, to 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with three to six years of experience, and 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs. Group or Principal PM roles go up to 55-90+ LPA. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and negotiation. For self-reported salary data from current and former employees, Glassdoor is a useful reference.

What kinds of products would I own as a PM at Hotelogix?

Hotelogix's core product areas include the PMS (front desk, reservations, billing), the channel manager (OTA sync, rate management), and the online booking engine (direct bookings from the hotel's own website). As a PM, you might own one of these areas or a cross-cutting focus like mobile, onboarding, or integrations. The role typically involves close collaboration with hotel clients, customer success, and engineering teams.

How should I prepare for a product case study at Hotelogix?

Practice cases grounded in the hospitality context. Take a real Hotelogix feature like the room calendar or channel manager and ask yourself: who uses this, what is their biggest frustration, what would you change and why, and how would you measure success. Candidates report that interviewers appreciate structured thinking over perfect answers. Use a clear user-first structure: name the user, state the problem, propose a solution, then define success metrics.

Is Hotelogix a good place to grow a PM career?

Hotelogix is an established SaaS product company with a global customer base, giving PMs exposure to real B2B challenges including enterprise sales cycles, integration complexity, and multi-market localization. For PMs who want deep hospitality domain knowledge combined with SaaS fundamentals, it can be a strong foundation. Growth depends on team size and business trajectory, so ask directly about PM career laddering and team structure during your interview rounds.
