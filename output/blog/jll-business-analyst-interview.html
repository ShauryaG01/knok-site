01 Overview

Overview

JLL (Jones Lang LaSalle) is one of the world's largest commercial real estate services companies, with a major presence across Indian cities. Their India operations span workplace solutions, project and development services, property advisory, and a growing technology and analytics function. Business Analysts at JLL typically sit within one of these verticals, so your interview will test both analytical thinking and comfort with real estate concepts.

As of July 2026, JLL has 404 open roles tracked on the knok jobradar snapshot. Across India, the broader Business Analyst market shows 398 openings, with Bangalore leading at 53, Delhi at 48, Mumbai at 24, Hyderabad at 21, Pune at 21, and Chennai at 16.

Candidates report the process typically runs two to four rounds: an HR screen, one or two analytical or case-based rounds, and a final discussion with a hiring manager or business head. Timelines vary by team. Compensation benchmarks are not available in this dataset; check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for current salary figures before your offer discussion.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in JLL BA interviews, based on what candidates report publicly:

  1. Walk me through a time you turned raw data into a recommendation that changed a business decision.
  2. How would you structure an analysis to help a corporate client decide whether to consolidate their office space?
  3. JLL serves both landlords and tenants. How do you handle a situation where two stakeholders have conflicting interests?
  4. Describe your hands-on experience with Excel or SQL. Give a specific example of a model or query you built and what business question it answered.
  5. How do you prioritise when three deliverables are due on the same day for different internal clients?
  6. Tell me about a project where your initial analysis turned out to be wrong. What did you do next?
  7. JLL works with large multinational clients. How do you adjust your communication style when presenting to a C-suite audience versus a project manager?
  8. What do you know about the commercial real estate market in India right now? Which trends are you watching?
  9. Describe a process you improved in a previous role. How did you measure whether it actually worked?
  10. How do you handle an ambiguous brief where the client has not clearly defined what they want?
  11. Give an example of working cross-functionally with Finance, IT, or Operations to deliver a BA output.
  12. Why JLL, and why a Business Analyst role in real estate services specifically?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioural questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Here are three worked examples.

Q: Walk me through a time you turned raw data into a recommendation that changed a business decision.

*Situation:* My team was reviewing vendor contracts and had a large spreadsheet of invoices but no clear picture of which vendors were costing the most relative to value delivered.

*Task:* I was asked to prepare a spend analysis for a leadership review. I realised the real question behind the brief was whether we should consolidate vendors.

*Action:* I cleaned the data, built a pivot table grouping spend by vendor category, and layered in service quality scores from internal feedback forms. I flagged three vendors where spend was high but satisfaction scores were below the team average.

*Result:* Leadership used the analysis to exit two vendor contracts and negotiate better terms on a third. My team lead said it was the first time the data had been presented in a way that made the decision obvious.

---

Q: Tell me about a project where your initial analysis turned out to be wrong. What did you do?

*Situation:* I was supporting a client engagement where we were modelling space utilisation for a large corporate office. My initial model suggested the client could reduce their footprint significantly.

*Task:* I presented a draft to my manager before the client call.

*Action:* My manager pointed out I had averaged occupancy across all days, but the client's peak days, typically Monday and Thursday, ran much higher. I rebuilt the model segmenting by day of week and added a buffer for planned headcount growth.

*Result:* The revised recommendation was more conservative and the client accepted it. More importantly, I now always check for time-based variation before finalising any utilisation model.

---

Q: Describe a process you improved in a previous role. How did you measure whether it worked?

*Situation:* The monthly reporting cycle in my previous team took four to five days because data was being pulled manually from three different systems.

*Task:* I volunteered to redesign the process after the third consecutive month of last-minute delays.

*Action:* I mapped the full workflow, identified the two steps that consumed the most time, and built a semi-automated template in Excel that merged standardised exports from each system. I also documented the steps so any team member could run it independently.

*Result:* The cycle came down to under two days. My manager later used this as a case study when onboarding new joiners.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions, use STAR every time: open with the Situation in one or two sentences, state your specific Task or responsibility, describe the Actions you personally took (use 'I', not 'we'), and close with a concrete Result. If you do not have a metric for the result, describe the qualitative impact clearly.

For case or analytical questions, structure your thinking before you speak. A simple approach: restate the business question in your own words, name the data you would need, describe the analysis you would run, and explain how you would present the output. JLL interviewers are typically more interested in your reasoning process than in a perfect answer, so narrate your thinking out loud.

For 'why JLL' questions, be specific. Generic answers like 'global brand' or 'great culture' will not stand out. Connect JLL's actual service lines to your background. If you have worked with workplace data or corporate real estate clients, say so directly.

For stakeholder conflict questions, show that you understand JLL's position as a service provider. Acknowledge both parties' interests, explain how you would escalate if needed, and avoid taking sides in your answer.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

JLL BA interviewers are typically looking for four things.

Structured thinking under pressure. Real estate advisory is deadline-driven. They want to see that you can organise your thoughts quickly, ask the right clarifying questions, and deliver a coherent output even with incomplete information.

Data fluency, not just tool knowledge. Saying you know Excel or Power BI is not enough. Interviewers want to hear how you have used data to change a decision. Specific examples carry far more weight than a list of software names.

Client orientation. JLL is a client-facing business. Even internal BA roles interact with senior stakeholders. Candidates who can simplify complexity for a non-technical audience consistently score better than those who lead with technical depth alone.

Curiosity about real estate. You do not need to be a sector expert to get the job, but you need to show genuine interest. Read up on office market trends in your target city, understand what Grade A office space means, and know roughly what is happening with flex and co-working demand in India. Candidates who arrive with no sector awareness are commonly screened out at early rounds.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your story bank. List six to eight situations from your past work or projects that cover: solving a problem with data, handling a difficult stakeholder, fixing a broken process, working under pressure, and recovering from a mistake. Write out the STAR structure for each. This is the raw material for almost every behavioural question.

Week 1: Sector basics. Spend a few hours reading about the Indian commercial real estate market. Understand the difference between Grade A, B, and C office space. Know which cities are seeing demand growth. JLL India publishes research reports that are publicly available and give you real talking points for the interview.

Week 2: Technical prep. If the job description mentions SQL, Excel, Power BI, or any other tool, do one or two practice exercises that you can describe in detail. You do not need to be perfect, but you need a concrete example for each tool you claim to know.

Week 2: Mock interviews. Practice answering questions out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself answering question 1 and question 7 from the list above. Check whether you are staying structured and whether your answers are under three minutes each.

Before the interview: Research the specific JLL team you are joining. Facility management, capital markets, and technology teams have different priorities. Tailor your examples to match the work that team actually does.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague answers. The most common failure in JLL BA interviews is describing what the team did rather than what you personally did. Interviewers are assessing your contribution. Use 'I' deliberately and consistently.

Ignoring the real estate context. Candidates who answer every question in a generic way, as though they are applying to any BA role anywhere, miss an easy opportunity. JLL wants people who are genuinely curious about property and place. Even one sector-specific observation per answer helps.

Over-spending time on the Situation. In STAR answers, most candidates spend too long on the Situation and not long enough on the Action and Result. Keep Situation and Task to two sentences combined, and give the rest of your time to what you did and what changed.

Not preparing questions to ask. At the end of most rounds you will be invited to ask questions. Candidates who say 'I think you have covered everything' signal low curiosity. Prepare two to three genuine questions about the team's current projects, how success is measured in the role, or how the BA function interacts with client-facing teams.

Raising salary too early. Candidates report that bringing up compensation in the first round creates a poor impression at JLL. Let the process develop first. When you do negotiate, use Glassdoor data or industry surveys to anchor your ask rather than a personal number alone.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the JLL Business Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically runs two to four rounds. You will usually have an initial HR screen, followed by one or two rounds focused on analytical thinking or case problems, and a final round with a hiring manager or senior business leader. The exact structure varies by team and city, so ask the recruiter at the start of the process what to expect.

Does JLL ask SQL or coding questions in BA interviews?

It depends on the team. Candidates applying to JLL's technology, data, or analytics teams report more SQL and tool-specific questions. Candidates in property advisory or workplace solutions report fewer. Read the job description carefully and prepare at least one concrete SQL or Excel example you can walk through, since analytical tool questions come up even in rounds that are not labelled as technical.

What salary can I expect as a Business Analyst at JLL in India?

This dataset does not include salary band data for JLL BA roles. Check Glassdoor and community discussions on LinkedIn for figures reported by JLL employees in India. Levels.fyi may also have relevant data points if the role sits within JLL's technology function. Cross-check multiple sources before entering any salary negotiation.

Do I need a real estate background to get a BA role at JLL?

A real estate background is not mandatory. Many BA hires at JLL come from consulting, banking, or general analytics backgrounds. What matters more is that you show genuine curiosity about the sector during the interview. Spending a few hours reading JLL's publicly available India research reports will put you ahead of most candidates who arrive with no sector awareness.

Which cities have the most BA openings in India right now?

Based on the knok jobradar snapshot from July 2026, Bangalore leads the Business Analyst market at 53 openings and Delhi follows at 48. Mumbai has 24 openings, and Hyderabad and Pune each have 21. Chennai has 16. These are market-wide counts across all companies, not JLL-specific numbers.

How should I prepare for the 'why JLL' question?

Avoid generic answers about global brand or company culture. Connect JLL's specific service lines to your background and career goals. If you have experience with corporate real estate data or workplace analytics, mention how JLL's advisory work interests you. Candidates who reference an actual JLL service line or a recent India market report consistently stand out more than those giving polished but vague answers.
