03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you debugged a critical production issue under time pressure.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a payment processing service started throwing errors late one night and disbursements to customers were failing.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and had to find the root cause and restore service before the morning business window opened.

*Action:* I pulled the logs, spotted a database connection pool exhaustion caused by a query that was not releasing connections properly after a recent deployment. I rolled back the specific service, confirmed the pool stabilised, then wrote a hotfix with proper connection handling and tested it in staging before pushing to production.

*Result:* Service was restored before the morning window opened, no customers were permanently affected, and we added a connection-pool alert to our monitoring so this class of issue would page the team earlier next time.

---

Q: How would you design and secure a REST API handling sensitive customer financial data?

*Situation:* At a previous role, we were building a loan status API that both customers and third-party agents would query.

*Task:* I was responsible for the API security layer, including authentication, authorisation, and data masking.

*Action:* I implemented OAuth 2.0 with short-lived JWT tokens, added role-based access control so agents could only see their own customers' data, enforced HTTPS with TLS, masked sensitive fields like PAN and account numbers in responses (showing only the last four digits), and added rate limiting to block brute-force attempts.

*Result:* The API passed the security audit on the first review, and the rate limiting blocked a spike of suspicious calls within the first month of go-live.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked with a non-technical team to deliver a feature.

*Situation:* Our compliance team needed a new document-checklist feature before a regulatory deadline, but their requirements kept changing mid-development.

*Task:* I was the lead developer and the main point of contact between engineering and compliance.

*Action:* I set up brief weekly syncs with the compliance officer, translated requirements into a plain-language checklist before writing any code, and shared mockups for sign-off at each stage. I also stored the checklist as a configurable table in the database rather than hardcoding the rules, so compliance could update them without a new deployment.

*Result:* We shipped well ahead of the deadline, and compliance made several rounds of rule changes post-launch without any engineering involvement, clearing a backlog of small-change tickets that would have otherwise occupied the team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For coding and algorithm questions, walk through your thinking before writing any code. State the brute-force approach first, estimate its time and space complexity, then explain your improvement. HDFC Credila's systems handle high transaction volumes, so showing you think about performance from the start signals the right instincts.

For system design questions, use a clear structure: clarify requirements and scale, sketch the high-level components, then go deeper on what the interviewer probes. For financial systems, always address data consistency (transactions, ACID properties), failure handling (retries, idempotency), and security (auth, encryption at rest and in transit) even if not explicitly asked.

For behavioural questions, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your answer on Action (what you personally did) and make the Result concrete. Candidates report that interviewers here appreciate personal ownership over shared team credit.

For regulation and compliance questions, you do not need to be a legal expert. Acknowledge that you understand the 'why' behind controls (protecting customer data, preventing fraud, meeting RBI norms) and give one concrete example of how that understanding shaped a real technical decision you made.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain awareness, not just algorithmic brilliance. HDFC Credila is an NBFC, not a product startup. Interviewers typically want engineers who understand that financial data is sensitive, transactions must be consistent, and compliance is non-negotiable. Showing this awareness signals you will need less hand-holding on the job.

Ownership and reliability under pressure. Candidates report that interviewers probe for examples where you took responsibility for a problem end-to-end, especially under pressure. Stories about fixing production issues, handling difficult stakeholders, or shipping under a hard deadline carry more weight than theoretical knowledge alone.

Clean, readable, testable code. In a fintech environment, maintainability matters as much as cleverness. Expect at least one round that checks whether your code is well-structured and whether you write tests for edge cases like zero amounts, duplicate requests, or null inputs.

Communication with non-technical stakeholders. Software teams at NBFCs work closely with operations, credit, and compliance. Interviewers look for engineers who can translate technical constraints into plain language and who ask clarifying questions before jumping into solutions.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical revision
Revise data structures, SQL query optimisation (indexes, joins, execution plans), and concurrency concepts (locks, transactions, ACID). Practice several medium-level coding problems focused on arrays, strings, and database queries, since these come up most in fintech screening rounds.

Week 2: System design for financial products
Study how to design systems like a payment gateway, a loan origination engine, or a notification service. Focus on idempotency, retry logic, distributed transactions, and API security. Read about how NBFC and banking platforms handle data consistency at scale.

Week 3: Behavioural prep and company research
Write down a handful of strong STAR stories covering: a production incident you resolved, a time you collaborated with a non-technical team, a feature you built from scratch, and a time you pushed back on a poor technical decision. Read HDFC Credila's public website and education loan product pages so you can answer 'why this company?' with real substance.

Week 4: Mock interviews and final polish
Do a couple of full mock interviews, one coding and one system design. Record yourself answering behavioural questions and check whether your answers are concise. Review your resume and be ready to go deep on every line item you mention.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'why' in system design. Candidates who jump straight to 'I would use microservices' without explaining trade-offs signal that they follow trends rather than think critically. Always connect your design choices to the specific constraints of a financial system: consistency, compliance, and auditability.

Ignoring error and edge cases in coding rounds. For a company handling loan disbursements, code with no handling for null inputs, duplicate requests, or partial failures raises red flags about production reliability. Always ask 'what happens if this fails midway?' and address it in your solution.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'we delivered the project on time' without naming a specific action you personally took is a common trap. Interviewers at financial companies typically want to know exactly what you did, not what the team collectively achieved.

Not asking clarifying questions. Jumping into a system design problem without confirming scale, SLA expectations, or compliance requirements suggests you build first and ask questions later. In a regulated environment, unstated assumptions can be costly.

Arriving unprepared on domain basics. Not knowing what an NBFC is, or being unable to explain a simple loan lifecycle, signals that you applied without researching the company. A small amount of reading on education lending goes a long way in making a strong first impression.

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does HDFC Credila typically have for Software Engineers?

Candidates report the process typically has three to four stages: an initial technical screening, one or two coding and system design rounds, and a final managerial or HR discussion. The exact number varies based on the seniority of the role. Confirm the structure with the recruiter at the start so you can prepare the right material for each stage.

Is domain knowledge of banking or NBFCs required before the interview?

You do not need deep regulatory expertise, but basic awareness helps. Understand what an NBFC does, how a loan lifecycle works (origination, disbursement, repayment), and why data security and transaction consistency matter in financial software. Even spending time on HDFC Credila's product pages before the interview gives you enough context to answer 'why this company?' convincingly.

What tech stack does HDFC Credila use?

HDFC Credila has not publicly listed its full engineering stack. Candidates report Java and Spring Boot coming up in technical discussions, along with relational databases and REST API design. Prepare in the backend language you know best, but be comfortable discussing Java if the job description mentions it. Always check the specific posting for stack hints before your screen.

How should I handle questions about working with sensitive financial data?

Lead with the principle: customer financial data must be protected at rest and in transit, and access must be role-based and auditable. Follow up with a concrete example from your experience, such as masking PAN numbers in logs, implementing encryption, or adding audit trails. If you lack direct fintech experience, explain how you would apply these principles from first principles and show that you understand the regulatory motivation behind them.
