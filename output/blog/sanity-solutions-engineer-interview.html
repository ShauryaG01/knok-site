01 Overview

Overview

Sanity is a headless content platform built around three ideas: a flexible document store called Content Lake, the GROQ query language for querying that store, and Sanity Studio, a fully customisable editing UI your team can extend with React components. A Solutions Engineer (SE) at Sanity sits at the intersection of technical pre-sales and consulting. You run live demos, scope implementations, write proof-of-concept code, and help enterprise buyers understand how Sanity fits their existing stack before they sign.

Candidates report a process that typically runs four to five stages. It usually starts with a recruiter screen, then a hiring manager conversation about your background in technical pre-sales or developer tooling. After that, candidates typically face a technical exercise, often a short take-home or a live session using the Sanity SDK and GROQ. A demo or role-play round follows, where you present Sanity to a mock customer. A final cross-functional panel typically closes the process.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 26 open roles at Sanity across its teams. Across all companies in India, Solutions Engineer demand was strongest in Bangalore (55 open roles), Mumbai (23), and Delhi (20), with additional openings in Pune (12) and Hyderabad (6).

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from Sanity SE interviews. Study the platform docs, practice a live demo, and have a migration or scoping story ready before your technical round.

  1. Walk us through how you would demo Sanity Studio to a developer who has never used a headless CMS before.
  2. A prospect's engineering team is already using Contentful. How would you make the case for switching to Sanity?
  3. Explain GROQ to a non-technical stakeholder in under two minutes.
  4. A customer is experiencing slow query performance on their Content Lake. How do you troubleshoot it?
  5. How would you scope a Sanity implementation for a media company with a large editorial team?
  6. Describe how you would structure a proof-of-concept for an e-commerce client who needs real-time product content.
  7. A developer tells you that Sanity's structured content model is too rigid for their use case. How do you respond?
  8. Walk us through the differences between Sanity's pricing tiers from a business value perspective, without just reciting the pricing page.
  9. How do you handle a situation where a prospect's requirements are genuinely a poor fit for Sanity?
  10. Describe how you collaborate with an Account Executive during a large enterprise deal.
  11. A customer needs to migrate a large volume of documents from WordPress to Sanity. What is your approach?
  12. How do you stay current with the Sanity ecosystem as it evolves quickly?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would demo Sanity Studio to a developer who has never used a headless CMS before.

*Situation:* In a previous role, I was supporting a pre-sales cycle with a mid-size fintech company. Their developers had only worked with WordPress and were skeptical about moving to a headless setup.

*Task:* I needed to run a focused demo that made the value tangible, not just theoretical, for an audience that cared about API responses and deployment pipelines, not marketing copy.

*Action:* I built a small custom Studio schema the night before, modeled on the content objects their team described in the discovery call. During the demo, I walked through creating a document in Studio, queried it live using GROQ in the Vision plugin, and then fetched the same data via their preferred frontend framework. I paused at each step and invited the lead developer to suggest schema changes, so they could see how fast iteration actually works.

*Result:* The developer said 'this is just JSON with a nice UI, which is exactly what we wanted.' The deal moved to a paid proof-of-concept the following week.

---

Q: How do you handle a situation where a prospect's requirements are genuinely a poor fit for Sanity?

*Situation:* A prospect came in needing a CMS that could also handle inventory management and order processing, not just content.

*Task:* My job was to be honest about fit without burning the relationship, since the company was also evaluating other tools our team partnered with.

*Action:* I acknowledged clearly that Sanity is purpose-built for content, not commerce transactions. I outlined which parts of their requirements Sanity handles well (product descriptions, landing pages, editorial workflows) and which parts would need a dedicated commerce layer. I then offered to walk them through a reference architecture combining Sanity with a commerce platform, so they could see a real integration pattern in action.

*Result:* The prospect appreciated the transparency. They moved forward with a hybrid setup where Sanity served as the content layer. Candidates who oversell Sanity into poor-fit situations typically lose the deal anyway, and they damage trust in the process.

---

Q: Describe how you would scope a Sanity implementation for a media company with a large editorial team.

*Situation:* A digital media client with multiple editorial brands approached us. Their team included journalists, editors, and SEO specialists, each needing different workflows inside the same CMS.

*Task:* I had to scope the implementation before a commercial proposal could go out, which meant turning vague requirements into concrete schema design, role configuration, and integration points.

*Action:* I ran a two-session discovery process. The first session was with editorial stakeholders to map content types, publishing workflows, and metadata requirements. The second was with their engineering team to understand their frontend stack, CI/CD setup, and existing data sources we needed to connect. From those sessions I produced a scoping document covering document schema, custom Studio input components, role-based access design, and a phased rollout plan starting with one brand.

*Result:* The scoping document became the foundation of the implementation statement of work. The client told us later that the phased approach reduced their internal training load significantly, and the first brand launched with minimal rework.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical product questions (GROQ, schema design, APIs): Start by confirming what the interviewer actually wants to test. Then explain the concept plainly, give a concrete example from your experience, and connect it to customer value. Avoid reciting documentation; show that you understand when and why you would use a feature, not just how.

For 'how would you handle' scenarios: Use a three-part structure: first, the information you would gather (discovery); second, the options you would evaluate; third, how you would communicate the outcome to both technical and business stakeholders. Interviewers at product-led companies like Sanity want to see that you think in customer outcomes, not just technical solutions.

For competitive questions (Contentful, Strapi, Prismic, etc.): Lead with honest strengths of the competitor, then explain Sanity's differentiation on specific axes: the flexibility of Content Lake, the programmability of Studio, the GROQ query model. The best SEs can explain trade-offs clearly enough that customers trust them to give unbiased advice.

For collaboration and process questions: Be specific about your working relationship with Account Executives, Customer Success, and Product. Sanity is a product-led company, so candidates who describe clear feedback loops from customers back to the product team tend to stand out.

For 'poor fit' or objection scenarios: Show that you can distinguish between a real objection (a genuine product gap) and a concern (a misunderstanding about what Sanity can do). Real objections deserve honest acknowledgment. Concerns deserve a demo or a reference architecture.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Sanity interviewers are typically looking for a combination that is harder to find than it sounds: genuine technical depth in modern JavaScript and TypeScript stacks, comfort with headless and composable architecture patterns, and the ability to communicate that depth clearly to a VP of Engineering and a CMO in the same conversation.

Technical fluency: You should be comfortable reading and writing GROQ queries, understand how Content Lake stores and serves documents, and be able to set up a basic Sanity Studio schema from scratch. Candidates who have only watched demo videos tend to struggle in live technical exercises.

Customer empathy: Sanity sells to developers and to business buyers. The SE role requires switching registers fluently. Interviewers will probe whether you can run a deeply technical session and then translate the outcome into business language for a procurement stakeholder.

Honesty about fit: Sanity has a strong product identity. Interviewers are wary of SEs who promise the moon to close a deal, because poor-fit customers churn and hurt the product's reputation in the developer community.

Collaborative instinct: The SE role at Sanity is not a solo position. Expect questions that test how you work with AEs, how you hand off to implementation partners, and how you loop customer feedback back to the product team.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Platform depth
Build something real with Sanity. Set up a free project, design a multi-type schema, query it with GROQ in the Vision plugin, and connect it to a Next.js or SvelteKit frontend. Read the official docs on Content Lake, document IDs, references, and portable text. Note where you get confused, because those are the gaps your interviewers will probe.

Week 2: Discovery and demo practice
Practice running a focused demo out loud, ideally to a friend or colleague who can ask awkward questions mid-demo. Prepare a discovery question set you would use in a first technical call. Map out how Sanity compares with Contentful, Strapi, and Prismic for common customer scenarios such as media, e-commerce, and SaaS documentation.

Week 3: Story preparation and role-play
Prepare three to four stories from past experience using the STAR format: one about a complex scoping situation, one about a competitive deal, one about a customer who was initially resistant, and one about a deal you lost and what you learned. Practice the demo role-play with someone acting as a skeptical CTO.

Before the interview: Review Sanity's recent product announcements and blog. Know whether they have launched any new Studio plugins, Content Lake features, or integrations recently. Sanity is developer-community-first, so showing you follow their changelog signals genuine interest rather than opportunistic job-seeking.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating GROQ as optional knowledge. Many candidates arrive having only used REST or GraphQL. If you cannot write a basic GROQ projection or filter in a live exercise, it signals you have not used the product seriously. Spend real time with GROQ before your technical round.

Pitching features instead of solving problems. Sanity Studio's customisability is genuinely impressive, but listing features in a demo without tying each one to a customer pain point is a common mistake. Every feature you show should answer a question the customer actually asked.

Underselling the content model. Candidates often focus entirely on the Studio UI and skip explaining why structured content (portable text, references, typed schemas) matters for omnichannel delivery. This is a core Sanity differentiator that many candidates overlook.

Being vague about past deal cycles. When asked about a deal you worked on, interviewers want specific details: what the customer's stack was, what the objections were, what you built for the proof-of-concept. Vague stories signal limited real experience.

Overselling into poor fits. Sanity interviewers typically ask at least one 'this is a bad fit' scenario. Candidates who try to spin every requirement into a Sanity win tend to fail this question. Being honest about product limitations while proposing a composable architecture that includes Sanity where it fits is the correct approach.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What is the typical interview process at Sanity for a Solutions Engineer?

Candidates report a process that typically runs four to five stages. It usually starts with a recruiter screen, moves to a hiring manager conversation about your background, then a technical round involving a GROQ exercise or short take-home, followed by a demo or role-play round where you present Sanity to a mock customer. A final cross-functional panel typically closes the process. Timelines and exact stage names vary, so confirm the structure with your recruiter early.

Do I need to know GROQ before applying?

You do not need to be an expert before applying, but you should be comfortable with GROQ basics before your technical round. Most candidates who reach the later stages have built at least one project using the Sanity SDK and run real queries in the Vision plugin. Arriving with only a surface-level understanding of the query language is a common stumbling block that interviewers notice quickly.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer role at Sanity in India?

Sanity has not publicly disclosed India-specific compensation bands. For current benchmarks, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for Solutions Engineer roles at similar-stage SaaS companies in the developer tooling space. Compensation at product-led growth companies commonly includes a base plus a variable component tied to bookings, so ask your recruiter to break down the on-target earnings structure clearly before you negotiate.

How important is prior CMS or headless architecture experience?

It is helpful but not always required. Candidates who come from adjacent roles such as developer relations, technical consulting, or frontend engineering and can pick up the Sanity platform quickly tend to do well. What matters more is your ability to run a technical discovery conversation, design a proof-of-concept, and communicate trade-offs clearly to both technical and business audiences. If you have no headless CMS background, build a real Sanity project before applying so you have something concrete to discuss.

What is the best way to stand out in a Sanity Solutions Engineer interview?

Build something with Sanity before your first technical conversation, not after. Candidates who arrive with a real schema they designed, a GROQ query they wrote, and a genuine opinion about a trade-off in the Studio configuration immediately signal hands-on depth. Equally important is showing that you understand the difference between a customer concern (a misunderstanding about the product) and a real product gap, since Sanity values honesty about fit as much as it values the ability to close a deal.
