01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Navi is one of India's fastest-growing fintech companies, founded by Sachin Bansal after he co-founded Flipkart. It offers personal loans, home loans, health insurance, and mutual funds, all through a mobile-first app built entirely in-house. Unlike older financial institutions, Navi treats technology as the core product, not a supporting layer, and that shapes everything about how they hire.

As of July 2026, Navi has around 60 open roles spanning engineering, product, data, operations, credit, and business functions. Most hiring is out of Bengaluru, though some roles support hybrid or remote arrangements. The company is in active growth mode, so roles open and close quickly and the hiring bar is consistently high.

If you are coming from a traditional bank or NBFC, expect a genuine culture shift. The pace is closer to a startup than a large corporate. If you are coming from another fintech or a product-first company, the adjustment is smaller, but the expectations are equally demanding. Navi tends to favour people who are comfortable with ambiguity, care about the end customer, and back their decisions with data.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Navi's interview process typically runs three to five rounds, varying by role and seniority. The stages below reflect what candidates commonly report, so treat them as a likely path rather than a guaranteed sequence.

Application and initial screening. The recruiter reviews your resume for a basic fit with the job description. Most shortlisted candidates hear back fairly quickly. A focused resume that speaks directly to the role, rather than a generic one, helps at this stage.

Recruiter or hiring manager call. A short introductory conversation covering your career background, what drew you to fintech and Navi specifically, and a rough check on compensation expectations. Be ready to explain your career story clearly and show genuine familiarity with the company and what it is building.

Technical or domain round. For engineering roles, this is usually a coding interview covering data structures, algorithms, and sometimes an introductory system design problem. For product roles, expect a case study or take-home assignment, often built around a fintech scenario. Data and analytics candidates are typically tested on SQL, statistical thinking, and experimental design. Business and operations candidates may face a structured problem-solving exercise.

Deeper technical or cross-functional round. Not every role includes this, but senior engineering and product candidates commonly face a second technical session. System design discussions at Navi often focus on building scalable financial systems, loan origination flows, or fraud detection pipelines.

Leadership interview. A conversation with a senior leader, sometimes a VP or a founder. The focus shifts from skills to thinking style. How do you handle situations where data is incomplete? How do you push back on a decision you disagree with? How do you prioritise when everything feels urgent? Answers grounded in specific past examples land far better than abstract ones.

Offer and negotiation. If all rounds go well, HR moves to an offer relatively quickly. Compensation discussions happen at this stage, so have your expectations and market data ready before you reach this point.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Navi consistently looks for a handful of qualities across most roles.

Ownership mindset. Navi is still building core systems, which means people who wait to be told what to do tend to struggle. They want candidates who spot a problem, design a solution, and see it through. In interviews, stories where you drove something to completion without being pushed will matter more than stories where you simply executed someone else's plan.

Data fluency. Even non-technical roles at Navi are expected to work with numbers. Whether you are in product, credit, or operations, being able to interpret a dashboard, read an experiment result, or frame a business question in data terms is close to a baseline expectation. For technical roles, this standard is even higher.

Fintech domain understanding. You do not need a finance degree, but you should understand how lending, insurance, or investments work from a customer perspective. Candidates who have thought about credit risk, loan underwriting, or what financial inclusion actually means for a first-time borrower tend to stand out.

Communication clarity. Navi moves fast and teams are lean. The ability to express a complex idea simply, whether in a document or in a meeting, is valued at every level.

Customer obsession. Navi's end customers are often first-time borrowers or first-time investors, people who may not have had easy access to financial products before. Candidates who connect their work to that customer impact tend to resonate more than those focused purely on internal metrics.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product before you apply. Download the Navi app and actually use it. Read their blog, study how they position their products against competitors, and if you are applying to a credit or compliance role, look at what is publicly available from their regulatory filings. Generic interview answers are easy to spot.

For engineering roles, sharpen your fundamentals in data structures and algorithms. LeetCode medium-level problems are a commonly cited benchmark for interviews at product-focused fintechs. Brush up on system design, especially around payment flows, loan origination systems, or fraud detection. Reliability and scale should be front of mind.

For product roles, practise fintech-specific case studies. Be comfortable framing problems as: what metric are we trying to move, what are the levers, how do we test it, and how do we measure success. Have a few strong examples of products or features you have shipped, backed by clear outcomes where possible.

For data and analytics roles, practise SQL, make sure you understand A/B testing end to end, and be ready to talk about how you have used data to change a business decision. Knowing the basics of credit scoring or risk modelling is a genuine plus.

Prepare your 'why Navi' answer. This comes up in almost every first round. Starting with 'Sachin Bansal is impressive' is fine as context but not sufficient on its own. Connect it to something specific: the problem they are solving, the market they are targeting, or a customer need you genuinely care about.

Apply across multiple roles if your background fits more than one. Navi currently has around 60 open roles across different functions. If you fit two or three of them, applying to each separately increases your chances. Recruiters handle different verticals, so there is no downside to multiple applications.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Navi operates like a startup even as it scales into a sizeable financial institution, and that tension shapes the culture in a few visible ways.

Speed is a core value, not just a tagline. Decisions get made faster than at traditional financial companies. That is energising for people who dislike bureaucracy, but it can feel overwhelming if you prefer more structured environments. New hires often say the first few months are a steep learning curve simply because of the pace.

The team skews technical and data-driven. Many senior leaders come from engineering or analytics backgrounds, which means data-backed arguments tend to carry more weight than seniority-based ones. If you can support your idea with evidence, you will be heard regardless of how long you have been at the company.

Reasonably flat for a company its size. Navi is not a small startup, but access to senior leadership is more real here than at most large organisations. People at early career stages report being able to present their work directly to leadership when the work merits it.

Financial inclusion is a genuine thread, not just marketing. Navi has extended credit and insurance to customer segments that larger banks often pass over. If you care about building products for Bharat and not just metro India, that purpose is present in day-to-day work.

Work hours are demanding. This comes up consistently in candidate and employee reviews. Navi is not a nine-to-five environment, and most people who work there acknowledge it openly. If work-life balance is a top priority for you, ask directly about team norms during interviews rather than assuming things will be different from what reviewers describe.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does Navi's hiring process usually take from application to offer?

Candidates commonly report the full process spanning several weeks, with the timeline depending on the role, how quickly rounds get scheduled, and how fast the team is moving at that point. Engineering roles with multiple technical rounds can take longer than business or operations roles. Being responsive and proactive with the recruiter helps move things along. If you have not heard back after a reasonable amount of time, a polite follow-up to the recruiter is usually appropriate.

Does Navi hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

Navi does hire freshers, particularly for engineering and data roles, often through campus placements or by converting strong interns into full-time hires. However, the majority of their current 60 open roles target candidates with prior work experience. If you are a fresher, look specifically for roles tagged as associate, junior, or trainee level, and emphasise any relevant project work or internship experience in fintech or product companies.

What is Navi's salary like compared to the market?

Specific LPA figures vary by role and seniority level, and publicly reported data on Navi compensation is limited given the company's stage. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have data points shared by candidates who received offers, and those are the most reliable benchmarks before you negotiate. Navi is generally competitive for a fintech of its size, though its compensation structures may differ from those at large, established tech companies.

Is there a referral programme at Navi, and does it actually help?

Most mid-to-large Indian companies, including fintech firms like Navi, run internal referral programmes. A referral typically gets your resume seen faster and sometimes helps you bypass initial screening. If you know someone at Navi, asking them to refer you is a legitimate and commonly used approach. If you do not have a direct contact, a respectful and specific LinkedIn message to a current employee can sometimes lead to an informal referral.

Which teams at Navi are hiring the most right now?

Based on Navi's current 60 open roles, hiring appears active across engineering, data, product, and credit functions. The specific mix shifts month to month as the business scales different areas. Checking their careers page regularly or setting up job alerts is the most reliable way to track which teams are actively adding headcount at any given time.

What are common reasons candidates get rejected at Navi?

From what candidates publicly share, frequent rejection points include a weak technical round where fundamentals are shaky, generic answers in the 'why Navi' conversation that show little real knowledge of the company, and compensation expectations that surface late in the process and create a mismatch. Preparing your fintech knowledge, practising your technical concepts, and discussing salary expectations early in the process reduce the risk across all of these areas.
