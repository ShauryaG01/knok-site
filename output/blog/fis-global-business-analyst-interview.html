01 Overview

Overview

FIS Global is one of the largest financial technology companies in the world, building software and infrastructure for banks, payment processors, capital markets firms, and insurers. In India, FIS runs significant engineering and business analysis teams supporting global product lines including Worldpay, core banking platforms, and regulatory reporting tools.

FIS currently has 55 Business Analyst openings listed across India, drawn from a wider market of 398 BA roles tracked by knok as of July 2026. Bangalore leads overall BA hiring with 53 openings, and Delhi follows with 48. FIS roles span payments, core banking, capital markets, and compliance domains.

Candidates who have interviewed at FIS typically report two to four rounds. Early rounds tend to cover your resume, domain background, and basic requirement-gathering concepts. Later rounds go deeper into stakeholder scenarios, process analysis, and sometimes a short case study or data exercise. A final HR or leadership discussion on compensation expectations and career goals is also common.

Coming prepared with knowledge of FIS's products and some familiarity with payment processing or banking workflows gives you a clear edge over candidates who treat this as a generic BA interview.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

FIS interviewers typically focus on three areas: domain knowledge in fintech and banking, core BA skills such as requirement gathering and documentation, and behavioural questions about stakeholder management and past project experience. Based on candidate reports, these are among the most commonly asked questions:

  1. Walk me through how you typically gather requirements from business stakeholders.
  2. How do you handle a situation where two stakeholders give you conflicting requirements?
  3. What do you know about FIS Global, and which of its products or business lines interests you most?
  4. Describe a time you improved a business process. What changed, and what was the result?
  5. How do you write user stories, and what makes a good acceptance criterion?
  6. Give an example of a time you explained a complex technical concept to a non-technical audience.
  7. How do you manage scope creep during a project?
  8. Have you worked with payment processing, core banking, or regulatory reporting? Walk us through your experience in detail.
  9. What tools do you use for process mapping, documentation, and data analysis?
  10. Describe a time a project you were part of did not go as planned. What did you do?
  11. How do you prioritize requirements when multiple business teams have competing demands?
  12. How do you verify that a delivered solution actually solves the original business problem?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for every behavioural question: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep answers focused and tie them to a clear business outcome. Here are three examples tailored to questions FIS candidates commonly face.

Q: How do you handle conflicting requirements from different stakeholders?

*Situation:* I was assigned to a digital onboarding project where the compliance team wanted a lengthy KYC form, but the product team wanted a short, user-friendly flow to reduce drop-offs.

*Task:* My job was to reach a requirements baseline that both teams could sign off on before development started.

*Action:* I scheduled a joint workshop with both stakeholders, mapped out each team's non-negotiable items versus preferences, and used a prioritization matrix to show where there was genuine conflict versus where teams had assumed conflict. I proposed a phased approach: capture mandatory KYC fields upfront, and move optional data collection to a secondary screen post-onboarding.

*Result:* Both teams agreed, the requirements document was signed off within the week, and the phased approach was later credited with reducing onboarding drop-off, as noted in an internal product review.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved a business process.

*Situation:* Our team was manually reconciling transaction reports from two systems every morning, which took several hours and introduced frequent errors.

*Task:* I was asked to document the current process and identify where automation or simplification was possible.

*Action:* I mapped the end-to-end process using a swimlane diagram, identified three redundant manual steps, and worked with the dev team to define requirements for an automated reconciliation script. I also rewrote the exception-handling workflow so errors were flagged directly to the right person instead of going through a shared inbox.

*Result:* The automated process cut reconciliation time significantly, reduced manual errors, and freed the operations team for higher-value work. The business sponsor cited the project as a model for future process improvement initiatives.

---

Q: How do you ensure a delivered solution actually meets the original business need?

*Situation:* On a payments reporting project, the dev team delivered a feature that technically matched the BRD but did not reflect how the finance team actually used the data in practice.

*Task:* I needed to bridge the gap between what was built and what the business actually needed, without a costly rework cycle.

*Action:* I ran a structured UAT session with the finance team using real-world scenarios rather than scripted test cases, documented the gaps, and categorised them by severity. I then worked with the PM and dev team to prioritise fixes and negotiate a phased acceptance.

*Result:* The critical gaps were resolved before go-live, and the remaining items were logged as a clear backlog for the next sprint. The finance team signed off with confidence, and post-launch issues were minimal.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the backbone of any FIS behavioural interview. Every answer should end with a measurable or observable result, not just 'it went well.' If you do not have a precise metric, describe a concrete change: a process that no longer exists, a team that no longer raises a certain complaint, a sign-off that previously took weeks and now takes days.

The 'so what' test. Before you finish any answer, ask yourself: so what? Why does this matter to FIS? If your action saved time in a payment reconciliation workflow or reduced rework in a requirements cycle, say that explicitly. Interviewers at a fintech company want to see that you think in terms of business impact, not just task completion.

Domain bridge. For questions about your past experience, always draw a connection to FIS's context. If you worked in retail banking, connect it to FIS's core banking products. If you worked in e-commerce payments, connect it to Worldpay. Interviewers are evaluating whether your background translates to their environment.

Clarify before answering case-style questions. If you are given a scenario or a case study, ask one or two clarifying questions before jumping in. This shows structured thinking and is a behaviour FIS interviewers look for in BAs who will work directly with clients and internal product teams.

Honest hedging on domain gaps. If you are asked about a domain you have limited experience in, such as capital markets or regulatory reporting, do not bluff. State what you know, acknowledge the gap briefly, and describe how you would close it. Interviewers generally respond well to this approach.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

FIS BA interviewers are typically looking for a combination of fintech domain knowledge, structured analytical thinking, and the ability to work across business and technology teams. Based on what candidates commonly report, here is what matters most.

Domain credibility. FIS operates in payments, banking, capital markets, and insurance. You do not need deep expertise in all four, but you should be able to speak fluently about at least one. Knowing the difference between a payment gateway and a payment processor, or understanding what a core banking system does, signals that you will not need hand-holding on domain fundamentals.

Requirement clarity. Can you turn an ambiguous business request into a precise, testable requirement? Interviewers often probe this with scenario questions: 'The client says they want better reporting. What do you do next?' They want to see that you ask the right follow-up questions and document clearly.

Stakeholder navigation. FIS BAs routinely work with compliance teams, technology leads, product owners, and client-facing teams simultaneously. Interviewers want evidence that you can manage conflict, build consensus, and communicate clearly across these groups.

Process orientation. Comfort with tools like JIRA, Confluence, Visio, or well-structured Excel documents is a plus. More important than any specific tool is the habit of documenting decisions, tracking open items, and maintaining a clear audit trail.

Curiosity about the product. Candidates who have explored FIS's website, read about Worldpay, or noticed recent news about FIS's business direction consistently stand out. This signals genuine interest rather than a spray-and-pray job search.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Research FIS Global (days 1-2). Read FIS's website and focus on their three main segments: Banking Solutions, Merchant Solutions (Worldpay), and Capital Market Solutions. Note two or three products you could reference in your interview. Scan recent news about FIS to understand their current business direction.

Step 2: Map your experience to FIS's domain (days 2-3). List your past BA projects and identify which ones connect most clearly to payments, banking, or financial services. For each project, prepare a STAR story covering the problem, your specific contribution, and the outcome. If your background is not in fintech, focus on transferable skills: data analysis, requirement gathering, stakeholder management.

Step 3: Practise the most-asked questions (days 3-5). Work through the twelve questions listed above. Record yourself or practise with a friend. Check that every answer has a clear result, not just a description of what you did. Aim for focused, complete answers that do not trail off.

Step 4: Refresh your tools knowledge (days 4-5). Brush up on any tools mentioned in the job description. If the role mentions SQL, practise basic queries. If it mentions JIRA, be ready to describe how you have used it to manage backlogs or track requirements.

Step 5: Prepare your questions (day 5). Arrive with two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer. Good options include: 'What does a typical BA engagement look like end-to-end at FIS?', 'How is success measured for a BA in this team?', or 'What is the biggest challenge the team is working through right now?'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Not researching FIS's products. Candidates who cannot name a single FIS product or explain what the company does leave a poor impression. Spend time understanding Worldpay, core banking platforms, and FIS's regulatory reporting tools before the interview.

Giving vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved the process' without explaining what changed, how you could tell it changed, and what the business impact was will not satisfy an FIS interviewer. Practise concrete, specific answers with observable outcomes.

Exaggerating domain experience. If an interviewer probes your claimed expertise in payments or banking and you cannot answer follow-up questions, credibility drops fast. Be honest about what you know and frame gaps as areas you are actively working to learn. Interviewers at FIS tend to ask pointed follow-up questions, so surface-level claims rarely hold up.

Skipping the 'so what'. Describing a situation without connecting it to a business outcome misses the point of a BA interview. Always close your answer with the impact: time saved, errors eliminated, stakeholder alignment achieved, or a decision unblocked.

Ignoring stakeholder conflict questions. FIS BAs work across technology, compliance, and business teams. Candidates who only talk about technical skills and skip soft-skills examples often struggle in later rounds where the focus shifts to cross-functional collaboration.

Forgetting to ask questions. Arriving with no questions for the interviewer signals low engagement. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions about the team's current projects or how success is measured in the role.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the FIS Global BA interview typically have?

Candidates typically report two to four rounds. The first round is usually a screening call covering your resume and basic domain knowledge. Subsequent rounds go deeper into requirement-gathering scenarios, case studies, or data exercises. A final HR discussion on compensation and career fit is common. Round count can vary by level and business unit, so treat this as a general pattern rather than a guarantee.

Does FIS Global ask technical questions in the BA interview?

It depends on the role and team. Some FIS BA roles, particularly those supporting technology or data products, include basic SQL queries or data interpretation exercises. Others focus entirely on business analysis skills such as requirement gathering, process mapping, and stakeholder management. Read the job description carefully: if it mentions SQL, Excel modelling, or data tools, prepare for a light technical component.

What domain knowledge does FIS look for in a Business Analyst?

FIS operates across payments, core banking, capital markets, and insurance. You are not expected to know all four deeply, but comfort with at least one is important. The most commonly probed areas are payment processing concepts (how a transaction flows from merchant to bank), core banking workflows, and regulatory or compliance reporting. If your background is not in fintech, focus on showing how your existing domain experience translates and demonstrate genuine curiosity about FIS's business.

Is the FIS BA interview difficult compared to other fintech companies?

Candidates generally rate FIS BA interviews as moderate in difficulty. The domain questions can be specific if the role is tied to a particular product area like Worldpay or capital markets. The behavioural questions follow a fairly standard STAR format. The main differentiator candidates mention is the depth of product knowledge FIS expects: knowing what FIS actually does, not just what a BA does in general, matters more here than at some other companies.
