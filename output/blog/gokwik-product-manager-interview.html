01 Overview

Overview

GoKwik is one of India's leading e-commerce checkout and RTO (Return to Origin) reduction platforms, powering the checkout experience for hundreds of D2C brands. The PM team works across checkout UX, buyer intelligence, merchant analytics, COD-to-prepaid conversion, and post-purchase flows. Roles demand strong product sense, comfort with e-commerce metrics, and the ability to serve two distinct users at once: the merchant (their B2B paying customer) and the shopper (the end consumer).

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in GoKwik PM interviews, based on candidate reports and the nature of their core products. Prepare a sharp, structured answer for each.

  1. Checkout metric design. GoKwik's core promise is better checkout conversion. How would you define and measure the success of a new one-click checkout feature?
  1. RTO reduction roadmap. D2C merchants lose a significant share of revenue to returns. How would you prioritise a set of features aimed at reducing RTO?
  1. COD-to-prepaid conversion. Many Indian shoppers default to COD. How would you design a feature that nudges them toward prepaid without damaging trust or dropping overall conversion?
  1. Two-sided product tension. GoKwik serves both merchants and shoppers. Walk us through a scenario where their needs conflict and how you would resolve it as a PM.
  1. Buyer risk scoring. How would you build a feature or model that predicts at checkout whether a buyer is likely to return the order?
  1. Conversion drop diagnosis. A merchant reports that their checkout conversion rate has dropped noticeably after a recent GoKwik platform update. How do you investigate and fix this?
  1. Payment method prioritisation. GoKwik wants to add a new payment method to the checkout flow. How do you decide which one to launch first?
  1. Competitive positioning. Other checkout products exist in the Indian market. How would you position GoKwik to win a large D2C brand that is currently evaluating options?
  1. Post-purchase expansion. GoKwik is known for checkout. How would you build a business case for expanding into post-purchase experience, such as returns management or buyer loyalty?
  1. A/B testing design. Describe how you would design and run an A/B test for a new checkout UI change across GoKwik's merchant network.
  1. Enterprise customisation. A high-GMV enterprise merchant requests a deeply custom checkout integration that does not fit the standard product. How do you handle this as a PM?
  1. Merchant health dashboard. How would you design a dashboard that helps a merchant understand the health of their checkout funnel on GoKwik?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure your answers. These three examples are tailored to GoKwik's domain.

Q: How have you used data to improve a checkout or payment flow?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a significant share of mobile shoppers were abandoning the checkout page after seeing the payment selection screen.

*Task:* I owned the checkout experience and was asked to diagnose the drop-off and propose a fix within one sprint cycle.

*Action:* I pulled funnel data and found that the payment screen showed a large number of visible options, which created choice paralysis on small screens. I spoke with several merchants and surveyed a sample of end users. Based on the findings, I proposed showing only the top payment methods by default (UPI, saved card, COD) with a 'show more' option. I worked with engineering to ship a configurable layout and set up a controlled A/B test.

*Result:* The test showed a lift in payment-step completion within our sample. I documented the learnings and handed off a rollout plan, which was later adopted as the default layout for new merchant onboarding.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to balance merchant needs against shopper experience.

*Situation:* A merchant wanted to remove the 'Cancel Order' button from the post-purchase page to reduce cancellations and protect their revenue.

*Task:* I had to evaluate the request against GoKwik's principle of building shopper trust, which drives long-term conversion rates.

*Action:* I analysed cancellation data and found that most cancellations happened within a short window after placing the order, often because the shopper had placed a duplicate order by mistake. I proposed keeping the cancel button but adding a short cooldown window and a confirmation step that surfaced the duplicate-order scenario. I aligned the merchant by showing that shopper trust metrics (repeat purchase rate, satisfaction scores) correlate with brand-level conversion over time.

*Result:* The merchant agreed to the compromise. Cancellation volume fell in our sample while shopper satisfaction held steady. The approach became a template for handling similar merchant escalations.

---

Q: Describe a time you identified a product metric that was misleading the team.

*Situation:* Our team was treating a rise in 'checkout page visits' as a sign that merchant integrations were performing well, even as GMV growth remained flat.

*Task:* As the PM responsible for growth metrics, I needed to investigate the disconnect.

*Action:* I found that a large share of the new 'visits' were bots and repeat test transactions from our QA environment that had not been filtered out. I worked with the data team to add environment filters and bot-detection tags, then proposed a cleaner north-star metric: 'unique buyer sessions reaching payment confirmation.'

*Result:* The new metric gave the team a more honest picture of growth and revealed two merchant segments that were genuinely underperforming. Those segments became the focus of the next quarter's growth sprint.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

CIRCLES (for product design questions). When asked to design a feature, such as a new checkout element or a merchant dashboard, use CIRCLES: Comprehend the situation, Identify the user, Report user needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate tradeoffs, Summarise. GoKwik interviewers appreciate structured thinking over rushed solutions.

HEART plus GSM (for metric questions). For any 'how would you measure success' question, start with user-level goals (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success) and tie them to GoKwik's business metrics: GMV, checkout conversion rate, RTO rate, and COD-to-prepaid shift. Always link your product metric to a business outcome.

Root Cause Tree (for diagnosis questions). When a metric drops, work top-down: external factors (platform outage, competitor promotion), internal factors (recent code change, config error), then segment-level factors (specific merchant, device type, city). State your hypothesis before pulling data, not after.

Now / Next / Later (for roadmap prioritisation). Organise features into what delivers value immediately (Now), what requires some build-up (Next), and what is a longer-term bet (Later). Tie each tier to a measurable outcome, such as RTO reduction or merchant retention rate.

Competitor framing. When discussing differentiation, anchor on three questions: 'What does the merchant care most about? Where does GoKwik have a structural advantage, such as its buyer data network or RTO intelligence? What would switching cost the merchant?' This shows commercial awareness without being vague.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep comfort with e-commerce and D2C metrics. Checkout conversion rate, RTO percentage, COD share, repeat purchase rate, and GMV are the working language of GoKwik's business. Interviewers quickly spot whether you are guessing at these terms or have genuinely worked with them.

Merchant empathy alongside shopper empathy. GoKwik's paying customers are merchants (B2B), but the end users are shoppers (B2C). Strong PM candidates can hold both perspectives and articulate when they conflict and how to navigate that tension.

Data-first instinct. Almost every question, even open-ended design ones, has a data angle. Candidates who jump to solutions without anchoring in metrics or a clear hypothesis are typically flagged.

Clarity in ambiguous situations. GoKwik moves fast. Interviewers want to see that you can define scope quickly, make a call with incomplete information, and communicate your reasoning to both technical and business stakeholders.

Demonstrated ownership. Candidates report that interviewers probe for examples where you drove something end-to-end, including handling pushback from merchants, engineering constraints, or conflicting business priorities. 'We did X as a team' is weaker than 'I drove X by doing Y.'

Awareness of the Indian e-commerce context. Understanding why COD remains widely used, how RTO economics affect D2C margins, and how UPI has changed payment behaviour signals that you genuinely understand the market GoKwik operates in.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product and market.
Experience GoKwik as a shopper and study their publicly available case studies and product updates. Map their product suite: checkout, RTO reduction, buyer intelligence, and any post-purchase offerings. Understand the RTO problem from first principles, including why it happens, who bears the cost, and how technology can reduce it.

Week 2: Build your metric vocabulary.
Practise explaining checkout funnel metrics out loud: what defines success at each step, how you would segment the data, and what could cause a sudden drop. When you need market benchmarks, draw on publicly reported figures from industry surveys and cite your source explicitly.

Week 3: Practise structured answers.
Take the 12 questions in this guide and write out STAR answers for at least five of them. Time yourself. Aim for tight answers with room for the interviewer to push back, as GoKwik PM rounds are typically conversational rather than monologue-style.

Week 4: Mock interviews and refinement.
Do at least two mock interviews with a peer or mentor who can challenge your assumptions. Practise the metric-drop diagnosis question specifically, as candidates report it appears in almost every GoKwik PM loop. Replace any vague 'we improved metrics' statements with specific, clearly sourced claims.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating GoKwik as a consumer app. GoKwik is a B2B2C platform. Candidates who frame every problem purely from the shopper perspective miss the merchant layer entirely, which is GoKwik's actual paying customer.

Vague metric answers. Saying 'I would track conversion rate' without specifying which step, which segment, and which time window reads as surface-level. GoKwik interviewers consistently dig into the detail.

Skipping the 'why' behind a feature request. When a merchant requests a feature, jumping straight to build vs. delay without first asking 'what outcome is the merchant trying to achieve?' is a common trap. Always clarify the job-to-be-done.

Over-indexing on Western frameworks. JTBD and RICE are useful, but applying them rigidly without adapting to the Indian context (COD behaviour, logistics last-mile, online payment trust gaps) can come across as textbook rather than practical.

Claiming ownership you cannot defend. If you say 'I launched X and it improved Y,' be ready for follow-ups like 'What was the exact test design?' or 'How did you isolate your change from other variables?' Interviewers probe the depth of ownership consistently.

Not asking clarifying questions. Candidates who dive into an answer without scoping the problem are seen as poor collaborators. A short 'Can I clarify the user and context first?' signals product maturity.
