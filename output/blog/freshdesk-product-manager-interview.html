01 Overview

Overview

Freshdesk is Freshworks' flagship customer support product, used by businesses of all sizes to manage tickets, automate workflows, and serve customers across email, chat, phone, and social channels. Freshworks is a B2B SaaS company with strong product and engineering teams in India, primarily in Chennai and Bangalore.

As of July 2026, there are 23 open PM roles at Freshworks, making this an active hiring cycle worth targeting. The interview process typically covers product sense, metrics and analytics, execution, and behavioural rounds. Candidates report a total of 4-6 rounds, often ending with a discussion with a senior leader or the hiring manager's skip-level. The B2B focus is central: interviewers pay close attention to how you think about business customers, because support agents, team leads, and CX managers are your primary users, not end consumers.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report that Freshdesk PM interviews test both product thinking and B2B domain intuition. These are the questions that come up most often:

  1. How would you improve the Freshdesk ticketing interface for a mid-size enterprise support team? Tests your ability to identify pain points for business users rather than end consumers.
  1. Freshdesk is launching a mobile app for support agents. How do you decide what goes into v1? Scope definition and ruthless prioritisation under real constraints.
  1. Walk me through how you would define the north star metric for Freshdesk. Tests your understanding of B2B SaaS metrics: are you thinking about agent productivity, CSAT, or ticket deflection rate?
  1. A key support metric such as CSAT or first-response rate has been declining for three months. Diagnose the root cause. A classic metrics question where structured, MECE thinking wins.
  1. A large enterprise customer is threatening to churn because a feature they need is not on your roadmap. How do you handle this? Tests stakeholder management, prioritisation, and customer empathy all at once.
  1. How would you prioritise between improving the core ticketing flow and building new AI-powered automation features? Expect follow-ups on how you weigh short-term retention against long-term product positioning.
  1. Freshdesk competes with Zendesk. Where do you see the biggest product opportunity for Freshdesk over the next two years? Competitive analysis and strategic thinking, grounded in the 2024-2026 market context.
  1. Design an onboarding flow for a new support team lead using Freshdesk for the first time. Classic product design question: go deep on user research, user flows, and success metrics.
  1. Your engineering team says a feature you committed to a customer will take three times longer than originally planned. What do you do? Execution under pressure and cross-functional collaboration.
  1. How would you measure the success of a new AI ticket-routing feature six months after launch? Tests your ability to define both leading and lagging success metrics for a specific product initiative.
  1. Describe a time you used data to change a stakeholder's mind about a product direction. A behavioural question that doubles as a test of analytical credibility.
  1. Freshworks wants to expand Freshdesk's reach in Southeast Asia. What product changes would you propose? Strategy and localisation: think language support, compliance requirements, and local integration needs.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A key support metric has been declining for three consecutive months. Walk me through your diagnosis.

*Situation:* At a previous role, average first-response time for support tickets had been worsening steadily over a quarter, even though team size had not changed.

*Task:* I was asked to identify the root cause and propose a fix before the next quarterly business review.

*Action:* I segmented the metric by ticket source (email, chat, phone), by product area, by agent, and by time of day. The spike was concentrated in tickets arriving via a newly integrated third-party chat tool. After speaking with the support team lead, I found the integration was creating duplicate ticket entries, which confused agents and delayed responses. I worked with engineering to patch the integration and set up a daily monitoring dashboard to catch similar issues early.

*Result:* First-response time returned to its baseline within the following month. The dashboard caught two smaller integration issues the next quarter before they could affect metrics. The support team lead described this as one of the most useful operational changes that year.

---

Q: A large enterprise customer is threatening to churn because a feature they need is not on your roadmap. How do you handle it?

*Situation:* A significant enterprise client told our account manager they were evaluating a competitor primarily because we lacked a bulk-export feature for compliance reporting.

*Task:* As the PM owning that product area, I had to decide whether to prioritise this feature without derailing other commitments.

*Action:* I first validated whether this was a one-off request or a broader gap. I reviewed support tickets and spoke with a few other enterprise customers, confirming that several had raised the same need in different forms. This shifted the framing from a single customer's demand to a gap affecting our entire enterprise segment. I presented the evidence to leadership with a scoped proposal: a lightweight export feature that could ship in one sprint and satisfy the compliance requirement without building a full reporting suite. I was transparent with the customer about the timeline and what the initial version would and would not cover.

*Result:* The customer stayed, and the feature shipped on time. A couple of other enterprise accounts upgraded their plans shortly after launch, which the sales team attributed directly to the feature being available.

---

Q: Describe a time you used data to change a stakeholder's mind about a product direction.

*Situation:* A senior engineering leader at my company was convinced that the biggest drop-off in our setup flow was happening at the final confirmation step, based on gut feel from customer calls.

*Task:* I needed to confirm or challenge this assumption before the team committed sprint capacity to redesigning that step.

*Action:* I pulled funnel data from our analytics tool and found that the actual drop-off was concentrated three steps earlier, at the account configuration screen, not at the confirmation step. I built a short deck showing the funnel breakdown and overlaid it with session recordings that showed users getting confused by a specific field label. I presented this to the engineering lead and design team together, framing it as 'here is where the data points us' rather than as a correction.

*Result:* The team agreed to focus on the configuration screen. After copy and layout changes to that step, funnel completion improved measurably. The engineering lead later called this a good example of letting data drive scope decisions rather than assumptions.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design and improvement questions, start by naming the specific user: a support agent, team lead, CX manager, or enterprise IT admin. State what job they are trying to do, what success looks like for the business, and where the current experience falls short. Only then move to solutions. This prevents the common mistake of jumping straight into feature ideas without grounding them in actual user need.

For metrics and diagnosis questions, use MECE segmentation. When a metric drops, break it down: is the issue in volume, speed, quality, or a specific segment? Segment by channel, user type, time period, or geography. Rule out instrumentation errors first, then move to product or process causes. Freshdesk interviewers respond well to candidates who distinguish between leading indicators (agent login rate, ticket intake rate) and lagging indicators (CSAT, churn rate).

For prioritisation questions, make your framework explicit before applying it. A simple impact vs. effort grid is fine, but explain why you weighted the factors the way you did. For a B2B product like Freshdesk, enterprise customer retention, compliance blockers, and sales-blocking feature gaps often outweigh raw engagement improvements.

For behavioural questions, follow the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation to two or three sentences. Spend most of your time on the Action: what did you specifically do, what alternatives did you consider, and why did you choose this path? End with a concrete result, even if it is qualitative.

For competitive and strategy questions, show awareness of the B2B SaaS landscape in 2025-2026. Relevant context includes the rise of AI-driven support automation, the shift toward omnichannel CX, and the competitive pressure Freshdesk faces from larger platforms. Acknowledge tradeoffs rather than presenting a single 'right answer,' and anchor your view in customer or business impact.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

B2B product instinct. Freshdesk serves businesses, not consumers. Interviewers want to see you think in terms of workflows, team productivity, admin controls, and business outcomes. Mentioning 'support agents' or 'team leads' as your user is more credible than speaking generically about 'users.'

Customer empathy grounded in research. Strong candidates at Freshworks typically demonstrate they have spoken with actual business users: support team leads, CX managers, or enterprise buyers. If your background is in consumer products, prepare examples of how you would gather insights in a business context.

Data fluency with judgment. Interviewers want structured thinking around metrics, but also want to see judgment. A candidate who always says 'I would run an A/B test' without explaining the hypothesis or what a meaningful result looks like will not score well.

Clear prioritisation reasoning. In a multi-product company like Freshworks, PMs face requests from sales, enterprise customers, and multiple product lines simultaneously. Interviewers look for candidates who can say no clearly and back it with evidence.

Cross-functional collaboration. Candidates report that Freshworks places high value on how you work with engineering, design, and sales. Expect questions about times you navigated disagreement with an engineer or aligned a sales team around a product decision.

Intellectual honesty. Freshworks interviewers generally prefer candidates who say 'I am not sure, but here is how I would find out' over candidates who bluff through knowledge gaps. Demonstrating awareness of what you do not know is itself a signal of maturity.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product deeply. Sign up for Freshdesk's free trial and use it as a support team lead at a small company. File tickets, set up automations, and explore the reporting section. Read recent Freshworks blog posts and analyst commentary on the B2B customer support market in 2025-2026 to build your competitive context.

Week 2: Build your metrics vocabulary. Be fluent in support-specific metrics: CSAT, first-response time, resolution time, ticket deflection rate, and agent utilisation. Know the difference between a leading and lagging indicator for a support product. Practice diagnosing a metric decline using a MECE structure, out loud, before your interviews.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories. Select four to five stories from your experience covering: a product you improved using data, a stakeholder conflict you resolved, a prioritisation decision made under pressure, and a launch that did not go as planned. Write each story out in full STAR format and practice telling it in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and competitive prep. Do at least two timed mock sessions, each covering a product design question, a metrics question, and a behavioural question. Prepare a clear view on where Freshdesk stands relative to Zendesk: what each does well, where the gap is, and what you would build next if you joined the team.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Freshdesk like a consumer product. The most common mistake is describing solutions in terms of 'user delight' or 'engagement' without anchoring to business outcomes. Support teams use Freshdesk to hit SLA targets, reduce churn, and scale their operations. Frame everything in those terms.

Collapsing all users into one persona. 'The user' is not specific enough in a B2B context. A support agent, a team lead, an enterprise IT admin, and a business owner all use Freshdesk differently and care about different outcomes. Candidates who treat all users as one persona lose credibility quickly.

Vague metric answers. Saying 'I would monitor key metrics' without naming them is a common failure point. Know what CSAT, first-response time, and ticket deflection rate mean, and be ready to explain how they connect to business health and product decisions.

Over-explaining the framework before getting to the answer. Some candidates spend so long describing their approach that they never reach the substance. Freshworks interviewers typically respond better to candidates who name the framework briefly and then dive into the actual analysis.

Ignoring the competitive context. Freshworks operates in a competitive B2B SaaS market. Candidates who have no view on where Freshdesk sits relative to competitors miss the chance to show genuine product conviction. Even a simple, well-reasoned perspective sets you apart.

Weak STAR endings. Many candidates give strong Situation and Action sections but trail off on the Result. Always end with a concrete outcome, even if it is qualitative ('the customer renewed' or 'the feature shipped on time'). If the result was mixed, say so and add what you learned.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Freshdesk PM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the Freshworks PM process typically runs between 4 and 6 rounds. This usually includes an HR screening call, one or two product sense or case rounds, a metrics and execution round, and a final discussion with a senior leader. Round count can vary by team and seniority level, so confirm the format with your recruiter early in the process.

Does Freshworks give a take-home assignment for PM roles?

Some candidates report receiving a product case or take-home assignment, particularly for senior roles. Others report that all case questions are handled live during interviews. Whether you get one appears to depend on the specific team and hiring manager. Ask your recruiter during the first call so you can prepare accordingly.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Freshworks?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, PM salaries at the 3-6 year experience level are typically in the 24-40 LPA range, with Senior PM roles ranging from 40-60 LPA. Actual offers vary based on experience, the specific team, and negotiation. For more data points on Freshworks specifically, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi, keeping in mind that sample sizes on those platforms can be small for any one company.

Is prior B2B experience required for a Freshdesk PM role?

Prior B2B experience is helpful but is not typically a strict requirement. Interviewers consistently emphasise B2B product thinking over a specific background. If your experience is in consumer products, you can still compete by demonstrating that you understand business users, workflows, and enterprise buying behaviour. Prepare at least one example of how you have thought about or worked with business customers, even in a tangential context.

Which cities are Freshworks PM roles typically based in?

Freshworks runs most of its product and engineering work from offices in Chennai and Bangalore, so the majority of PM openings are typically in these two cities. If you are open to either location, your chances of finding a matching role increase. Some teams offer hybrid flexibility, though this varies by role and hiring manager.

How do I show genuine product conviction in the Freshdesk interview?

The most effective way is to use Freshdesk before your interview and form real opinions about what works well and what you would change. Interviewers can tell the difference between surface-level answers and candidates who have actually used the product. Come prepared with one specific improvement you would propose, a clear rationale for why it matters to the business, and a concrete way to measure its success.
