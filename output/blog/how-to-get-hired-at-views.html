03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Interview processes at growing companies tend to follow a predictable structure, though the exact number of rounds varies by role and seniority. Based on how companies at this scale commonly run hiring, here is a likely path for most VIEWS roles.

StageWhat happensFormat
1. Resume screenHR reviews your application for basic fit with the roleAsync
2. Recruiter callA short introductory call to confirm background and expectationsPhone or video
3. Skills roundA task, assignment, or live test relevant to the roleTake-home or live
4. Manager interviewDeeper questions about your experience and how you solve problemsVideo or in-person

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Growing companies with active hiring pipelines consistently look for three qualities before anything else: the ability to do the job, the drive to grow, and the temperament to work well with existing teams.

Relevant, demonstrable skills. Listing job titles is not enough. Interviewers at companies in a growth phase want to see concrete output: campaigns you ran, systems you built, revenue you contributed to, problems you resolved. Use figures from your own work history freely, because those are yours to cite.

Clear communication. Candidates who explain their thinking in simple, direct sentences consistently perform better than those who give dense or overly technical answers. Practice structuring your answers around what you did, why you did it, and what happened as a result.

Ownership and initiative. With 40 open roles being filled simultaneously, teams will form fast and direction will sometimes be unclear. Hiring managers value candidates who have identified a problem and taken action without waiting to be told, rather than those who hold back for detailed instructions.

Adaptability. Scaling companies change quickly. New tools get adopted, priorities shift, teams get restructured. Showing that you have navigated change well in the past is relevant regardless of the function you are applying for.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Match your resume to the job description. Read the listing carefully and reflect its language in your resume where it is accurate. If the description says 'client relationship management', use that phrase where it applies. Automated screening tools match keywords before a human ever reads your application.

Research VIEWS before every round. Look at their LinkedIn page, recent announcements, and any publicly available information about their products, customers, or business model. Knowing what the company actually does allows you to connect your experience to their real needs during the interview.

Prepare strong STAR stories. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. Most behavioural interview questions, whether 'tell me about a time you handled a difficult stakeholder' or 'describe a project that did not go as planned', are best answered with a structured story from your past. Prepare a few that show ownership, problem-solving, and impact from your own history.

Know your own numbers. Whatever field you work in, be ready to talk about impact in concrete terms: volume of work handled, improvements delivered, outcomes achieved. These are your own figures and you can cite them freely.

Prepare thoughtful questions to ask. Asking about team structure, what success looks like in the early months, or what the biggest current challenge is, shows genuine interest. It also helps you evaluate whether the role is actually right for you.

On the assignment round, prioritise quality. If there is a take-home task, read the brief carefully, ask a clarifying question if anything is ambiguous, and submit work that is clean and well-explained. A rushed submission rarely recovers.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Detailed public information about VIEWS's internal culture is limited, so the guidance below is about how to read culture signals during your interviews rather than a definitive description of what working there is like.

Signals from the interview itself. The questions you are asked reveal a lot. Questions about handling ambiguity suggest a fast-moving environment. Questions about cross-functional collaboration suggest that working across teams is common. Questions about how you prioritise under pressure suggest the workload is real. Pay attention to the conversational tone as much as the content.

Questions you should ask. Direct questions tend to give the clearest signal. Try asking: 'what does the team's day to day look like right now', 'how are decisions typically made here', and 'how does the company respond when something goes wrong'. Good interviewers will answer honestly, and evasive answers are themselves informative.

What scaling companies typically look like. With 40 roles being filled at once, VIEWS is building teams, not just filling seats. Companies in this phase tend to reward initiative and move quickly, but can also feel less structured than established firms. If you thrive with clear processes and defined responsibilities, ask specific questions about how work gets structured and how performance is measured.

Growth potential. Companies that hire aggressively often create new roles and team leads from within as they scale. If internal mobility and learning opportunities matter to you, ask for concrete examples of people who grew into bigger responsibilities after joining.

Frequently asked

How many jobs are open at VIEWS right now?

VIEWS has 40 open roles as of early July 2026. That is a sizeable pipeline and suggests the company is in an active expansion phase. Roles at this volume tend to fill and refresh frequently, so applying early and checking back regularly is the practical move.

What is the salary range at VIEWS?

Publicly reported salary data for VIEWS is hard to find and verify. For LPA benchmarks by role and experience level, Glassdoor and levels.fyi are the most commonly cited public sources for Indian companies. Cross-check across both before discussing your salary expectations in any interview round.

How long does the VIEWS hiring process take?

Most hiring processes at growing companies move from application to offer in a few weeks, though senior or technical roles can take longer because they involve more rounds or more decision-makers. If two weeks pass after any stage with no update, one polite follow-up email to HR is the right next step. Avoid following up more frequently than that.

Can I apply for more than one role at VIEWS at the same time?

Yes, if multiple roles genuinely match your background, applying to more than one is reasonable. Be upfront about it if the recruiter asks, framing it as serious interest in the company rather than a scattershot approach. Tailor each application separately to the specific role description rather than submitting one generic resume everywhere.

What should I do if my application gets no response?

Wait at least two weeks before following up, then send one short, polite email to HR asking for an update. If there is still no response, it usually means your resume did not match the specific requirements of that listing closely enough to pass screening. Review the job description carefully, update your resume to reflect its language, and look at other open roles at VIEWS that may be a closer fit to your background.

Does getting a referral from a VIEWS employee actually help?

A referral can sometimes get your resume reviewed faster, but it does not change the outcome of the actual interviews. If you connect with a VIEWS employee on LinkedIn, keep your message short and genuine, asking about the role or team rather than immediately asking for a referral. If a conversation develops naturally and they offer to refer you, that is valuable. A cold referral request with no prior interaction rarely works well.
