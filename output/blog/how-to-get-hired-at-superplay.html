01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

SuperPlay is a mobile gaming studio with a strong track record in casual and mid-core games. With 27 open roles listed as of July 2026, the company is actively hiring across game design, engineering, product, marketing, and live operations. Competition for these positions is real, but not out of reach for candidates who prepare with genuine intent.

Whether you are a fresher eager to break into the gaming industry or an experienced professional moving across from a different tech vertical, knowing how SuperPlay structures its hiring process helps you spend preparation time on the right things. This guide walks you through what to expect at each stage, what the team values, and how to give yourself the best shot.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at SuperPlay typically spans several stages, though the exact count varies by role and team. Here is what candidates commonly report:

Stage 1: Application and Resume Screen
Your resume goes through an initial filter, either via an ATS or a recruiter review. Engineering roles often require a skills match on languages like C# or Unity. Product and design roles look for a portfolio or evidence of shipped work. Tailor your resume to the specific job description rather than sending a generic one.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR Call
A short introductory call to confirm your background, notice period, and salary expectations. Be ready to explain why you want to work in mobile gaming and why SuperPlay specifically.

Stage 3: Technical or Portfolio Assessment
Engineering candidates commonly receive a take-home assignment or an online coding test. Game designers may be asked to review a SuperPlay title and suggest improvements, or complete a short design brief. This stage filters out a significant share of applicants, so treat it as seriously as a live interview.

Stage 4: Technical or Functional Interview
A deeper conversation with a hiring manager or a senior team member. Expect questions about past projects, problem-solving approach, and domain knowledge. Engineering candidates should be ready for live coding or system design questions depending on the seniority level.

Stage 5: Culture and Leadership Interview
A final round with a senior leader or a cross-functional panel. Questions here focus on how you collaborate, how you handle feedback, and how invested you are in the gaming industry long term.

Offer and Verification
If you clear all rounds, you typically receive a verbal offer followed by a written one. Background verification is standard practice.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

SuperPlay values people who genuinely play and enjoy games, not just build them. Beyond technical skills, a few traits come up repeatedly in candidate feedback:

Passion for mobile gaming. You do not need prior gaming industry experience, but you should be able to speak about games you play, what makes them engaging, and what you would change. Generic enthusiasm is easy to spot and rarely impresses a games-focused interviewer.

Data-driven thinking. Mobile gaming runs on metrics like retention, daily active users, and lifetime value. Candidates who can connect their decisions to measurable outcomes stand out, whether in product, marketing, or creative roles.

Ownership mindset. SuperPlay moves fast. The team favours people who drive things to completion rather than waiting to be told what to do next.

Cross-functional communication. Games are built by designers, engineers, artists, and marketers working in close coordination. The ability to communicate clearly across disciplines matters more here than in many enterprise tech companies.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A focused preparation plan makes a meaningful difference. Here is a practical approach:

Play their games first. Download and play SuperPlay titles before any interview round. Note what works well, what could be improved, and why certain design choices were probably made. This single step sets you apart from most applicants.

Sharpen role-specific skills. Engineers should be solid on the stack listed in the job description (commonly Unity or Unreal for game-facing roles), core data structures, and any platform requirements. Product and analytics candidates should be comfortable with A/B testing concepts and funnel analysis.

Prepare structured stories. For behavioural rounds, use the Situation, Task, Action, Result format. Pick examples that show ownership, navigating ambiguity, and cross-team collaboration.

Know your impact numbers. If you have shipped a feature or run a campaign before, know the results in measurable terms. Even modest figures signal that you track your own work.

Research compensation before the HR call. Look up current ranges on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for similar roles at comparable gaming companies. Walking in with a market-informed expectation puts you in a stronger negotiating position.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

SuperPlay operates with a lean, high-ownership culture common in gaming studios. Teams move fast, ship frequently, and iterate based on player data. The environment suits people who are curious, self-directed, and genuinely invested in the player experience.

Work-life balance in gaming can be uneven, particularly around launch cycles. Candidates who have worked in startups or other fast-paced product companies tend to adapt more easily. Publicly reported employee reviews suggest the internal culture is collaborative rather than cutthroat, with a shared focus on craft and quality.

Communication style at SuperPlay is direct. Feedback is given openly, and disagreement is expected to surface in meetings rather than in side conversations after the fact. If you prefer a more hierarchical or heavily process-driven environment, it is worth reflecting on whether this dynamic suits your working style.

For Indian professionals specifically, check whether the role is office-based, remote, or hybrid before applying, as the answer can vary significantly by function and seniority level.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does SuperPlay have right now?

As of July 2026, SuperPlay has 27 open roles across functions. The count changes frequently as gaming studios typically hire in bursts around product cycles. Check their careers page regularly and apply as soon as a relevant role goes live, because positions at competitive studios can close quickly once strong candidates apply.

Do I need a gaming industry background to get hired at SuperPlay?

Not necessarily. Many roles in engineering, analytics, and marketing are open to strong candidates from other industries. What matters more is demonstrating genuine interest in games and connecting your past experience clearly to the role you are applying for. Coming into the interview with informed opinions about SuperPlay's own titles goes a long way toward showing that interest is real.

How long does the SuperPlay hiring process usually take?

Candidates commonly report that the process spans several weeks from application to offer, though this varies by role and team availability. Engineering roles with multiple technical rounds can take longer than business or marketing roles. Staying responsive to recruiter messages and completing take-home assignments promptly keeps things moving on your end.

What salary should I expect from SuperPlay?

Compensation varies by role, seniority, and location. For current figures, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi using SuperPlay as the employer, as those platforms reflect self-reported data from actual candidates and employees. Going into the HR call with a market-researched number gives you a clearer anchor for the offer conversation rather than letting the recruiter set the range entirely.

How hard is the technical interview at SuperPlay?

Difficulty is relative to the role and seniority level. Engineering interviews at product-led gaming studios are typically calibrated closer to a strong product startup than to FAANG-style competitive rounds. The focus tends to be on practical problem-solving and domain knowledge rather than obscure algorithmic puzzles. Preparing with role-specific projects and real past work examples is usually more valuable than grinding hard competitive coding problems.

How do I stand out among many applicants?

Personalization is the clearest differentiator. A resume tailored to the job description, a cover note that references specific SuperPlay products, and interview answers grounded in the company's actual games all signal genuine interest. Generic applications are easy for recruiters to deprioritize, especially when a studio has 27 open roles attracting a high volume of inbound candidates.
