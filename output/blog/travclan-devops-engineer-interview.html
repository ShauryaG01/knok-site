01 Overview

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for every behavioural and scenario question. Keep each answer to about two minutes when spoken. Below are three worked examples you can adapt to your own experience.

---

Q: Walk me through how you set up a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices platform.

*Situation:* My previous team ran a B2B SaaS product with a dozen independent services, each deployed by hand. Releases consumed most of a Friday afternoon and we had rollbacks nearly every second week.

*Task:* I was asked to own the CI/CD redesign and get the team to automated, same-day deployments.

*Action:* I mapped each service to a GitHub Actions workflow that ran unit tests, built a Docker image, pushed to ECR, and triggered an ArgoCD sync to a staging namespace on EKS. I added a manual approval gate before production promotion and wrote a rollback runbook using ArgoCD history. I also set up Slack notifications so the whole team could see every deployment state change in real time.

*Result:* We moved from Friday afternoon deployments that blocked the whole team to automated releases on any working day. Rollbacks became rare and release anxiety dropped noticeably across the team.

---

Q: A critical service goes down at 2 AM. Walk me through your incident response.

*Situation:* At a previous role, our payment callback service went silent in the early hours of a Friday night. PagerDuty woke me up. Bookings were queuing and customers were not getting confirmations.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and needed to restore service, communicate status, and keep the team informed.

*Action:* I opened the monitoring dashboard first and saw memory usage had spiked to the limit on two pods just before the crash. I checked recent deploys in ArgoCD and found a change merged six hours earlier. I rolled back to the previous image in under three minutes. I sent a brief status update to the on-call Slack channel, then stayed on to confirm bookings were processing again. Once stable, I wrote a short incident summary covering the timeline, root cause, and a follow-up ticket to add memory limits and a pre-deploy load test.

*Result:* Service was restored quickly. The changes we shipped the following week prevented the same class of out-of-memory crash from happening again.

---

Q: How have you handled cloud cost optimisation at a growing startup?

*Situation:* At a Series A startup I joined, the cloud bill had grown faster than headcount. No one owned costs and rightsizing had never been done.

*Task:* I took on a two-week cost review alongside my regular responsibilities.

*Action:* I used the cloud provider cost explorer to identify the top five drivers. The biggest win was switching always-on dev and staging instances to scheduled stop/start using a serverless function, and converting steady-state production workloads from on-demand to reserved capacity. I also set up budget alerts so the team would know before a spike became a surprise at month end. I documented everything so the next engineer could repeat the process without starting from scratch.

*Result:* Monthly spend dropped meaningfully within the first two billing cycles. The alerting system has since caught unplanned spikes before they escalated.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For infrastructure design questions: Start with requirements (availability SLA, expected traffic, geography), then move to components (compute, networking, storage), then talk about failure modes. Interviewers want to see you think about 'what breaks' before they have to ask.

For incident response questions: Use a four-part structure: Detect (how you knew), Diagnose (how you found root cause), Resolve (what you did), Prevent (what you changed afterward). Candidates who skip the Prevent step typically get a follow-up question pushing them there anyway.

For tool-choice questions: State the requirement first, then explain why your chosen tool fits it better than the obvious alternative. Saying 'I picked Terraform because the team already knew HCL and the module registry saved us weeks of setup' is far stronger than simply listing Terraform as a skill.

For cost or performance questions: Anchor on a specific metric (latency, monthly spend, deployment frequency) before describing your action. Concrete numbers from your own work carry more weight than general statements, even if the numbers are modest.

For motivation or cultural fit questions: Connect your answer specifically to the B2B travel-tech domain. Mentioning the reliability demands of a booking platform or the complexity of supplier integrations signals you have done genuine research about the company.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

TravClan's DevOps panel is typically looking for engineers who can operate independently in a fast-moving startup, not someone who needs a large platform team around them.

Ownership over tools. They want evidence you have run production infrastructure end to end, including on-call rotations, post-mortems, and capacity planning. Writing pipelines as a task handed down by someone else is a much weaker signal.

Startup operating style. Travel platforms need to ship fast without breaking live bookings. Interviewers probe whether you can balance speed and reliability, and whether you can prioritise ruthlessly when there is not time to do everything perfectly.

Domain awareness. TravClan handles supplier APIs, agent-facing portals, and booking transactions. Candidates who connect infrastructure decisions to business impact tend to stand out. Explaining why a booking confirmation service needs higher availability than an internal analytics dashboard shows you think beyond pure engineering.

Practical cloud depth. Surface answers about Kubernetes are common. Candidates report that follow-up questions quickly go to specifics: how you handle persistent volumes, how you debug a CrashLoopBackOff, or how you manage cluster upgrades without downtime.

Clear communication under pressure. Incidents affect real bookings and real agents. Interviewers often include a crisis scenario to see whether you stay structured and communicate clearly when things are broken.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations and system design

Revisit core concepts you will be asked to explain out loud: Kubernetes architecture (control plane, scheduler, kubelet), CI/CD pipeline design, and Infrastructure as Code principles. For each topic, prepare a two-minute spoken explanation you could deliver without notes.

Pick one system design scenario relevant to travel tech, for example a high-availability booking API or a multi-tenant configuration management system, and diagram it. Practice talking through your design choices and where it would fail under load or partial outage.

Week 2: Hands-on and company research

Spin up a small project on AWS or GCP involving a CI/CD pipeline deploying a containerised service. Even a simple application deployed via GitHub Actions to EKS or GKE gives you a concrete example to reference in the interview room.

Review TravClan's public job descriptions carefully and note every tool mentioned. For each tool you have not used, spend 30 minutes on the official documentation so you can speak to it honestly rather than bluffing.

Interview week: Practice and logistics

Do at least two mock interviews with a peer, not just solo preparation. Prepare STAR answers for your three most impactful incidents or projects. Write down two or three sharp questions for the panel about on-call culture, deployment frequency, and how the team handles major incidents.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Listing tools without depth. Writing Kubernetes, Terraform, Ansible, and Prometheus on your resume and then giving surface-level answers is the pattern interviewers mention most often. Prepare to go two or three levels deep on at least three tools.

Skipping the 'why' in design questions. Saying 'I would use RDS with Multi-AZ' without explaining the availability or consistency reasoning signals you are reciting patterns rather than thinking through trade-offs.

Generic incident answers. Describing 'a time services went down' without specific details (which service, what the symptoms were, how long recovery took, what you changed afterward) makes the answer forgettable. Real details, even mundane ones, build credibility.

Ignoring cost and observability. Candidates who focus only on 'making it work' without touching monitoring, alerting, or cost controls often reveal a blind spot in follow-up questions. Weave these into every design answer without waiting to be asked.

Not asking questions. A DevOps interview is also your chance to evaluate on-call burden, team size, and the current state of the infrastructure. Candidates who ask nothing often come across as uninterested and also miss important signals about the role.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does TravClan typically have for a DevOps Engineer role?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds covering infrastructure, scripting, and system design, and a final round that may involve a hiring manager or culture conversation. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level. Always confirm the format with the recruiter upfront so you can focus your preparation on what matters most.

What cloud platform does TravClan primarily use?

TravClan has not publicly detailed its full cloud stack, so prepare broadly rather than assuming a single provider. AWS is the most common platform among Indian travel-tech startups, but be ready to discuss GCP or Azure if the question comes up. Demonstrating that your skills transfer across providers is a positive signal in a startup interview where the stack may still be evolving.

Is there a take-home assignment or coding task as part of the process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home task involving writing a Dockerfile, a CI/CD configuration, or a small Terraform or Helm module. Others report going straight to a live technical discussion. Prepare a clean, well-commented example of each format so you are ready for either. If you receive a task, treat documentation and security hygiene as part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.

What salary can a mid-level DevOps Engineer expect at TravClan?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level DevOps roles (3-5 years of experience) in India broadly run 15-28 LPA. Startup compensation often mixes base salary with equity, so ask specifically about the equity component and vesting schedule during the offer stage. Glassdoor and levels.fyi may have TravClan-specific data points worth checking before you negotiate.

How should I prepare for the system design round?

Focus on designs relevant to a travel booking context: high-availability APIs, event-driven booking confirmation flows, and multi-tenant infrastructure for travel agents. Practice drawing and explaining your design out loud, not just on paper. Interviewers want to see how you reason through failure modes and trade-offs, not just a clean diagram with no thought behind it.
