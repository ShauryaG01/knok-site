01 Overview

  1. How would you improve the onboarding experience for a first-time UPI user who is switching from cash?

  3. A key metric on the PhonePe app, say transaction success rate, has dropped noticeably over the past week. How do you diagnose and fix it?
  4. How would you prioritise features for the PhonePe merchant dashboard over the next quarter?

  10. How would you design a feature to increase repeat transactions among users who transact only once or twice a month?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you improve onboarding for a first-time UPI user?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a high drop-off rate at the bank-account linking step during onboarding for our payments feature.

*Task:* My goal was to reduce that drop-off and get more users to their first successful transaction within 24 hours of install.

*Action:* I ran a discovery sprint: user interviews with a group of first-time users in Tier-2 cities, session recordings, and a short survey to identify where confusion was highest. The biggest blocker was entering IFSC codes and understanding which account to link. I worked with design to replace manual entry with a bank search-and-select flow, added a short video tooltip in Hindi and Tamil, and introduced a small micro-transaction option so users felt safe completing the flow. I also coordinated with engineering to surface clear, plain-language error messages for failed linking attempts.

*Result:* Cohort data after six weeks showed a meaningful lift in 'first transaction within 24 hours.' Industry surveys and publicly reported case studies from similar fintech onboarding revamps commonly cite double-digit percentage point improvements in completion rates after simplifying bank-linking flows.

---

Q: A key metric has dropped. Walk me through your approach.

*Situation:* During a Monday morning review, our team noticed a drop in daily active transactions on our merchant-facing product.

*Task:* I was the PM on call and needed to identify the root cause quickly and coordinate a response.

*Action:* I started by segmenting the data: was the drop across all merchants or concentrated in a segment, region, or device type? I checked recent releases, third-party service health, and whether competitors had launched anything that weekend. The data pointed to a spike in payment failures for a specific bank integration. I looped in engineering to confirm using the bank API logs, then set up a response team with engineering, support, and the bank's technical contacts. We shipped a hotfix to surface better error messages so users could retry with a different payment method.

*Result:* Transactions recovered within hours. We also added a monitoring alert for bank-specific failure rates so similar issues would be caught faster going forward.

---

Q: Tell me about a product you built from idea to launch.

*Situation:* Our team noticed that many small merchants were not reconciling their daily collections because the existing report was too complex for users without accounting backgrounds.

*Task:* I was asked to design a simpler daily settlement summary that merchants could understand without finance expertise.

*Action:* I spent two weeks shadowing merchants in Pune and listening to support calls. I defined the core user need as: 'Did I get paid today, for which orders, and is anything pending?' I scoped an MVP as a single-screen daily summary with plain-language copy, a downloadable PDF, and a WhatsApp share option. I wrote the PRD, ran a design sprint, and worked with a small engineering pod to ship in six weeks.

*Result:* Merchants using the new summary raised fewer reconciliation support tickets. The feature was folded into the standard merchant onboarding flow.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for every behavioural question. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on Action, and always quantify the Result where you honestly can. If you cannot share real numbers, describe the direction and the mechanism of the improvement.

Root Cause Analysis tree for metric-drop questions: start at the total metric, then branch by segment (user type, geography, device, payment method), then by funnel step (entry, processing, confirmation). Eliminate branches with data before naming a root cause.

Product Design (CIRCLES-lite): Clarify the goal, Identify the user, list their pains, Prioritise one pain, propose Solutions, evaluate trade-offs, then summarise your recommendation. PhonePe interviewers want you to pick one user and go deep rather than list five vague ideas.

Prioritisation (RICE or ICE): For roadmap questions, score options on Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort. Always state your assumptions out loud. PhonePe values PMs who defend prioritisation with reasoning, not gut feel alone.

North Star plus guardrails: When asked how to measure success, name one North Star metric, then two or three guardrail metrics that prevent gaming the number. This shows systems thinking and signals PM maturity.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep product sense for the Indian market. PhonePe serves a very large and diverse user base, many of whom are first-generation smartphone or internet users. Interviewers want to see that you instinctively think about low-data environments, regional language needs, trust barriers with digital payments, and the needs of small merchants, not just urban power users.

Comfort with ambiguity. You will often get an open-ended prompt with no single right answer. Interviewers are evaluating your thinking process: do you clarify scope, define the user, use data, and reason through trade-offs? Jumping straight to a solution without framing the problem is a common red flag.

Analytical rigour. Expect at least one question where you dig into a metric, design an experiment, or explain how you would measure a feature. Practice talking through your logic step by step, not just your conclusion.

Ownership and execution mindset. PhonePe moves quickly. Candidates who talk about shipping iteratively, unblocking engineering, and making decisions with incomplete information tend to resonate more than those who describe lengthy planning cycles.

Cross-functional empathy. Show that you have worked closely with design, engineering, data, and business teams. Interviewers look for PMs who earn trust rather than simply coordinate between functions.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product inside out. Download and use the PhonePe app deeply across every major flow: UPI payments, recharges, insurance, mutual funds, and the merchant experience. Note what works well and what feels rough. Read the company blog and any public product updates from 2024-2026.

Week 2: Practice product sense. Take three PhonePe features and run the CIRCLES-lite framework on each. Practice out loud or with a peer. A strong product design answer should feel complete in roughly eight to ten minutes.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories. Write out five stories covering: a product you launched, a metric you improved, a failure you learned from, a cross-functional conflict you resolved, and a time you made a decision with incomplete data. Practise delivering each in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and research. Do at least two full mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Read publicly reported interview experiences on Glassdoor for additional question patterns. Study the UPI ecosystem, NPCI guidelines, and how PhonePe's merchant network compares to competitors.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating PhonePe like a generic tech company. Candidates who give answers centred on Western user personas or ignore the Bharat user segment tend to struggle. Always anchor your answers in the real Indian context, including Tier-2 and Tier-3 city users, small merchants, and first-time digital payment adopters.

Jumping to solutions too fast. The biggest signal interviewers watch for is whether you define the problem and the user before proposing features. Slow down, ask a clarifying question or two, and frame the problem explicitly before diving into ideas.

Using jargon without substance. Saying 'I would use data-driven decision making' without explaining what data, what analysis, and what decision framework sounds hollow. Be specific about which metrics you would look at and why.

Ignoring trade-offs. Every feature or prioritisation question has trade-offs. Candidates who present only the upside of their recommendation raise red flags. Acknowledge the risks and explain why you are accepting them.

Underestimating execution questions. Many candidates over-prepare for product design and under-prepare for questions about how they actually ship software: handling scope creep, working with engineering teams, managing stakeholder expectations during a delay.

Not asking clarifying questions. In real product work, ambiguity is normal. Asking one or two smart clarifying questions at the start of a product design question shows maturity and mirrors how good PMs actually work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the PhonePe PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report four to six rounds, though the exact structure varies by team and level. These commonly include a recruiter screening, one or two product sense rounds, an analytical or metrics round, a behavioural or leadership round, and sometimes a final conversation with a senior leader. Some teams also add a take-home case study between rounds.

Does PhonePe hire freshers or only experienced PMs?

PhonePe typically hires for Associate PM roles from top MBA programmes and occasionally runs APM programmes for freshers. Most open PM roles require two or more years of product or adjacent experience. If you are a fresher, focus on Associate PM or APM tracks and be ready to discuss product thinking through internships, side projects, or case competitions.

What salary can I expect as a PM at PhonePe?

Based on knok salary data, Associate PMs can expect broadly 12-20 LPA, mid-level PMs with three to six years of experience typically see 24-40 LPA, and Senior PMs fall in the 40-60 LPA range. Group PM and Principal PM roles can reach 55-90 LPA and above. These figures are directional and actual offers vary based on your background, the specific team, and your negotiation.

What domain knowledge should I study before a PhonePe PM interview?

Focus on three areas: how UPI works and how NPCI governs the ecosystem, the competitive landscape among PhonePe, Google Pay, and Paytm, and the specific products PhonePe is expanding such as insurance, mutual funds, and merchant solutions. You do not need deep finance expertise, but you should understand basic concepts like KYC, payment success rates, and how merchant settlement cycles work.

How important is data and SQL knowledge for PhonePe PM interviews?

You are unlikely to be asked to write raw SQL in most PM rounds, but you should be comfortable talking through how you would analyse data, which metrics you would track, and how you would design an A/B test. Some analytical rounds include a scenario where you are given a metrics drop and asked to walk through your reasoning step by step. Interviewers value structured thinking over specific tool knowledge.

How long does the PhonePe PM hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates publicly report the process typically runs between three and eight weeks from the first recruiter call to an offer, though timelines vary by team and how active hiring is at that moment. There are currently 64 open PM roles at PhonePe, which suggests hiring is active right now. Responding to interview requests quickly and following up politely after each round can help keep your candidacy moving.
