02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions Zensar BA candidates most commonly report encountering. Prepare structured answers for all of them before your interview.

  1. Walk me through your experience as a Business Analyst and how it maps to this role at Zensar.
  2. How do you gather and document requirements from stakeholders who have no technical background?
  3. Describe a time you had to manage conflicting priorities from multiple stakeholders on the same project.
  4. What tools have you used for requirement documentation and project tracking? Give a specific example of how you used one.
  5. Explain the difference between functional and non-functional requirements with a real example from your own work.
  6. How would you handle a situation where the client keeps changing requirements after the document is signed off?
  7. Have you worked on process improvement or gap analysis? Walk us through how you approached it from start to finish.
  8. Zensar works heavily with banking and manufacturing clients. How do you get up to speed in a domain you have not worked in before?
  9. How do you verify that what the development team delivered actually matches the original requirement?
  10. Describe your experience writing use cases, user stories, or a Business Requirements Document (BRD). Who owned it?
  11. Tell me about a time a project you were involved in went off-track. What did you do?
  12. How do you prioritise a backlog when the business team and the development team have strong and opposing views?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you gather requirements from stakeholders who have no technical background?

*Situation:* At my previous company, I was assigned to a retail banking project where the primary stakeholder was the branch operations head with no IT background whatsoever.

*Task:* I needed to document requirements for a new loan-processing workflow within three weeks so the development team could begin sprint planning.

*Action:* I scheduled short, focused workshops instead of long meetings, used whiteboard process maps and real examples from the stakeholder's daily routine rather than technical diagrams, and followed up each session with a plain-language summary email for sign-off.

*Result:* We completed the BRD two days ahead of schedule with no major change requests during UAT, and the stakeholder specifically praised the clarity of the documentation in the project review.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to manage conflicting priorities from multiple stakeholders.

*Situation:* On an ERP migration project, the finance team and the supply-chain team both wanted their module prioritised in the first release, but development capacity only allowed for one.

*Task:* I had to facilitate a decision without damaging relationships on either side.

*Action:* I built a simple priority matrix mapping each module against business impact and technical dependency, presented it in a joint meeting, and let the data drive the conversation rather than making the call myself.

*Result:* Both teams agreed that the supply-chain module had a harder dependency on the go-live date. The finance module was scheduled for release two. Both teams left feeling heard, and the project stayed on track.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a project went off-track. What did you do?

*Situation:* A regulatory reporting project I was on missed its midpoint milestone because a third-party data vendor was late delivering sample data files.

*Task:* I had to keep the rest of the team productive and revise the timeline without alarming the client.

*Action:* I immediately flagged the risk to the project manager, identified which downstream tasks could continue using mock data, drafted a revised milestone plan with a buffer, and set up a weekly check-in with the vendor to track their delivery.

*Result:* The team lost only one week instead of the projected three-week delay. The client appreciated the transparent communication, and we delivered the final report two days before the revised deadline.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions is the most useful structure for Zensar BA interviews. Every question that starts with 'tell me about a time' or 'describe a situation' calls for Situation, Task, Action, and Result in that order. Keep your Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences together) and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Gap analysis structure works well for process or domain questions. State the current state, identify what is missing or broken, describe the desired future state, and explain how you would close the gap. Zensar teams frequently work on process improvement engagements, so interviewers respond well to candidates who think in this structure.

MoSCoW prioritisation (Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have) is worth naming explicitly if you are asked how you prioritise requirements or a backlog. It signals that you use a recognised framework rather than gut feel.

Stakeholder mapping as a concept (classifying stakeholders by level of influence and level of interest) is a useful reference when answering questions about managing conflict or communicating with non-technical audiences. Mention it by name and briefly explain how you applied it in a past project.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Zensar BA interviewers typically look for four things.

Domain adaptability. Zensar serves banking, manufacturing, insurance, and retail clients. Interviewers want to know you can ramp up on a new domain without needing hand-holding. Bring a clear example from your past where you moved into an unfamiliar domain and still delivered solid output.

Structured communication. BAs at Zensar bridge client business teams and internal delivery teams across geographies. Interviewers pay close attention to whether you can explain a complex situation without jargon. Use the STAR format and speak in specifics, not generalities.

Ownership over your documents. Candidates report that interviewers probe the quality and ownership of artefacts: who wrote the BRD, who got the user stories signed off, who tracked change requests. Be ready to describe documents you personally owned, not just contributed to.

Comfort with ambiguity. IT services projects often start with vague client briefs. Interviewers want to see that you ask the right clarifying questions and take initiative rather than waiting to be told exactly what to do.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

One week before the interview:

Research Zensar's recent client work and verticals through their public website and LinkedIn page. Note which industries appear most in their news and case studies, as these are likely the domains your interviewer works in. Map each requirement in the job description to a specific example from your own experience before you step in.

Three to five days before:

Prepare answers for all 12 questions in this guide using the STAR format. Write them out rather than just rehearsing in your head. Writing forces you to be specific about the Action and Result. Also review the tools commonly mentioned in Zensar BA job descriptions: JIRA, Confluence, Visio or Lucidchart are frequently cited.

One to two days before:

Do one mock interview out loud and time yourself. Most BA answers should run between ninety seconds and two and a half minutes. Shorter lacks detail; longer loses the interviewer. Prepare two or three questions to ask the interviewer about the team structure, the client engagement model, or what success looks like for a BA in their unit.

On the day:

Candidates report most Zensar rounds are conducted as video calls. Test your setup at least thirty minutes before. Have your resume, a notepad, and any portfolio documents open so you can reference them quickly.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving vague behavioural answers. Saying 'I always make sure to communicate clearly with stakeholders' is not an answer. Give a specific project, a specific challenge, and a specific outcome with every behavioural question.

Confusing BA and PM responsibilities. Interviewers sometimes test this deliberately. A BA gathers and documents requirements, bridges business and tech understanding, and validates the solution. A PM owns the timeline, budget, and delivery. Know where the line is and do not claim PM responsibilities that were not yours.

Not knowing your own documents. If you list a BRD or a use case document on your resume, expect detailed questions: what sections it had, how many stakeholders reviewed it, what the final sign-off process looked like. If you cannot answer with confidence, it raises doubts about actual ownership.

Skipping the Result in STAR answers. Many candidates describe a situation and their actions but forget to close with an outcome. Even a qualitative result ('the client approved the scope with no revisions') is better than leaving the story unfinished.

Not asking questions at the end. Asking nothing signals low interest in the role. Prepare at least two specific questions about the team, the project type, or what success looks like in the first few months.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Zensar BA interview typically have?

Candidates commonly report two to four rounds: a recruiter or HR screen, one or two domain and situational rounds with a hiring manager or senior BA, and sometimes a final discussion with a delivery lead or department head. The number varies by team and location. Treat any round count you see online as an estimate rather than a fixed rule.

Does Zensar ask technical questions in a BA interview?

Zensar BA interviews are primarily domain and communication focused rather than coding or database heavy. Candidates report occasional questions about SQL basics or data interpretation, especially for data analytics BA roles. If the job description mentions SQL, prepare to read or write a basic query. Otherwise, direct your prep toward requirements gathering, stakeholder management, and process mapping.

What salary can I expect for a BA role at Zensar?

Zensar does not publish salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly cite ranges for BA roles at mid-size Indian IT services companies, though figures vary widely by experience level, city, and team. Check those platforms for recent self-reported numbers before your negotiation conversation. Come in with a number anchored to your current compensation and market data rather than waiting for Zensar to set the range first.

Is prior experience in banking or manufacturing required to get a BA role at Zensar?

A background in Zensar's key verticals (banking, manufacturing, insurance, retail) is a plus but candidates without it do get hired. Interviewers typically care more about how quickly you have picked up new domains in the past than about specific prior domain experience. Prepare a clear example of a time you learned an unfamiliar business domain on the job and still delivered solid results.

How long does the Zensar hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report that the process from first contact to offer typically spans two to five weeks, though senior roles or paused hiring pipelines can stretch this further. Following up with the recruiter after each round is normal and expected. If two weeks pass without an update, a short polite follow-up email is a reasonable next step.

Should I bring a case study or portfolio to the Zensar BA interview?

You are not typically required to present a formal case study, but candidates report that interviewers ask detailed questions about past projects and documents. Have ready any anonymised samples of BRDs, user stories, or process maps you personally wrote and can walk someone through. Talking an interviewer through a real artefact is far more convincing than describing it in the abstract.
