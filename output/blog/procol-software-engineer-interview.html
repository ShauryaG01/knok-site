01 Overview

Overview

Procol is a B2B procurement and spend management SaaS platform that helps enterprises automate buying, run reverse auctions, and manage supplier relationships. As of mid-2026, knok jobradar shows 12 open Software Engineer roles at Procol, part of a nationwide market with 5,395 SE openings across India.

Candidates report that the process typically runs 3-4 rounds: an initial recruiter or HR screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager. All rounds are typically conducted over video call. Procol's engineering spans backend services, REST API integrations with ERP systems like SAP and Oracle, and a React-based frontend, so expect questions rooted in real procurement workflows and data-intensive systems.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently, based on Procol's product domain and what candidates typically report from SE interviews at B2B SaaS companies:

  1. How would you design a multi-level approval workflow engine for purchase orders?
  2. Procol runs reverse auctions with many simultaneous bids. How would you handle concurrent bid submissions safely?
  3. Walk me through how you would design a multi-tenant SaaS platform where each enterprise client has isolated data.
  4. How would you implement role-based access control for a platform used by procurement managers, finance teams, and suppliers?
  5. Tell me about a time you optimised a slow query or improved database performance in a transaction-heavy system.
  6. How do you ensure data consistency when integrating with third-party ERP systems that have unreliable APIs?
  7. How would you design an audit trail so every procurement action is logged and tamper-evident?
  8. Describe your approach to API versioning when external supplier clients cannot upgrade quickly.
  9. How would you build a spend analytics dashboard that aggregates data across many transactions without slow page loads?
  10. Tell me about a production incident you helped resolve. What was your debugging process?
  11. How do you write code that is easy to test? Give a concrete example.
  12. How would you secure sensitive procurement and pricing data in transit and at rest?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you optimised a slow query or improved database performance.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a report listing open purchase orders was taking several seconds to load during peak hours, frustrating the finance team who ran it daily.

*Task:* I was responsible for investigating the root cause and fixing it without disrupting the live system.

*Action:* I used the database query explain plan to find a full table scan on a large orders table. I added a composite index on status and created_at, rewrote a subquery as a JOIN, and tested on a staging replica before deploying. I also routed report queries to a read replica to stop them competing with write traffic.

*Result:* Load time dropped dramatically. The finance team stopped raising tickets about the report, and I documented the indexing pattern for the rest of the team.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you integrated with an unreliable third-party system.

*Situation:* Our platform needed to sync purchase order status with a client's SAP system that had frequent timeouts and occasional duplicate responses.

*Task:* I had to build a reliable sync mechanism that would not corrupt order data even when SAP behaved inconsistently.

*Action:* I implemented an idempotent job queue backed by our own database. Each sync event carried a unique idempotency key. Before processing, we checked whether that key already existed so duplicates were skipped automatically. Failed jobs retried with exponential backoff, and after three failures an alert fired for manual review.

*Result:* Duplicate order updates dropped to zero. The system handled SAP outages gracefully, and the client reported much higher confidence in the data showing on their dashboard.

---

Q: Tell me about a production incident you helped resolve.

*Situation:* A reverse auction froze for a large enterprise client mid-bidding because a background job was holding a database lock longer than expected.

*Task:* I joined the incident call as the on-call engineer and had to restore the auction quickly while vendors were still waiting.

*Action:* I identified the blocking query using the database active sessions view, killed the offending job safely, and restored the auction. I then added a timeout to all background jobs touching auction tables and moved the heavy processing to a separate worker queue so it could never block live auction writes again.

*Result:* The auction resumed within minutes. We documented a runbook for similar incidents, and no recurrence was reported in the following months.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions: Structure every 'tell me about a time' answer as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (2-3 sentences each) and spend most of your time on Action. Quantify the Result wherever possible.

Design framework for system design questions: Start by clarifying requirements and scale. Outline the main components (API layer, service logic, database, async jobs, caching). Discuss trade-offs at each step. For Procol, tie your design back to procurement realities: concurrent bids, multi-tenant data isolation, ERP integrations, and audit requirements.

Diagnosis framework for debugging questions: Use a funnel approach. Start broad (was it a deploy, a config change, a data spike?), narrow to the affected component, then to the specific query or function. Interviewers want to see systematic thinking, not lucky guessing.

Security framing: For any question touching sensitive data, mention least privilege, input validation at system boundaries, and encryption of sensitive fields. Procol handles pricing and spend data, so raising these points shows genuine domain awareness.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Procol engineers work on systems where correctness matters more than raw speed. A double-charged purchase order or a missed approval step is a real business problem. Interviewers typically look for three things.

Domain curiosity. Candidates who have thought about how procurement, auctions, or ERP integrations work stand out. You do not need prior procurement experience, but connecting your answers to the domain shows you will ramp up quickly.

Systems thinking under failure. Procol's platform serves many enterprise clients simultaneously. Questions about multi-tenancy, data isolation, and consistent state under failure test whether you think about edge cases before they become incidents.

Clear communication. B2B products involve non-technical stakeholders like finance managers and procurement heads. Interviewers want engineers who can explain a technical trade-off in plain language.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core coding. Practice problems on arrays, hashmaps, trees, and graphs. Focus on medium-difficulty problems on competitive coding platforms. Pay special attention to problems involving queues and concurrency since these map directly to Procol's auction and workflow logic.

Week 2: System design. Study how to design a workflow engine using the state machine pattern, a multi-tenant SaaS data model, and an event-driven notification system. Practice talking through your design out loud, explaining trade-offs as you go.

Week 3: Domain preparation. Read Procol's product blog and any publicly available case studies. Prepare two or three examples from your own work involving data consistency, API integrations, or performance fixes.

Week 4: Behavioural and mock interviews. Write out five STAR stories: a hard debugging experience, a performance improvement, a conflict with a colleague, delivering under pressure, and a system you designed from scratch. Do at least two mock interviews out loud, not just in your head.

Throughout: Review Procol's current job postings to see which technologies they mention, and align your examples to their stated stack wherever you can.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Jumping into design before clarifying requirements. Candidates who start drawing architecture before asking about scale or multi-tenancy usually get redirected. Spend the first two minutes asking clarifying questions.
  1. Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved performance' without specifics sounds thin. Interviewers remember the candidate who described a concrete fix, not the many who said 'things got faster'.
  1. Ignoring the procurement context. Designing a notification system without mentioning approval workflows, or designing a database without audit trails, signals you have not thought about what Procol actually builds.
  1. Over-engineering in coding rounds. Procol interviews typically reward clean, working code over impressive but incomplete solutions. Finish a correct solution first, then discuss optimisations.
  1. Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing come across as uninterested. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team's stack, deployment practices, or on-call setup.

How many rounds does the Procol Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report 3-4 rounds in total. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority, so ask the recruiter upfront what to expect.

What programming languages does Procol use?

Procol's engineering typically involves backend services and a React-based frontend. Job descriptions at similar B2B SaaS companies commonly mention Java or Node.js on the backend. In interviews, candidates usually have the option to code in their strongest language. Fluency in one language is more valuable than surface-level knowledge of many.

Is system design asked for junior Software Engineer roles?

For entry-level roles (0-2 years experience), system design questions are typically lighter: you might be asked to design a small component or explain how a feature could be built, rather than architect a full distributed system. Full system design rounds become standard at mid and senior levels. Confirm with the recruiter whether a dedicated design round is part of your loop.
