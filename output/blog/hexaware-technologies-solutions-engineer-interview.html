01 Overview

Overview

Hexaware Technologies is a global IT services company with deep expertise in cloud, automation, AI, and business process services. A Solutions Engineer here typically sits at the intersection of technical depth and client engagement, helping customers see how Hexaware's capabilities solve real business problems. Responsibilities often span pre-sales discussions, solution design, demo delivery, and coordination with delivery teams.

Candidates report the process typically involves a recruiter screening call, one or two technical or solution-focused rounds with senior engineers or architects, and a final discussion with a business leader or senior stakeholder. The number and format of rounds can vary by team, so confirm the structure with your recruiter after the first contact.

As of July 2026, Hexaware had 173 open roles across India, signalling active hiring. Bangalore leads with the highest concentration of Solutions Engineer openings, followed by Mumbai, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, and Chennai.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are questions candidates commonly report being asked in Hexaware Solutions Engineer interviews:

  1. Walk me through a time you explained a complex technical solution to a client with no technical background.
  2. How do you approach scoping a solution when a client's requirements are vague or incomplete?
  3. Describe your experience supporting pre-sales activities such as demos, RFP responses, or proof-of-concept exercises.
  4. Tell me about a situation where a proposed solution had to change mid-engagement. How did you handle it?
  5. How do you stay current with trends in cloud, AI, or automation that could shape client solutions?
  6. Describe a time you had to manage conflicting expectations between a client and your internal delivery team.
  7. How do you build a business case or ROI argument for a technology investment?
  8. Walk me through a complex project end-to-end, from initial discovery to handoff.
  9. What is your process for running a successful proof of concept with an enterprise client?
  10. How do you qualify whether a client's problem is a strong fit for your company's service offerings?
  11. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a client request. How did you handle that conversation?
  12. What tools or approaches do you use to document and present solution architectures to mixed audiences?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you explained a complex technical solution to a non-technical client.

*Situation:* I was working with a mid-sized manufacturing client who wanted to automate their invoice processing but had no visibility into what AI or automation could realistically deliver.

*Task:* My job was to present a solution proposal without losing a room full of finance and operations leaders who had no technical background.

*Action:* I dropped all technical terminology from my slides. Instead, I walked them through a 'before and after' story: 'Right now, your team manually touches every invoice. With this solution, the system reads, validates, and routes it for you. Your team only handles exceptions.' I used a simple flow diagram and paused for questions at each step rather than presenting everything at once.

*Result:* The client approved the proof of concept within the same week. They later told us our presentation was the clearest they had seen across all vendors they evaluated.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a solution had to change significantly mid-engagement.

*Situation:* We were three weeks into a cloud migration scoping exercise when the client's IT team revealed a legacy dependency that made our original architecture unworkable.

*Task:* I needed to re-scope the solution quickly without losing client confidence or causing a significant delay.

*Action:* I called an urgent working session with the client's technical lead and our internal architect. We mapped out two alternative paths with their trade-offs and presented both options to the client within two days rather than sitting on the problem. I was transparent about why the change was necessary, which helped maintain trust.

*Result:* The client appreciated the transparency and chose the phased migration approach we recommended. The engagement stayed on track and the client extended the scope in the following quarter.

---

Q: How do you qualify whether a client's problem is a good fit for your company's offerings?

*Situation:* A prospect approached us about a large-scale data warehousing project. On the surface it sounded like a strong fit, but something felt off in the initial call.

*Task:* I needed to quickly determine whether we could deliver real value or whether this would become a difficult engagement.

*Action:* I ran a structured discovery call focused on four areas: their current state, desired outcomes, timeline, and internal team readiness. I asked directly about budget range, decision process, and what had stopped them from solving this internally. This surfaced that their timeline was unrealistic and internal buy-in was weak.

*Result:* I had an honest conversation about realistic expectations and proposed starting with a smaller scoping exercise. They agreed. A well-scoped smaller engagement is far better than a large one that fails.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use the STAR structure: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you actually did, step by step), and Result (a clearly stated outcome). Keep Situation and Task short, and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

For solution design or technical questions, use a four-part approach. First, restate the problem in your own words to confirm understanding. Second, outline two or three possible approaches with their trade-offs. Third, recommend one path and explain why. Fourth, flag your assumptions and risks openly. Interviewers at Hexaware typically value structured thinking over a single 'right answer.'

For client-handling questions, lead with empathy before the technical fix. Show that you first listen and validate the client's concern, then propose a path forward. This mirrors how Solutions Engineers actually operate in client-facing engagements.

For 'tell me about yourself,' use a three-part format: your core background in one sentence, your most relevant experience for this specific role in two or three sentences, and why Hexaware in one sentence connecting their service areas to your interests. Keep it under two minutes.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hexaware Solutions Engineer interviews typically assess a combination of technical credibility and communication ability. Interviewers are looking for candidates who can do two things well: understand technology deeply enough to design solutions, and communicate clearly enough to win client confidence.

Technical credibility means being comfortable discussing cloud platforms, integration approaches, automation tools, or AI use cases at a conceptual level. You do not need to be a hands-on developer, but you need to speak the language and know where the limits are.

Client empathy is equally important. Candidates who talk only about technology without connecting it to business outcomes tend to struggle in these rounds. Show that you think about the client's perspective first and the technical approach second.

Structured communication is a strong differentiator. Interviewers notice when a candidate can take a messy, open-ended question and give a clear, organised answer. Practice framing your answer before launching into it.

Ownership and proactivity round out the picture. Hexaware works across large enterprise accounts where things go wrong. Candidates who demonstrate they step up, flag issues early, and drive resolution rather than waiting for direction tend to stand out.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Understand the company and role. Read Hexaware's public case studies and service descriptions. Focus on their core areas: cloud and infrastructure, AI and analytics, business process services, and digital engineering. Map your past experience against the specific job description requirements before anything else.

Step 2: Build your story bank. Prepare five to six STAR stories covering: working directly with a client, designing or contributing to a complex solution, navigating stakeholder disagreement, managing a project that went off track, and learning a new technology under pressure. Practice each story out loud until it flows in under three minutes.

Step 3: Practice your delivery. Do mock interviews with a peer or record yourself answering the questions listed above. Focus on cutting filler words and connecting every technical point to a business outcome. Practice explaining a basic solution architecture in simple language, since candidates report that some rounds include scenario or sketch-based discussions.

Step 4: Prepare your questions. Bring two or three thoughtful questions about the team's current client challenges, how success is measured in this role, and what onboarding looks like.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going too deep technically without connecting to business value. Solutions Engineers are not pure engineers. Spending a long time explaining architecture without mentioning what it means for the client's operations or costs is answering the wrong question.

Giving vague, story-free answers. 'I am good at client communication' is not an answer. A specific example of a difficult client conversation you handled, with a clear outcome, is. Every behavioral question needs a concrete example.

Not researching Hexaware before the interview. Candidates who cannot name a single Hexaware service area or explain why they want to work there specifically tend to be filtered out early. A short review of their website and recent announcements is the minimum preparation.

Underestimating the soft skills component. Many candidates prepare heavily on technical topics and are caught off guard by questions about conflict resolution, stakeholder management, or communication style. In a Solutions Engineer role, these are first-class interview topics, not afterthoughts.

Talking past the interviewer without confirming understanding. In pre-sales and solutions work, listening is as important as presenting. Show in your interview that you pause, check comprehension, and tailor your answer rather than broadcasting everything you know.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Hexaware typically have for a Solutions Engineer position?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four rounds: a recruiter or HR screening, one or two technical or solution-focused discussions, and a final round with a business or sales leader. The exact structure varies by team and business unit. Confirm the format with your recruiter after the first call so you can prepare for the right type of questions in each round.

What salary range can I expect for a Solutions Engineer at Hexaware?

Hexaware has not published official salary bands for this role publicly. Based on Glassdoor and industry surveys for IT services pre-sales and solutions roles in India, compensation varies significantly by experience level, domain, and the specific team you join. Research current benchmarks before your HR discussion so you can negotiate from a clear baseline rather than guessing.

Will there be coding tests or hands-on technical assessments?

Candidates report that Solutions Engineer interviews at Hexaware are typically not heavy on coding assessments. The focus tends to be on solution design, client scenarios, and communication ability. Some roles may include a technical architecture walkthrough or a written case study exercise. Ask your recruiter about the specific format in advance so there are no surprises on interview day.

How important is prior pre-sales experience for this role?

Pre-sales experience is valued but not always mandatory, depending on the specific team and seniority of the role. Candidates from implementation, consulting, or technical account management backgrounds also get through, particularly when they can demonstrate client-facing communication and solution design ability. Focus your preparation on showing that you can bridge business needs and technical capabilities effectively.

Which cities in India have the most Hexaware Solutions Engineer openings?

Based on job listing data tracked as of July 2026, Bangalore leads with 55 Solutions Engineer roles, followed by Mumbai with 23 and Delhi with 20. Pune has 12 openings, Hyderabad has 6, and Chennai has 5. Bangalore is the strongest market by volume if you have flexibility on location.

What should I do if an interviewer asks about a technology I have not worked with?

Be honest rather than bluffing. Technical interviewers will probe further if your answer sounds shallow, and getting caught is far worse than admitting a gap. A strong approach is to acknowledge you have not used that specific tool directly, describe a related technology you do know well, and explain how you would approach learning the new one. This demonstrates intellectual honesty and a learning mindset, both of which matter in client-facing roles.
