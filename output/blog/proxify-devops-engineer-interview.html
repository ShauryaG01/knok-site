01 Overview

Overview

Proxify is a curated remote-work platform that vets engineers and places them with tech companies, primarily in Europe and North America. For DevOps engineers, the process typically includes two to three technical stages plus a client-matching step. Candidates report that Proxify cares about both technical depth and the ability to communicate clearly with remote clients in English.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These 12 questions come up frequently in Proxify DevOps interviews, based on candidate reports and the role's remote-client context.

  1. Walk me through a CI/CD pipeline you designed and built from scratch.
  2. How do you manage infrastructure as code, and which tools do you prefer?
  3. Describe your Kubernetes experience: cluster setup, scaling strategies, and debugging.
  4. How do you handle secrets and credentials securely across environments?
  5. A deployment just failed in production. Take me through your incident response process.
  6. How do you set up monitoring and alerting for a distributed system?
  7. What is your experience with multi-cloud or hybrid cloud architectures?
  8. How do you integrate security into a DevOps workflow (DevSecOps)?
  9. Describe a time you reduced infrastructure costs without affecting reliability.
  10. How do you work with developers who are unfamiliar with DevOps practices?
  11. How have you handled asynchronous collaboration across different time zones?
  12. How do you evaluate and adopt new tools in a fast-moving DevOps ecosystem?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three STAR-format answers for common Proxify DevOps questions. Adapt the details to match your own projects.

Q: Walk me through a CI/CD pipeline you built from scratch.

*Situation:* My team was doing manual deployments every sprint, which caused delays and frequent rollback headaches.

*Task:* I was responsible for automating the full release cycle, from code commit through to production.

*Action:* I built a GitHub Actions workflow with separate jobs for linting, unit tests, Docker image build, and a Helm-based deploy to Kubernetes. I added a staging approval gate before the production step so no release went live without at least one reviewer sign-off.

*Result:* Deployment became a predictable, repeatable process. Rollbacks went from a manual scramble to a single revert command, and the team stopped dreading release day.

---

Q: Describe a time you responded to a production outage.

*Situation:* The primary database cluster for a client's platform became unresponsive during peak traffic hours.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer, I had to restore service quickly and keep the client informed throughout the incident.

*Action:* I checked Grafana dashboards first and traced the root cause to connection pool exhaustion. I scaled up the read replica count, rerouted write traffic, and posted a brief status update to the client's Slack channel every few minutes.

*Result:* Service was restored within the agreed SLA window. I followed up with a post-mortem that led to auto-scaling policies, preventing the same issue from recurring.

---

Q: How did you reduce infrastructure costs for a client?

*Situation:* A client's cloud setup had not been reviewed in a long time, with many instances running at low utilisation.

*Task:* I was asked to audit and right-size the entire infrastructure.

*Action:* I used AWS Cost Explorer and CloudWatch metrics to identify idle and over-provisioned resources. I moved non-critical workloads to Spot instances and configured scheduled scaling for off-peak windows. I also set up a weekly cost report so the client could track spend on an ongoing basis.

*Result:* Monthly cloud spend dropped meaningfully. Industry surveys commonly cite 30-40% waste in unoptimised cloud setups, and this project brought the client well below that baseline. They adopted the cost review cadence as a permanent practice.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Most Proxify DevOps interviews reward answers grounded in real experience rather than textbook definitions. Two frameworks work well across different question types.

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result): Use this for any 'tell me about a time' question. Keep Situation and Task brief (one to two sentences each) and spend most of your time on Action and Result. Quantify results where you honestly can, and reference publicly reported benchmarks for context if your own numbers are confidential.

Problem-Approach-Trade-offs: For 'how would you design' questions, state the problem you are solving, walk through your chosen approach step by step, then name one or two trade-offs you considered. This signals senior-level thinking without overcomplicating your answer.

For communication questions specific to Proxify's remote-work context, always mention async tools (Slack, Confluence, Loom) and explain how you keep client stakeholders informed without pulling them into every technical discussion. Proxify clients value engineers who communicate proactively, not just competently.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Proxify interviewers are typically looking for four qualities in DevOps candidates.

Technical depth without hand-waving. They will follow up on every tool you mention. If you say you use Terraform, expect questions about state management, remote backends, and how you handle configuration drift. Only list tools you can discuss with real depth.

Client-ready communication. Since you will represent Proxify to external clients, interviewers want to see that you can explain technical decisions in plain language. Practice summarising a complex incident in two to three sentences for a non-technical audience.

Ownership and autonomy. Proxify's clients expect engineers who can identify problems and solve them without waiting to be asked. Answers where you spotted a gap and fixed it on your own initiative land much better than answers where you simply executed what you were told.

Reliability and process discipline. Candidates who mention runbooks, on-call rotations, post-mortems, and documented processes stand out. Proxify clients have often had bad experiences with undocumented, hero-driven ops, and they want to see that you bring structure and predictability.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Follow this sequence in the weeks before your Proxify DevOps interview.

  1. Review your own projects. Pick three to four pieces of work you are proud of and prepare a STAR story for each. Know the impact: deployment frequency improvements, incident response outcomes, cost changes. Use publicly reported benchmarks for context if your own numbers are confidential.
  1. Sharpen your core tools. Focus on Kubernetes, Terraform or Pulumi, a major CI/CD platform (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, or Jenkins), and at least one major cloud (AWS, GCP, or Azure). Do not list tools on your resume that you cannot discuss in depth under questioning.
  1. Practice system design out loud. Prepare to design a CI/CD pipeline, a monitoring stack, and a multi-environment Kubernetes setup from scratch. Think about trade-offs, not just the happy path. Practice explaining your choices as if speaking to a client who is not a DevOps engineer.
  1. Prepare for the communication stage. Candidates report a stage focused on English fluency and async work style. Practice explaining a technical incident to a non-technical manager clearly and concisely.
  1. Research the specific Proxify role. Read the job description carefully and map your experience to each requirement. Candidates who mirror the language of the job description typically make a stronger first impression.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Listing tools without depth. Saying 'I have experience with Kubernetes and Terraform' is not enough at Proxify. Interviewers will probe every claim. If you mention a tool, be ready to discuss a real problem you solved with it and a trade-off you navigated.

Ignoring the client communication angle. This is a remote-placement role. Candidates who focus only on technical answers and skip the 'how do you keep clients informed' dimension often do not pass the later stages.

Vague impact statements. Saying 'I improved performance' is weak. Explain what you changed, why you changed it, and what measurable shift you observed. Use publicly reported benchmarks for context if your own data is sensitive.

Over-engineering hypothetical answers. When asked to design something, start simple and add complexity only when the interviewer prompts for it. Proxify interviewers want to see sound judgment, not the most elaborate possible architecture.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask nothing about the role, the client type, or the remote work setup come across as disengaged. Prepare two to three genuine questions about how teams collaborate and what kinds of clients you would be placed with.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Proxify typically have for DevOps engineers?

Candidates report two to three technical stages, typically a screening call, a technical interview focused on infrastructure and tooling, and sometimes a live troubleshooting or practical exercise. There is usually a final stage focused on communication and client fit. Round structures can vary by role and client, so treat candidate reports as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Does Proxify pay in USD or INR for Indian engineers?

Proxify places engineers with international clients, and compensation arrangements vary. Candidates report that contracts can be structured in USD or EUR with payments routed to Indian bank accounts. Clarify payment currency, applicable taxes, and whether the engagement is full-time employment or a freelance contract early in your recruiter conversation.

What cloud platforms does Proxify focus on for DevOps roles?

Job descriptions typically mention AWS most often, followed by GCP and Azure. Multi-cloud experience is a plus but not always required. Proxify clients span a wide range of industries, so strong depth on at least one major cloud platform matters more than shallow familiarity with all three.

Is the Proxify interview hard compared to product companies?

Candidates generally describe the technical bar as comparable to a mid-to-senior round at a well-funded startup. The bigger differentiator is the communication assessment, which most product-company interviews skip entirely. You are being evaluated both on technical competence and on whether international clients will trust you to work independently and communicate proactively across time zones.

How long does the Proxify hiring process take?

Candidates report the process from first screening to an offer typically spans two to four weeks, though it can extend if the client-matching step takes longer. Once you pass the vetting stages, Proxify may take additional time to find the right client project for your profile and availability.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps role placed through Proxify?

Based on knok's jobradar data for DevOps Engineer roles in India, typical ranges are 6-12 LPA for entry level (0-2 years), 15-28 LPA for mid-level (3-5 years), 30-50 LPA for senior (6-9 years), and 45-70+ LPA for lead or staff roles. Proxify roles that involve international client billing may sit toward the higher end of each band. Always verify current compensation directly with the Proxify recruiter.
