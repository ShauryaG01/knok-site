03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Curran Daly + Associates typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact structure can vary by role, team, and seniority level. The stages below reflect what candidates at similar professional services firms commonly report.

Application and resume screening. Your resume goes through an initial filter by a recruiter or the hiring team. Tailoring your application to the job description, using relevant keywords, and quantifying your achievements increases your chances of getting shortlisted.

Recruiter screening call. If shortlisted, expect a short introductory call with someone from HR or talent acquisition. They will ask about your background, current salary and expectations, notice period, and why you are interested in the company. Keep your answers clear and confident.

Hiring manager interview. This is usually a structured conversation focused on your experience, your approach to problems, and how you have handled specific situations in the past. Behavioural questions in the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) are common at this stage.

Skills assessment or case discussion. Depending on the role, you may be asked to complete a short written task or walk through a practical case scenario. For client-facing or analytical roles, this stage tests whether you can apply your knowledge in a real-world context.

Final round or panel interview. Shortlisted candidates typically meet a panel of senior stakeholders or cross-functional leaders. This round often covers cultural fit, your longer-term goals, and how you see yourself contributing to the team.

Offer and background check. If successful, you receive a verbal offer followed by a formal written offer. Background verification and reference checks are standard at this point.

The full process from application to offer is commonly reported to take a few weeks to over a month, depending on the role's seniority and how urgently the team needs to fill the position.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Curran Daly + Associates, like most professional services firms, values a combination of technical competence and interpersonal capability. Here is what tends to stand out in candidates.

Relevant domain experience. Hiring managers look for proof that you have done similar work before and can hit the ground running. Concrete examples from past roles carry more weight than general claims.

Strong communication. Whether the role is client-facing or internal, the ability to explain complex ideas clearly, in writing and in conversation, is consistently valued.

Problem-solving mindset. Interviewers frequently test how you approach ambiguous or difficult situations. They want to see structured thinking, not just the right answer.

Ownership and accountability. Candidates who can speak to situations where they took initiative, handled a challenge independently, or delivered results without constant supervision tend to do well.

Cultural alignment. Most professional services environments are collaborative and fast-paced. Showing that you work well in a team, manage your time, and adapt to shifting priorities matters as much as your technical skills.

Attention to detail. Especially relevant for roles involving client deliverables, data, or reporting. Errors in your application materials, including a poorly formatted resume or typos in your cover note, can hurt your chances before you even reach the interview stage.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Do your homework on the company. Before any interview, read the company website, their LinkedIn page, and any recent news. Understand what Curran Daly + Associates does, who they work with, and what makes them different from competitors. Being able to speak to this shows genuine interest and sets you apart from candidates who clearly did not prepare.

Tailor your resume for each role. With 61 open roles currently listed, there is real variety to explore. Pick roles that genuinely match your background and adjust your resume to reflect the specific requirements in each job description. Generic applications rarely make the cut at firms that receive high volumes of applicants.

Prepare your STAR stories. Write down several examples from your career that demonstrate different competencies: leading a project, handling a difficult stakeholder, solving a complex problem, delivering under pressure. Practice telling each story clearly in a couple of minutes so it flows naturally in conversation.

Know your impact. Wherever your work involved measurable outcomes, be ready to speak to them. Interviewers at professional services firms respond well to candidates who can show impact, not just describe activities.

Prepare smart questions. At the end of most interview stages, you will be invited to ask questions. Avoid asking about salary too early. Instead, ask about team structure, what success looks like in the first few months on the job, or how teams collaborate across functions.

Sort out the practical basics. Confirm the interview format, test your connection if it is a video call, dress professionally, and log in or arrive a few minutes early. These things sound obvious, but they genuinely affect first impressions in a professional services context.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Professional services firms like Curran Daly + Associates typically build their culture around a few consistent themes: client focus, collaboration, and continuous improvement. Internal culture can vary across teams and locations, but a few patterns tend to show up broadly across the organisation.

Client-first thinking. Delivering value to clients is usually at the centre of how decisions get made. Employees are expected to understand client needs, communicate proactively, and take responsibility for outcomes rather than passing problems along.

Team-oriented work style. Most work happens in cross-functional teams or in close partnership with colleagues. Being someone who contributes, communicates clearly, and supports teammates is valued above individual performance at the expense of others.

Growth and learning. Firms in this space tend to invest in skill development, whether through formal training programmes, stretch assignments, or mentorship. If you actively seek out opportunities to grow your skills, this kind of environment can work well for you.

Professionalism and reliability. Showing up prepared, following through on commitments, and maintaining high standards in your work are baseline expectations, not optional extras. Consistency matters as much as peak performance.

The best way to understand the actual culture is to speak with current or former employees on LinkedIn. Look for people who have worked there recently and ask specific questions about team dynamics, work-life balance, and growth opportunities.

How long does the hiring process typically take?

At professional services firms of this type, the process from application to offer is commonly reported to take anywhere from a few weeks to over a month. If you have not heard back after an interview, a polite follow-up email to the recruiter after a couple of weeks is perfectly acceptable and often appreciated.

Should I send a cover letter when applying?

Not all applications require one, but a short and specific cover note almost always helps your application stand out. A good cover letter is not a repeat of your resume. It should explain why you want this particular role at this company, and what specific experience you bring that matches what the job description is asking for. Keep it brief, targeted, and free of generic phrases.

What kind of questions should I expect in a behavioural interview?

Behavioural questions ask you to describe real situations from your past, such as 'Tell me about a time you handled a difficult client' or 'Describe a situation where you had to meet a tight deadline.' Use the STAR format: describe the Situation, the Task at hand, the Action you took, and the Result you achieved. Preparing several strong examples from your career before the interview means you will not be caught off guard when the questions come.

How should I negotiate salary at Curran Daly + Associates?

Wait until you have a formal offer before starting the salary conversation. Research market rates for the role and location using publicly reported data from sources like Glassdoor or LinkedIn Salary. When you make a counter, give a specific figure with a clear reason behind it, such as your current compensation or a competing offer you have received. Stay polite and professional throughout, and give the recruiter time to come back to you.
