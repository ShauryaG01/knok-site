01 Overview

Overview

KPMG India is actively hiring Business Analysts across its advisory, consulting, and digital transformation practices. As of July 2026, knok's job radar shows 233 open roles at KPMG India alone, out of 398 BA positions tracked across the country. Bangalore leads with 53 openings, followed by Delhi at 48 and Mumbai at 24.

The interview process typically spans multiple rounds and combines behavioural questions, structured case discussions, and domain knowledge checks. Candidates report that KPMG places strong emphasis on structured thinking, clear communication, and the ability to handle ambiguous client problems. This guide walks you through the most commonly asked questions, how to frame your answers, and what interviewers are looking for.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report that KPMG BA interviews typically include a mix of the following:

  1. Tell me about yourself and why you want to join KPMG.
  2. Walk me through a project where you converted raw data into a business recommendation.
  3. How do you handle a stakeholder who pushes back on your analysis?
  4. Describe a time you managed competing priorities under a tight deadline.
  5. What is your approach when a client brief is unclear or incomplete?
  6. Give an example of a process inefficiency you identified and resolved.
  7. How do you explain a complex finding to a non-technical audience?
  8. Tell me about a time your recommendation was not accepted. How did you respond?
  9. How do you validate data quality before sharing insights with a client?
  10. KPMG works across many industries. How do you get up to speed in an unfamiliar domain?
  11. Describe a cross-functional project where team members had conflicting views.
  12. Why do you prefer consulting over an in-house analyst role?

Some rounds may also include a short case or data exercise. Candidates report being asked to interpret a chart, spot a data anomaly, or structure an unfamiliar business problem on the spot.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three sample answers using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Q: Give an example of a process inefficiency you identified and resolved.

*Situation:* At my previous employer, the monthly MIS report was compiled manually by two team members and frequently delayed, which pushed back decisions that depended on it.

*Task:* My manager asked me to investigate the bottleneck and recommend a fix.

*Action:* I mapped every step in the process and found that most of the delay came from reconciling data from two systems that used different column formats. I standardised the input template and built simple macros to merge the feeds automatically.

*Result:* The report was ready several days earlier each cycle, freeing the team to spend that time on analysis rather than data wrangling. My manager shared the template with two other business units.

---

Q: How do you handle a stakeholder who disagrees with your analysis?

*Situation:* I was presenting a cost-optimisation study to a senior operations head who was convinced the current vendor was the right choice.

*Task:* I needed to keep the analysis credible while keeping the conversation constructive.

*Action:* I asked him to walk me through his concerns. He pointed to a seasonality factor I had not weighted correctly. I acknowledged the gap, re-ran the model with adjusted seasonal weights, and shared the updated output before the next meeting.

*Result:* The revised analysis still supported my core recommendation but addressed his concern directly. He endorsed the updated vendor shortlist in the follow-up meeting.

---

Q: Describe a cross-functional project where team members had conflicting priorities.

*Situation:* A product launch I was coordinating involved the technology, finance, and marketing teams, each with a different view on the rollout timeline.

*Task:* As the BA on the project, I had to build a single plan all three teams could commit to.

*Action:* I spoke to each team lead separately to map their constraints, then created a dependency diagram showing how each team's schedule affected the others. I ran a joint session where we worked through the diagram together and negotiated a sequenced plan.

*Result:* The teams reached a consensus in that session without escalating to senior leadership. The launch proceeded on the agreed schedule.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Four frameworks that work well for KPMG BA interviews:

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard structure for behavioural questions. Keep each component tight. Interviewers often probe the 'Action' step, so be ready to explain what you specifically did versus what the broader team did.

MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) is a consulting staple. When asked to break down a problem, show that your categories do not overlap and together cover the full scope. KPMG interviewers notice when candidates naturally organise their thinking this way.

Pyramid Principle for recommendations: lead with your conclusion, then give the supporting arguments, then the underlying data. This is the opposite of how most people naturally tell a story, so practise it deliberately.

Issue Tree for case-style questions: split the main problem into branches (for example, revenue and cost for a profitability question), then drill into each branch systematically. Stating your structure out loud before diving in signals organised, consulting-style thinking.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who have interviewed at KPMG report that interviewers pay close attention to a few core traits:

Structured thinking. Can you break an ambiguous problem into clear components? Interviewers often give open-ended prompts deliberately to see if you impose structure before jumping to an answer.

Communication clarity. KPMG BAs work with clients who range from data-savvy to completely non-technical. Show that you can adjust your language for the audience without losing the insight.

Data integrity. Consulting firms build their credibility on accurate analysis. Expect questions about how you verify data, handle missing values, or catch errors before a client sees the output.

Learning speed. KPMG works across industries. Interviewers want confidence that you can absorb a new sector quickly, form a hypothesis, and ask the right questions rather than needing to be a domain expert from day one.

Client handling. Even junior BAs at KPMG interact with client stakeholders. Demonstrating that you can manage pushback, communicate with credibility, and build trust early is a clear differentiator.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A four-step preparation approach:

Step 1: Know KPMG's service lines. KPMG India's advisory practice covers management consulting, deal advisory, risk consulting, and technology consulting. Know which practice you are applying to, the type of engagements it handles, and any recent work the firm has published in that space.

Step 2: Build your story bank. Prepare five or six STAR stories covering process improvement, stakeholder conflict, data-driven decisions, cross-functional work, and a situation where your recommendation was challenged or rejected. Each story should be specific enough to be memorable and short enough to tell in under two minutes.

Step 3: Practise case and data exercises. Candidates report that some rounds include a short case discussion or a data interpretation task. Practise structuring profitability, market entry, and operations cases. Be comfortable explaining what a chart or pivot table shows quickly and clearly.

Step 4: Review your tools. KPMG BA roles commonly require Excel and PowerPoint. SQL is a clear advantage, and Power BI or Tableau experience is increasingly valued. Brush up on the functions and features you use most so you can discuss them naturally if asked.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Six mistakes that trip up candidates in KPMG BA interviews:

  1. Jumping to solutions. Starting with an answer before structuring the problem signals that you skip analysis in real work. Always state your framework first.
  1. Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved the process' without specifying what you did, which tool or method you used, and what changed as a result gives the interviewer nothing concrete to assess.
  1. Not researching KPMG's practice areas. Candidates who cannot name the service line they are interviewing for, or who confuse advisory with audit, lose credibility in the opening minutes.
  1. Using jargon without explanation. Mentioning a specific model or method is fine, but only if you can immediately explain why you chose it and what it told you.
  1. Skipping clarifying questions in case discussions. Strong candidates ask one or two questions before they start structuring. It signals genuine consulting behaviour rather than a rehearsed response.
  1. Over-crediting the team. Collaboration is valued, but interviewers are assessing your individual contribution. Use 'I' deliberately for actions you personally took, and save 'we' for team-wide outcomes.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a KPMG India BA interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves two to four rounds, though the exact number varies by practice area and seniority level. Earlier rounds tend to be competency-based, while later rounds may include a case discussion or a conversation with a senior manager or partner. Some roles add a short written exercise or data task.

Does KPMG ask case interviews for BA roles?

Not always, but candidates report that practices such as management consulting and deal advisory often include a case or problem-solving discussion. For technology and risk consulting BA roles, the interview may lean more toward domain knowledge and behavioural questions. It is safer to prepare for a case element regardless of the practice you are targeting.

What tools or skills does KPMG typically expect from a BA candidate?

Excel and PowerPoint are the baseline expectation for most BA roles. Candidates report that SQL knowledge is a clear advantage, and familiarity with Power BI or Tableau is increasingly common as a stated requirement. For analytics-heavy practices, Python or R may come up in conversation, though it is rarely a hard filter at the BA level.

How do I answer 'Why KPMG?' convincingly?

Tie your answer to a specific service line or type of client engagement rather than giving a generic 'Big Four brand' response. Mention a sector or category of problem you want to work on, and connect it to what that particular practice at KPMG is known for. Interviewers respond better to candidates who have clearly researched the practice, not just the firm's name.

What questions should I ask the interviewer at the end?

Ask about the type of projects a BA in this practice typically works on in the early months on the job. Ask what distinguishes the strongest BAs on the team from average performers. Avoid questions whose answers are easily found on the firm's website. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest and show you have considered the role seriously.

Is a KPMG BA interview harder than interviews at other consulting firms?

Difficulty varies by practice area and the specific interview panel. Industry surveys suggest Big Four consulting BA interviews are broadly similar in structure, combining behavioural and case elements. The preparation approach is the same regardless of firm: strong STAR stories, a clear structure when tackling problems, and genuine knowledge of the firm's work in your target practice.
