01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

SatSure is a Bengaluru-based space-tech company that converts satellite imagery and geospatial data into intelligence products for agriculture, banking, insurance, and infrastructure. Their platforms help banks assess crop-loan risk before disbursal, help insurers verify claims without sending field surveyors, and help governments track land use and crop patterns at scale.

The company has grown steadily over the past several years, and as of mid-2026 there are 30 open roles across machine learning, remote sensing, backend engineering, product management, and go-to-market functions. Because the team is compact and highly specialised, each hire is expected to contribute meaningfully from day one.

If you have a background in geospatial tech, agricultural analytics, or Python-heavy data science, SatSure is one of the more distinctive employers in the Indian deep-tech ecosystem. Non-technical candidates with experience in agri-finance, crop insurance, or government advisory work also find relevant openings on the business and partnerships side.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Most candidates report a process of three to four rounds, though the exact structure can vary by team and seniority. The details below are based on publicly reported candidate experiences and may not reflect every hiring track.

Round 1: HR screening call. A recruiter reaches out (often via LinkedIn or email) to confirm your background, notice period, and salary expectations. This is also your chance to clarify the role and the team you would be joining. The call typically lasts around half an hour.

Round 2: Domain or technical interview. For engineering and data science roles this usually covers Python proficiency, ML concepts, and geospatial knowledge including working with raster data, satellite indices like NDVI, and tools such as QGIS or Google Earth Engine. For business roles the conversation centres on your domain knowledge in agri-lending, crop insurance, or government programmes.

Round 3: Take-home assignment or case study. Candidates for technical roles are commonly given a dataset (satellite imagery or tabular agri data) and asked to produce an analysis or a small working model. Business candidates may be asked to prepare a market entry plan or a partner pitch. Expect to spend a few hours on this, and treat it like a real deliverable.

Round 4: Leadership discussion. A final conversation with a senior manager or a founder-level stakeholder. This round tests cultural fit, long-term goals, and how you think about impact-driven work. Decisions on compensation and joining timelines are usually discussed here or in a follow-up call from HR.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

SatSure hires people who are comfortable sitting at the intersection of domain knowledge and data. A few qualities that consistently matter across roles:

For engineering and data science roles: Strong Python skills are non-negotiable. Familiarity with geospatial libraries (rasterio, geopandas, shapely) and ML frameworks (PyTorch, scikit-learn) sets you apart. Experience working with satellite data pipelines or remote sensing projects, even from academic work or open-source contributions, is a significant plus.

For business and partnerships roles: Understanding of how agricultural credit or crop insurance works in India matters more than a generic sales background. Candidates who can speak the language of the customer (bankers, insurers, government officials) and translate that into product feedback tend to move faster through the process.

Across all roles: SatSure values people who are intellectually curious about the problem space, not just the job description. Interviewers will often ask why you want to work on satellite-based agriculture intelligence specifically, so have a genuine answer ready.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the product landscape before your first call. SatSure's public-facing products include Sage (crop monitoring) and Sparta (agricultural lending analytics). Read whatever is publicly available about how these work. Understanding the customer pain point (a bank branch officer approving crop loans without visiting the field) will make your answers sharper.

Brush up on remote sensing basics. Even if you are not applying for a geospatial role, knowing what NDVI means, how Sentinel or Landsat imagery is used, and what crop health indices look like will impress interviewers. Free resources from ISRO and NASA are widely available online.

Prepare a portfolio piece if you can. Candidates who submit a clean Jupyter notebook or a GitHub repository showing geospatial analysis, even on public datasets like MODIS or Sentinel-2, consistently report better outcomes. Treat the take-home assignment as a chance to show production-quality thinking, not just a quick answer.

Research the sectors SatSure serves. Banking correspondents, Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), and district-level agri data are topics that come up in interviews. A quick read of how crop insurance settlement works in India will give you a genuine edge.

On compensation: Publicly reported ranges on platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest competitive deep-tech pay, but SatSure's packages vary by role and seniority. Prepare your expected CTC range and be ready to justify it based on your current package and market benchmarks.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

SatSure operates with a strong sense of mission around using technology for real-world agricultural and social impact. The team is relatively small, so the culture tends to be collaborative and non-hierarchical. Engineers often work directly with domain scientists and business leads, which means you can pick up adjacent knowledge fast, but also that role boundaries are fluid.

The company attracts people who are comfortable with ambiguity and motivated by the idea that their work directly affects farmer livelihoods and credit access. If you are looking for a large corporate environment with rigid processes, this is probably not the right fit. If you want to see your ML model influence a lending decision for a farmer in Vidarbha, the work here is unusually tangible.

Growth paths tend to be merit-based and visible. Because the team is lean, strong performers pick up responsibility quickly. Cross-functional exposure (working with remote sensing scientists if you are an engineer, or sitting in on product reviews if you are in sales) is common and encouraged.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does SatSure hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

SatSure does hire freshers, particularly for data science and geospatial analysis roles, but the bar is high. A strong academic project involving satellite data, ML, or agricultural analytics can substitute for work experience. Most fresher hires come from IITs, NITs, or specialised programmes in geoinformatics. Experienced professionals with two or more years in relevant domains have a broader range of roles to apply to.

How long does the hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on publicly reported candidate timelines, the process typically runs two to four weeks from the first HR call to offer letter. The take-home assignment can add time if you need a few days to complete it properly. Following up with the recruiter after each round is standard practice and is not considered pushy.

What salary can I expect at SatSure?

Glassdoor and publicly reported data suggest compensation at SatSure is competitive within the Indian deep-tech startup bracket, but specific figures vary widely by role, experience, and negotiation. Come into the final round with a well-researched expected CTC based on your current package and market data for your skill set. Equity or ESOPs may be part of the package for senior roles, so ask about the full compensation structure early.

Is remote work available or is the job location-specific?

SatSure is headquartered in Bengaluru and most roles are based there, either fully in-office or on a hybrid schedule. Some senior or field-facing roles may allow partial remote work, but this is decided case by case. Check the specific job listing for location requirements, and clarify during the HR screening call if you have a location constraint.

I do not have a geospatial background. Can I still get hired?

Yes, especially for backend engineering, product, and business roles. However, even non-geospatial candidates benefit from showing basic literacy in what the company does. Spending a few hours understanding satellite indices, what Sentinel imagery looks like, and how SatSure's products are used will make you stand out. Interviewers test for genuine interest in the mission, not just technical fit on paper.
