01 Overview

Overview

Synthesia is a London-based AI video platform that lets companies create professional videos using AI avatars, without cameras, studios, or on-screen presenters. Its primary market is enterprise learning and development, internal communications, and customer training at scale. As of mid-2026, Synthesia has 78 open roles globally, reflecting strong hiring momentum across product, engineering, and go-to-market.

PM roles at Synthesia typically sit at the intersection of AI product development, enterprise SaaS, and responsible AI. Candidates report that interviewers pay close attention to how you reason through the ethical implications of synthetic media alongside the usual product sense and metrics questions. Written communication is weighted more heavily here than at many companies.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Synthesia PM interviews, based on what candidates report and the nature of the product. Grounding every answer in Synthesia's actual use cases, rather than generic PM theory, is the clearest signal you are prepared.

  1. How would you prioritise features for Synthesia's avatar library to serve enterprise customers across different regional markets?
  1. Synthetic media can be misused to create deceptive content. How would you design guardrails inside Synthesia's platform that prevent harm without adding unnecessary friction for legitimate users?
  1. Walk through how you would define success metrics for a new video template feature. What does 'good' look like six months after launch?
  1. A large enterprise customer says their L&D team finds Synthesia too complex and is thinking of churning. How do you diagnose the problem and what do you do?
  1. How would you evaluate adding a self-serve pricing tier alongside Synthesia's existing enterprise motion? What are the risks and the opportunities?
  1. If you had to pick one area to invest in next quarter: better video quality, more language and accent support, or deeper integrations with tools like Workday or SharePoint. How would you make that call?
  1. How would you decide whether Synthesia should build a native AI script-writing assistant versus partnering with an existing AI writing tool?
  1. The AI video space is getting more competitive. How would you approach Synthesia's product differentiation over the next 12-18 months?
  1. How would you measure the ROI of Synthesia for a corporate training team? What data would you need, and from whom?
  1. How would you approach localising Synthesia's avatar and voice offerings for markets like India, the Middle East, or Southeast Asia?
  1. Describe how you would run a discovery sprint to surface the top unmet needs of Synthesia's enterprise users.
  1. Engineering estimates a high-priority feature will take six months. Sales needs it in two months to close a major deal. How do you handle it?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you prioritise features for Synthesia's avatar library to serve enterprise customers across different regional markets?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we ran a B2B SaaS product used by corporate training teams across multiple geographies. Our avatar and localisation backlog had grown large with no agreed order, and every team had a different top priority.

*Task:* I needed to bring a ranked roadmap to quarterly planning within two weeks, with buy-in from sales, customer success, and engineering.

*Action:* I pulled customer usage data and support tickets to identify which markets had the highest activation drop-off after signup. I then interviewed several enterprise customers directly (coordinated through CS) and ran a RICE scoring session with leads from sales, design, and engineering. I also separated requests into two tracks: 'expansion' (new markets) and 'depth' (making existing markets work better), treating them as separate resource pools so one did not crowd out the other.

*Result:* We reached alignment in a single planning cycle rather than three rounds of debate. The 'depth' track shipped two targeted improvements in the following quarter, and one at-risk account renewed after we delivered the localisation fix they had flagged.

---

Q: How would you design guardrails inside Synthesia's platform to prevent misuse without making the product harder to use?

*Situation:* At a previous role, I worked on an AI-powered content tool where we started seeing edge cases of users generating content that violated our terms. Leadership wanted guardrails fast, but the product team was worried about adding friction that would hurt activation.

*Task:* My job was to propose a guardrail system that legal, policy, and product teams could all agree on, without delaying an upcoming enterprise launch.

*Action:* I mapped the risk surface: what content could be generated, who was generating it, and at what volume. I found that violations clustered in a small number of accounts. I proposed a tiered approach: automated content filtering for all users, a human review queue for flagged accounts, and a consent layer for any content involving real people's likenesses. I worked with legal to define what 'consent' meant across different jurisdictions and designed the consent flow to appear at the right moment in the product without blocking the standard creation path.

*Result:* We launched on time. Policy violations dropped measurably in the first quarter after launch, per our internal moderation dashboard. The consent flow added minimal time to the setup experience and customer feedback on it was largely positive.

---

Q: A large enterprise customer says their L&D team finds Synthesia too complex and is thinking of churning. How do you diagnose and respond?

*Situation:* At a previous role, a key enterprise account told their account manager that the platform 'required too much training to use' and they were evaluating alternatives.

*Task:* As the PM for the onboarding and editor experience, I needed to find out whether this was a product problem, an onboarding gap, or a support issue, and respond with a concrete plan quickly.

*Action:* I joined the customer call alongside CS and asked open questions about their team's day-to-day workflow. I then ran a shadow session, watching two of their L&D admins use the product live. The core issue was that the editor showed too many options at once, which overwhelmed non-technical users. I worked with design to prototype a 'simplified mode' that hid advanced controls behind a toggle. In parallel, I worked with CS to set up a tailored onboarding programme for their team size and use case, and kept the customer updated via a shared progress document throughout.

*Result:* The customer decided not to churn. Their team's usage picked up measurably over the following two months per our analytics. The 'simplified mode' concept was later added to the broader product roadmap and shipped for all enterprise customers.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For prioritisation questions (avatars, features, languages, integrations): Use RICE scoring (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort). Show you can separate 'expansion' work from 'depth' work so one track does not starve the other. Always tie your priority back to a north star metric: for Synthesia, think about weekly active creators, videos published per seat, or time-to-first-published-video.

For metrics and success definition: Name a north star metric first, then name two or three input metrics that drive it. For Synthesia, relevant inputs include template adoption rate, editor session length, and enterprise seat utilisation. Avoid listing metrics without explaining what would actually cause them to move.

For product sense and build-vs-buy questions: Use a Jobs-to-be-Done lens. What is the user genuinely trying to accomplish? Then ask whether Synthesia has a structural advantage in building this, or whether a partner does it better. Consider distribution, proprietary data, and switching cost before recommending either path.

For ethical and responsible AI questions: Structure your answer in three layers: who is at risk, what is the mechanism of harm, and what is the least-friction intervention at each layer. Show you can hold user experience and policy requirements at the same time rather than treating them as opposites.

For behavioural questions: STAR format throughout. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences each) and spend most of your time on Action. Quantify the Result where you can, but be honest when specific data is not available to you.

For stakeholder conflict questions (sales deadline vs. engineering timeline): Show you separate facts from feelings first. What is actually achievable vs. what people want? Then identify what the real business risk is and propose a path that does not require anyone to simply capitulate.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Product sense grounded in Synthesia's use cases. Generic PM answers land poorly here. Interviewers want to see that you have thought about the specific workflows of an L&D manager, an internal comms lead, or a customer training team. The more concrete your examples, the better.

Ethical reasoning about synthetic media. This is not optional at Synthesia. The product involves AI-generated likenesses and voices, which carry real risks around consent, misinformation, and identity. Candidates who treat ethical guardrails as a compliance checkbox rather than a design problem typically do not progress past early rounds.

Enterprise software instincts. Synthesia sells to large companies with long sales cycles, procurement processes, and multiple stakeholders inside each account. Show you understand seat-based adoption, the difference between admin and end-user personas, and the gap between signing a deal and driving actual usage.

Data fluency without overengineering. You should be comfortable defining metrics and interpreting data, but interviewers also look for judgement about when to ship and learn rather than waiting for perfect information.

Cross-functional collaboration evidence. PM roles at Synthesia mean working closely with engineering, design, sales, legal, and customer success. Answers that sound like solo heroics, without showing how you brought others along, raise flags.

Clear, structured communication. Candidates report that written exercises (where assigned) are weighted seriously. Practise writing your thinking in plain, direct sentences before any round that involves a take-home component.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before your first round: Sign up for Synthesia's free trial and build a short video end to end. Read their published case studies, especially the enterprise ones. Watch product demo content on their site and YouTube channel. This hands-on familiarity makes your answers concrete rather than theoretical.

One to two weeks before: Write out STAR answers for each of the twelve questions listed above. Focus especially on the ethics, prioritisation, and churn scenarios, as candidates report these come up most often. Time yourself saying answers out loud. This keeps Situation and Task tight and stops you from rambling.

One week before: Run at least two mock interviews with someone who will give honest, specific feedback. Prepare two or three questions to ask your interviewer that show genuine curiosity about the product direction and team structure, not just compensation or headcount.

Day before: Check Synthesia's recent blog, press coverage, and LinkedIn company page for any product launches or company news from the past few months. Knowing a recent context point shows you are engaged with the business, not just the job description.

On the day: Candidates report that Synthesia interviewers typically appreciate directness. If you do not know something, say so and explain how you would find out. Attempting to bluff on a specific metric or product claim tends to hurt more than a clear 'I would need to validate that before committing to a number.'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

1. Giving generic PM answers. Saying 'I would talk to users and prioritise by impact' without referencing Synthesia's actual product, customers, or constraints is the clearest signal you have not prepared for this specific company.

2. Ignoring the ethics dimension. Candidates who jump straight to growth metrics and feature shipping without acknowledging the responsibility that comes with synthetic media rarely clear the first substantive round. Responsible AI is a first-class product concern at Synthesia.

3. Treating it like a B2C consumer app interview. Synthesia is an enterprise product. Framing answers purely around consumer app examples, without bridging to enterprise dynamics like procurement, admin roles, and seat adoption, signals a mismatch.

4. Vague metrics answers. Saying 'I would track engagement' without naming specific metrics, explaining what drives them, or linking them to business outcomes is a common gap at every level. Name a north star, then name the inputs.

5. Underselling cross-functional work. PMs at Synthesia work across a large number of teams. Answers that do not show how you moved a diverse group toward a decision, without having formal authority, miss what the role actually requires.

6. Not preparing for a written or take-home exercise. Candidates report that Synthesia sometimes assigns a written product exercise as part of the process. Submitting a rushed or generic document is a costly mistake given how seriously the team weighs written thinking.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What rounds does the Synthesia PM interview process typically involve?

Candidates report the process typically includes an initial recruiter screen, one or two rounds of product and behavioural interviews with hiring managers or senior PMs, and often a written exercise or take-home case study. Some candidates also report a final round involving cross-functional stakeholders. Round names and order vary by team and level, so ask the recruiter for a clear outline at the start of the process.

How important is domain knowledge in AI or video technology?

You do not need to be an AI researcher or video engineer, but you do need to understand how AI video generation works at a product level and what the business model around it looks like. More important is showing you can reason about technology trade-offs and translate them into product decisions. Candidates who have actually used Synthesia's product before the interview consistently report an advantage over those who have only read about it.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Synthesia if I am based in India?

Synthesia is a London-headquartered company and typically hires PMs close to its offices. For reference, publicly reported PM salary bands in India run from 24-40 LPA for mid-level roles (3-6 years experience) and 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs, though exact compensation at Synthesia specifically is not publicly confirmed. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for the most current figures before any salary discussion.

How long does the interview process usually take from application to offer?

Candidates report the end-to-end process typically runs anywhere from three to six weeks, though it can move faster for urgent roles or slower if panel availability is limited. Asking the recruiter for a rough timeline at the start helps you plan. Delays most commonly happen at the written exercise stage, so submit that component promptly.

Does Synthesia hire PMs remotely from India?

Synthesia's core PM roles are typically based in London or at offices near their main hubs. Fully remote Indian-based PM positions are not commonly cited in recent job postings, though policies can and do change. It is worth reading the specific job description carefully and asking the recruiter directly early in the process rather than assuming either way.
