01 Overview

Overview

Skeps is a fintech company building embedded lending and Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) solutions for merchants and financial institutions. A Technical Program Manager (TPM) at Skeps sits at the intersection of engineering, product, compliance, and business teams, keeping complex programs on track in a regulated, fast-moving environment.

As of July 2026, Skeps has 11 open roles, and the broader TPM market across India lists 313 openings, with Bangalore leading at 41 positions. Skeps interviews tend to focus on your ability to drive cross-functional programs, manage regulatory constraints, and communicate clearly with both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

This guide covers the questions candidates most commonly report, three sample STAR answers, and a practical preparation plan to help you walk in confident.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates typically report from Skeps TPM interviews, shaped by the company's fintech and embedded lending context:

  1. How would you manage a cross-functional program involving engineering, product, and compliance teams simultaneously?
  2. Describe a time you handled a conflict between an engineering deadline and a regulatory or compliance requirement.
  3. How do you track program health and communicate status to senior leadership when things are not going to plan?
  4. Skeps works with merchant partners and financial institutions. How do you manage expectations across external stakeholders?
  5. Walk us through how you would build a program roadmap for a new BNPL feature from scratch.
  6. How do you identify and manage dependencies between multiple engineering teams on a large program?
  7. Describe a time you had to escalate a risk. What was your escalation process, and what was the outcome?
  8. How do you handle scope creep in a fast-moving product environment?
  9. What metrics would you use to measure whether a technical program is on track and delivering business value?
  10. How would you approach a program involving third-party API integrations, such as payment or lending partner APIs?
  11. Tell us about a time you brought alignment between teams that had conflicting priorities.
  12. How do you ensure engineering teams stay unblocked when working on complex, multi-team programs?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you managed a cross-functional program with tight dependencies.

*Situation:* My company was launching a new credit underwriting feature that required inputs from data science, backend engineering, product, legal, and compliance teams, all running on different timelines.

*Task:* I was the TPM responsible for delivering the feature to production by a fixed quarter-end deadline.

*Action:* I started by mapping all cross-team dependencies into a shared tracker and ran weekly syncs where each team flagged blockers early. When the legal review ran longer than planned, I worked with the engineering lead to reorder tasks so the team could build the parts that did not need legal sign-off. I then escalated the legal delay to leadership with a clear impact analysis and two options: a limited launch without the blocked feature, or a two-week slip with full scope.

*Result:* Leadership chose the limited launch. We shipped on time and released the remaining scope two weeks later, with zero compliance issues.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you handled a risk that threatened a program.

*Situation:* Mid-program, I learned that a key third-party lending API our product depended on was being deprecated several months earlier than the vendor had originally communicated.

*Task:* My role was to assess the impact and present a revised plan to leadership before the next review cycle.

*Action:* I pulled together the engineering lead and product manager for an immediate triage. We listed every program milestone that depended on the old API and rated each by impact and migration effort. I drafted a risk memo with three options: accelerate migration, negotiate an extension with the vendor, or reduce launch scope. I also set up a direct call with the vendor, which led to a short extension being granted.

*Result:* The vendor agreed to the extension. We used that time to migrate cleanly, and the program launched with only a minor, pre-communicated delay. Stakeholder trust stayed intact throughout.

---

Q: How do you keep stakeholders aligned when a program is slipping?

*Situation:* A multi-team integration program I owned fell behind schedule after an unexpected infrastructure issue caused a delay spanning two sprints.

*Task:* I needed to re-align five stakeholder groups, including two external partners with contractual dependencies, without losing their confidence.

*Action:* I sent a structured status update the same day I confirmed the delay. The update covered root cause, revised timeline, recovery actions underway, and what was not at risk. I followed up with individual calls for both external partners. I also set up a daily stand-up for the next two weeks to catch any further slippage early.

*Result:* Both external partners stayed on board. The program delivered on the revised timeline and the stakeholder relationships remained strong.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioral questions, use STAR: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, step by step), Result (measurable or observable outcome). Skeps interviewers typically care most about the Action section, so spend the most time there and be specific about your individual contribution, not the team's.

For program design questions (such as 'build a roadmap from scratch'), use a structured breakdown:

  1. Clarify scope and success criteria first
  2. Identify stakeholders and their dependencies
  3. Break work into milestones with clear owners
  4. Define a communication cadence
  5. State how you would track and escalate risks

For stakeholder conflict questions, lead with the principle that you stay neutral and focus on program outcomes, not team politics. Describe how you surface trade-offs clearly to decision-makers rather than resolving conflicts unilaterally.

For fintech-specific questions, show that you understand the stakes of working in a regulated environment. Frame your answers around risk management, compliance timelines, and the cost of getting things wrong in a lending or payments context.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Skeps TPM interviewers typically look for a combination of strong program management fundamentals and fintech context awareness.

Program management depth. Can you run a complex, multi-team program without constant hand-holding? Interviewers look for candidates who proactively identify blockers, manage dependencies, and escalate risks early with clear options, not just status updates.

Communication clarity. TPMs at Skeps interface with engineering, product, compliance, and external partners. Interviewers want to see that you adapt your style: technical with engineers, concise with leadership, and relationship-focused with external stakeholders.

Fintech and regulatory awareness. Skeps operates in lending and embedded finance, where compliance timelines are non-negotiable. Candidates who show they treat a regulatory deadline differently from a product deadline tend to stand out.

Ownership mindset. Skeps is a growth-stage fintech, which means the TPM role requires more initiative than at a large enterprise. Interviewers look for candidates who describe driving outcomes, not just tracking tasks.

Structured thinking. When given an open-ended scenario, strong candidates quickly structure their answer into clear steps rather than thinking out loud without direction.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand Skeps deeply.
Read everything publicly available about Skeps: their product (embedded BNPL and lending APIs for merchants), their merchant and financial institution partnerships, and their position in the Indian fintech market. Understand what a TPM at a lending platform actually manages, including API integrations, compliance programs, partner onboarding, and product launches.

Week 2: Build your STAR story bank.
Write out at least six STAR stories from your past work. Cover these themes: cross-functional dependency management, risk escalation, stakeholder communication, timeline recovery, scope negotiation, and aligning conflicting teams. Fintech or payments experience is a plus, but frame non-fintech stories around regulatory or compliance constraints where possible.

Week 3: Practice out loud.
Practice answering the 12 questions listed in this guide, spoken aloud or with a peer. Keep each answer under three minutes. Record yourself once and check that your Action section is specific, not generic.

Before the interview:
Prepare three strong questions to ask the interviewer. Good examples: what does success look like in the first few months for this role, what are the most complex external dependencies the team is currently managing, and how does the TPM team coordinate with compliance. Ask what programs you would own early on to show you are thinking about impact, not just process.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about what you personally did. TPM interviews fail most often because candidates describe what 'the team' did instead of what they personally drove. Use 'I' when describing your actions, not 'we'.

Not knowing Skeps' business. Candidates who walk in without knowing that Skeps is an embedded lending and BNPL platform lose credibility quickly. Spend time understanding the product before the interview.

Weak answers on risk and escalation. Skeps operates in fintech where risk management is central. Candidates who describe only smooth program executions, with no examples of handling real problems, come across as inexperienced.

Treating all deadlines as equal. In a lending company, a compliance deadline carries a different weight from a feature launch deadline. Showing that you understand this distinction sets you apart.

Not asking good questions. A TPM who asks only generic questions, or asks nothing at all, signals low preparation and low curiosity. Prepare specific, thoughtful questions about the programs you would own and the team structure.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What is the typical interview process for a TPM role at Skeps?

Candidates typically report a process of two to four rounds, often starting with a recruiter screen, followed by a hiring manager conversation, and then one or two panel interviews with cross-functional stakeholders. Some candidates report a program design or case round as well. The exact structure can vary, so ask the recruiter upfront what to expect and how many rounds are planned.

What salary can I expect as a TPM at Skeps?

Skeps has not publicly disclosed salary bands for this role. Based on industry surveys and Glassdoor data for TPM roles at growth-stage fintechs in India, compensation varies significantly by years of experience and location. Ask the recruiter for the band early in the process to avoid surprises at the offer stage.

Does Skeps prefer candidates with fintech experience?

Fintech or payments experience is typically preferred, but candidates report it is not always a hard requirement. Showing a clear understanding of regulated environments, API-driven products, and partner-facing programs can compensate for a lack of direct fintech experience. Frame your past work around compliance constraints, risk management, and external integrations wherever possible.

How important is technical depth for a TPM role at Skeps?

TPM roles at product companies like Skeps generally require enough technical understanding to work credibly with engineering teams and evaluate technical trade-offs. You are typically not expected to write code, but you should be comfortable discussing system architecture, integration dependencies, and technical risk at a conceptual level. Candidates who can read a system design and ask informed questions tend to do well.

Is there a coding round for TPM interviews at Skeps?

Candidates typically do not report a coding round for TPM roles at Skeps. The interviews are more focused on program management scenarios, stakeholder situations, and design questions. That said, confirm with the recruiter whether any technical assessment is included, as formats can change between hiring cycles.

How competitive is the TPM job market in India right now?

As of July 2026, there are 313 TPM openings across India tracked by the knok jobradar, with Bangalore leading at 41 openings. Skeps alone has 11 open roles currently. The market is active and candidates with cross-functional program experience in tech or fintech are in demand, though competition for roles at well-funded fintechs like Skeps is real.
