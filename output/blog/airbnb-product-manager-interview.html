01 Overview

Overview

Airbnb currently has 242 open Product Manager roles, making it one of the more active hirers in the PM space right now. The Airbnb PM interview is known for its depth. It tests product sense, data fluency, strategic thinking, and cultural fit with the company's mission of creating a world where anyone can belong anywhere.

Candidates typically go through multiple stages. Processes vary by team and level, but candidates commonly report a recruiter call, a product sense or case screen, and then a full interview loop covering product design, metrics, strategy, and behavioral questions. This guide covers the questions most frequently cited by candidates, proven answer frameworks, and what Airbnb interviewers are actually looking for.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are most commonly cited by candidates who have interviewed for PM roles at Airbnb. They reflect the company's emphasis on community, trust, belonging, and data-informed decisions across a two-sided marketplace.

  1. How would you improve the host onboarding experience to increase the number of quality listings?
  2. A key booking metric drops significantly week-over-week. Walk me through how you would diagnose it.
  3. Design a feature that helps first-time guests feel confident booking a listing they have never seen in person.
  4. How would you prioritize between improving search relevance and improving checkout conversion?
  5. Tell me about a product you built or improved using limited or conflicting data.
  6. How would you define and measure success for Airbnb Experiences?
  7. What is Airbnb's biggest competitive threat right now, and what product response would you recommend?
  8. How would you design a product or feature to deepen trust between hosts and guests?
  9. Tell me about a time you drove alignment across engineering, design, and business stakeholders on a difficult decision.
  10. If you were the PM for Airbnb's reviews system, what changes would you make and why?
  11. How does Airbnb's mission of 'belonging anywhere' show up concretely in your product decisions?
  12. Design a new product targeting a demographic that Airbnb currently underserves.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Answer 1

Q: Tell me about a product decision you made with limited data.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were launching a new feature for gig workers to manage their earnings calendar. This user segment was new to the platform and we had very little usage data to guide us.

*Task:* I needed to decide between a simple list view and a full calendar interface, with only a short window before the engineering freeze.

*Action:* I ran a short discovery sprint. I conducted unmoderated user tests with a paper prototype, interviewed a small group of gig workers, and reviewed proxy data from competitor app store reviews to understand pain points. I also set up a lightweight test on the feature entry point to measure which framing drove more engagement.

*Result:* The signal pointed clearly to the list view as the faster path to value. We shipped on schedule, and early retention for the segment improved meaningfully in the following quarter. I documented open questions and planned a follow-up research sprint for the next cycle.

---

Answer 2

Q: How would you improve the host onboarding experience?

*Situation:* In a product design exercise during a mock interview, I was asked to redesign Airbnb's host onboarding for new hosts in tier-2 cities in India, where smartphone-first users are common but listing quality is often inconsistent.

*Task:* I needed to identify the biggest friction point in the onboarding journey and propose a focused improvement.

*Action:* I mapped the host journey from signup to the first booking received. I identified photo quality as the biggest drop-off point. Hosts in smaller cities often lacked guidance on photography or room presentation. I proposed an in-app guided photo checklist with real-time tips, combined with an optional 'photo review' feature where a community superhost could give async feedback before the listing went live.

*Result:* The interviewer responded positively. They noted that I had anchored on a specific user segment, tied the proposed feature to a measurable outcome (listing approval rate), and brought in the host community angle, which felt strongly aligned with Airbnb's values.

---

Answer 3

Q: Tell me about a time you drove alignment across stakeholders on a difficult decision.

*Situation:* I was PM for a payments feature at a fintech company. Engineering wanted to delay the launch for additional security hardening. Marketing had already committed to a campaign date. Both teams escalated to me.

*Task:* I needed to find a path that protected users without derailing the campaign entirely.

*Action:* I set up a structured trade-off session with leads from both teams. I created a risk matrix mapping each open security item to its actual user exposure. Several of the items turned out to be low-risk edge cases. I proposed a phased rollout: launch to a small cohort on the original date with the high-risk items addressed, then expand after the remaining items were resolved.

*Result:* Both teams agreed on the phased approach. The campaign launched on schedule with the limited rollout. No security issues arose in the initial cohort, and we expanded to full rollout ahead of the revised schedule.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

HEART Framework (for metrics questions)
Use this when asked how you would measure a feature or product. HEART stands for Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, and Task success. For Airbnb, ground each dimension in platform context. For example, 'Retention' for a host means repeat listings and hosting activity, not just logins.

CIRCLES Method (for product design questions)
Comprehend the situation, Identify the user, Report the user's needs, Cut through prioritization, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarize. This gives structure to open-ended design questions. At Airbnb, always define your user across both sides of the marketplace: guest and host. Skipping the host side is one of the most common ways candidates lose points.

Root Cause Analysis Tree (for metric drop questions)
Start by clarifying the metric definition and the timeframe. Then split potential causes into internal factors (product bugs, recent releases, experiment leakage) versus external factors (seasonality, competitor action, news events). At Airbnb, also separate supply-side causes (host behavior) from demand-side causes (guest behavior), since these require different responses.

Influence Without Authority (for behavioral questions)
For alignment and stakeholder questions, structure your answer around: the competing interests at play, how you understood each side's underlying concern rather than just their stated position, the data or framework you used to build shared understanding, and the process you facilitated to reach a decision. Airbnb values collaborative decision-making, so show your process, not just the outcome.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Airbnb interviewers are typically looking for signals that go beyond standard PM competency.

Deep user empathy. Airbnb's product philosophy centers on belonging. Interviewers want to see that you move beyond generic answers and can articulate the emotional and social context behind product decisions, especially in a two-sided marketplace where hosts and guests have very different motivations and concerns.

Data fluency without data dependency. Airbnb PMs are expected to be comfortable with metrics, funnels, and experimentation. But interviewers also want to see that you can make sound, confident decisions when data is thin or ambiguous. Show that you know when to run an experiment and when to move with conviction.

Community and trust thinking. Reviews, safety, belonging, and reputation are core to the Airbnb model. Candidates who can reason about network effects, trust systems, and community dynamics tend to stand out from those who think purely about conversion or engagement.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Interviewers often leave questions deliberately open-ended. They are testing whether you ask the right clarifying questions before diving in, and whether you can organize your thinking in real time without being prompted.

Genuine mission alignment. Candidates who treat 'belonging anywhere' as a real product lens, not just a talking point, tend to resonate more strongly. The best answers let the mission constrain or shape the solution choices, rather than appearing as a decorative mention.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundation
Read Airbnb's publicly available design philosophy, founder interviews, and blog posts to internalize the belonging mission. Study the end-to-end host and guest journeys on the live product as an active user. Practice one product design question per day using the CIRCLES method, focused specifically on Airbnb products.

Week 2: Metrics and Strategy
Practice root cause analysis with metric-drop scenarios. Review publicly available writing on marketplace metrics, supply-demand balance, and trust systems. Pick two or three Airbnb features (search, reviews, Experiences) and define HEART metrics for each.

Week 3: Behavioral and Mock Interviews
Map your past projects to Airbnb's values: belonging, community, trust, and design quality. Prepare STAR stories for common themes: driving alignment across stakeholders, making decisions with limited data, advocating for the user, and handling setbacks. Do at least two full mock interviews with a peer or coach.

Week 4: Polish and Active Search
Review your answers for specificity and cut any generic statements. Research the specific Airbnb team you are interviewing with. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Ignoring the two-sided marketplace. A very common error is designing or analyzing Airbnb products only from the guest's perspective. Every product decision at Airbnb has host-side implications. Always address both sides in your answers.

Being too generic on product sense. Answers like 'I would talk to users and look at the data' do not land well. Airbnb interviewers expect specificity: which users, what data, and why those choices matter in a trust-based marketplace.

Jumping to solutions before defining the problem. On design questions, many candidates skip clarification and jump straight to features. Take a moment to frame the user segment, the core goal, and the constraints before proposing any solution.

Treating mission as decoration. Mentioning 'Airbnb believes in belonging' and then ignoring it in your answer reads as rehearsed. Let the mission actually constrain your recommendations. A feature that increases bookings but erodes host trust is not a good Airbnb product trade-off.

Vague outcomes in STAR stories. Results like 'the feature did well' are a red flag. Use relative language ('ahead of our cohort target,' 'top performer in the quarter') or reference publicly reported benchmarks where honest and relevant.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Airbnb PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screen, one or two product or case screens, and then a full interview loop with multiple interviewers. The loop commonly covers product design, metrics, strategy, and behavioral questions. Processes vary by role and level, so confirm the exact format with your recruiter early in the process.

Does Airbnb give a take-home assignment for PM roles?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home product case as part of the screening process, while others move directly to live interview rounds. This varies by team and role level. Ask your recruiter at the start so you know what to expect and can prepare the right way.

How important is marketplace experience for an Airbnb PM role?

It is valued but not always required. Airbnb operates a two-sided marketplace connecting hosts and guests, so experience with supply-demand dynamics, trust systems, or network effects strengthens your candidacy. If you do not have direct marketplace experience, demonstrating the right mental models through your prep and answers goes a long way.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Airbnb in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level up to 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level. Mid-level PM roles with 3-6 years of experience typically fall in the 24-40 LPA range. Actual offers vary based on experience, the specific team, and how well you negotiate.

Should I focus more on product design or metrics questions for Airbnb?

Candidates report both types appearing consistently across rounds, so you need to prepare for both. Product design questions at Airbnb often have a trust or community angle, while metrics questions frequently involve diagnosing drops in booking-related or supply-side indicators. Choosing one over the other to prioritize is a preparation mistake.

How should I handle a question I genuinely do not know the answer to?

Think out loud and structure your uncertainty. Airbnb interviewers are often more interested in how you reason through an unfamiliar problem than whether you arrive at a specific answer. State what you know, name the assumptions you are making, and explain how you would seek out the missing information. Silence or random guessing are the things to avoid.
