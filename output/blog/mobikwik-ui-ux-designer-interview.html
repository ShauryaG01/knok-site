01 Overview

Overview

MobiKwik is one of India's established fintech companies, known for its digital wallet, buy-now-pay-later offering (Zip), merchant payments, and insurance products. Designing for MobiKwik means making financial tools simple and trustworthy for a very wide range of users, from tech-savvy urban professionals to first-time digital payment users in smaller cities.

The interview process typically includes a portfolio walkthrough, a design exercise (whiteboard or take-home), and a final discussion with a product or design lead. Candidates report that interviewers focus heavily on design thinking, how you handle constraints, and whether you can simplify complex financial flows for everyday users.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 29 UI/UX Designer openings across India, with Delhi (8 openings) and Bangalore (6 openings) showing the highest activity. MobiKwik currently has 32 open roles across all functions.

Most Asked Questions

  10. What metrics do you look at to know if a design change was successful?
  11. Describe your experience with design systems. Have you built one or contributed to one?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a project where you improved a payment flow.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the analytics team flagged that drop-off during the checkout step was noticeably high.

*Task:* I was asked to audit the existing flow and propose a redesign that would reduce friction, especially for first-time users.

*Action:* I ran several user interviews, mapped the journey, and identified three main pain points: too many form fields, unclear error messages, and a confusing OTP screen. I prototyped two alternative flows in Figma, ran usability tests with a small group of participants, and iterated based on what we heard. I worked closely with the front-end team to keep the final design within their technical constraints.

*Result:* After launch, the product team reported a measurable improvement in checkout completion, which they shared in the quarterly review. The redesigned OTP screen specifically received positive comments in in-app feedback.

---

Q: How do you design for trust in a fintech app?

*Situation:* While working on a lending product, user research showed that many applicants were abandoning the application mid-way because they were unsure whether their data was safe.

*Task:* I needed to redesign the application screens to build confidence without adding extra steps or friction.

*Action:* I broke the form into smaller, clearly labelled steps, added progress indicators, and included short trust signals near sensitive fields (for example, 'Your data is encrypted'). I used familiar iconography rather than custom illustrations that users might not recognise. I then tested the revised screens with a group of users who were new to digital lending, including participants from smaller cities.

*Result:* The product manager noted improved completion rates in the pilot cohort. Candidates report that this kind of iterative, user-tested approach is exactly what fintech teams look for in a portfolio walkthrough.

---

Q: Describe a time you pushed back on a product manager's brief.

*Situation:* A product manager asked me to add several new features to the wallet home screen in a single release, citing pressure from the leadership team.

*Task:* My concern was that adding everything at once would clutter the screen and confuse users already familiar with the layout.

*Action:* I prepared a short document showing how each addition would affect the visual hierarchy, referenced findings from a previous usability study, and proposed a phased rollout instead. I presented two clear options with trade-offs spelled out for each, so the PM could make an informed call.

*Result:* The PM agreed to phase the releases. The first phase launched on schedule, user feedback was positive, and the approach built stronger trust between design and product for future decisions.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most commonly expected format for behavioural questions. Structure every story with a clear problem, your specific role, the steps you took, and a concrete outcome. Practice this out loud before your interview so it feels natural rather than rehearsed.

Design critique format. When asked to critique MobiKwik's app or a competitor's product, follow this order: state what you observed (not what you assume), explain the likely user impact, then suggest one improvement with a clear rationale. Avoid vague feedback like 'the UI looks cluttered' without tying it to a specific user need.

Problem-framing before solutions. For whiteboard or take-home tasks, spend the first few minutes stating your assumptions, defining the target user, and naming the success metric before you sketch anything. Interviewers care more about your thinking than pixel-perfect output.

Trade-off framing. When asked how you handle constraints (time, engineering scope, business requirements), structure your answer as: here is what I prioritised, here is what I deferred, and here is why. This shows maturity and judgment rather than avoidance of hard calls.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

MobiKwik interviewers typically look for designers who can balance simplicity with the real complexity of financial products. Money is high-stakes: users can lose trust instantly if a flow is confusing or a transaction feels unsafe. Interviewers want to see that you take that seriously.

Portfolio depth over breadth. Bring two or three case studies you can speak to in detail. Interviewers will probe your process at every step, not just admire your screens. Be ready to explain why you made each major decision.

User empathy for diverse audiences. MobiKwik serves users across income levels, languages, and levels of digital familiarity. Candidates who show they have thought about accessibility, regional language considerations, or low-bandwidth scenarios tend to stand out.

Collaboration signals. Interviewers want evidence that you work well with product managers, engineers, and data analysts. Use 'we' when the work was shared, but be specific about what you personally contributed.

Business awareness. Understanding why a feature exists, not just how it looks, is valued. Know MobiKwik's main product lines: the wallet, Zip (BNPL), merchant payments, and insurance. Referencing their actual product in your answers signals genuine preparation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Research the product first.

Download the MobiKwik app and use it end-to-end. Note screens that feel smooth and ones that feel confusing. Read publicly reported news about MobiKwik's product direction and any recent updates. Explore how competitor apps in the payments space handle similar flows so you have a point of comparison ready.

Tighten your portfolio.

Select two or three case studies most relevant to fintech or consumer apps. Write out the STAR narrative for each. Practice presenting each case study with a time limit, because candidates report that portfolio walkthroughs often have tight time slots. Make sure every key screen has a 'why' you can articulate on the spot.

Practice design exercises.

Give yourself a vague brief (for example, 'redesign the MobiKwik onboarding for a first-time user in a Tier 2 city') and a strict time limit. State your assumptions and success metric before sketching. Ask a peer or mentor to give you feedback using the critique format described in the Answer Frameworks section.

Run a mock interview.

Prepare answers to all the questions in the Most Asked Questions section. Review your Figma proficiency, because MobiKwik teams typically work in Figma and candidates report occasional live design exercises. Research UI/UX Designer compensation on Glassdoor and levels.fyi so you can negotiate from a position of knowledge.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Showing screens without explaining decisions. Many candidates present a polished portfolio but cannot say why they chose a specific layout, colour, or interaction. Interviewers ask 'why' at every step. Practice explaining every major decision before the interview.

Skipping the user in your answers. Saying 'I made it cleaner' is weaker than 'users in our testing found the previous layout confusing because there were too many competing actions.' Always anchor your design decisions to a user insight.

Not critiquing MobiKwik's own app. Interviewers sometimes ask you to evaluate their product. Candidates who give only compliments come across as unprepared. Be respectful, specific, and solution-oriented when you spot an opportunity to improve something.

Ignoring constraints. Presenting a design that does not account for engineering timelines or business goals signals that you work in isolation. Always mention the constraints you were operating within and how they shaped your choices.

Underselling collaboration. UI/UX is a team activity. If you only describe individual contributions and never mention working with product, engineering, or data teams, interviewers may worry about how you operate in a cross-functional environment.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask thoughtful questions about the team's design process, tooling, or upcoming product priorities leave a stronger impression than those who have nothing to ask.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does MobiKwik typically have for a UI/UX Designer role?

Candidates report typically going through two to three rounds: a portfolio walkthrough, a design exercise (whiteboard or take-home), and a final discussion with a product or design lead. The exact number of rounds can vary by seniority level and the hiring manager's preference. Confirm the format with the recruiter when you receive your interview invite so you can prepare the right materials.

Does MobiKwik give a take-home design assignment?

Candidates report that MobiKwik sometimes uses a take-home assignment, particularly for mid-to-senior level roles. The brief is typically open-ended, asking you to redesign a specific flow or propose a new feature for an existing product. Focus on showing your thinking process and your rationale for each decision rather than just polished visuals. You will typically be asked to present your solution in a follow-up call, so practice walking through your choices out loud.

What design tools should I be proficient in for a MobiKwik interview?

Figma is the most commonly cited tool for product design teams in India, and candidates report it is used by MobiKwik design teams as well. Familiarity with prototyping in Figma, working with component libraries, and preparing designs for developer handoff is expected at most experience levels. Knowledge of usability testing tools is a useful bonus but is not typically a hard requirement based on candidate reports.

What salary can I expect as a UI/UX Designer at MobiKwik?

MobiKwik has not publicly listed salary bands for this role, so specific figures are not available here. For market benchmarks, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi, which carry publicly reported compensation data for UI/UX Designers at Indian fintech companies across different experience levels. Going into salary discussions with a well-researched range puts you in a stronger negotiating position.

Is fintech experience required for this role at MobiKwik?

Fintech experience is helpful but candidates report it is not always a hard requirement, especially for junior roles. Interviewers typically care more about your ability to simplify complex flows, design for diverse users, and show empathy for people who may be new to digital financial products. If your portfolio does not include fintech work, frame it around complexity, user empathy, and measurable outcomes, and you can still make a strong impression.
