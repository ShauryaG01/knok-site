01 Overview

Overview

Outside Capital is an investment firm focused on backing founders and management teams across private markets. The Business Analyst role puts you at the centre of deal evaluation, financial modelling, market research, and portfolio monitoring. With 92 open roles at the company as of mid-2026, hiring is active and competition for the BA position is strong.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a situation where your analysis directly influenced a business or investment decision.

*Situation:* My team was evaluating whether to expand our product line into a new geographic market. The initial assumption was that the move would be profitable within the first year.

*Task:* I was asked to build a financial model to validate that assumption and present findings to the leadership team.

*Action:* I gathered publicly available market data, benchmarked against comparable expansions, and built a scenario model with base, bull, and bear cases. I found that the bear case showed a loss in year one due to higher-than-expected distribution costs. I flagged this clearly and recommended a phased entry with a smaller pilot first.

*Result:* Leadership adopted the phased approach. The pilot confirmed the distribution cost issue, and the company avoided a larger capital commitment at the wrong time.

---

Q: How do you prioritise when two deal teams need your analysis at the same time?

*Situation:* During a previous role, two senior managers came to me on the same day, both with urgent modelling requests tied to live deals.

*Task:* I needed to deliver quality work for both without compromising either.

*Action:* I quickly assessed the deadlines, complexity of each request, and the stage each deal was at. I spoke directly with both managers, explained the situation transparently, and agreed on a sequencing plan. I completed a quick preliminary output for the more time-sensitive deal first, then followed up with the fuller analysis for the second.

*Result:* Both deals moved forward on schedule. The managers appreciated the direct communication rather than silent delays.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you spotted a risk that others had overlooked.

*Situation:* My team was preparing a market entry analysis and had built revenue projections based on total addressable market data from a single industry report.

*Task:* I was reviewing the model before it went to senior leadership.

*Action:* I noticed the report was based on data that was several years old and had not accounted for a major regulatory change in that sector. I cross-referenced with more recent government filings and industry news, recalculated the addressable market, and found the actual opportunity was smaller than the model showed.

*Result:* The team revised the projections before presenting to leadership, avoiding an overly optimistic case that could have led to a poor capital allocation decision.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The STAR framework (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the default for behavioural questions. Keep each component tight: two to three sentences per section is enough. The Result must be concrete, but if you do not have a precise number, describe the outcome qualitatively and honestly.

For case and market sizing questions, use a top-down or bottom-up approach and state clearly which one you are using and why. Think out loud. Interviewers at Outside Capital are typically more interested in your reasoning process than the final number.

For financial modelling questions, structure your answer around: inputs and assumptions, model mechanics, sensitivity analysis, and how you would present findings. Mentioning scenario analysis (base, bull, bear) signals analytical maturity.

For 'why Outside Capital' questions, connect your specific interest to what the firm does. Reference their focus on private markets or backing founder-led businesses if that matches your career goals. Generic answers read as low-effort.

A simple framework for any 'how would you approach X' question:

StepWhat to cover
1. ClarifyConfirm the goal, scope, and constraints
2. StructureBreak the problem into components
3. AnalyseWork through each component with logic
4. SynthesisePull the pieces into a recommendation
5. CommunicateExplain your reasoning simply and clearly

This structure works whether you are answering a market sizing case, a 'how would you build this model' question, or a general problem-solving prompt.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Outside Capital interviewers are typically looking for a few things that go beyond technical skills.

Analytical rigour without arrogance. They want to see that you check your assumptions, flag uncertainty, and know the limits of your own model. Candidates who present numbers without caveats often raise red flags.

Structured communication. Investment teams deal with large volumes of information. They want to hire people who can take a messy dataset and tell a clean story. Practice giving your answer in a clear structure: context first, then analysis, then recommendation.

Commercial awareness. You are not just building spreadsheets. You need to understand why the numbers matter. Candidates who connect model outputs to business decisions tend to stand out.

Coachability. Interviewers may push back on your assumptions or your approach mid-answer. This is often a test of how you respond to challenge. Stay calm, engage with the feedback, and adjust your reasoning if it makes sense.

Proactiveness. The BA role at Outside Capital reportedly involves working across multiple deal teams with limited hand-holding. Showing that you take initiative and surface problems early is valued.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build the technical foundation.

Refresh your financial modelling skills covering DCF, comparable company analysis (comps), and LBO basics. If you are newer to these, free resources from corporate finance training sites are a solid starting point. Practice building a model from scratch in Excel or Google Sheets without a template.

Week 2: Case and behavioural prep.

Practice at least five market sizing cases out loud and time yourself. Then prepare STAR answers for the most common behavioural themes: handling pressure, influencing stakeholders, dealing with ambiguity, and catching errors in analysis.

Research Outside Capital specifically. Understand their investment focus, any publicly reported portfolio companies, and the sectors they cover. This directly feeds into your 'why us' answer and helps you tailor case responses to the firm's actual context.

Mock interviews. Do at least one mock interview with someone who can give honest feedback, ideally a peer who has been through finance interviews. Record yourself if no one is available and watch it back.

Day before the interview: Review your STAR stories, recheck your model basics, and prepare two to three sharp questions to ask the interviewer. Good questions show genuine curiosity about the work, not just the offer.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'so what'. Many candidates walk through their analysis in detail but never land on a clear recommendation. Always end your answer with what you concluded and what action you recommended.

Overclaiming precision. Saying 'the market is worth exactly X' without caveats sounds confident but reads as naive to experienced investors. Hedge appropriately: 'based on publicly available data, the market is estimated at around X, though the range could be wider given...'

Not asking clarifying questions in case interviews. Jumping straight into a case without confirming the goal and constraints is a common error. Take a moment to clarify scope before you start.

Weak 'why Outside Capital' answers. Saying you want to work here because it is a 'great firm with smart people' tells the interviewer nothing. Research the firm's focus and connect it to your specific goals.

Ignoring the process itself. Candidates sometimes focus entirely on the answer and forget to explain their thinking. Interviewers at Outside Capital typically want to see how you approach a problem, not just what you concluded.

Rushing through behavioural questions. These are not warm-up questions. A well-structured STAR answer with a clear outcome can be just as impressive as a strong technical response.

How many rounds does the Outside Capital BA interview process typically have?

Candidates typically report two to four rounds, starting with a screening call, followed by one or more technical rounds covering financial modelling and case studies, and ending with a senior or partner-level conversation. The exact structure can vary by team, so it is worth asking your recruiter at the start. Some candidates also report a take-home modelling assignment between rounds.

What level of financial modelling skill do I need for the BA role?

You are expected to be comfortable with DCF modelling, comparable company analysis, and basic LBO concepts. You do not need to be an Excel power user, but you should be able to build a model from scratch and explain your assumptions clearly. Candidates who can also walk through scenario and sensitivity analysis tend to get stronger feedback from interviewers.

Is the Outside Capital BA interview more technical or behavioural?

Candidates report it leans technical, especially for those coming from finance or consulting backgrounds. However, the behavioural component is not lightweight. Interviewers care about how you communicate, how you handle pressure, and whether you can work across deal teams. Prepare equally for both sides of the interview.
