01 Overview

Overview

Narmi builds digital banking software for community banks and credit unions, helping smaller financial institutions offer modern online and mobile banking to their customers. As a PM at Narmi, you sit between fintech engineering and bank operators who are your direct clients, while keeping end depositors in mind at all times. The interview process typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two product rounds, and a final conversation with senior leadership, though candidates report variations by role level. Narmi currently has 12 open PM roles, reflecting active product team growth. For general compensation context, industry surveys place fintech PM market bands at 24-40 LPA for mid-level roles (3-6 years of experience) and 40-60 LPA for senior-level roles.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you prioritize a backlog when three different bank clients each want a different feature shipped next quarter?

*Situation:* At my previous company, three enterprise clients simultaneously escalated feature requests, each claiming their ask was blocking a contract renewal.

*Task:* I needed to decide what to build next without losing any of the three clients or committing the engineering team to an impossible sprint.

*Action:* I mapped each request to its underlying problem and found that two of the three traced back to the same workflow gap, just described differently. I scheduled a joint discovery call with those two clients, showed them a unified solution, and got their buy-in. For the third unique request, I negotiated a timeline extension by offering a workaround using existing configuration options.

*Result:* We shipped one feature that satisfied two clients, kept the third engaged with a clear roadmap date, and engineering delivered on schedule with no scope creep.

---

Q: A community bank client tells you their mobile app loan completion rate is dropping. What do you do?

*Situation:* At a B2B SaaS company, a key client flagged that a core workflow metric was declining and threatened to escalate to our leadership team.

*Task:* I had to diagnose whether this was a product bug, a UX issue, an external factor, or a data error, and give the client an honest update within two business days.

*Action:* I pulled funnel analytics to find the exact drop-off step, cross-referenced it with the recent release log, and interviewed two of the client's branch managers. The issue traced back to a field validation change we had shipped that confused users on older Android versions. I coordinated a hotfix with engineering and sent the client daily updates until it resolved.

*Result:* Completion rate returned to baseline within a week, and the client cited our responsiveness in their renewal conversation as a strong positive.

---

Q: How do you ensure a new feature stays compliant with banking regulations without slowing down the release cycle?

*Situation:* My team was building a new document-upload flow for loan onboarding that touched KYC requirements.

*Task:* Legal and compliance needed review time, but product had a committed launch date to a client that had already announced the feature to their members.

*Action:* I brought compliance into the design sprint at week one instead of week six. We agreed on a compliance checklist embedded in our definition of done, so no ticket moved to staging without a compliance sign-off comment. I also scoped the first release to exclude edge cases needing deeper legal review, shipping the core flow on time.

*Result:* We hit the launch date, passed the compliance review with zero blocking issues, and the remaining scope shipped in the following sprint without any rework.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The dual-customer lens is critical at Narmi. Always name both your direct customer (the bank or credit union) and the end customer (the depositor or member) in your answer. Interviewers want to see that you hold both perspectives at once without conflating them.

RICE for B2B backlog decisions. When prioritizing, apply Reach (how many bank clients benefit), Impact (depth of value to their end users), Confidence (how well you understand the problem), and Effort (engineering cost). This keeps your reasoning concrete and easy to defend in the room.

Compliance-first scoping. For any feature touching data, payments, or onboarding, show that you think about regulatory constraints upfront rather than as a late gate. Mentioning FFIEC, KYC/AML, or data residency where it fits naturally signals genuine fintech domain awareness.

Metrics layering. Lead with a business outcome (client retention, contract renewal), connect it to a product metric (feature adoption, task completion rate), and add a guardrail metric (error rate, compliance flag rate). This three-layer structure shows PM maturity and works for almost any question at Narmi.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Narmi interviewers typically look for candidates who understand the specific pressures community banks face: limited IT budgets, legacy core systems, tight regulatory oversight, and competition from large national banks and neobanks. They want PMs who can translate that context into clear product decisions rather than generic feature lists.

Fintech fluency. You do not need a banking background, but knowing terms like core banking, KYC, ACH, and FFIEC at a conversational level matters. Candidates who clearly researched Narmi's market before the interview consistently report stronger outcomes than those who treat it like any other SaaS interview.

Client empathy without being sales-driven. Narmi is B2B, so you will discuss bank operators as customers. But the strongest answers keep the depositor experience in view as well. Interviewers flag candidates who think only about the bank and forget the end user entirely.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Expect open-ended 'how would you' questions with no single right answer. Think out loud, state your assumptions, and show a repeatable method. Narmi PMs regularly work on complex integrations where ambiguity is the default, and interviewers want to see how you navigate that.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know Narmi's product. Look for any public product demos, walkthroughs, or case studies Narmi has published. Read their blog and press releases to understand their current product focus and recent launches. Note the exact language they use to describe community banking challenges, then mirror that language in your answers.

Week 2: Build your fintech vocabulary. Get comfortable with these concepts: digital account opening, KYC/AML, ACH transfers, core banking integrations (FIS, Fiserv, Jack Henry), and FFIEC compliance basics. You do not need deep expertise, but you should be able to speak to each term naturally without fumbling.

Week 3: Practice out loud. Take the questions in this guide and time yourself answering each in under three minutes. Record yourself once and listen back for vague metrics and filler phrases. Narmi interviews typically involve live problem-solving, so verbal fluency matters alongside the structure of your answer.

Before the interview. Review Narmi's recent news, check LinkedIn for your interviewers' backgrounds, and prepare two or three specific questions about their product direction. Asking about their biggest current product challenge signals genuine curiosity rather than checkbox preparation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating the bank as the only customer. Many candidates talk only about the bank client's needs and never mention the depositor or member. At Narmi, strong PM thinking holds both in frame simultaneously.

Generic prioritization answers. Saying 'I would talk to stakeholders and prioritize by impact' without naming specific trade-offs signals you have not thought about B2B fintech constraints. Use a structured method like RICE and name real constraints such as compliance review cycles or core banking integration timelines.

Ignoring regulatory context. Candidates who skip past compliance when designing a feature often raise flags at Narmi. Even a brief mention that you would loop in compliance early shows domain awareness without overstating your expertise.

Overselling customization to a single client. When asked about a client requesting a unique feature, candidates who immediately say 'yes, let us build it' miss the tension between client satisfaction and platform scalability. Show that you weigh both and have a principled way to decide.

Not researching Narmi's actual product. Interviewers notice quickly when answers could apply to any SaaS company. Use Narmi's market context, community banking terminology, and specific product area language wherever it fits naturally in your answers.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Narmi PM interview process typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to five conversations. This usually includes a recruiter screen, one or two product or case rounds, and a final round with a senior leader. The exact format varies by role level and team, so confirm the structure with your recruiter at the very start of the process.

Do I need a banking or fintech background to get a PM role at Narmi?

A formal banking background is not required, but familiarity with community banking concepts helps significantly. Candidates report that knowing terms like KYC, ACH, and core banking integrations, and understanding the pressures smaller financial institutions face, makes a noticeable impression. You can build this foundation in one to two weeks of focused reading before the interview.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Narmi?

Narmi is a US-based company, so compensation for US-based roles follows US market rates. For fintech PM roles in India more broadly, industry surveys suggest mid-level PM bands of 24-40 LPA and senior PM bands of 40-60 LPA as a general reference point. Always confirm current compensation directly with Narmi's recruiter, as ranges vary significantly by location and experience level.

What does the Narmi case study or take-home assignment typically look like?

Candidates report receiving a product case focused on a community banking problem, though the exact format varies by role. Typical prompts ask you to design a feature, prioritize a roadmap, or diagnose a dropping product metric. Interviewers look for structured thinking, clear B2B fintech trade-off reasoning, and how you handle regulatory or integration constraints within your solution.

How important is metrics knowledge for the Narmi PM interview?

Very important. Narmi interviewers typically expect you to define success metrics for any feature you design or discuss. Practice layering a business outcome like client retention with a product metric like feature adoption or task completion rate, plus a guardrail metric such as error rate or compliance flag rate. Vague answers that mention only 'engagement' without specifics are commonly flagged as weak in B2B PM interviews.

How competitive is the Narmi PM hiring process?

Narmi currently lists 12 open PM roles, which suggests active hiring across the product team rather than a single highly contested position. Competitiveness varies by seniority and the specific team you are interviewing for. Candidates who demonstrate genuine knowledge of community banking challenges alongside strong product fundamentals tend to stand out against applicants who bring only general SaaS PM backgrounds.
