01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Testbook is one of India's fastest-growing edtech platforms, built around competitive exam preparation for government jobs, banking, SSC, railways, and defence services. The platform serves millions of students across the country and has expanded significantly since 2024. As of mid-2026, Testbook has 96 open roles spanning engineering, product, content, marketing, sales, and operations. Most positions are based in Mumbai, with select remote and hybrid options depending on the team.

Hiring at Testbook is active and structured, but the company moves at a startup pace. Expect quicker turnarounds than a large corporate. The culture values ownership, data-driven thinking, and genuine passion for education. If you care about helping exam aspirants land government jobs, that enthusiasm will come across in every round.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Testbook's interview process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact number varies by role and team. The stages below reflect commonly reported patterns.

Stage 1: Application and resume shortlisting. Your resume is reviewed against the job description. For technical roles, a short coding or domain assignment may be sent at this stage to filter candidates before any calls happen.

Stage 2: HR screening call. A brief introductory call with a recruiter. They cover your background, notice period, salary expectations, and whether your goals fit what Testbook is building. Keep your answers crisp and be ready to explain why edtech interests you. No specific duration is standard, so treat any slot they offer as a focused conversation.

Stage 3: Technical or domain round. This is the main evaluation. For engineers, expect data structures, algorithms, and system design questions relevant to your level. For product roles, expect case studies on product thinking, metrics, and prioritisation. Content and teaching roles may involve a topic presentation or a writing sample. This round is sometimes split into two separate calls.

Stage 4: Hiring manager round. A senior team member assesses culture fit, depth of experience, and how you handle ambiguous problems. They will ask about past projects, what you built, and how you responded to setbacks. Be specific with examples rather than speaking in general terms.

Stage 5: Offer and verification. If you clear the above rounds, HR shares a verbal offer followed by a written letter. Background and document verification happens before your joining date.

The overall timeline from application to offer commonly spans a few weeks, though urgent roles or senior-level searches can move faster or slower.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Testbook looks for people who combine strong functional skills with a genuine interest in education and scale.

For engineering roles: Solid fundamentals in data structures and algorithms, experience building for large user bases, and the ability to write clean and maintainable code. Familiarity with consumer internet or edtech products is a plus.

For product roles: Structured thinking, comfort with metrics, and the ability to define clear problem statements. Testbook's products touch millions of students, so product managers who balance speed with user empathy tend to do well.

For content and teaching roles: Deep subject matter expertise, the ability to break down complex topics simply, and familiarity with how competitive exams are structured. Hindi proficiency is often relevant given the student base.

For sales and operations: Strong communication, a target-driven mindset, and comfort working in an environment where priorities shift quickly.

Across all roles, Testbook values people who take ownership, are comfortable with ambiguity, and genuinely care about student outcomes rather than just personal KPIs.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product before your first call. Download the Testbook app, attempt a mock test, and explore the interface as a student would. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually used the product versus just skimmed the website.

Understand the competitive exam space. Know which exams Testbook covers, who the students are (first-time job seekers, government exam aspirants, students from Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities), and what problems they face. This context helps in every round, not just the HR call.

For technical candidates: Revise core computer science fundamentals and practice coding problems focused on data structures and algorithms. Be ready to discuss system design for mid or senior-level roles.

For product candidates: Prepare structured answers using standard product frameworks. Think through how you would improve one Testbook feature and be ready to discuss metrics you have worked with and how you prioritised competing demands.

For content roles: Have a short teaching demo ready. Pick a topic from a government exam syllabus and be prepared to explain it clearly and engagingly, as if speaking to a student hearing it for the first time.

Follow up thoughtfully. After each round, send a brief note to your recruiter. It keeps you top of mind and signals professionalism.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Testbook operates with a startup mindset despite its scale. Speed, ownership, and impact are the norms rather than exceptions. Teams are lean, which means you are expected to make decisions rather than wait for approvals to come down from above.

Mission-driven work. The company's core belief is that quality exam preparation should be accessible and affordable for every Indian student, regardless of where they live. That mission shapes hiring. People motivated purely by compensation tend to find the pace difficult; people who want to see their work reach millions of exam aspirants find it energising.

Data over opinion. Decisions at Testbook are expected to be backed by data. Whether you are in product, marketing, or content, being comfortable reading metrics and drawing conclusions from them is part of daily life.

Flat and direct. Communication is fairly direct. Junior team members are expected to voice opinions, push back with reasoning, and take ownership of outcomes. There is less hierarchy than at a large corporate.

Fast growth for the right people. Because the company is scaling, roles evolve quickly. People who joined in one capacity often find their responsibilities expanding within a year. That is exciting if you want to grow fast, though it also means your role today may look quite different in the months ahead.
