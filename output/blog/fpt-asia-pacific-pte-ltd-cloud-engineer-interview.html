01 Overview

Overview

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these topics coming up frequently in FPT Asia Pacific Cloud Engineer interviews:

  1. Walk us through a complex cloud migration you led. What was your approach to planning and execution?
  2. How do you design a high-availability, fault-tolerant architecture for a client workload on AWS or Azure?
  3. A client's cloud bill has spiked significantly over one quarter. How do you diagnose and fix the cost overrun?
  4. What is your hands-on experience with Infrastructure as Code? Walk us through a Terraform or CloudFormation project you built.
  5. How do you set up CI/CD pipelines for applications being moved from on-premise to cloud?
  6. Describe how you handle cloud security and compliance requirements, especially for clients in regulated sectors like BFSI or healthcare.
  7. What monitoring and alerting stack have you used to ensure SLA compliance for cloud-hosted applications?
  8. You deploy a change to production and the application goes down. Walk us through exactly what you do next.
  9. FPT works with clients across multiple industries. How do you get up to speed on a new client's environment quickly?
  10. How do you handle a situation where the client insists on an architectural approach you believe is wrong?
  11. Explain the difference between containerisation and serverless. When would you recommend each to a client?
  12. How do you keep your cloud certifications and knowledge current given how fast the platforms evolve?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a complex cloud migration you led.

*Situation:* My previous team was asked to migrate a legacy on-premise data processing application for a manufacturing client to AWS. The application had not been updated in years, had no documentation, and the client needed zero downtime during the move.

*Task:* I was responsible for the migration architecture, the timeline, and coordinating with the client's internal IT team, who had limited cloud experience.

*Action:* I started with a thorough discovery phase, mapping every dependency before writing a single line of Terraform. I used AWS Application Migration Service for the lift-and-shift phase, while simultaneously working with the client's team to refactor the most critical components for better cloud-native performance. I set up parallel running environments and used feature flags to gradually shift traffic.

*Result:* The migration completed with no production downtime. The client's team felt confident managing the environment going forward because I had documented everything and ran two knowledge-transfer sessions with them. The project was cited internally as a reference case for future client migrations.

---

Q: A client's cloud costs have spiked well beyond the agreed baseline. How do you respond?

*Situation:* On a previous engagement, I noticed mid-quarter that a client's AWS costs had grown sharply compared to the baseline we had set at project start.

*Task:* I was expected to identify the root cause and present a remediation plan to the client within a week.

*Action:* I pulled detailed Cost Explorer reports broken down by service, tag, and region. The spike traced to untagged EC2 instances that a development team had spun up for testing and never terminated. I also found a data transfer charge that was avoidable by moving a workload to the same region as its data source. I set up AWS Budgets with alerts and enforced a tagging policy via Service Control Policies to prevent recurrence.

*Result:* Costs returned to the original baseline within the following billing cycle. The client appreciated the proactive communication and asked us to conduct a quarterly cloud cost review as an ongoing service.

---

Q: You deploy a change to production and the application goes down. What do you do?

*Situation:* During a routine deployment at a previous job, a configuration change I pushed caused the application's load balancer health checks to fail, taking the service offline.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and needed to restore service as fast as possible while keeping stakeholders informed.

*Action:* My first move was to roll back the deployment immediately rather than trying to fix forward under pressure. I then opened a bridge call with the client's technical lead and gave a clear, calm status update every few minutes. Once service was restored, I did a thorough root-cause analysis and found that our staging environment had not replicated the production load balancer configuration, which is why the issue was not caught earlier.

*Result:* Service was restored quickly and cleanly. The client's trust was maintained because of the clear communication during the outage. I introduced a configuration parity check between staging and production as a standard step in our deployment checklist, which prevented similar issues from occurring again.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Two frameworks that work well for FPT Asia Pacific interviews:

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard for behavioural questions. Keep the Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences each) so you have time to go deep on the Action. The Result should be concrete wherever possible: what changed, what the client observed, what was documented or adopted going forward.

Explain-Justify-Tradeoff works well for technical design questions. First, explain what you would build. Second, justify why (what problem does this solve for the client). Third, name the tradeoff you are accepting. FPT interviewers are working with real clients, so showing that you understand tradeoffs signals maturity.

For cost or optimisation questions, use a Diagnose-Fix-Prevent structure: explain how you identified the root cause, what you changed to fix it, and what guardrails you put in place so it does not recur.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

FPT Asia Pacific is a services company, so interviewers are evaluating whether you can deliver results for clients, not just pass a cloud certification exam. They typically look for:

Client-facing communication. You will often need to explain outages, architecture decisions, or cost reports to people who are not engineers. Interviewers want to see that you can translate technical concepts clearly and stay calm under pressure.

Hands-on depth. Expect to be asked about specific commands, tools, and configurations, not just high-level concepts. Terraform state management, IAM policy structure, and VPC design come up often.

Ownership and accountability. If something goes wrong on a project, FPT needs engineers who step up and fix it rather than passing blame. Your answers should show that you take responsibility, communicate proactively, and close the loop.

Adaptability across clients. FPT works across industries. Candidates who can describe how they learned a new domain quickly, or how they adapted their approach to a client's constraints, stand out from those with narrow experience.

Cloud cost awareness. Managed services companies are judged on whether they help clients control spend. Any experience with FinOps, cost tagging, rightsizing, or reserved instances is worth highlighting naturally in your answers.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-to-three week plan:

Week 1: Technical foundation review. Revisit core services on your primary cloud platform (compute, storage, networking, IAM, serverless, containers). Practice writing Terraform or CloudFormation templates from scratch without looking up syntax. Review a recent architecture you worked on and be ready to talk through every design decision, including what you would change today.

Week 2: Client scenarios and behavioural prep. Write out five to six STAR stories covering migration, incident response, cost management, client disagreement, and cross-team coordination. Practice saying them out loud, keeping each under three minutes. Prepare a 'client communication' example where you had to explain something technical to a non-technical audience.

Week 3: FPT-specific research and mock interviews. Read FPT Corporation's public annual reports and recent press releases to understand which industries and geographies they are growing in. Map your experience carefully to each requirement listed in the job description. Do at least one mock technical interview with a peer where they play a sceptical client stakeholder, not just a fellow engineer.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the client context. Generic cloud answers that ignore the client-facing nature of FPT's work miss the mark. Always frame your answers around what the client needed and how they benefited from your work.

Overclaiming certifications. Having an AWS Solutions Architect certificate is fine, but interviewers will probe whether you can apply it. If you list a certification, be ready for deep follow-up questions on that specific domain.

Vague results in STAR answers. Saying 'the project went well' without explaining what changed or what the client said leaves interviewers with nothing to evaluate. Even qualitative results ('the client asked us to expand the engagement') are stronger than no result at all.

Ignoring cost and governance. Engineers who only talk about building and never about cost control or policy enforcement come across as junior in a managed services context. Weave FinOps thinking into your answers naturally.

Talking too long about the Situation. Interviewers at a services company want to hear what you did and what happened, not a long setup. Keep the context brief and move quickly to your actions and the outcome.

Not asking questions. At the end of the interview, not asking anything signals low interest. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the team's delivery model, client mix, or how Cloud Engineers grow into senior or architect roles.

Candidates report the process typically runs two to three rounds. What cloud platforms does FPT Asia Pacific work with most?

FPT Asia Pacific's public project portfolio and job descriptions mention AWS, Azure, and GCP, with AWS and Azure appearing most frequently in India-based roles. Candidates report that interviewers typically ask about your strongest platform in depth rather than expecting equal expertise across all three. Certifications are typically listed as preferred rather than mandatory in FPT Asia Pacific job descriptions, but candidates report that having at least one relevant certification (AWS Solutions Architect, Azure Administrator, or GCP Associate Cloud Engineer) signals foundational knowledge.
