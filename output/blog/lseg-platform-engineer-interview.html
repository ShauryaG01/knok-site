01 Overview

Overview

LSEG (London Stock Exchange Group) is one of the world's largest financial markets infrastructure businesses, running trading venues, data platforms, and post-trade services used by banks, brokers, and asset managers globally. Their technology teams, including Platform Engineering, keep these high-stakes systems running around the clock.

Platform Engineers at LSEG typically work on cloud infrastructure, Kubernetes orchestration, CI/CD pipelines, and observability tooling for systems where uptime directly affects financial markets. The role demands both deep technical skill and an appreciation for regulatory compliance, given the financial services context.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows LSEG has 129 open roles, and there are 204 Platform Engineer positions listed across India, with the largest concentration in Bangalore (29 openings). If you are preparing for an LSEG Platform Engineer interview, expect multiple rounds covering system design, hands-on technical skills, behavioral questions, and situational scenarios drawn from real financial infrastructure challenges.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report the interview process typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds, and a final round that may include system design or behavioral questions. Here are the questions that come up most often for Platform Engineer roles at LSEG:

  1. Walk us through a production incident you owned end-to-end. How did you detect it, mitigate it, and prevent recurrence?
  2. How do you design a Kubernetes platform for high availability in a regulated financial environment?
  3. Describe your approach to CI/CD for services that cannot tolerate downtime.
  4. How do you implement infrastructure as code at scale? What tools have you used and what trade-offs did you make?
  5. How do you manage secrets, credentials, and access control across cloud environments?
  6. What is your approach to container security and image vulnerability management?
  7. How would you set up observability (metrics, logs, traces) for a platform processing real-time market data?
  8. Describe a time you automated a manual, repetitive operational task. What was the impact?
  9. How do you handle capacity planning for a platform with unpredictable traffic spikes, such as during market open or close?
  10. What experience do you have with data streaming platforms like Apache Kafka or Pulsar?
  11. How do you ensure your platform changes satisfy audit and compliance requirements in a financial services company?
  12. How do you work with development teams to shift security left in the delivery pipeline?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a production incident you owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a memory leak in a containerised service caused our order management API to restart repeatedly during peak trading hours, affecting downstream clients.

*Task:* I was the on-call platform engineer and had to restore service quickly while also identifying and fixing the root cause.

*Action:* I immediately rolled the deployment back to the last stable image using our GitOps pipeline, which restored service within minutes. I then used Prometheus metrics and Grafana dashboards to correlate the memory growth with a recent configuration change that had raised the batch size. I coordinated with the dev team to reproduce the leak in a staging environment, confirmed the cause, and pushed a fix through a fast-track pipeline after peer review.

*Result:* Total customer impact was under ten minutes. We also introduced a memory utilisation alert threshold and a mandatory load-test step in the pipeline, so similar issues get caught before reaching production.

---

Q: How do you design a Kubernetes platform for high availability in a regulated environment?

*Situation:* At a previous role, we were migrating a batch data processing system for financial reports to Kubernetes and needed to meet strict uptime and audit requirements.

*Task:* I was responsible for designing the cluster topology and the operational processes around it.

*Action:* I set up a multi-zone cluster with node pools separated by workload type, used pod disruption budgets and topology spread constraints to avoid single points of failure, and enforced network policies to isolate sensitive workloads. For compliance, I enabled audit logging at the API server level and integrated it with our SIEM. All infrastructure was defined in Terraform and reviewed via pull requests to create an auditable change trail.

*Result:* The platform met availability requirements across quarterly reporting windows and passed an internal compliance audit without any findings.

---

Q: Describe how you implemented infrastructure as code at scale.

*Situation:* My team was managing cloud resources manually through the console, which made changes inconsistent and impossible to audit, a serious problem in a financial services context.

*Task:* I led the initiative to migrate all infrastructure to Terraform with a modular structure.

*Action:* I designed a module library covering networking, compute, IAM, and monitoring. I set up a CI pipeline that ran terraform plan on pull requests and required two reviewers before apply. I also introduced automated compliance checks using a policy-as-code tool to catch configurations that violated our security baseline before they reached production.

*Result:* Within three months, all new infrastructure was managed through code. Deployment errors dropped and the team could onboard new environments in hours rather than days.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for behavioral questions. Situation and Task together should take two or three sentences. The bulk of your answer should be in Action, specifically what you did, not what the team did. Result should be concrete, even if you keep it directional.

For system design questions, structure your answer as: requirements first, then components, then failure modes. LSEG interviewers care about reliability and compliance, so always call out how your design handles failure and how changes are audited.

For technical depth questions (Kubernetes, Terraform, Kafka), lead with your direct experience, then explain your reasoning. Saying 'I chose X over Y because of Z constraint' signals engineering judgment, not just familiarity with tool names.

For incident questions, a Timeline plus Learning structure works well: walk through what happened chronologically, then separately discuss what you changed afterward. This shows both technical skill and the blameless, learning mindset that LSEG values in an always-on financial infrastructure role.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

LSEG Platform Engineer interviewers are typically looking for a combination of deep technical competence and financial services awareness. A few things stand out consistently.

Reliability mindset. Financial systems run continuously, including weekends and holidays. Interviewers want to hear that you think proactively about failure modes, not just happy paths.

Compliance and auditability. Unlike a startup, LSEG operates under strict regulatory oversight. Candidates who explain how they have baked audit logging, access controls, and change traceability into platform design make a strong impression.

Automation over heroics. LSEG invests in platform teams to reduce toil for developers. Candidates who show a pattern of finding repetitive work and automating it stand out.

Clear communication under pressure. Given the high-stakes environment, interviewers look for candidates who can communicate crisply during incidents and in postmortems, not just candidates who can fix things.

Collaboration with developers. Platform Engineers at LSEG work closely with application teams. Showing that you treat internal developers as customers, and that you actively gather their feedback, signals the right mindset.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your story bank. Write out five to seven situations from your experience that cover incidents, automation wins, compliance challenges, and cross-team collaborations. Map each to the STAR format. Having these ready prevents going blank under pressure.

Week 2: Sharpen your technical depth. Review your core tools: Kubernetes (scheduling, networking, security policies), Terraform (state management, modules, remote backends), CI/CD (pipeline design, secrets handling), and observability (Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry or similar). For each, prepare to explain a real decision you made and why.

Week 3: Practice system design out loud. Take a prompt like 'design a platform for real-time market data ingestion' and talk through it. Focus on availability, latency, failure handling, and how you would audit changes. Record yourself once; it reveals filler words and gaps faster than notes do.

In the days before your interview, read LSEG's recent announcements and understand their main product lines: FTSE Russell indices, Refinitiv data, and trading platform infrastructure. Connecting your answers to their actual business context shows genuine interest and makes your responses more memorable.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'so what'. Candidates describe what they built but not the outcome. Always close with impact, even if it is directional. Saying 'reduced on-call pages' is far stronger than leaving the result open.

Being vague about your personal role. In team contexts, say 'I designed' or 'I led', not 'we built'. Interviewers are assessing you, not your team.

Ignoring compliance angles. Treating regulatory requirements as an afterthought signals inexperience with financial services. Weave audit logging and access control naturally into your answers wherever they are relevant.

Overloading on tool names. Listing every tool you have touched without depth reads as resume padding. Pick the tools you know well and explain the decisions you made with them.

Not asking questions back. LSEG interviewers typically expect you to ask about the team, the platform's current pain points, and how success is measured. Silence at the end of a round reads as lack of interest.

How many rounds does the LSEG Platform Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds covering system design and hands-on skills, and a final round that combines behavioral questions with a deeper technical or architecture discussion. The exact structure can vary by team and location, so confirm the format with your recruiter before your first round.

What cloud platforms does LSEG use?

LSEG has publicly reported investments in multi-cloud infrastructure, with Azure being prominent given their Microsoft partnership announced in 2022. Candidates should be comfortable with at least one major cloud provider, and familiarity with Azure-specific services is a practical advantage. Core platform skills like Kubernetes, Terraform, and CI/CD are transferable across clouds and remain the priority in interviews.

Is the LSEG Platform Engineer role more infrastructure or DevOps?

The role sits at the intersection of both. Candidates report that LSEG Platform Engineers own the developer platform, including internal Kubernetes clusters, CI/CD tooling, and golden paths, rather than application code directly. The focus is on reliability, scalability, and making application developers productive. A strong background in either SRE or DevOps engineering is a good fit.

What salary can a Platform Engineer expect at LSEG India?

LSEG does not publish salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list compensation ranges for financial services platform roles in India, but sample sizes are small and figures vary widely by experience level and city. Check those sources for directional guidance and come into the offer stage with your own number backed by research.

Does LSEG allow remote work for Platform Engineer roles?

Candidates report a hybrid model is most common, with in-office days required at major hubs like Bangalore and Delhi. Fully remote arrangements are rarely offered for platform roles that involve close collaboration with infrastructure and security teams. Confirm the specific expectation with your recruiter during the screening call.
