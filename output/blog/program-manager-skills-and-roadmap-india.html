01 Role Overview

Role Overview

A Technical Program Manager (TPM) owns the end-to-end delivery of complex engineering programmes. Unlike a Product Manager who decides *what* to build, a TPM focuses on *how* it gets built across multiple teams, timelines, and dependencies. In large tech companies and Global Capability Centres (GCCs), TPMs run release trains, manage risks, and keep engineering and business stakeholders aligned.

As of July 2026, knok's jobradar shows 313 active TPM openings across India. Bangalore leads with 41 open roles, followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), Hyderabad (12), Chennai (5), and Mumbai (1). The bulk of demand comes from GCCs, product companies, and large IT services firms shifting toward a platform delivery model.

Salary data for TPMs in India is thin in public sources. Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly cite wide bands depending on company tier and experience, so treat any figure you see online as directional rather than definitive.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

TPM roles blend hard technical knowledge with programme management discipline. Here is what Indian employers consistently ask for in job descriptions.

Technical depth you need to be credible with engineers: systems design fundamentals, understanding of APIs and microservices, basic cloud concepts (AWS, GCP, or Azure), and familiarity with data pipelines or ML workflows if targeting AI-first companies.

Programme management muscle: Agile and Scrum fluency (not just theory), OKR writing, dependency mapping across teams, and formal risk tracking. Tools like Jira, Confluence, and Asana appear in most job descriptions.

Communication and influence: TPMs rarely have direct authority over engineers. The ability to drive alignment without authority, write crisp status updates, and escalate clearly is what separates average from strong candidates.

Certifications that help: PMP, CAPM, or SAFe certifications are valued in GCC and enterprise hiring. They are not mandatory at product-first companies, but they signal seriousness to recruiters.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

Step 1: Audit where you stand. Pull up several TPM job descriptions from Naukri or LinkedIn and map them against your current experience. Identify the gaps, technical or managerial, and write them down. This gap list becomes your learning plan.

Step 2: Get your technical foundation solid. If you come from a non-engineering background, work through systems design basics and cloud fundamentals. Free resources from cloud providers and structured courses on Coursera or Udemy cover this well. If you are already an engineer, the gap is more often on the programme management side.

Step 3: Take on delivery ownership in your current role. You do not need a TPM title to build TPM experience. Volunteer to run a cross-team release, own a sprint retrospective, or write the project's risk log. Concrete delivery wins matter far more than coursework on a resume.

Step 4: Get a relevant certification. PMP or SAFe Agilist is worth doing before you start applying seriously. It satisfies a checkbox in many Indian JDs, especially at GCCs, and gives you a shared vocabulary with hiring managers.

Step 5: Build a portfolio of programme artefacts. Collect anonymised examples: a risk register you built, a dependency map you drew, an OKR you wrote. These become your talking points in interviews.

Step 6: Target GCCs and product companies. GCCs (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Walmart, Goldman Sachs India) and product companies are the best entry points for TPM roles. IT services firms have programme management roles too, but the scope is often narrower. In interviews, speak to cross-team impact, not just individual delivery.

Step 7: Prepare for the TPM interview loop. Indian TPM interviews typically cover a programme design round (given a scenario, how do you structure and execute it), a technical round (enough to discuss a system without designing it from scratch), and a behavioural round (STAR format). Practise all three, not just the PM-style product questions.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

This roadmap assumes you are starting from a software engineering or project management background. Timelines will shift based on your starting point.

Months 1-3: Foundation building. Close your technical or PM gaps. Complete a systems design primer or a cloud fundamentals course. Shadow a TPM or PM in your current org if possible. Finish reviewing job descriptions and finalise your gap list.

Months 4-6: Build visible delivery experience. Take on a cross-team initiative in your current role. Start documenting your programme artefacts. Begin studying for your PMP or SAFe certification.

Months 7-9: Certify and apply. Clear your certification exam. Start applying to TPM roles. Target associate or junior TPM titles if you are making a career switch; senior TPM if you already have programme delivery experience.

Months 10-12: Interview sprint. Treat the interview process as a project. Track your applications, follow up systematically, and run retrospectives after each interview to improve. Candidates who prep thoroughly across all three interview formats tend to convert within a handful of loops.

Year 2 and beyond. Once in role, the path splits: deepen into a domain (infrastructure, ML, platform) or move into broader delivery leadership. Titles like Group TPM, Director of Engineering Programme Management, and VP of Delivery open up with a consistent delivery track record.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

Where the jobs actually are. The 313 active openings on knok's radar are concentrated in Bangalore (41 roles). If you are in a smaller city, most of these roles will require relocation or offer a hybrid model. Remote-first TPM roles exist but are still a minority in Indian hiring.

Naukri versus LinkedIn. Naukri still dominates for GCC and IT services hiring. LinkedIn is stronger for product companies and startups. You need a strong profile on both. On Naukri, keyword density matters: make sure your headline and summary mention 'Technical Program Manager', 'Agile', 'cross-functional delivery', and your domain.

College network reality. If you are from a top IIT, NIT, or BITS campus, your alumni network is a genuine shortcut. A warm referral from an alumnus already working at a GCC often bypasses initial screening. If you are not from these colleges, community networks fill the gap.

Communities worth joining. The 'PM India' and 'TPM India' groups on LinkedIn are active. Nasscom forums, local PMI chapters, and the APMG India network run events and webinars where hiring managers present. These are worth attending in person when possible.

Understand the GCC landscape. A large share of Indian TPM roles sit inside GCCs, which are India-based delivery centres of global companies. The work is often more execution-heavy than strategy-heavy compared to HQ roles, but the exposure to global programmes and the compensation are strong. Do not dismiss GCC roles early in your career.

Salary conversations. Public data on TPM compensation in India is limited and varies widely by company tier. Glassdoor India and levels.fyi have some data points, but sample sizes for this specific role are small. Go into negotiations knowing your target and the company's tier rather than relying on broad averages.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a computer science degree to become a TPM in India?

A CS or engineering degree is common among TPMs but not a strict requirement. Many successful TPMs in India come from electronics, mechanical, or even non-engineering backgrounds. What matters more is demonstrated technical fluency and a track record of owning complex deliveries. If your degree is non-technical, invest time in closing the technical gap through coursework and hands-on projects.

How is a TPM different from a Project Manager or Engineering Manager?

A Project Manager typically runs a single project with defined scope and timeline. A TPM spans multiple engineering teams and manages interdependencies across a programme, often with a deeper technical lens. An Engineering Manager owns a team's performance and growth but does not manage delivery programmes directly. In practice, the boundaries blur at different companies, so always read the job description carefully before applying.

Which certifications are most valued for TPM roles in India?

PMP from PMI and SAFe Agilist are the most commonly cited credentials in Indian TPM job descriptions, especially at GCCs and large IT firms. At product-first startups, certifications matter less than a demonstrated delivery track record. The CAPM is a good starting point if you do not yet have enough project experience to sit the PMP exam. Google's Project Management Professional Certificate on Coursera is popular as a beginner foundation.

Is Bangalore the only city where TPM jobs are available?

Bangalore has the highest concentration with 41 active openings in the knok jobradar snapshot, but TPM roles also exist in Delhi (14), Pune (13), Hyderabad (12), Chennai (5), and Mumbai (1). Many listings across all cities also offer hybrid arrangements. If you are open to relocation, Bangalore gives you the most options by a clear margin.

What does a TPM interview look like at an Indian GCC or product company?

Most TPM interview loops in India include a programme design round, a technical discussion, and a behavioural round. The programme design round gives you a scenario (say, a multi-team platform migration) and asks how you would structure execution, manage risks, and communicate status. The technical round checks that you can discuss systems at a high level without needing to write code. The behavioural round uses STAR-format questions around conflict resolution, stakeholder management, and delivery setbacks.

Can a software engineer switch to TPM without prior management experience?

Yes, and this is one of the most common paths in India. Engineers who have led cross-team features, managed release cycles, or run on-call rotations already have transferable TPM skills. The key is to reframe your resume around programme outcomes: what did you coordinate, what risks did you mitigate, and what was the cross-team impact. A certification like PMP or SAFe helps signal the transition formally to recruiters.
