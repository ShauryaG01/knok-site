01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Andela is a global talent network that connects skilled tech professionals with companies around the world. Originally built to identify and train top engineering talent across Africa, Andela has expanded its platform to developers from many countries, including India. You can engage with Andela in two ways: apply to join their internal team directly, or enter their talent network, where Andela vets you and matches you with client companies looking for your exact skill set.

As of July 2026, Andela has 17 open roles across engineering, product, and operations. These are internal positions at Andela itself. If you are a developer looking to work remotely with global companies via the Andela marketplace, you follow a separate vetting path described in the interview process section below.

Andela is a fully remote company. Almost every role involves working across time zones with distributed teams. This makes it a strong option for Indian professionals who want global exposure without relocating. Salaries for Andela talent network placements are publicly reported to be competitive with international benchmarks, though exact figures vary widely by role and client company.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process differs slightly depending on whether you are applying for an internal Andela role or entering their talent network. Here is a stage-by-stage breakdown for both paths.

For internal Andela roles:

Stage 1: Application. Submit your resume through Andela's careers page. Tailor your application to highlight remote work experience and the specific role requirements. Andela currently has 17 open roles, so competition exists but the field is not enormous.

Stage 2: Recruiter screen. A recruiter typically reaches out within one to two weeks for a brief call. They check your background, your motivation for joining Andela, and your availability.

Stage 3: Technical assessment. Depending on the role, you may receive a take-home coding challenge or a timed online test. Engineering roles usually cover algorithms, system design, or language-specific tasks.

Stage 4: Technical interview. A panel of engineers interviews you live. Expect coding problems, questions on your past projects, and a system design round for senior roles.

Stage 5: Final interview. A culture and values conversation with a hiring manager or senior leader. They assess how well you operate in distributed, async-first environments.

For the Andela talent network (freelance or contract placement):

Step 1: Profile creation. Build a detailed profile with your skills, experience level, and availability.

Step 2: Automated vetting. Andela runs you through a series of tests covering your core technical skills. This stage is rigorous and filters out a large share of applicants.

Step 3: English and communication screen. Andela places heavy weight on written and verbal English, since you will be working with international clients daily.

Step 4: Matching and client interviews. Once vetted, Andela matches your profile to client companies. You may then go through additional interviews set by those clients before getting placed.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Andela hires for a mix of technical depth and remote-readiness. Here is what matters most across most roles.

Strong core technical skills. For engineering roles, Andela expects clean, well-structured code. They care about problem-solving fundamentals, not just framework familiarity. JavaScript, Python, and Java are commonly sought, but Andela hires across many stacks.

Remote work habits. Andela is fully distributed. They want people who communicate proactively, document their work, and do not need constant hand-holding. If you have worked with international teams or managed async workflows, highlight that prominently in your application.

Clear English communication. Written English is essential. Andela's clients are global, and most day-to-day work happens over chat and video. You do not need a particular accent, but clarity and confidence matter a great deal.

Ownership mindset. Andela looks for people who take responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks. In interviews they ask about past situations where you identified a problem and solved it without being directed to do so.

Adaptability. You will often work with unfamiliar codebases and tools. Being comfortable with ambiguity and quick to ramp up on new technology is a genuine advantage here.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Polish your remote work story. Even if you have not worked with an international company, you likely have examples of async communication, self-directed projects, or cross-functional collaboration. Frame these clearly before your interview.

Practise coding in a shared editor environment. Andela's technical rounds often involve live coding. Use platforms like CoderPad to practise explaining your thought process out loud as you code, not just writing the solution silently.

Review system design basics if applying for senior roles. Senior and lead engineering interviews at Andela commonly include a system design component. Revisit concepts like load balancing, database indexing, caching, and API design before your interview.

Write a strong profile if joining the talent network. Your Andela profile is your first impression with client companies. List every technology you are comfortable with, include links to GitHub or portfolio work, and be specific about your years of experience per skill.

Research Andela's client focus. Andela places talent primarily with US and European product companies. Knowing the kinds of products these companies build helps you tailor your examples in interviews to contexts that resonate with interviewers.

Prepare for the communication screen. Speak slowly and clearly. Use structured answers (situation, action, result) for behavioural questions. Avoid heavy technical jargon when explaining concepts to non-technical interviewers.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Andela has built its identity around the idea that talent is equally distributed but opportunity is not. This shapes how the company hires and how it expects people to work every day.

Inclusion and global perspective. Andela's workforce spans multiple continents. Respect for different working styles, cultures, and time zones is not just encouraged, it is built into how teams operate. Bringing a genuinely global mindset, rather than assuming one way of working is the default, will help you fit in quickly.

Async-first communication. Meetings are minimised. Teams rely on written updates, shared documents, and clear handoffs. If you are used to in-office cultures where you ask a colleague face-to-face, this will take some deliberate adjustment.

Continuous learning. Andela started as a training organisation and still emphasises growth. Employees are expected to keep upskilling, share knowledge with peers, and be honest about skill gaps rather than hiding them.

Transparency. Andela encourages open feedback and direct communication. Managers are generally accessible, and people are expected to raise concerns early rather than letting problems grow quietly in the background.

Impact over activity. Results matter more than hours logged. You are measured on what you ship and how it contributes to team and company goals, not on how many messages you send in a day.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the Andela hiring process take?

For internal roles, the full process from application to offer commonly takes three to six weeks, though this varies by role and team. The talent network vetting process can sometimes move faster, depending on demand from client companies. Following up politely with the recruiter after a week of silence is completely acceptable and often helpful.

Can Indian candidates apply to Andela's talent network?

Yes. Andela has expanded well beyond its original African markets and accepts applications from developers in India and other countries. Your eligibility for specific client placements may depend on the client's preference for time zones or regions, but the vetting process itself is open to Indian applicants. Check Andela's website directly for the latest eligibility details, since their market coverage is updated periodically.

What salary can I expect from an Andela talent network placement?

Salaries vary widely by role, client company, and seniority level. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest placements for mid-to-senior engineers can be competitive with international market rates. Andela's model typically pays in USD, which is attractive for Indian developers given the exchange rate. Review recent contractor and employee reviews on those platforms for the most accurate current picture.

Is the Andela technical assessment very hard?

The talent network assessment is rigorous and Andela is widely reported to be highly selective in how many applicants it approves through vetting. You should be comfortable with data structures, algorithms, and writing clean production-quality code under time pressure. For internal roles, the bar is more in line with a standard senior tech company interview. Practising on coding challenge platforms before attempting the assessment is strongly recommended.

Do I need experience with African or US companies to join Andela?

No specific regional experience is required. Andela cares more about your technical skills and your ability to work effectively in distributed, international teams. Having worked with any remote team or international client is useful to mention, but it is not a strict requirement. Focus on demonstrating clear communication, ownership, and adaptability throughout your interviews.

What happens if I fail the Andela vetting assessment?

You can reapply after a waiting period. Andela is commonly reported to ask candidates to wait several months before reapplying after an unsuccessful attempt. In some cases Andela provides feedback on where you fell short, which is worth requesting if it is not offered automatically. Use the waiting period to strengthen weak areas on coding challenge platforms before trying again. Many successful Andela network members did not pass on their first attempt.
