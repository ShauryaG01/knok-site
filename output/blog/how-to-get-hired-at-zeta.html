01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Zeta is a modern banking technology company that builds core banking and credit infrastructure for banks and fintechs. Founded by Bhavin Turakhia, it operates in India and global markets, powering credit card and payments programs for some of the largest financial institutions. Hiring at Zeta is competitive but structured, with a process designed to test both technical depth and cultural fit.

As of July 2026, Zeta has 21 open roles spanning engineering, product management, design, and go-to-market functions. If you are targeting a role in fintech infrastructure, Zeta is worth serious preparation.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Zeta's hiring process typically runs through four to five stages, though the exact structure can vary by role and team.

Application and shortlist: Your resume is screened against the job description. Zeta values clean, specific resumes that show impact, not just responsibilities. Tailor yours to highlight fintech, payments, or distributed systems experience wherever relevant.

Recruiter screening: A brief introductory call with a recruiter or HR partner. Expect questions about your background, your interest in Zeta, and a basic check on notice period and salary expectations. This is also your chance to ask about the role and team structure.

Technical or domain round: For engineering roles, this typically involves one or two rounds covering data structures and algorithms, system design, or language-specific problem solving. Product roles may include a case study or product sense exercise. The difficulty is commonly reported on platforms like Glassdoor as moderate to high, with a strong emphasis on fundamentals and clear communication of your thinking.

Leadership or culture round: A conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder. This round tests whether you think like an owner, handle ambiguity well, and can work across teams. Be ready to talk through decisions you have made and why.

Offer and background check: Shortlisted candidates receive an offer followed by standard background verification. Compensation details are discussed after the final round.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Zeta looks for people who combine strong technical or domain fundamentals with a builder's mindset.

Technical depth: For engineering roles, solid command of data structures, system design, and at least one backend or frontend stack is expected. Experience with distributed systems, APIs, or payments infrastructure is a strong plus.

Product and business acumen: For non-engineering roles, the bar is on understanding how fintech products work end to end, and how decisions affect both the bank partner and the end customer.

Clear communication: Zeta teams work across time zones and with enterprise clients. Being able to explain complex ideas simply, in writing and in conversation, is taken seriously at every level.

Ownership mindset: The company values people who do not wait to be told what to do. Examples from your past where you identified a problem, drove a solution, and measured the outcome will land well here.

Adaptability: Zeta operates in a fast-moving space where regulations, client needs, and technology all shift quickly. Showing that you have navigated change and learned from it counts in your favour.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Study the product: Spend time understanding Zeta's core offering: its credit card processing platform, its BaaS stack, and the banks or fintechs it serves. The more concretely you can connect your experience to what Zeta builds, the stronger your interviews will be.

Sharpen your fundamentals: For engineering candidates, practise data structures and algorithm problems on platforms like LeetCode, with a focus on arrays, graphs, and concurrency. For system design, practise designing payment processing and high-availability systems.

Prepare your story: Zeta interviewers frequently ask behavioural questions using a structured format. Prepare specific examples for ownership, conflict resolution, working with ambiguity, and driving results without direct authority.

Research the fintech space: Know the difference between core banking, BaaS, and payment processing. Understand how co-branded credit cards work and why banks partner with technology companies like Zeta to run them.

Ask good questions: Zeta interviewers note when candidates ask thoughtful questions about the roadmap, team culture, or technical challenges. Prepare at least two or three questions for each round.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Zeta describes itself as a company where engineers and product people are given significant ownership over what they build. The culture leans toward first-principles thinking: teams are expected to question assumptions and build from the ground up rather than copy existing patterns.

Speed and quality together: Zeta operates in a regulated industry where errors in banking infrastructure carry real consequences. The culture rewards people who move fast while maintaining rigour, not one at the expense of the other.

Flat-ish, but expert-led: The org structure is relatively flat compared to a legacy bank or large IT services company, but deep domain expertise is respected and visible. Senior engineers and product leaders stay hands-on.

Remote-friendly: Zeta has embraced distributed working, with teams spread across India and the US. Strong async communication skills matter here more than at companies where everyone is in one office.

Growth-stage energy: Despite being a well-funded, established fintech, Zeta retains a growth-stage feel where the problems are unsolved and the impact is direct. If you prefer defined playbooks and stable processes, this may feel fast-paced. If you thrive in building, it is likely a strong fit.

Zeta's open roles are predominantly aimed at experienced professionals, particularly in engineering and product.
