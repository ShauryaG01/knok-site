01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Writer is an enterprise AI company that builds a full-stack AI platform for large businesses. Think of it as the AI layer companies plug into their workflows, from generating content to automating complex internal processes. The company is growing steadily in the enterprise AI space, which makes it an interesting place to work if you want to be close to how AI is changing the way large organizations operate.

As of July 2026, Writer has 57 open roles across teams including engineering, product management, sales, customer success, and marketing. Most roles are US-based, with offices in New York and San Francisco, but the company does list some remote-eligible positions. If you are based in India and targeting Writer, engineering and technical roles are your most realistic entry point, though the company's growth means other functions are opening up too.

Competition for these roles is real. Writer attracts candidates from top tech companies and well-known AI startups, so coming in well-prepared makes a genuine difference.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Writer's interview process typically runs through several stages, though the exact steps vary by role and team. Here is what commonly reported experiences suggest:

Recruiter Screen
A call with a recruiter to check your background, motivations, and basic fit. Be ready to explain why you want to work at an AI company and why Writer specifically.

Hiring Manager Interview
A conversation with the team lead or hiring manager. For technical roles this often includes a discussion of past projects and how you approach problems. For go-to-market roles, expect questions about deals you have worked on or accounts you have grown.

Technical or Skills Assessment
For engineering roles, this is usually a coding round or a take-home assignment. For product roles, you might get a case study or a product teardown. Writer tends to test practical skills over theoretical knowledge.

Panel or Loop Interviews
A set of back-to-back interviews with several team members covering technical depth, cross-functional collaboration, and culture fit. This stage can run a few hours in total.

Final or Executive Round
Some roles include a final conversation with a senior leader. This is often more of a two-way conversation than a strict evaluation, but it still carries weight.

Decisions commonly come within a couple of weeks of the final round, though timelines can stretch if the team is evaluating multiple candidates.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Writer looks for people who understand how AI is actually used inside large enterprises, not just people who can talk about AI in the abstract. A few things that come up consistently in publicly reported hiring experiences:

Genuine AI curiosity. Writer builds AI products, so they want people who use AI tools themselves and think critically about what works and what does not. This applies to engineers as much as to salespeople and product managers.

Enterprise context. Many of Writer's customers are large companies with complex needs. Experience working with or inside enterprise environments, whether in sales, product, or engineering, is a real advantage.

Clear communication. Writer operates across functions and moves fast. People who can explain technical ideas simply, or translate customer needs into product requirements, tend to stand out.

Ownership mindset. Commonly cited in AI startup hiring broadly, this is especially valued at Writer. They want people who drive outcomes, not just complete tasks.

For engineering roles specifically, experience with large language models, APIs, or building developer-facing products is a strong signal. For go-to-market roles, a track record of closing or expanding enterprise accounts matters more than company pedigree.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A few concrete steps that make a real difference:

Research the product deeply. Sign up for Writer's free tier or read their documentation before your first interview. Know what Palmyra (their foundation model) is, how the platform works, and what kinds of companies use it. Interviewers notice when candidates have actually used the product.

Prepare examples around AI impact. For any role, think of a few stories where you used AI tools, built AI features, or helped a team adopt AI to get better results. These examples matter more at Writer than at most companies.

Study their customer use cases. Writer's blog and publicly available case studies show how their customers use the platform. Understanding these will help you speak to enterprise needs during interviews without guessing.

Practice explaining technical concepts simply. Whether you are an engineer or a sales lead, clear communication is valued. Practice explaining what you do in plain language, free of jargon.

For coding rounds, focus on practical problem-solving and system design relevant to AI applications, APIs, and data pipelines. Showing you can build real things matters more than grinding competitive programming problems.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Writer operates with the pace and intensity typical of a well-funded AI startup. A few things stand out from publicly reported employee experiences:

Speed over process. Writer moves fast and expects people to make decisions with incomplete information. If you thrive only in structured, process-heavy environments, this pace might feel uncomfortable at first.

Cross-functional collaboration. Engineering, product, and go-to-market teams work closely together. Siloed work is not the norm, and people who can operate across functions tend to do well.

Writing quality is taken seriously. Perhaps not surprising for a company called Writer, but internal communication standards are reportedly high. Clear, concise writing is valued across all teams, not just in product or marketing.

AI-first thinking. The team is expected to use AI tools in their day-to-day work. Employees who avoid or resist using AI internally would stand out for the wrong reasons.

Writer has publicly emphasized building a culture of direct feedback. If you are used to indirect or hierarchical communication styles, practicing giving and receiving blunt, constructive feedback before you join is a good idea.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Writer hire candidates based in India?

Most of Writer's 57 current open roles are US-based, with a focus on New York and San Francisco. That said, the company does list some remote-eligible roles, and the engineering function is often more open to distributed hiring than other teams. Check each job description carefully for location requirements before spending time on an application.

Do I need a machine learning or AI background to get hired at Writer?

Not necessarily, though it helps for engineering roles. Writer hires across many functions, including sales, customer success, marketing, and operations, where deep ML knowledge is not required. What matters more across all roles is that you use AI tools, understand the enterprise AI space, and can speak to how AI creates business value. A surface-level 'I have heard of ChatGPT' answer will not cut it.

How long does the Writer hiring process take from first contact to offer?

Based on publicly reported experiences, the process commonly runs a few weeks from recruiter screen to offer, assuming no scheduling delays. Panel interview stages can add time, and if the team is comparing multiple candidates, timelines can stretch further. Following up politely after each stage is reasonable and unlikely to hurt your chances.

What is the best way to stand out in a Writer interview?

Using the product before your interview is probably the single most effective thing you can do. Candidates who can speak specifically about Writer's platform, its use cases, and where they see room for improvement immediately signal they are serious. Beyond that, having a clear story about how you have driven outcomes in past roles, rather than just listing responsibilities, tends to resonate well with Writer's interviewers.

Are there take-home assignments in Writer's process?

For engineering and product roles, take-home assignments or case studies are commonly reported as part of the process. These tend to be practical in nature, testing your ability to build or think through real problems rather than abstract puzzles. Budget a few hours for these and treat them as a chance to show how you actually work, not just what you know in theory.

How important is cultural fit at Writer compared to technical skills?

Both matter, but Writer's culture is distinct enough that fit is not an afterthought. The company values direct communication, fast decision-making, and genuine enthusiasm for AI, and publicly reported interviews often include questions that probe these directly. Strong technical skills without alignment to the pace and communication style of the team are unlikely to carry an offer across the finish line.
