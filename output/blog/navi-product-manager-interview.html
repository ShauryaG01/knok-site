01 Overview

Overview

Navi is one of India's fastest-growing fintech companies, founded by Sachin Bansal, co-founder of Flipkart. The company offers personal loans, home loans, health insurance, UPI payments, and mutual funds through a fully digital, paperless platform. Navi's core focus is making financial services accessible to first-time borrowers and underserved segments, including salaried professionals in smaller cities who have had limited access to formal credit.

As of July 2026, Navi has 60 open roles, reflecting active hiring across product and tech. PM roles sit at the intersection of technology, risk, and customer experience. You will work on problems like improving loan approval rates, reducing defaults through better data, and building simple, trustworthy products for users who may be accessing formal credit for the first time in their lives.

The interview process typically spans four to five rounds: an initial recruiter screening, a product case (take-home or live), a metrics and analytical discussion, a behavioural round, and a final conversation with a senior leader. Candidates report the process moves at a reasonable pace. Before your first interview, download and use the Navi app across all its main products: loans, insurance, and mutual funds.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up regularly in Navi PM interviews, based on what candidates typically report.

  1. How would you improve Navi's personal loan onboarding funnel to increase approval rates for thin-file customers?
  2. Navi wants to expand into a new financial product category. How would you decide which one to build first?
  3. Define the North Star metric for Navi's health insurance product and explain how you would measure it.
  4. A key metric on the Navi app has dropped sharply week-on-week. Walk us through your diagnosis process.
  5. How would you design a feature to help first-time borrowers understand their repayment schedule in plain, simple language?
  6. Navi competes with banks and NBFCs. How would you differentiate Navi's product for a salaried user in Tier 2 India?
  7. Tell me about a product you built or shipped that failed. What did you learn?
  8. How do you balance speed of delivery with product quality in a regulated fintech environment?
  9. Walk through how you would prioritise the roadmap for Navi's mutual funds product over the next two quarters.
  10. How would you use data to reduce loan defaults without hurting the approval rate for genuine customers?
  11. Describe a time you had to convince a sceptical stakeholder to move forward with a product decision.
  12. How would you build trust with Navi's customers, many of whom have had poor experiences with informal lenders?

Questions 1, 4, and 10 come up most often for mid and senior PM roles. Questions 7 and 11 appear in nearly every behavioural round.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these as templates. Replace the details with your own real experiences before the interview.

Q: A key metric on the Navi app dropped sharply week-on-week. How would you diagnose and respond?

*Situation:* At my previous company, the loan application completion rate fell noticeably on a Monday morning with no obvious external trigger.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause quickly, without waiting for others to surface the issue or relying on guesswork.

*Action:* I segmented the drop by platform (Android vs iOS), geography, and user cohort to isolate where the fall was concentrated. I found it was specific to Android users on a recently released app version. I pulled crash logs and traced the issue to a bug in the KYC document upload step. I flagged it to engineering immediately and we issued a fix within a few hours. I also set up a real-time alert for that funnel step so we could catch similar issues faster going forward.

*Result:* Completion rates recovered within two days. The funnel monitoring we added then caught two smaller issues in the weeks that followed before they caused significant drops.

---

Q: Tell me about a product you built that failed. What did you learn?

*Situation:* I led the launch of a savings goal feature in a consumer finance app, aimed at engaging users who had already taken at least one loan.

*Task:* The goal was to increase repeat engagement and build a savings habit among existing borrowers.

*Action:* We designed a feature that linked savings targets to the user's loan repayment calendar. We moved quickly to hit a quarterly milestone and did very little user research before committing to the full build.

*Result:* Adoption was very low in the first month. Post-launch interviews showed users found the concept confusing: we had mixed two distinct mental models (saving and debt repayment) without a clear narrative. I revised the onboarding copy and added a short explainer screen, which helped recovery. The real lesson was to validate the core concept with even a small group of target users before committing to a full build.

---

Q: Describe a time you convinced a sceptical stakeholder about a product decision.

*Situation:* I proposed simplifying the income verification step in our loan application flow. Our compliance lead was concerned that reducing document requirements could increase fraud exposure.

*Task:* I needed to address the fraud risk concern credibly while making a case for improving the experience for legitimate applicants.

*Action:* I pulled funnel data showing that the income document upload had the highest drop-off in the application journey. I proposed a tiered approach: auto-approve smaller ticket loans for applicants with strong bank statement signals, and require additional documents only for larger amounts. I framed it as a limited pilot with a defined review date rather than a permanent policy change, and asked for a capped user pool to test it.

*Result:* The compliance lead agreed to the pilot. After the review period, the data showed meaningful improvement in application completions with no significant increase in early delinquency. The tiered approach was extended more broadly after that.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

A few frameworks that work well in Navi PM interviews, based on candidate feedback.

For product design and feature questions, use CIRCLES (compressed).
Start by clarifying the goal and the user segment. List user needs, prioritise by impact and feasibility, propose a solution, and discuss trade-offs. At Navi, ground your design in the realities of Indian fintech users: limited credit history in some segments, first-time digital borrowers, and UPI-first behaviour.

For metric drop or diagnostic questions, use structured segmentation.
Step 1: Confirm the data is accurate (rule out tracking errors). Step 2: Segment by platform, geography, user type, and time. Step 3: Generate hypotheses (product bug, external event, competitor action, seasonal shift). Step 4: Validate the most likely hypothesis with data. Step 5: Propose a fix and a monitoring plan.

For prioritisation questions, use an Impact vs Effort grid or a scored matrix.
For Navi specifically, add a third dimension: regulatory or risk feasibility. A feature that needs RBI sign-off will have a longer delivery runway than a purely UX-driven change, and interviewers appreciate candidates who acknowledge this reality.

For behavioural questions, use STAR.
Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two or three sentences). Spend most of your time on Action (what you personally did) and Result (quantify where possible; if numbers are confidential, describe the before-and-after clearly).

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Navi PMs work in a fast-paced, resource-conscious environment where products must serve users who may be first-time digital financial services customers. Interviewers typically look for four qualities.

Customer obsession for the underserved user. Navi's core market includes people in smaller cities and those with thin credit histories. Interviewers want to see that you understand this user's context, not just the metro power user. Candidates who frame their decisions around 'the person in Tier 2 India accessing formal credit for the first time' tend to make a stronger impression.

Data fluency without data dependency. Navi moves quickly. Interviewers value candidates who can work with imperfect data, make reasonable assumptions, and arrive at a defensible decision. Asking for more data at every step reads as hesitation, not rigour.

Comfort with regulatory and risk constraints. You do not need to be a compliance expert, but you should show that you factor in risk and regulatory feasibility when prioritising. Proposing features without any mention of RBI guidelines or fraud risk signals a gap in fintech domain awareness.

Ownership and bias for action. Navi prizes people who take responsibility and move things forward. In behavioural rounds, interviewers are listening for evidence that you drove outcomes, not just participated. Use 'I' more than 'we' when describing your specific contribution.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week plan to get ready for Navi PM interviews.

Week 1: Know the product and the user.
Download and use the Navi app across its main products: personal loans, home loans, health insurance, and mutual funds. Pay close attention to onboarding flows, the language used for first-time users, and any steps that feel confusing or friction-heavy. Write down three specific improvements you would suggest and why. Read any public blog posts or founder interviews from Navi to understand their stated priorities.

Spend time on the Indian fintech regulatory environment at a high level. Know what RBI digital lending guidelines mean for product decisions and why data privacy rules matter at a company like Navi. You do not need to be an expert, but showing awareness signals fintech maturity.

Week 2: Practice out loud.
Take three questions from the list in this guide and answer them end to end without stopping. Record yourself if you can. Ask a peer to give feedback on whether your answer was structured and whether you asked good clarifying questions before jumping to solutions.

Prepare three STAR stories from your own work: a product decision that led to a good outcome, a product or feature that failed and what you learned, and a time you navigated a difficult stakeholder situation. Know these cold.

Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer about Navi's roadmap priorities, how PMs work with risk and compliance teams, or what a strong first three months looks like in the role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

These are the most common errors in Navi PM interviews, based on what interviewers and past candidates typically report.

Treating Navi like a generic tech company. Navi is a regulated fintech with a specific customer segment. Candidates who give product answers that could fit any consumer app, without acknowledging credit risk, KYC flows, or RBI guidelines, come across as unprepared for the domain.

Jumping into solutions before clarifying the problem. In product case questions, many candidates skip clarifying questions and go straight to feature ideas. Interviewers at Navi want to see you define the problem and the user before proposing anything.

Using 'we' throughout behavioural answers. If every sentence starts with 'we decided' or 'we built,' it is hard for the interviewer to understand what you specifically contributed. Be direct about your own role and your own decisions.

Ignoring risk and compliance trade-offs. Proposing a feature without acknowledging that it may need compliance review or carries fraud risk signals a gap in fintech domain understanding. You do not need to know every regulation, just show you factor in the dimension.

Not using the Navi app before the interview. Candidates who clearly have not used the product struggle when asked 'what would you improve about our app?' Use all the main product flows before your interview, not after.

Vague results in STAR answers. 'The outcome was positive' is not a result. Where you cannot share internal numbers, describe the before-and-after in specific qualitative terms: 'support tickets about this issue dropped noticeably,' or 'the team shipped this feature a full sprint earlier than the previous quarter's equivalent.'

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Navi PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report four to five rounds: a recruiter or HR screening, a product case (take-home or live), a metrics and analytical discussion, a behavioural round, and a final conversation with a senior leader. The exact sequence can vary by team, and some candidates report that the product case and metrics round are combined into one session. Confirm the structure with your recruiter after you receive the interview invite. Preparing for all four types regardless is the safest approach.

Do I need a fintech background to get a PM role at Navi?

Not necessarily. Navi does hire PMs from non-fintech backgrounds, but you will be expected to demonstrate a solid understanding of Navi's products, its target customer, and the broader fintech regulatory environment. Candidates with general consumer tech experience who prepare thoroughly on the domain can compete effectively. Those who arrive without any preparation on fintech context tend to struggle, especially in product case and stakeholder questions.

What salary can I expect for a Product Manager role at Navi?

Based on knok jobradar data from July 2026, PM compensation at Navi broadly follows Indian fintech market ranges: Associate PM roles typically fall in the 12-20 LPA band, mid-level PM roles (three to six years of experience) in the 24-40 LPA band, and Senior PM roles in the 40-60 LPA band. Group and Principal PM roles can reach 55-90 LPA and above. Actual offers vary based on your experience level, the specific team, and your negotiation.

How should I prepare for the product case round at Navi?

Pick two or three of Navi's own products and practise diagnosing a metric drop or designing a new feature for each one. Use a structured approach: clarify the problem, define the user, generate hypotheses, prioritise, and propose a solution with trade-offs. Make sure your answers reflect the realities of Navi's user base, including first-time borrowers, Tier 2 city users, and people with thin credit histories. Practise out loud rather than just reading frameworks, because live cases reward fluency and pacing, not just knowledge.

How long does the Navi PM hiring process take from first contact to offer?

Candidates typically report the process moves within two to four weeks from first contact to offer, though this varies with team urgency and scheduling. If you do not hear back after a round within a week, a polite follow-up message to your recruiter is generally appropriate. Navi currently has 60 open roles, which suggests active hiring momentum across teams.

What questions should I ask the interviewer at the end of a Navi PM interview?

Ask about the product areas the team is prioritising over the next two quarters, how PMs collaborate with risk and compliance teams when shipping new features, and what a strong first three months looks like in the role. These questions signal that you are thinking about real work, not just trying to get an offer. Avoid asking about compensation in early rounds and save that conversation for your recruiter or HR contact.
