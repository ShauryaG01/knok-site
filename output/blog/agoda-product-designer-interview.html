01 Overview

Overview

Agoda is one of Asia's largest online travel platforms, part of the Booking Holdings group. Its product and design teams work across multiple hubs, with a culture that is heavily data-driven and experiment-led. Designers here are expected to think like product owners: own the problem, back decisions with data, and ship work that moves measurable business outcomes.

As of July 2026, Agoda has 299 open roles across functions, reflecting active hiring across the company. The broader Product Designer market in India currently has 393 openings, with Bangalore leading at 62 roles, followed by Delhi at 33 and Mumbai at 13.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have gone through Agoda's Product Designer interview process typically report questions across three themes.

Process and craft

  1. Walk us through a product or feature you designed from scratch. What was your process?
  2. How do you use data to validate a design decision? Give a specific example.
  3. Describe your approach to designing for users across different cultures and languages.
  4. Walk us through a time you had to simplify a complex user flow. What trade-offs did you make?
  5. How do you collaborate with engineers during handoff to make sure design intent is preserved?

Collaboration and conflict

  1. Describe a time you disagreed with a product manager on a design direction. How did you resolve it?
  2. Tell us about a design that shipped but did not perform as expected. What did you learn?
  3. How do you prioritise which UX problems to solve when you have limited time?

Business and product thinking

  1. How do you balance user needs against business goals when they conflict?
  2. How would you redesign Agoda's hotel search results page to improve booking conversion?
  3. What metrics would you use to measure the success of a checkout redesign on a travel booking platform?
  4. How do you stay current with design trends and apply them selectively rather than following them blindly?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these as templates and replace the details with your own real experience.

Q: Walk us through a product or feature you designed from scratch.

*Situation:* At my previous company, users were dropping off during the mobile app's onboarding flow before completing their profile setup.

*Task:* I was given full ownership of the onboarding redesign, from discovery through to post-launch review.

*Action:* I started with user interviews and session recordings to identify where people got confused. I mapped the full journey, spotted three key friction points, and benchmarked how other apps handle progressive disclosure. I built low-fidelity wireframes, ran usability tests with a small group, iterated on the feedback, and delivered high-fidelity specs along with a component library for engineering.

*Result:* Post-launch tracking showed a meaningful lift in onboarding completion, and users who finished onboarding had noticeably stronger first-week retention. I am happy to walk through the exact numbers from my portfolio.

Q: Describe a time you disagreed with a product manager on a design direction.

*Situation:* A PM on my team wanted to add a prominent upsell banner to the confirmation screen immediately after a user completed a transaction.

*Task:* I believed this would hurt user trust at a sensitive moment, but I needed to make the case with evidence rather than just pushing back.

*Action:* I pulled session data showing that users who saw interruptive prompts right after checkout had lower satisfaction scores in follow-up surveys. I proposed a softer, contextual recommendation shown only after the user dismissed the confirmation. I mocked up both versions and we agreed to A/B test them.

*Result:* The test confirmed that the softer version performed better on the metric the PM cared about most: repeat booking rate. The PM appreciated that I came with data and a concrete alternative.

Q: How would you redesign a key flow, such as hotel search results, to improve booking conversion?

*Situation:* In a previous role, I was asked to audit and improve the search results page for a travel product.

*Task:* The goal was to reduce time-to-decision and increase the rate of users proceeding to a property detail page.

*Action:* I reviewed heatmaps, scroll depth data, and exit surveys to understand what was causing hesitation. Price anxiety and information overload were the two biggest blockers. I redesigned the card layout to surface the three details users said mattered most, added a price calendar component, and reduced the number of default-visible filter options. I tested the new design with a small cohort before a wider rollout.

*Result:* The redesigned page showed a measurable improvement in click-through to property detail pages. The specifics are in my portfolio and I am happy to walk through them.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Agoda interviewers respond well to structured thinking. Three frameworks are worth having ready.

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for behavioural questions. Keep the Situation and Task brief, spend most of your time on Action, and always close with a concrete Result. If the outcome was a failure, own it and state what you changed as a result.

Double Diamond works well for 'how do you approach design' questions. Show that you diverge before you converge: research and problem framing first, then ideation and delivery. Agoda values designers who can articulate why they are solving the problem they are solving, not just how they executed it.

Impact-Effort matrix is useful when asked about prioritisation. Frame your answer around what moves a key metric most for the least resource cost. Agoda's culture rewards people who make smart trade-offs under constraints rather than asking for more time.

For live design challenges, talk out loud throughout. Interviewers want to see your reasoning process, not just the output. Start by clarifying scope, state your assumptions, and check in with the interviewer before going deep on any single direction.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates who receive offers for Product Designer roles at Agoda typically demonstrate four things clearly.

Data fluency. Agoda runs on experimentation. Interviewers want to see that you have worked with real metrics, can interpret test results, and have used data to change a design decision, not just confirm one you already liked.

Business ownership. Designers here are expected to care about conversion, retention, and revenue outcomes. Frame your portfolio work in terms of results, not deliverables. 'I shipped a redesign' is weaker than 'the redesign improved a metric the business cared about.'

Cross-functional collaboration. Many questions are designed to probe how you work with PMs, engineers, and data analysts. Show that you can hold your ground on user needs while staying open to trade-offs.

Craft at scale. Agoda serves users across many countries, devices, and languages. Interviewers look for designers who have thought seriously about accessibility, localisation, and system-level design, not just polished individual screens.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A structured approach to Agoda prep covers five areas.

Step 1: Use the product as a real customer. Book a mock trip on Agoda's app and website. Explore filters, go through the checkout flow, check the confirmation screen. Note what works well and what you would change. Interviewers often ask you to critique or redesign parts of the product, and firsthand experience gives you specific, credible observations.

Step 2: Curate your portfolio for impact. Pick three to four projects that show range: one involving heavy user research, one requiring navigation of a cross-functional disagreement, and one with a clear measurable outcome. For each, prepare a two-minute verbal walkthrough structured as: problem, process, key decision, result.

Step 3: Practise articulating trade-offs out loud. Agoda interviewers frequently ask 'why did you choose X over Y?' Practise giving structured answers using the Double Diamond and Impact-Effort frameworks. Record yourself and listen back.

Step 4: Get comfortable with design metrics. Understand the difference between leading and lagging indicators, what a north star metric is, and how A/B testing works in a product context. You do not need to be a data analyst, but you should be able to discuss metrics confidently.

Step 5: Prepare questions for your interviewers. Candidates report that Agoda interviewers appreciate questions about the team's design process, how design decisions get made, and what success looks like in the first months on the job. Avoid generic questions and ask about specific design challenges the team is actively working on.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Showing process without outcomes. The most common mistake in Agoda Product Designer interviews is presenting a portfolio that does not connect design decisions to business or user results. For every project, prepare a clear answer to: 'What changed because of this design?'

Being vague about your personal contribution. Many candidates say 'we did this' throughout their portfolio walkthrough. Interviewers cannot evaluate you if they cannot tell what you specifically owned. Use 'I' when describing your choices and decisions.

Jumping straight to wireframes in design challenges. Under time pressure, candidates often skip problem framing. Interviewers at Agoda want to see you clarify scope, state assumptions, and define the right problem before you start sketching.

Treating interviewer pushback as criticism. Agoda's culture is collaborative and direct. Candidates who become defensive when an interviewer challenges a design decision leave a poor impression. Engage with the challenge: restate the concern, explore it, and show you can think flexibly.

Not using the product before your interview. Surprisingly common: candidates interviewing for a Product Designer role at a travel platform without ever having used it. Use Agoda's app before every round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Agoda Product Designer interview typically involve?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screen, a portfolio review with the hiring manager or a senior designer, a design challenge (take-home or live), and a final round with cross-functional stakeholders. The exact number varies by team and seniority level. Confirm the format with your recruiter after the first call so you can plan your preparation accordingly.

What should I include in my portfolio for Agoda?

Focus on three to four projects that show your full process, from problem framing through to measurable outcomes. Agoda's teams care about data-driven decisions, so highlight moments where research or analytics changed your design direction. Include at least one example of working through a cross-functional conflict, as collaboration is a key signal they screen for.

Does Agoda give a take-home design challenge, and how much time is typically given?

Candidates report that Agoda sometimes uses a take-home design challenge, though the format varies by team. Typical briefs ask you to redesign a specific flow or solve a product problem in a travel context. Spend as much time on problem framing as on the visual solution, and always present your reasoning alongside the final output.

What salary can I expect as a Product Designer at Agoda?

Based on knok job radar data, mid-level Product Designers (3-5 years) see ranges of 14-24 LPA in India, while senior designers (6-9 years) commonly see 26-40 LPA. Lead and Principal-level roles go to 36-55+ LPA. Final compensation at Agoda will depend on your specific level, team, and negotiation, so treat these as market reference points rather than guarantees.

How important is travel industry experience for this role?

Agoda does not require prior travel industry experience for most Product Designer roles. What matters more is your ability to design for scale across cultures and devices, and to use data to drive decisions. That said, understanding the specific UX challenges of travel bookings, such as price anxiety, trust signals, and multi-step flows, will make your answers sharper and more credible in the interview.
