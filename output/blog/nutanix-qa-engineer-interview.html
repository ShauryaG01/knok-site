02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Nutanix QA interviews, based on what candidates report publicly:

  1. How would you design a test plan for a distributed storage system where individual nodes can fail independently?
  2. Walk us through how you have tested REST APIs in a microservices or platform environment. Which tools did you use?
  3. Nutanix products run on customer-managed hardware clusters. How do you approach compatibility and environment testing across different hardware configurations?
  4. Describe your experience with Python-based automation frameworks such as Pytest or Robot Framework. Why would you choose one over the other for infrastructure testing?
  5. How do you decide which test cases to automate and which to keep as manual tests?
  6. Tell us about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle. How did you communicate it, and what happened?
  7. How would you test a feature that spans the hypervisor layer and the management UI simultaneously?
  8. What is your approach to performance and scalability testing for software that customers run at large scale?
  9. How do you work with developers to catch bugs earlier in the software development lifecycle?
  10. Nutanix ships updates that customers apply directly in production environments. How do you think about upgrade and rollback testing?
  11. Describe how you have integrated automated tests into a CI/CD pipeline. What were the biggest challenges?
  12. How do you deal with flaky tests in a large automation suite without slowing down the release pipeline?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you design a test plan for a distributed storage system where nodes can fail independently?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built a distributed caching layer used by multiple product teams, and I owned the QA strategy for it.

*Task:* I needed to create a test plan that covered not just happy-path reads and writes but also failure scenarios, since the system had to tolerate node crashes without data loss.

*Action:* I broke the plan into three layers. First, unit and integration tests for individual node behaviour. Second, chaos-style tests where I simulated node failures mid-operation by scripting process kills at random intervals. Third, recovery tests that verified data consistency after a node rejoined the cluster. I documented each scenario with expected behaviour and acceptance criteria, then got sign-off from the dev lead before we started.

*Result:* We caught several data-consistency bugs during chaos testing that would not have surfaced in normal integration tests. All were fixed before the feature reached staging, and the post-release defect rate for that component was zero across the first two quarters after launch.

---

Q: Tell us about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* Two days before a scheduled release, I found a bug in our upgrade path: when upgrading from the previous version, user-configured settings were silently reset to defaults.

*Task:* I had to decide quickly whether to raise a blocker or document it as a known issue, and get the right people involved fast.

*Action:* I wrote a clear bug report with exact reproduction steps, an impact assessment (any customer upgrading would lose their settings), and a short screen recording of the issue. I flagged it as a blocker, pinged the dev lead and release manager directly, and stayed available to retest. The fix was ready within a day.

*Result:* The release was delayed by one day but shipped clean. The release manager later said the screen recording saved hours of back-and-forth on understanding the issue.

---

Q: Describe how you have integrated automated tests into a CI/CD pipeline. What were the biggest challenges?

*Situation:* My team had a large Selenium suite that ran only nightly, so broken builds sometimes stayed broken for hours before anyone noticed.

*Task:* I was asked to move the critical smoke tests into the pull request pipeline so that every merge triggered at least a fast verification run.

*Action:* I identified the highest-priority smoke test cases, refactored them to run independently with no shared state between tests, and worked with the DevOps engineer to add a dedicated test stage in our Jenkins pipeline. I also added a Slack alert that tagged the author of the failing commit directly.

*Result:* Mean time to detect a regression dropped sharply. The team could merge with confidence, and the nightly suite became a deeper regression check rather than a first-line detector.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical 'design a test plan' questions: Start by clarifying scope (what system, what is in and out of scope, what failure modes matter). Then describe layers: unit, integration, system, and edge-case or chaos testing. Finish by explaining how you decide what to automate versus what stays manual.

For behavioural questions: Use STAR. Situation sets context in one or two sentences. Task says what your specific responsibility was. Action is the longest part and stays in 'I did' language, not 'we did.' Result is concrete: a bug caught, a release unblocked, a metric improved.

For 'how do you work with developers' questions: Nutanix values shift-left thinking. Mention reviewing specs or design documents early, writing test cases before development starts, and using pull request comments to flag testability concerns before code is merged.

For debugging or root-cause questions: Walk the interviewer through your thinking step by step. State your hypothesis first, then what you checked to confirm or rule it out. Interviewers care about how you think, not just the answer you reached.

For 'tools and frameworks' questions: Name the tool, give one specific use case from your experience, and mention one limitation or tradeoff. This shows real experience rather than a memorised list.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Nutanix QA interviewers are typically looking for a few things that go beyond standard testing knowledge.

Systems thinking: Because Nutanix products operate at the infrastructure layer, interviewers want to see that you understand how distributed systems fail, how data consistency works, and why testing a cluster is different from testing a monolith. Candidates who have only tested web UIs often struggle here.

Automation depth, not just familiarity: Saying you 'know Selenium' is not enough. Interviewers want to hear about framework design decisions, how you handled flakiness, how you structured test data, and how your suite connected to the build pipeline.

Ownership mindset: Nutanix typically values QA engineers who treat quality as their responsibility, not a checklist handed to them by a developer. Answers that show you proactively raised concerns, pushed back on under-specified requirements, or went beyond the assigned test cases tend to land well.

Clear communication under pressure: The critical-bug scenario question is partly a communication test. Interviewers want to see that you can write a crisp bug report, involve the right people without causing panic, and stay calm when a release is at risk.

Curiosity about the product: Candidates who have looked at Nutanix's AOS, Prism, or AHV documentation before the interview tend to ask better questions and give more relevant answers. You do not need to be a Nutanix expert, but showing you did your homework helps.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product and domain foundation
Read the public documentation for Nutanix AOS and Prism. Understand what a hyper-converged infrastructure cluster does and the difference between the data plane and the management plane. Note the kinds of failures the system is designed to tolerate, such as node failure, network partition, and drive failure.

Week 2: Automation and tooling
Practise writing Pytest test cases for a small Python project if you have not done so recently. Set up a simple CI pipeline using GitHub Actions or Jenkins locally. Review how to mock external dependencies in unit tests and how to write parameterised tests.

Week 3: Behavioural and design prep
Write out three to five stories from your past experience using STAR format. Cover: finding a critical bug, improving an automation suite, working with a developer to fix a quality issue, and a time you disagreed with a decision but committed anyway. Practise saying each story aloud in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and questions
Ask a peer or mentor to run a mock technical round using the questions listed in this guide. Pay attention to whether you clarify scope before diving into answers. Prepare two or three questions to ask the Nutanix panel, focused on team structure, current automation coverage, and how QA integrates with the release process.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Nutanix like a web-app company: Candidates sometimes give answers built entirely around browser testing or mobile apps. Nutanix QA is heavily focused on backend, API, and platform testing. Anchor your answers in that context.

Vague automation answers: Saying 'I wrote automated tests' without specifying the framework, what the test covered, how it was triggered, or what it caught tells the interviewer very little. Be specific.

Skipping the 'why' in test design questions: When asked to design a test plan, many candidates list test types but never explain why they chose that coverage. Interviewers at product companies want to see your reasoning, not just a checklist.

Underplaying manual testing: Some candidates dismiss manual testing to sound more senior. At Nutanix, exploratory testing of complex failure scenarios is genuinely valuable. Do not apologise for manual work when the scenario calls for it.

Not preparing questions for the panel: Ending an interview with 'I think I am good' signals low interest. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions about the team's testing infrastructure, release cadence, or what the first few months would look like.

Over-claiming on tools you have not used recently: If you listed a tool on your resume years ago and have not used it since, be honest about the gap. Interviewers notice when practical follow-up questions reveal surface-level knowledge.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Nutanix QA Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves three to five rounds. These commonly include an initial recruiter call, one or two technical rounds covering automation and test design, and a behavioural or hiring manager discussion. Some candidates report an additional system-design round for senior roles. Round count can vary by team and level, so it is worth confirming with the recruiter at the start.

What programming language is most important for Nutanix QA roles?

Python is the most commonly cited language for QA automation at Nutanix, given that much of the internal tooling and testing infrastructure is Python-based. Candidates report that strong Pytest skills are particularly valued. Knowing some shell scripting is also useful for environment setup and debugging test infrastructure. Java or JavaScript knowledge may help in specific teams, but Python is the safe default to prepare in.

Do I need prior experience with hyper-converged infrastructure to get the role?

Not necessarily, but it helps significantly for senior roles. Candidates report that interviewers care more about whether you understand distributed systems concepts (consistency, fault tolerance, node failure) than whether you have used Nutanix products specifically. Reading Nutanix's public documentation before your interview shows initiative and helps you speak the same language as the panel.

What salary should I expect for a QA Engineer at Nutanix in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level QA Engineers with 3-5 years of experience earn 9-17 LPA across India, and senior engineers with 6-9 years earn 17-30 LPA. Nutanix is considered a strong employer by industry surveys, so compensation is likely toward the upper end of these bands, though actual offers depend on your experience and negotiation. Always verify current Nutanix-specific numbers on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before accepting an offer.

Is the Nutanix QA interview more technical or behavioural?

Candidates report a roughly even mix, though the technical rounds tend to be more demanding than average for a QA role. Expect deep questions on automation framework design, CI/CD integration, and how you would test specific infrastructure failure scenarios. The behavioural round typically focuses on ownership, communication during incidents, and cross-team collaboration.

Where are most Nutanix QA Engineer roles based in India?

Among the 459 QA Engineer openings tracked by knok jobradar across India, Bangalore leads with 87 and Delhi follows with 67. Nutanix is publicly known to have a strong engineering presence in Bangalore. If location matters to you, confirm the work arrangement and office location directly with the recruiter, since hybrid and remote policies vary by team.
