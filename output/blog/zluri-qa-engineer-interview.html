01 Overview

Overview

Zluri is a SaaS management platform that helps IT and finance teams discover, manage, and optimise software subscriptions across an organisation. Their product connects with hundreds of business tools, which makes integration quality and reliability central to what the QA team works on every day.

Knok data shows Zluri currently has 10 open QA Engineer positions. The interview process typically spans three to five rounds: a screening call with HR, one or two technical rounds focused on testing concepts and automation, a practical assignment or live coding session, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or hiring manager.

QA at Zluri is not just about finding bugs. Candidates report that interviewers look for people who can think about system-level reliability, write maintainable automation code, and understand how SaaS integrations fail in the real world. If you have experience testing REST APIs, OAuth flows, or multi-tenant applications, that background is directly relevant here.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would test a new third-party app integration added to the Zluri platform.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built an integration between our internal tool and a widely used HR platform.

*Task:* My responsibility was to own the end-to-end test strategy before the feature went to production.

*Action:* I started by mapping every data flow: what gets sent, what comes back, and what happens when the external API is slow or returns an error. I wrote test cases for the happy path first, then focused on edge cases like token expiry, rate limiting, and partial data responses. I used Postman for exploratory API testing, then moved the stable cases into our automation suite. I specifically checked error-response handling because silent failures in integrations are harder to catch than outright crashes.

*Result:* We caught a silent data-loss bug where a null field from the external API was overwriting existing records in our database. Fixing it before release saved a major customer escalation.

---

Q: A test that was passing last week is now failing in CI. Walk me through how you would debug it.

*Situation:* On a project I was on, a regression test for a dashboard feature started failing consistently in our nightly pipeline.

*Task:* I was the QA owner for that module and needed to diagnose and resolve the failure without blocking the release.

*Action:* I first checked whether the failure was in the test code or the application by replicating it locally. I then compared the last passing build against the current one using git diff on both the app code and the test code. The issue turned out to be a selector change a developer had made to a dropdown component without updating the corresponding page object. I isolated the variable, confirmed the hypothesis, and verified the fix in CI.

*Result:* I fixed the selector, added a comment explaining the naming convention we had agreed on, and shared a short team note so developers knew to flag UI changes that could affect test locators. The failure did not recur.

---

Q: How do you validate that numbers shown on a customer-facing dashboard are accurate?

*Situation:* A previous employer built a spend analytics dashboard for procurement teams, similar in purpose to what Zluri shows for SaaS spend.

*Task:* I was asked to create a validation strategy before a major release that updated the calculation logic.

*Action:* I worked with the data engineering team to understand the source tables and transformation logic. I then wrote SQL queries to compute expected values independently and compared them against what the UI displayed. I also tested boundary conditions: zero usage, currencies other than INR, and apps added mid-month where proration logic applied.

*Result:* We found a rounding inconsistency that caused small discrepancies at scale. The fix was straightforward once identified, and the feature shipped with full confidence from the business stakeholders.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'how would you test X' questions, structure your answer in three layers: inputs and outputs (what data goes in, what comes back), failure modes (what can go wrong, including network errors and bad data), and coverage trade-offs (what you would automate versus explore manually). This shows breadth without turning into an exhaustive list of every possible test case.

For 'tell me about a time' questions, use the STAR structure: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific role), Action (what you actually did, in concrete steps), Result (what changed because of your work). Keep Situation and Task short. Interviewers want to hear the Action and Result in detail.

For tool or framework questions, name the tool, describe what you used it for, and mention one non-obvious thing you learned or one limitation you worked around. This signals hands-on experience rather than resume-padding.

For 'how do you collaborate' questions, give a specific example of a process you put in place or a conversation you initiated, not a general statement about being a team player. Zluri's QA role is cross-functional, so interviewers want evidence you can work effectively with developers, product managers, and data teams.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Zluri's QA interviewers typically look for a few things beyond basic testing knowledge.

Product curiosity. Candidates who understand what a SaaS management platform actually does, and why data accuracy and integration reliability matter to customers, tend to stand out. You do not need to have used Zluri before, but you should be able to speak to why integration testing is harder than testing a standalone application.

Automation depth. Writing tests is the baseline expectation. Candidates who can also discuss test architecture, maintainability, and CI/CD pipeline integration show they think beyond the individual test case.

Debugging rigour. Interviewers commonly ask candidates to walk through a debugging scenario. They want to see a systematic approach: isolate the variable, check the evidence, form a hypothesis, verify it.

Communication with developers. Quality at a product company depends on QA working closely with engineering. Interviewers look for examples of candidates who surfaced issues early, influenced how features were designed for testability, or helped a team move testing earlier in the process.

Ownership mindset. Zluri is a growth-stage company. Candidates who take initiative, write documentation, and improve processes without being asked tend to fit the culture well.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: understand the product and domain.
Sign up for a free trial or watch product walkthrough videos on Zluri's website. Read about SaaS management as a category so you can speak intelligently about why IT teams need it. Understand what app integrations mean in this context: OAuth connections, data syncs, and permission management across business tools.

Week two: sharpen your technical skills.
Practise API testing using a public API. Focus on testing error responses, authentication flows, and schema validation, not just the happy path. If you use Selenium or Cypress, write a small project from scratch so you can walk through your code in an interview. Review SQL basics since dashboard validation questions are common at analytics-heavy products.

Week three: prepare your stories.
Map four to five experiences from your past to likely question themes: a bug you found, a process you improved, a time you collaborated with a developer, and a time you handled a difficult deadline. Write out the STAR structure for each and practise saying them aloud.

Before the interview:
Check Zluri's recent blog posts or product updates so you can mention something current. Prepare two or three questions to ask about the team's test coverage, their CI/CD setup, or how QA is involved in sprint planning. This signals genuine interest rather than just going through the motions.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating API testing as an afterthought. Many candidates mention API testing but only describe happy-path checks. For a platform like Zluri, error handling and partial-failure scenarios are where real bugs hide. Prepare specific examples of edge cases you have tested at the API layer.

Vague automation answers. Saying 'I have worked with Selenium' without being able to discuss page object models, waits, or CI integration raises doubts. Be ready to talk about the structure of your automation suite, not just the tools you listed on your resume.

Not knowing the product. Candidates who cannot describe what Zluri does struggle to answer 'how would you test X' questions convincingly. Spend time on the product before the interview.

Ignoring data validation. Zluri surfaces analytics and spend data to customers. Skipping any mention of data accuracy testing suggests a gap in thinking for this kind of product.

Being passive about quality. Saying 'I report bugs to developers' is a weak answer at a growth-stage company. Show that you have opinions about process, take initiative on test coverage, and can influence how the team works.

Skipping questions for the interviewer. Candidates who ask nothing at the end of an interview often come across as less engaged. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team's testing practices and the product roadmap.

How many rounds does the Zluri QA interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to five rounds. This usually includes an HR screening call, one or two technical rounds covering testing concepts and automation, a practical task or coding exercise, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or hiring manager. The exact structure can vary by team and role level. It is worth asking your recruiter to outline the process when you schedule the first call.

What automation tools should I know for a Zluri QA role?

Selenium and Cypress come up most often in candidate reports for this type of role. Playwright is increasingly mentioned as well. More important than the specific tool is your ability to explain how you structure a maintainable test suite, handle dynamic elements, and integrate tests into a CI/CD pipeline. Pick the framework you know best and be ready to discuss it in depth, including its limitations.

Is there a coding test or take-home assignment?

Candidates report that many QA roles at product companies include a practical component, either a take-home assignment or a live exercise. For a QA role this could involve writing test cases for a described feature, automating a scenario against a sample application, or reviewing and improving existing test code. Prepare by practising small end-to-end automation projects in your preferred framework so you can work confidently under time pressure.
