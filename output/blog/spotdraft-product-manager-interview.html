01 Overview

Overview

SpotDraft is a Bangalore-based legal tech company building a contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform. Their product helps legal and business teams create, negotiate, sign, and track contracts faster, with AI powering clause review and contract summarisation. As a Product Manager at SpotDraft, you work at the intersection of enterprise software design, legal workflows, and AI product thinking.

SpotDraft currently has 19 open roles, making it one of the more active legal tech hirers in India right now. The PM interview process typically covers product sense, analytical thinking, case studies, and cross-functional collaboration. Candidates report a mix of behavioural and product design rounds, though the exact format varies by seniority and team.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report that SpotDraft PM interviews focus heavily on the product itself, legal workflow familiarity, and your ability to reason through AI feature decisions. Here are the questions that come up most often:

  1. SpotDraft competes with global CLM players. How would you position SpotDraft's product for the Indian mid-market?
  2. Walk us through how you would prioritise features for a legal team using SpotDraft for the first time.
  3. SpotDraft uses AI to review and summarise contracts. How would you measure whether that AI feature is actually saving lawyers time?
  4. A large enterprise client says the contract approval workflow is too complex. How do you decide what to change and what to leave alone?
  5. How would you design an onboarding flow for a non-technical legal professional who has never used a CLM tool before?
  6. SpotDraft integrates with tools like Salesforce and Slack. A customer requests a new integration. How do you decide whether to build it?
  7. How would you improve SpotDraft's contract template library to drive more daily active usage?
  8. A key metric (say, contract turnaround time) suddenly spikes. Walk us through how you would diagnose the problem.
  9. Legal buyers are often risk-averse. How does that shape how you think about launching new or experimental features?
  10. Tell us about a product you genuinely admire. What would you borrow from its design and apply to contract management software?
  11. Describe a time you had to push back on an engineering team's timeline. How did you handle it?
  12. How would you think about pricing a new AI-powered add-on for SpotDraft's enterprise customers?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you measure whether SpotDraft's AI contract review feature is saving lawyers time?

*Situation:* At a previous company, we shipped an AI document-analysis tool with no agreed success metric upfront. Six weeks in, nobody could tell whether users were finding it useful or quietly ignoring it.

*Task:* My job was to define the right metric, validate it with legal users, and instrument the product before the next release cycle.

*Action:* I ran brief interviews with six legal professionals to map their actual workflow. The real bottleneck was not reading the contract but extracting key clauses into a review checklist. So I proposed 'median time to complete a review checklist' as the primary metric, and 'contracts reviewed per user per week' as the secondary. I worked with engineering to add timestamps at each workflow step and ran a four-week pilot alongside a control group using the old manual process.

*Result:* The pilot showed a clear, consistent reduction in checklist time across users. That data gave our sales team something concrete to share with enterprise buyers, and the AI review feature became the lead selling point in two new client conversations.

---

Q: A large enterprise client says SpotDraft's approval workflow is too complex. How do you decide what to change?

*Situation:* In a past role at a B2B SaaS company, a flagship client escalated that our multi-step approval flow was slowing down their procurement team. The CEO was copied on the complaint.

*Task:* I had to figure out quickly whether this was a product problem affecting many users or a configuration issue specific to this client, without breaking the experience for others.

*Action:* I pulled usage data for that client's account and compared it to our top five accounts. I found they had configured seven approval layers where our design assumed three. I set up a call with their admin to understand why. It turned out two of those layers existed to work around a missing 'conditional approval' feature we had never built. I wrote a brief spec for conditional approvals, confirmed three other clients had the same gap, and prioritised it in the next sprint while offering the current client a short-term workaround.

*Result:* We shipped conditional approvals in the following release cycle. The escalating client renewed their contract, and two other clients upgraded their plan specifically to access the new feature.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to push back on an engineering team's timeline. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* We were building a new contract signing flow and engineering estimated three months. Our sales team had already promised a key prospect the feature in six weeks.

*Task:* I needed to either close the gap or help sales reset expectations, without damaging trust with either the engineering team or the prospect.

*Action:* I sat down with the engineering lead to understand what was driving the estimate. About half the time was for edge cases covering non-standard document formats our product rarely saw in practice. I proposed shipping a scoped version covering the most common signing scenarios first, with a clear roadmap for edge case coverage. I brought usage data showing which document types our prospect actually used, and suggested sales demo the scoped version to them.

*Result:* The scoped version shipped in seven weeks. The prospect was satisfied, signed the deal, and the full feature followed the next quarter with no customer complaints about the gap.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product sense questions, start by clarifying the goal (what problem are we solving and for whom), then map the user journey to find where the friction is, brainstorm solutions, and close by defining how you would measure success. For SpotDraft specifically, always ground your answer in the legal user context. The people using this product are lawyers and legal-ops professionals who value accuracy and auditability above novelty, and who will notice if your answer treats contracts like social media posts.

For prioritisation questions, use a simple impact-versus-effort framing. Name the metric you are trying to move, list your options, score each on user impact and build effort, then explain which you would pick and why. Candidates report that SpotDraft interviewers want to hear you acknowledge stakeholder trade-offs, not just a clean ranked list.

For metrics and diagnostic questions, start at the top of the funnel and check where the number breaks. For SpotDraft, the contract funnel typically looks like: contract created, sent for review, reviewed, sent for signature, signed. A spike in turnaround time could break at any stage, so naming the stage is more impressive than jumping straight to a root cause.

For behavioural questions, use the STAR structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation short (two sentences at most), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did), and close with a concrete Result. Avoid vague outcomes like 'the team was happier.' Candidates report that SpotDraft interviewers typically probe on how you handled disagreement or ambiguity, so prepare at least one story where things did not go smoothly and you adapted.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

SpotDraft PMs work closely with legal professionals and enterprise buyers. Interviewers are typically looking for a few things specific to this context.

Empathy for legal users. Legal and legal-ops teams are not a typical consumer audience. They care about compliance, auditability, and risk. Candidates who talk about 'moving fast' without acknowledging the consequences of errors in a legal document tend to be filtered out early.

Comfort with AI product decisions. SpotDraft is actively building AI into its core product. Interviewers want to see you apply PM rigour to AI features: defining what 'good output' looks like, measuring real-world accuracy, and deciding when the AI should surface a result versus ask for human review.

Enterprise sales awareness. A significant part of the SpotDraft PM role involves working with sales and customer success teams. Being able to talk about how you have supported a sales cycle, managed a client escalation, or built a feature based on enterprise feedback signals that you understand the B2B context.

Data-driven prioritisation. Candidates report that interviewers push hard on prioritisation reasoning. 'I would do this because it feels right' does not land well. Come prepared to explain how you would measure impact, what proxy metric you would use if direct data is unavailable, and how you weigh competing stakeholder needs.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the product.
Sign up for a SpotDraft trial or request a demo. Walk through the contract creation, review, and signing flow yourself. Note where you find friction and where the product does well. Read SpotDraft's blog and any publicly available case studies to understand which customer segments they focus on.

Week 2: Build your story bank.
Prepare four to five STAR stories covering: a feature you prioritised under real constraints, a time you used data to make a tough decision, a time you managed a difficult stakeholder or cross-functional conflict, and a product launch or improvement you owned end to end.

Week 3: Practice out loud.
Pick two or three of the questions listed above and answer them out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself if possible. Aim for four to five minutes per answer. Ask a peer to play the interviewer and push back on your reasoning.

Before each round:
Review SpotDraft's recent product announcements and any news from the last three to six months. Prepare two or three specific questions for the interviewer about the team, the roadmap, or how PM success is measured internally. Candidates report that back-to-back rounds can cover overlapping themes, so varying your examples across rounds matters.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating legal users like general consumers. Legal professionals have different risk tolerances. A candidate who says 'we should A/B test the contract signing flow on all users' without acknowledging the compliance implications will lose the interviewer's confidence quickly.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would track engagement' is not enough. Name the specific metric, the numerator, the denominator, and how you would instrument it. SpotDraft interviewers typically probe here and expect precision.

Ignoring the enterprise sales context. SpotDraft sells to businesses, not individuals. A feature prioritisation answer that ignores the sales team, customer success, or renewal risk will feel incomplete to most interviewers.

Over-engineering the AI answer. When asked about AI features, candidates sometimes go deep into model architecture. SpotDraft interviewers typically want PM thinking: what is the user experience, how do you handle errors, how do you build user trust in the AI output. Technical depth is a bonus, not the point.

Not using the product before the interview. Several candidates report being asked about specific SpotDraft features or workflows during rounds. Not having used the product at all is a visible gap. Even a brief hands-on session changes how confidently you can answer.

How many rounds does the SpotDraft PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically covers three to five rounds, including an initial screening call, a product case or take-home assignment, one or two in-depth interviews, and a final leadership or culture round. The exact structure varies by level and team. It is always worth confirming the format with your recruiter after the first call so you know what to prepare for.

Does SpotDraft give a take-home case study?

Candidates report that SpotDraft sometimes assigns a take-home product case as part of the process, often asking you to analyse a feature or propose an improvement to their product. If you receive one, focus on user empathy and clear metrics rather than just a feature list. Legal tech buyers care about risk and reliability, so your recommendations should reflect that.

Do I need prior legal tech experience to get a PM role at SpotDraft?

Prior legal tech experience is a plus but candidates report it is not a strict requirement for most PM roles. What matters more is showing you understand the mindset of legal and legal-ops users: their aversion to errors, their need for audit trails, and the multi-stakeholder nature of contract approvals. Coming in with examples from compliance, fintech, or enterprise B2B software is a solid substitute.
