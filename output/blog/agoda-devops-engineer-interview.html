01 Overview

Overview

Agoda is a global travel technology company and one of the more competitive engineering employers in India. As of mid-2026, knok's job radar found 299 open DevOps Engineer roles at Agoda, out of 811 total DevOps Engineer openings tracked across India. Bangalore leads with 187 of those openings, making it the primary hiring hub for this role.

The interview process at Agoda typically spans multiple rounds. Candidates report a combination of scripting assessments, infrastructure design questions, and a cultural alignment discussion. Agoda's platform runs at considerable scale in the travel tech space, so interviewers focus heavily on reliability engineering, automation, and how you respond to production issues. Expect questions that go deep on Kubernetes, CI/CD pipelines, observability, and cloud infrastructure. The sections below walk you through the most commonly reported questions, how to answer them, and what interviewers are actually looking for.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from Agoda DevOps Engineer interviews. The key focus areas are container orchestration, CI/CD design, cloud infrastructure, observability, and incident response.

  1. Walk me through how you would design a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices application from scratch.
  2. How have you used Kubernetes in production? What challenges did you face at scale?
  3. Describe your approach to infrastructure as code. Which tools have you used, and why?
  4. How do you manage secrets and credentials in a cloud-native environment?

  10. How do you secure a DevOps pipeline end to end, from code commit to production?
  11. What is your experience with cloud providers such as AWS, GCP, or Azure? How do you choose between managed services and self-hosted tools?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a production incident you led. How did you resolve it, and what did you change afterwards?

*Situation:* Our primary API gateway started returning errors for a large share of requests on a Friday evening. The on-call page fired and I picked it up.

*Task:* I needed to diagnose the root cause quickly, restore service, and then prevent the same issue from recurring.

*Action:* I pulled recent deployment logs and found a config change had been pushed thirty minutes before the error spike. I coordinated with the developer who made the change, rolled back the config through our IaC pipeline, and confirmed that metrics returned to normal. I then wrote a post-mortem and proposed a mandatory canary deployment policy for all config changes touching the API gateway.

*Result:* Service was restored within twenty minutes of the page. The canary policy was adopted by the team and caught two similar misconfigurations in the following quarter before they reached production.

---

Q: How have you used Kubernetes in production? What challenges did you face at scale?

*Situation:* At my previous company we migrated a monolithic application to a Kubernetes-based microservices architecture over about a year.

*Task:* I owned the cluster infrastructure, node autoscaling, and the migration runbook for the first six services we moved over.

*Action:* I set up cluster autoscaler and horizontal pod autoscalers tuned to our traffic patterns. I introduced pod disruption budgets to ensure rolling updates did not break availability. I also migrated our secrets management from plain environment variables to a Vault integration using the Kubernetes auth backend.

*Result:* Deployment time dropped significantly, and we had zero unplanned downtime during the migration window for the services I owned.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved deployment frequency or reduced deployment risk.

*Situation:* Our team was deploying to production manually once every two weeks. Every release was stressful because the change batch was large, which made rollbacks painful.

*Task:* I proposed moving to continuous delivery with automated testing gates and feature flags.

*Action:* I rebuilt our Jenkins pipelines using a declarative approach, added automated integration tests as a required gate, and introduced a feature flag library so developers could ship code dark and enable features independently. I ran a workshop to help the team get comfortable with the new flow.

*Result:* Within two months the team was deploying multiple times per week. Rollback time dropped dramatically because changes were smaller and feature flags let us disable specific functionality without a full redeploy.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For infrastructure and system design questions, start with requirements. Ask about scale, traffic patterns, reliability targets, and team size before proposing a solution. Agoda interviewers typically want to see that you think before you draw an architecture.

For incident response questions, use a clear structure: detection (how you knew something was wrong), diagnosis (how you found the cause), mitigation (what you did to restore service), and follow-through (what you changed to prevent recurrence). Skipping the follow-through step is a common reason candidates are marked down.

For behavioral questions, use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation and Task brief, two or three sentences each. Spend most of your time on the Action, because that is where your technical judgement shows. Quantify the Result wherever you can, but a qualitative outcome is still better than no result.

For tool and technology questions, avoid just listing tools. Explain why you chose a particular tool over alternatives and what tradeoffs you accepted. Agoda's scale means interviewers are skeptical of candidates who picked a tool without understanding its limitations.

For security questions, show that security is a first-class concern in your workflow, not a checklist at the end. Mention where you enforce controls (pipeline gates, policy as code, image scanning) rather than only through manual review.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Ownership over polish. Agoda's engineering culture, as described publicly, values engineers who take full ownership of their systems. Interviewers want to see that you stay involved through incidents, post-mortems, and follow-up fixes, not just the initial deployment.

Practical depth, not resume keywords. Expect follow-up questions on every tool you mention. If you list Terraform on your resume, be ready to explain how you handle state locking, module versioning, and secrets. Mentioning a tool you have only used lightly is a risk.

Scale awareness. Travel tech platforms see traffic spikes around holidays and major sale events. Interviewers will probe whether you have thought about autoscaling, graceful degradation, and load shedding, even if your previous employer was smaller.

Communication under pressure. A common scenario question is how you handle an incident where the root cause is not obvious. They want to see structured thinking and calm communication, not just technical ability.

Collaboration with developers. DevOps at Agoda is not a pure ops function. Candidates who show experience pairing with developers on CI/CD pipelines, reviewing application configs, and influencing deployment practices tend to score better.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical review
Revise Kubernetes internals: scheduling, networking, storage, and RBAC. Practice writing Terraform and Helm configurations from scratch. Review your CI/CD tool of choice deeply enough to explain every design decision you made in it.

Week 2: System design practice
Practice designing a monitoring stack for a high-traffic API, covering metrics, alerts, and dashboards. Design a zero-downtime deployment strategy that includes database schema migrations. Sketch a secrets management architecture using Vault or a cloud-native equivalent.

Week 3: Behavioral and incident stories
Prepare three to four STAR stories covering: a production incident you led, a time you improved a deployment process, a time you disagreed with a technical decision and how you handled it, and a time you helped a developer debug a production issue. Write each story out and time yourself telling it in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and Agoda context
Do at least two timed mock technical interviews with a peer or mentor. Read publicly available Agoda engineering blog posts to understand their technical choices and scale. Review the specific job description carefully, since requirements vary between infrastructure, platform, and SRE tracks.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Listing tools without depth. Saying you have used Kubernetes, Terraform, and Prometheus is a starting point, not an answer. Agoda interviewers will ask follow-up questions immediately. Go into the interview ready to discuss one or two tools at a genuinely deep level.

Skipping the 'why' in architecture answers. Candidates who jump straight to a solution without asking clarifying questions or stating their assumptions tend to be marked down. Ask about scale, constraints, and existing systems before proposing a design.

Underplaying incident experience. Some candidates avoid talking about incidents where things went wrong, worrying it reflects badly. In practice, strong incident stories that show learning and genuine follow-through are among the most valued signals in a DevOps interview.

Treating security as an afterthought. A common mistake is describing a pipeline design and only mentioning security when the interviewer prompts for it. Weave security thinking in from the beginning.

Not quantifying results. Vague results like 'it improved performance' are weaker than specific ones. Even rough numbers, such as 'deployment time went from two hours to roughly fifteen minutes,' are much more convincing than qualitative-only answers.

Ignoring the on-call and reliability angle. DevOps roles at scale companies involve real on-call responsibility. Candidates who have no stories about alert management, runbooks, or post-mortems can struggle in the final rounds.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Agoda DevOps Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds, including a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering scripting and infrastructure design, and a final discussion on team fit. The exact structure can vary by team and seniority level. It is worth asking your recruiter at the start what the process looks like for the specific role you applied to.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer role at Agoda in India?

Glassdoor and publicly reported figures for DevOps Engineers in India vary widely by experience. Based on knok's job radar data, mid-level engineers (3-5 years) are commonly cited in the 15-28 LPA range across the market, and senior engineers (6-9 years) in the 30-50 LPA range. Agoda is generally considered a top-tier tech employer, so packages tend to be competitive within these bands. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for the most current and role-specific figures.

Which city has the most DevOps Engineer openings in India?

Based on knok's job radar data as of mid-2026, Bangalore leads with 187 of the 811 total DevOps Engineer openings tracked across India. Delhi had 40 and Pune had 37. If you are flexible on location, Bangalore gives you the largest pool of opportunities for this role.

Do I need to know a specific cloud provider for Agoda DevOps roles?

Agoda is known to use multiple cloud providers, and job descriptions typically mention AWS or GCP experience as preferred. Candidates report being tested on cloud-agnostic concepts like autoscaling, managed Kubernetes, networking, and object storage rather than on console-level details for one specific provider. Strong fundamentals across cloud concepts, combined with depth in at least one provider, is the safer preparation strategy.

Is coding ability tested in an Agoda DevOps interview?

Candidates report that scripting ability is tested, typically in Python, Bash, or Go, covering tasks like parsing logs, writing automation scripts, or debugging a broken pipeline snippet. It is typically not a full algorithmic coding round like you would see for a software engineering role. Brushing up on practical scripting and basic API calls is more useful than practicing LeetCode-style problems for this role.

How should I talk about a time things went wrong in production?

Be direct and own your part in it. Interviewers value engineers who can clearly narrate what went wrong, what they did to fix it, and what they changed so it would not happen again. Trying to minimise or deflect from the incident is a red flag. A well-told incident story that shows structured thinking, calm communication, and genuine follow-through is one of the strongest answers you can give in a DevOps interview.
