01 Overview

Overview

Warner Bros Discovery (WBD) is one of the world's largest media and entertainment companies, home to Max, HBO, CNN, DC, and the Warner Bros studio. Frontend Engineers here build products that millions of viewers use every day, from streaming interfaces and live sports apps to news platforms.

WBD currently has 55 open roles in frontend engineering. Candidates report that the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen, one or two coding rounds focused on React and JavaScript, and a final loop with engineering leadership. The full process typically takes 3-5 weeks.

The company values engineers who combine strong technical craft with genuine product curiosity. Come prepared to talk about performance, accessibility, and how your code decisions connect directly to the viewer's experience.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these questions appearing frequently across WBD frontend interview loops:

  1. Walk us through how you would architect a large-scale video streaming UI from scratch.

  5. How do you manage state in a complex frontend application, and what trade-offs did you weigh?
  6. Describe a time you collaborated with a backend team to define and consume an API contract.

  8. What is your approach to frontend testing, and which layers do you prioritise?

  10. How would you design a multi-region, multi-language frontend for a global streaming product?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would design a large-scale video streaming UI.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were rebuilding the video player interface for an OTT platform. The existing player was a monolith that made A/B testing new features extremely slow and risky.

*Task:* I was responsible for designing the new component architecture and ensuring the player loaded quickly on slower mobile connections.

*Action:* I broke the player into composable React components with lazy loading for non-critical UI elements like social sharing and 'up next' panels. I introduced a custom hook to abstract playback state from the visual layer, set up Lighthouse CI to catch performance regressions at the PR stage, and documented the component contracts so other teams could contribute safely.

*Result:* Feature iteration cycles shortened noticeably, and we could run concurrent A/B experiments on the player without deployment conflicts.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved page performance significantly.

*Situation:* Our content discovery page was loading slowly because it fetched all shelf data upfront, even for sections well below the fold.

*Task:* I needed to reduce the initial load time without a full rewrite, within a single sprint.

*Action:* I implemented intersection-observer-based lazy loading for below-fold shelves, switched to skeleton screens for a progressive loading feel, and added HTTP-level caching headers in coordination with the backend team. I also audited and removed a bloated third-party analytics script that was blocking the main thread.

*Result:* First contentful paint dropped to within our target budget in internal testing, and the product team reported a positive shift in engagement metrics in the weeks after launch.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you advocated for better accessibility on your team.

*Situation:* During a design review, I noticed our new modal component lacked keyboard trap logic and had no ARIA roles, which meant screen-reader users could not interact with it at all.

*Task:* I needed to raise this with the design and product teams and fix it without delaying the launch date.

*Action:* I recorded a short screen-reader demo showing how broken the experience was, which made the impact tangible to non-technical stakeholders. I then built a reusable accessible modal using the WAI-ARIA dialog pattern, wrote usage documentation, and added unit tests for focus management so the behaviour could not regress silently.

*Result:* The component shipped on time and became the team standard, preventing the same class of issue from recurring across other features.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the go-to for behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend the most time on Action, and close with a concrete, specific Result. Vague endings like 'the team was happy' are a common miss.

'Why, What, Trade-offs' works well for architecture and design questions. Start with why you made a decision, describe what you built, then be honest about limitations or what you would do differently. WBD interviewers respond well to candidates who acknowledge trade-offs rather than presenting every past decision as perfect.

'Diagnose, Hypothesise, Verify' suits debugging and performance questions. Walk the interviewer through how you narrowed down the root cause before jumping to a solution. This shows systematic thinking rather than trial-and-error.

Clarify before coding. In live coding rounds, candidates report that taking a few minutes to ask clarifying questions and state your approach out loud is viewed positively. Jumping straight into code without scoping the problem first is a frequent red flag.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

WBD frontend teams build at significant scale, so interviewers typically look for a specific combination of skills and mindset.

Product empathy. Can you explain how a technical decision improves or harms the viewer's experience? Engineers who speak only in technical abstractions tend to score lower in final-loop rounds.

Performance instinct. Streaming UI questions come up often. Know how browsers render, how to use the network tab, and how to interpret Core Web Vitals. Be ready to discuss trade-offs between bundle size, render time, and developer experience.

Collaborative problem-solving. Interviewers pay close attention to how you describe working with hypothetical teammates during system design questions. Mention API contracts, design reviews, and cross-functional communication naturally.

Ownership mentality. WBD is a large organisation and teams need engineers who take initiative. Expect questions about times you identified a problem and drove it to resolution without being asked.

Code quality and testing. Bring examples of test coverage strategy, code review habits, and how you have improved developer experience on a past team.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Fundamentals and system design
Revisit JavaScript core concepts: closures, the event loop, prototypal inheritance, and async patterns. Practice one React-focused system design problem per day, such as a streaming feed, a live score ticker, or a video player. Read up on Core Web Vitals and how to measure them in DevTools.

Week 2: Behavioural stories
Write out 6-8 STAR stories covering performance wins, cross-team collaboration, accessibility decisions, and ownership moments. Practise saying them out loud, and check which stories can flex to cover multiple question types.

Week 3: WBD-specific prep
Use Max, CNN, and other WBD products as a regular viewer, then revisit them with DevTools open. Audit their lazy loading patterns, check the network waterfall, and note accessibility patterns. Think about how you would improve or rebuild specific components you see.

Week 4: Mock interviews and polish
Do at least two timed mock coding rounds with a peer or on a practice platform. Review your weakest areas from that feedback. Prepare 3-4 thoughtful questions for the interviewer about the team's biggest technical challenges right now.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'why' on technical decisions. Saying 'I used Redux' is not enough. WBD interviewers want to understand why you chose it, what alternatives you considered, and what problem it actually solved.

Treating accessibility as an afterthought. Accessibility questions come up regularly at media companies. Practise talking about it as a design default, not a retrofit added at the end.

Ignoring the product context. Candidates who only talk about code without connecting it to the viewer or the business tend to score lower in final-loop rounds. Always tie your work back to user impact.

Over-engineering in live coding rounds. Candidates report that clean, readable, well-explained code scores better than an over-engineered solution that is hard to walk through.

Not asking clarifying questions. Jumping straight into a solution without scoping the problem first is a common red flag. Take a moment to confirm assumptions before writing a single line.

Weak results in STAR answers. Ending a story with 'it went well' is a missed opportunity. Describe measurable change, shifted metrics, or concrete team impact wherever possible.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the WBD Frontend Engineer interview process typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves 4-5 rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen, one or two coding or design rounds, and a final loop with engineering leadership. The structure can vary by team and seniority level, so ask your recruiter upfront for a clear picture of what to expect.

What frameworks and languages does WBD expect me to know?

React is the most commonly expected framework for WBD frontend roles, and candidates report that strong JavaScript (ES6+) fundamentals are tested directly. TypeScript knowledge is a practical plus. You are generally free to use your preferred setup in coding rounds, but confirm this with your recruiter beforehand.

What salary can I expect as a Frontend Engineer at WBD in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level Frontend Engineers (3-5 years) see ranges in the 12-22 LPA band, while senior engineers (6-9 years) are commonly cited in the 24-40 LPA range. Lead and Staff roles can reach 38-58+ LPA. Always cross-check current figures on Glassdoor or levels.fyi, as compensation bands shift over time.

Is there a system design round in the WBD frontend interview?

Candidates report that senior and lead-level roles typically include a frontend system design round, covering topics like streaming UI architecture, component library design, or performance at scale. For entry and mid-level roles, the focus is usually on coding fundamentals and React patterns rather than full system design.

Where are most WBD Frontend Engineer roles in India located?

Based on knok jobradar data, Bangalore has the highest concentration with 102 of the 405 total Frontend Engineer openings tracked. Delhi follows with 36, and Pune has 11. Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Chennai each have a smaller number of listings.

How long does the WBD hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report that the end-to-end process typically takes 3-5 weeks, though this varies by team and how quickly interview slots open up. Following up politely with your recruiter after each round is perfectly acceptable and unlikely to work against you.
