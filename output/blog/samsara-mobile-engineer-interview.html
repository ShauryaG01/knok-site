01 Overview

Overview

Samsara is a US-headquartered connected operations platform that helps companies manage fleets, monitor driver safety, and track industrial assets in real time. Their mobile apps sit at the heart of the product. The Driver App serves commercial drivers for ELD (Electronic Logging Device) compliance, and mobile engineers work on genuinely hard problems: offline-first sync, Bluetooth connectivity to in-cab hardware, real-time telemetry, and strict regulatory requirements.

As of July 2026, Samsara had 350 open roles globally. Knok's job radar found 79 Mobile Engineer openings across India, with Bangalore leading at 21 openings. The India engineering hub is growing and actively hiring.

Candidates report the interview process typically runs 4-5 rounds: a recruiter call, a technical screen, a coding or take-home round, a system design round, and a final panel. Samsara typically adjusts the process for senior candidates. Expect strong emphasis on real-world constraints (connectivity, battery, hardware) throughout every technical round.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

  1. How would you design the Samsara Driver App to work reliably in areas with poor or no cell connectivity?
  2. Walk us through your approach to background location tracking on Android and iOS without draining battery.
  3. How would you implement ELD-compliant Hours of Service (HOS) logging in a mobile app?
  4. Describe how you would establish and maintain a Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) connection between a mobile device and a vehicle gateway.
  5. How do you architect an offline-first mobile app with conflict resolution when data syncs back to the server?
  6. How would you design push notifications for real-time driver safety alerts?
  7. How do you approach performance profiling and memory optimisation in a large mobile codebase?
  8. Samsara's fleet data includes GPS traces, sensor readings, and video clips. How would you handle large data payloads efficiently on mobile?
  9. How would you write automated tests for a feature that depends on physical hardware like a dashcam or vehicle gateway?
  10. How do you manage feature flags and phased rollouts for a mobile app used in safety-critical workflows?
  11. Tell us about a time you debugged a hard-to-reproduce crash in production. What tools and process did you use?
  12. How would you migrate a legacy screen from an older architecture (like MVC) to a modern one (like MVVM or MVI) without breaking existing behaviour?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you design the Driver App to work reliably in poor connectivity?

*Situation:* At my previous company, I worked on a delivery partner app used in rural India where 3G was unreliable and drivers frequently entered tunnels or industrial zones with no signal.

*Task:* I was responsible for making order status updates and navigation instructions available offline without data loss.

*Action:* I introduced a local SQLite database as the source of truth for all driver state. Every action the driver took was written locally first, then queued in a WorkManager job for background sync. I used exponential backoff with a cap for retries and built a conflict resolution layer that applied a 'last write wins with server authority' rule for safety-critical fields.

*Result:* Driver app reliability improved significantly. Missed status updates dropped and support tickets related to data loss fell sharply. The architecture became the standard sync pattern for other apps in the organisation.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you debugged a hard-to-reproduce crash in production.

*Situation:* Months after launch, we saw a small but steady stream of NullPointerException crashes in our trip summary screen, only on certain Android OEM devices.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause without being able to reproduce it locally.

*Action:* I added breadcrumb logging to Firebase Crashlytics capturing state transitions in the fragment lifecycle. After reviewing dozens of crash traces, I noticed the crash always occurred when the app was restored from the background on devices with aggressive memory management. A late-arriving network response tried to update a view that no longer existed in the expected state.

*Result:* I added a lifecycle-aware observer check before applying the response and wrote a unit test covering the back-stack restoration path. The crash rate dropped to zero within two subsequent release cycles.

---

Q: How do you manage feature flags for safety-critical mobile workflows?

*Situation:* We were rolling out a new HOS calculation engine to replace a legacy one. Both had to run in parallel for a period to validate correctness.

*Task:* I was the lead engineer for the rollout. We could not afford any regression in compliance features used by commercial drivers.

*Action:* I worked with the product and data teams to define a shadow mode. The new engine ran alongside the old one, both computed HOS status, but only the old engine's result was shown to drivers. A backend service compared the two outputs and flagged discrepancies. We used a percentage-based rollout flag, starting at a small initial percentage of drivers and increasing weekly only when discrepancy rates stayed below an agreed threshold.

*Result:* We caught several edge cases in the new engine that only appeared in real-world driving patterns. All were fixed before full rollout. Zero compliance incidents were reported post-launch.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions, candidates report that Samsara interviewers expect you to start with constraints: connectivity (assume intermittent), battery (assume always a concern), and regulatory requirements (assume strict). A good structure: first clarify the user journey and device environment, then define the data model, then design the local storage and sync strategy, and finally address edge cases like conflicts and retries.

For coding questions, think out loud. Samsara engineers care about production readiness, not just correctness. After your first working solution, ask yourself: 'What happens if this runs on a device with limited RAM?' or 'What if the process is killed mid-sync?'

For behavioral questions, use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep the Result concrete. If you don't have a precise metric, describe the qualitative outcome clearly. For example: 'zero regression in QA, shipped on schedule'.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Samsara's mobile team builds apps that run in safety-critical, hardware-connected, low-connectivity environments. Interviewers look for a few specific things.

Domain instinct. You should understand why offline-first matters, what ELD compliance means for an app's data model, and why BLE reliability is hard. You don't need prior fleet tech experience, but you need to reason about these constraints naturally.

Production mindset. Candidates who focus only on the happy path rarely clear later rounds. Show that you think about failure modes, memory limits, OS-level interruptions, and background process restrictions.

Platform depth. Whether your background is iOS or Android (or cross-platform), interviewers want to see that you know the platform well beneath the framework layer. Can you explain the Android job scheduler versus WorkManager trade-offs? Do you know how iOS background modes work and their limitations?

Communication. Samsara teams are distributed. Interviewers assess whether you can explain a technical trade-off clearly, as if to a teammate in a different time zone. Think out loud, summarise your reasoning, and invite feedback.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core mobile fundamentals. Review Android lifecycle (Activity, Fragment, ViewModel, LiveData or StateFlow) or iOS (UIKit or SwiftUI lifecycle, Combine). Write a small app that persists state through process death using Room or CoreData.

Week 2: Offline-first and sync. Build or study a sync architecture with a local database as the source of truth. Practice conflict resolution strategies. Read about WorkManager (Android) or BGTaskScheduler (iOS).

Week 3: Samsara-specific domain. Read Samsara's engineering blog and job descriptions. Understand what ELD compliance means (Hours of Service, FMCSA rules). Study BLE connection management. Practice designing a driver workflow that works offline from end to end.

Week 4: System design and mock interviews. Practice a few system design questions out loud. Record yourself if possible. Focus on how you scope the problem, how you handle failure modes, and how you communicate trade-offs. Do at least one mock behavioral round using STAR.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Ignoring connectivity constraints. Many candidates design for always-on internet. Samsara's products live in the real world. Always address offline behaviour, even if the interviewer hasn't explicitly asked.
  1. Skipping the 'why'. Saying 'I used Room for local storage' is less compelling than explaining why: 'Our sync needed atomic transactions across multiple tables, and Room's type-safe queries reduced schema mismatch bugs.'
  1. Over-engineering the take-home. Candidates sometimes add too many features to take-home assignments. Samsara interviewers typically care more about code clarity and test coverage than extra functionality.
  1. Not asking about device constraints. In system design, failing to ask about target devices, OS version support, and memory limits signals inexperience with real-world mobile development.
  1. Weak behavioral answers. Vague answers like 'I worked with the team to fix it' don't show your individual contribution. Be specific about what you decided, what trade-off you made, and what happened as a result.
  1. Not knowing the product. Candidates who haven't used or studied the Samsara Driver App or Fleet Dashboard struggle to connect their answers to the company's actual problems. Download the app and read the product documentation before your first round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Samsara require experience with fleet management or IoT?

Candidates report that prior fleet or IoT experience helps but is not a hard requirement. What interviewers care about is your ability to reason about constrained environments such as low connectivity, battery limits, and hardware integration. If you have worked on any app with real-world constraints (field service, logistics, health monitoring), frame your experience in those terms. Strong fundamentals plus a genuine interest in the domain is typically enough to clear early rounds.

Is the Samsara Mobile Engineer interview more iOS-focused or Android-focused?

Candidates report that Samsara hires for both platforms, and some roles are open to cross-platform experience (React Native or Flutter). The job description for the specific role you apply to will usually specify the required stack. Prepare for platform-depth questions specific to whichever stack you claim as your primary, and be ready to discuss trade-offs with other approaches.

How long does the Samsara interview process typically take?

Candidates report the full process from first recruiter call to offer typically spans 3-5 weeks, though timelines vary by role and team. The India-based roles in Bangalore may have a slightly different panel structure than US-based roles. Ask your recruiter what to expect after you clear the technical screen.

What salary can I expect for a Mobile Engineer role at Samsara in India?

Samsara does not publicly list India salary bands. Levels.fyi and Glassdoor show compensation data contributed by candidates, and industry surveys suggest senior mobile engineers in Bangalore command competitive packages relative to the broader market. Ask your recruiter directly for the band early in the process so you can plan your negotiation.

Is there a take-home assignment in the Samsara Mobile Engineer interview?

Some candidates report a take-home coding assignment, typically a small feature implementation or architectural design. Others go through a live coding session instead. Ask your recruiter which format to expect after you clear the initial technical screen so you can prepare accordingly.

How important is system design for mobile engineer roles at Samsara?

System design is typically a dedicated round for mid-to-senior roles. Candidates report questions focus on mobile-specific design such as offline sync, data pipelines, and notification systems, rather than backend-heavy distributed systems. Junior candidates may face a lighter version focused more on component design within the app.
