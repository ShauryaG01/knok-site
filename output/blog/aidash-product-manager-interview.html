01 Overview

Overview

AiDASH builds climate intelligence software for utilities, infrastructure owners, and insurers. Their core products use satellite imagery and AI to track vegetation encroachment, wildfire risk, and asset health across power lines, pipelines, and roads. The company is US-headquartered but hires PMs globally, including in India, for roles spanning enterprise product, data products, and platform.

As of mid-2026, knok's jobradar shows 2 open Product Manager roles at AiDASH. The PM role here sits at the crossroads of enterprise B2B SaaS, geospatial data, and AI-driven outcomes. You do not need to be a data scientist, but you should be comfortable discussing model accuracy, data pipelines, and how customers interpret AI-generated insights.

Candidates report the process typically involves multiple rounds: an introductory HR screen, a product sense or case round, a technical depth round, and a behavioral or leadership round. Some candidates also report a take-home assignment covering a product roadmap or feature design scenario.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect what candidates have reported across AiDASH PM interview rounds. Themes cluster around enterprise product sense, AI and data literacy, and customer empathy in regulated industries.

  2. AiDASH uses satellite imagery to detect infrastructure risk. How would you define and measure success for a new wildfire risk alert feature?
  3. Walk me through how you would build an annual product roadmap for an enterprise SaaS product with long sales cycles and slow customer feedback loops.
  4. A major utility client says your AI model's accuracy is not good enough for their operations team to trust it. How do you respond as a PM?
  5. How would you explain AiDASH's value proposition differently to a utility's procurement team versus their engineering team?
  6. Describe a time you worked with a data science or ML team to ship a product feature. What made it harder than a typical engineering project?
  7. How do you balance near-term enterprise customer requests with longer-term platform investments that benefit all customers?

  9. If you were designing a new ESG reporting feature for infrastructure clients, walk me through your discovery process from scratch.
  10. How do you detect and manage churn risk when a large enterprise customer is unhappy with product performance?
  11. What metrics would you track for a field-crew-facing dashboard that operations staff use daily on mobile devices?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How did you prioritize conflicting feature requests from multiple enterprise clients?

*Situation:* I was PM for a B2B SaaS platform with a small but high-value utility client base. Three of our top clients submitted conflicting feature requests in the same quarter, and our engineering team had capacity for only one new initiative.

*Task:* I needed to make a prioritization call that kept all three clients satisfied without building a separate one-off customization for each.

*Action:* I mapped each request to the underlying business outcome rather than the stated feature. I found that all three clients were trying to solve the same problem in different ways: faster anomaly triage for their field crews. I then ran a short call with each client's product champion to validate this hypothesis. I proposed a single configurable triage workflow that each client could adapt to their own process, and I briefed our sales team on the roadmap logic so they could manage expectations proactively.

*Result:* We shipped one feature that satisfied all three clients. Renewal conversations for that quarter went smoothly, and multiple clients expanded their contracts in the following quarters. The configurable approach became a reusable pattern for future enterprise customization requests.

---

Q: Describe a time you worked with an ML team to ship an AI-powered feature.

*Situation:* My team was building a predictive maintenance feature that flagged infrastructure assets likely to fail within a set time window, using a model built by our data science team.

*Task:* My job was to translate model outputs into something field engineers would actually trust and act on, not just a score sitting in a dashboard.

*Action:* I ran user research sessions with field engineers over two weeks. The consistent feedback was: 'I need to know why the model flagged this asset, not just that it did.' I worked with the data science team to add a plain-language explanation layer to each prediction, showing the top contributing factors driving the flag. I also pushed for a confidence indicator and a 'report incorrect' button so the model could learn from field feedback over time.

*Result:* Field engineer adoption grew substantially between the pilot phase and the first full quarter after launch. The feedback loop improved model performance in subsequent quarters, which the data science team tracked in their quarterly reviews.

---

Q: How did you handle a situation where a key customer was unhappy with product quality?

*Situation:* Several months after go-live, our largest enterprise client reported that AI-generated risk scores were causing their team to over-dispatch field crews, wasting significant time and budget.

*Task:* I needed to restore client trust without overpromising a fix we could not deliver quickly.

*Action:* I set up a joint session with the client's operations lead, our data science team, and customer success within two days. Rather than defending the model, I asked the client to walk us through specific cases where the alert felt wrong. We identified a calibration issue specific to a certain asset type in their geography. I committed to a two-week investigation with weekly updates and gave the client a direct line to me. I also worked with our CS team to establish a clear escalation protocol so the client never felt passed around.

*Result:* We shipped a targeted recalibration within three weeks. The client moved from at-risk to stable in our CRM, and their operations lead provided a reference call for a new prospect in the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

RICE for prioritisation. Rate features on Reach (how many clients or users are affected), Impact (how much does it move the key metric), Confidence (how certain are you of the estimate), and Effort (engineering cost). For AiDASH, 'Reach' in an enterprise context often means revenue concentration, not raw user headcount. A feature used by one client representing a large share of ARR may outscore a feature used by many smaller clients.

Jobs-to-be-Done for discovery. When clients request a feature, ask what job they are trying to get done. AiDASH clients often ask for 'more alerts' when the real job is 'give my ops team a shorter, more trustworthy list to act on.' Framing problems this way opens non-obvious solutions and avoids building feature clutter.

CIRCLES for product design cases. Comprehend the situation, Identify the customer, Report customer needs, Cut features based on priority, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise. This is especially useful for take-home assignments where you have time to structure your thinking before writing.

The trust ladder for AI features. When presenting an AI-powered feature, think in layers: raw output (the prediction), explainability (why the model flagged it), confidence (how certain the model is), and feedback loop (how users correct errors). AiDASH interviewers respond well when candidates articulate all four layers rather than treating the model as a black box.

OKR framing for roadmap discussions. Tie your roadmap to Objectives (the strategic outcome) and Key Results (measurable milestones). For regulated-industry clients, include at least one OKR tier addressing compliance or auditability. This signals that you understand the operating constraints of AiDASH's market.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity, not domain expertise. AiDASH does not expect PM candidates to arrive knowing geospatial data or power grid operations. They do expect you to have done basic homework: understand what vegetation management means for a utility, why wildfire risk is a commercial problem, and how satellite revisit rates affect data freshness. Candidates who treat the domain as interesting rather than intimidating tend to stand out.

Comfort with ambiguity in AI products. Interviewers want to see that you understand the difference between a deterministic software bug and a probabilistic model error. If a customer says 'your product is wrong,' a strong PM asks: wrong how often, wrong in which context, and what does the customer do when it is wrong? Candidates who treat model accuracy as a binary pass/fail tend to underperform here.

Enterprise empathy. AiDASH's customers are large organisations with procurement, compliance, and change management challenges. Interviewers look for candidates who have shipped products for enterprise buyers before, or who can speak credibly about long sales cycles, multi-stakeholder sign-off, and the cost of downtime in critical infrastructure.

Structured thinking under pressure. In case rounds, clarity of structure matters more than arriving at the 'right' answer. State your assumptions, explain your prioritisation logic, and flag trade-offs explicitly. Interviewers typically report that candidates who think aloud and invite pushback perform better than those who present polished answers with no visible reasoning.

Ownership signals. AiDASH is a growth-stage company. They want PMs who take responsibility for outcomes, not just outputs. Use language like 'I decided' and 'I pushed back on' rather than 'the team decided' or 'we were told to.'

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the company and the domain.
Read AiDASH's publicly available case studies and press releases. Understand their core products: vegetation intelligence, wildfire risk, and ESG reporting for infrastructure. Learn the basics of how satellite imagery is used in asset management (publicly available explainers exist and no specialised knowledge is needed). Write a two-paragraph answer to: 'Why AiDASH, why now?'

Week 2: Sharpen your PM fundamentals.
Prepare at least two strong STAR stories for each of these themes: prioritisation under constraints, working with ML or data teams, managing a difficult enterprise client, and a product that failed and what you learned. Practise the RICE and Jobs-to-be-Done frameworks until you can apply them in under two minutes.

Week 3: Practise case rounds.
Work through at least two enterprise B2B product design cases. One should involve an AI or data-heavy product. Practise thinking aloud and structuring your answer before diving into solutions. Ask a peer or mentor to play the role of a sceptical interviewer and push back on your assumptions.

Week 4: Prepare your questions.
AiDASH interviewers typically leave time for candidate questions. Prepare thoughtful questions about their roadmap philosophy, how they handle model quality issues with customers, and how the PM team is structured. Good questions signal genuine interest and domain engagement.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating AiDASH like a consumer tech company. Candidates sometimes walk in with playbooks built for B2C apps and focus on metrics like daily active users or funnel conversion. AiDASH PMs care about enterprise renewal, expansion revenue, field adoption, and compliance. Reframe your answers around these before you walk into the room.

Overconfidence about AI quality. Saying 'we just improve the model' when a customer reports inaccuracies misses the PM's role entirely. Interviewers flag candidates who defer all AI quality questions to the data science team. You do not need to retrain the model. You do need to own the customer experience around its outputs.

Vague answers in STAR stories. Saying 'the team achieved great results' without a specific outcome reads as unsubstantiated. If you cannot share real numbers due to confidentiality, say so and describe the qualitative signal instead. 'The client moved from at-risk to stable' is more credible than 'it went well.'

Not asking about the product during the interview. Candidates who ask no product-focused questions in the closing round leave a weak impression at growth-stage companies. At minimum, ask one question that shows you have thought about their roadmap or their customer challenges.

Ignoring the regulatory context. Missing that AiDASH operates in regulated industries means your feature proposals may skip compliance considerations entirely. Before proposing any feature in a case round, ask or state explicitly: 'Are there regulatory or audit requirements I should account for here?'

Frequently asked

How hard is it to get a PM role at AiDASH without a climate tech background?

Candidates without climate tech experience do get hired, but you need to show genuine curiosity about the domain. Do basic homework on what vegetation management means for a utility and why wildfire risk is a commercial problem. AiDASH typically values enterprise B2B PM experience and AI product sense more than sector-specific knowledge, especially at the mid-level. Demonstrating that you find the problem space genuinely interesting goes a long way.

Does AiDASH hire PMs in India, or are these roles only for their US office?

AiDASH has hired PMs in India, particularly for roles tied to their engineering and product teams based there. knok's jobradar showed 2 open PM roles at AiDASH as of mid-2026. Check each listing carefully for location and remote or hybrid policy, as this can vary by team and seniority level.

What salary should I expect for a PM role at AiDASH in India?

knok's jobradar data puts PM salaries at 24-40 LPA for those with 3-6 years of experience in India. Senior PM roles typically fall in the 40-60 LPA range. These are indicative figures. Is there a take-home assignment in the AiDASH PM interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home case as part of the process, typically covering a product roadmap scenario or a feature design problem. This is not confirmed as a universal step and processes can change between roles and hiring managers. Preparing a sample one-page product brief or roadmap before your interview is good practice regardless of whether it is required.

How much technical depth does AiDASH expect from PM candidates?

You are not expected to write code or build models, but you should be comfortable discussing APIs, data pipelines, and model accuracy in plain business terms. Interviewers typically probe whether you can translate technical constraints into customer-facing decisions. Reading up on how satellite data is processed and served to end users will help you sound credible in any technical discussion.

What is the best way to stand out in an AiDASH PM interview?

Show that you understand their customer's world, not just their product. Utilities and infrastructure operators have slow procurement cycles, field crews who distrust new tools, and strict uptime requirements. Candidates who bring this operational empathy into case answers, and who ask sharp questions about how AiDASH measures customer success, typically leave a stronger impression than those who focus only on feature ideas.
