01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

FIS Global is a financial technology company that builds core banking software, payment platforms, and capital markets solutions used by financial institutions worldwide. In India, FIS runs large engineering and operations centers across cities like Pune, Bangalore, Chennai, and Hyderabad, making it one of the more established fintech employers in the country.

As of July 2026, 55 open roles at FIS Global are being actively tracked across major job platforms. These span software engineering, quality assurance, business analysis, product management, and operations support. FIS hires across experience levels, though most openings lean toward mid-level technical and functional roles.

For Indian job seekers, FIS offers the appeal of working on systems that power real banking infrastructure globally. Roles here tend to value domain knowledge alongside technical skills, so professionals from banking or financial services backgrounds often find a natural fit alongside software engineers and data professionals.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

FIS Global's hiring process usually runs through four to six stages. The exact number varies by role, team, and location, so treat this as a general guide rather than a fixed sequence.

Application and resume screening: You apply through the FIS careers portal or a job aggregator. Recruiters and automated systems filter resumes for keywords like Java or .NET, SQL, and banking domain terms. Tailor your resume to mirror the language in the job description as closely as possible.

HR screening call: A recruiter verifies your basics: current role, notice period, expected CTC, and location preference. Be honest about your notice period because FIS often has specific joining timelines tied to project schedules.

Technical assessment: Many engineering and analyst roles include an online test covering coding problems, SQL queries, aptitude questions, or domain-specific scenarios. Platforms like HackerRank or Codility are commonly used. Prepare for timed conditions and practice under similar pressure.

Technical interview (one or two rounds): This is where most candidates are evaluated most closely. For engineers, expect questions on Java, microservices, REST APIs, databases, and system design. For business analysts and operations roles, expect scenario-based questions on banking workflows, payments, or compliance. Walk through past projects in concrete detail.

Managerial or panel interview: A hiring manager or cross-functional panel assesses communication, problem-solving, and how you handle working in large global teams. Situational questions about deadlines, stakeholder management, and ambiguous requirements are common at this stage.

HR final round and offer: Once you clear the earlier rounds, HR discusses the offer package, variable pay, benefits, and joining date. If you have competing offers or publicly reported data from Glassdoor or levels.fyi showing market rates for your level, this is the right moment to negotiate.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

FIS Global hires people who combine strong technical or functional skills with an understanding of how financial systems work. A purely technical candidate with no sense of banking or payments often struggles in interviews; a domain expert with weak execution skills may not make the cut for delivery roles either.

Technical skills that matter most:

  • Java or .NET for software engineering roles
  • SQL and database management across Oracle, SQL Server, or similar platforms
  • Microservices, REST APIs, and cloud experience (AWS or Azure appear commonly in FIS job descriptions)
  • Automation testing tools for QA roles
  • ETL and data pipeline experience for data engineering positions

Domain knowledge FIS values:

  • Core banking systems and how banks manage accounts, loans, and transactions
  • Payments networks: SWIFT, NEFT, RTGS, and card schemes
  • Capital markets concepts: trade lifecycle, settlement, and risk management
  • Regulatory and compliance frameworks relevant to financial institutions

Soft skills and working style: FIS operates in large, globally distributed teams. Interviewers look for candidates who communicate clearly across time zones, document their work properly, and can manage long-running enterprise projects with multiple stakeholders. If you have Agile or waterfall delivery experience on banking or fintech projects, mention it explicitly during your interview.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Align your resume to the JD: FIS job descriptions are detailed. Pull out the exact technologies and domain areas mentioned and make sure your resume reflects them if you have the experience. Applicant tracking systems at companies the size of FIS filter heavily on keyword match before a human ever reads your profile.

Brush up on banking domain basics: Even for engineering roles, FIS interviewers often ask about the domain. Know how core banking systems work, what a payment gateway does, or how settlement works in capital markets. Publicly available fintech primers and banking certification prep materials are good starting points and available at no cost.

Practice SQL and Java: These come up in almost every technical role. Practice writing complex joins, subqueries, and stored procedures for SQL. For Java, review collections, multithreading, and design patterns. If the JD mentions microservices, review Spring Boot patterns as well.

Prepare STAR-format answers for behavioral rounds: For each behavioral or managerial question, structure your answer around Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Focus on examples where you worked in large teams, handled stakeholder pressure, or delivered under tight timelines. Vague answers do not land well at FIS.

Research FIS products: FIS has flagship solutions in core banking, payments (including Worldpay), and capital markets. Knowing which product family the team works on helps you ask sharper questions during the interview and signals genuine interest rather than a spray-and-pray application.

Negotiate with market data: Before the offer stage, look up publicly reported salary figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for your role and experience level. A specific, sourced number is far more persuasive in a negotiation than a vague ask for more.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

FIS Global describes its culture around innovation in financial technology, global collaboration, and customer focus. In practice, working at FIS in India reflects the realities of a large, publicly listed enterprise software company.

Global and matrix structure: FIS teams in India frequently work with counterparts in the US, UK, or Europe. Calls across time zones are routine, especially for senior roles. If you prefer a tight local team with minimal cross-border coordination, factor this in when evaluating a specific role before you accept.

Stability over speed: FIS serves large regulated financial institutions, which means change is deliberate and processes are well-documented. Professionals who value stability, clear scope, and long-term projects tend to thrive here. Those who prefer fast-moving, experimental environments may find the pace slower than they expect.

Learning and certification: FIS has internal learning programs and encourages employees to pursue certifications in cloud platforms and financial domain areas. Employee reviews on Glassdoor commonly cite this as a genuine benefit rather than a checkbox item, particularly for cloud and fintech certifications.

Inclusion and diversity: FIS has publicly stated commitments to gender inclusion, and India offices run programs aligned to these goals. Women in tech programs and internal networking groups are mentioned regularly in publicly reported employee feedback across review platforms.

Work-life balance: Publicly reported reviews on Glassdoor show mixed feedback on work-life balance, and the experience varies significantly by team and project phase. Delivery phases for large banking clients can involve demanding periods, while steady-state support and maintenance roles often run more predictable hours.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many jobs is FIS Global currently hiring for in India?

As of July 2026, 55 open roles at FIS Global are being actively tracked across major job platforms. These cover engineering, QA, business analysis, product management, and operations. The count changes regularly as roles open and close, so checking job sites frequently or setting up alerts gives you the most current picture.

What is the typical salary at FIS Global for software engineers in India?

FIS does not publish salary bands publicly. For the most accurate picture, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi, where FIS India employees commonly cite figures for different roles and experience levels. Always cross-check a few sources before your offer stage so you have a specific, publicly reported number ready when you negotiate rather than guessing.

Does FIS Global hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

FIS does hire freshers, mainly into testing, support, and graduate trainee programs. However, most open roles at any given time call for candidates with two or more years of relevant experience. If you are a fresher, look specifically for campus recruitment cycles or trainee program listings on the FIS careers portal rather than applying to general open roles.

How long does the FIS Global hiring process take from application to offer?

The process typically takes two to six weeks, though senior or niche roles can take longer. Approvals in large enterprises involve multiple stakeholders, which sometimes slows the final offer stage considerably. Following up politely with your recruiter every week or ten days after each completed round is reasonable and generally expected.

Is banking domain knowledge mandatory to get hired at FIS Global?

It depends heavily on the role. For business analyst, product, and operations positions, banking domain knowledge is almost always required or strongly preferred. For software engineering roles, strong technical skills can compensate for limited domain knowledge at junior levels, but you will be expected to pick it up quickly on the job. Showing basic familiarity with core banking or payments concepts during your interview always helps, regardless of the role.

What should I do if I do not hear back after applying to FIS?

Large companies receive high volumes of applications and automated silences are common even for well-qualified candidates. Try finding the recruiter or hiring manager on LinkedIn and send a short, polite note referencing the specific role and your application date. Reapplying to a related but different opening is also worth doing if your profile fits. Persistence without being pushy is the right balance.
