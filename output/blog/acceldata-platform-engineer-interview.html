01 Overview

Overview

Acceldata builds a data observability platform that helps enterprises monitor and manage their data pipelines at scale. A Platform Engineer at Acceldata typically owns the infrastructure layer that keeps this product running reliably, covering Kubernetes clusters, cloud deployments, CI/CD pipelines, and internal developer tooling.

With 45 open roles at Acceldata as of mid-2026 and 204 Platform Engineer openings across India in the same period, this is a role with clear market demand. Bangalore leads with 29 openings across companies, Delhi follows with 12, and Pune adds 10 more. If you are targeting Acceldata specifically, expect the conversation to lean heavily on cloud-native infrastructure, observability tooling, and how you think about reliability for data workloads.

Candidates report a process that typically includes an initial screening call, one or two technical rounds covering system design and hands-on problem-solving, and a closing discussion with a senior engineer or hiring manager. The full process typically spans two to three weeks end to end.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect the themes that candidates and interviewers most commonly associate with Platform Engineer interviews at Acceldata. The exact set varies by interviewer, but preparing these topics will give you strong coverage.

  1. Walk us through how you have designed or scaled a Kubernetes cluster for data-intensive workloads. What trade-offs did you make?
  2. Acceldata surfaces data pipeline issues in real time. How would you design platform infrastructure to support low-latency observability at scale?
  3. Describe your experience with infrastructure-as-code. Which tools have you used, and how do you manage state across environments?
  4. How do you handle secrets management and access control in a cloud-native setup?
  5. Tell us about a production incident you owned from detection to resolution. What did you change afterward?
  6. How have you set up or improved a CI/CD pipeline for a team that ships frequently?
  7. What is your approach to multi-tenant infrastructure? How do you isolate workloads without driving up costs?
  8. How do you approach cloud cost optimisation? Give a concrete example from your past work.
  9. Describe how you have collaborated with a data engineering or data science team. How did you balance their delivery speed with platform stability?
  10. How do you instrument a distributed system so on-call engineers can pinpoint the root cause of a failure quickly?
  11. Acceldata integrates with systems like Spark, Databricks, and Kafka. How familiar are you with the infrastructure requirements of these platforms?
  12. If a data pipeline job starts failing intermittently in production and no obvious error appears in logs, how do you investigate?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a production incident you owned from detection to resolution.

*Situation:* At my previous company, a critical nightly batch job that loaded data into our analytics warehouse started failing silently. No alerts fired, but dashboards showed stale data the next morning.

*Task:* I was on-call and responsible for identifying the root cause, restoring the pipeline, and preventing silent failures from recurring.

*Action:* I pulled logs from the job orchestrator and found the Kubernetes pod was being evicted due to memory pressure on the node. I moved the workload to a node with more headroom as an immediate fix, then worked with the data engineering team to add resource requests and limits to the job manifest. I also added a Prometheus alert on job completion status so any future failure would notify the on-call engineer within minutes.

*Result:* The pipeline ran cleanly that same night. The new alert caught two smaller failures the following quarter before they impacted any business dashboards.

---

Q: How have you set up or improved a CI/CD pipeline for a team that ships frequently?

*Situation:* My team was manually deploying Terraform changes to three environments. Deployments took a long time, errors crept in, and engineers were reluctant to push changes toward the end of the week.

*Task:* I was asked to automate the deployment process so the team could ship confidently on any day.

*Action:* I built a GitHub Actions pipeline that ran 'terraform plan' on every pull request and posted the plan output as a comment for review. After a manual approval gate, it ran 'terraform apply' automatically on merge to main. I also added a nightly drift-detection job that opened a ticket if live infrastructure diverged from the state file.

*Result:* Manual errors disappeared and the team moved from shipping once a week to shipping multiple times a day. Engineers no longer avoided end-of-week deployments.

---

Q: How do you instrument a distributed system so on-call engineers can find the root cause of a failure quickly?

*Situation:* After joining a new team, I noticed that engineers spent more time hunting for logs than fixing problems during incidents.

*Task:* I had two sprints to improve our observability setup before the next major feature launch.

*Action:* I standardised structured logging across services using a shared library, added trace IDs that propagated across service calls, and built a Grafana dashboard that correlated error rates, latency, and pod resource usage in one view. I also wrote a runbook that walked engineers through the dashboard step by step during an incident.

*Result:* Post-incident reviews showed a clear reduction in mean time to identify root cause. New engineers reported they could follow the runbook and find the problem without asking for help.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural and experience questions, use the STAR method: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, not what the team did), Result (a concrete outcome with a measurable before/after where possible). Spend most of your time on Action and Result, and keep Situation short. A long setup with a thin Action section is the most common reason strong candidates get passed over.

For system design questions, structure your answer in layers. Start by clarifying requirements and scale. Cover infrastructure components (compute, storage, networking), then reliability mechanisms (retries, health checks, failover), and finally observability (metrics, alerts, dashboards). Because Acceldata's core value proposition is visibility into data systems, weaving monitoring into every layer of your design will land well with interviewers.

For debugging and troubleshooting questions, narrate your thought process out loud. Interviewers are not just checking whether you reach the right answer. They want to see how you eliminate hypotheses methodically. Start broad (is it infrastructure or application?), narrow using logs and metrics, and always treat recent changes as a prime suspect.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

End-to-end ownership: Platform Engineers at Acceldata are expected to take full responsibility for the systems they build. Interviewers look for candidates who have driven improvements from idea to production, not just executed tickets. Use phrases like 'I proposed,' 'I built,' and 'I led,' rather than defaulting to 'we did.'

Depth in cloud-native tooling: Kubernetes, Terraform, and at least one major cloud provider are central to most Platform Engineer roles. Interviewers want to see that you understand not just how to use these tools but why certain choices matter. For example: when would you use a DaemonSet over a Deployment, and how do you handle Terraform state safely in a shared team environment?

Familiarity with data infrastructure: Because Acceldata's product lives inside data pipelines, interviewers want to know you understand the world their customers operate in. Familiarity with Spark, Airflow, Databricks, or Kafka signals that you will be effective working alongside data teams and with the product itself.

Observability as a default mindset: Candidates who treat monitoring and alerting as an afterthought consistently underperform in these interviews. Strong candidates talk about instrumentation proactively and give concrete examples of reducing on-call pain.

Cross-team collaboration: Platform teams serve data engineers, product engineers, and security teams at the same time. Interviewers listen for evidence that you can manage competing requests, push back thoughtfully, and communicate trade-offs without creating friction.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the company and the role

Read Acceldata's product documentation and any public engineering blog posts to understand what data observability means in practice and which infrastructure patterns support it. Note the integrations they advertise (Spark, Kafka, Databricks, cloud warehouses) because these come up in interviews. Map your past experience to each requirement in the job description before your screening call.

Week 2: Sharpen your technical depth

Practise explaining Kubernetes concepts out loud: pod scheduling, node affinity, resource requests and limits, persistent volumes, and RBAC. Revisit your Terraform work and be ready to explain module design and state management decisions. If you have gaps in observability tooling, set up a local Prometheus and Grafana instance and explore it hands-on rather than just reading about it.

Week 3: Prepare your stories and practise out loud

Write out three to five STAR stories covering: a production incident you resolved, an infrastructure improvement you drove, a time you collaborated closely with a non-platform team, and a situation where you pushed back on a request. Practise each story out loud and keep it under three minutes. Do at least one mock system design session on a topic like 'design the infrastructure for a real-time data monitoring product at scale.'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too generic: Answers like 'I managed Kubernetes clusters and handled deployments' tell the interviewer nothing useful. Replace vague statements with the specific problem you solved and the decision you made.

Ignoring the data context: This is not a generic DevOps or SRE role at a typical SaaS company. Acceldata's customers run data pipelines, and their platform team works close to that world. Candidates who cannot speak to Spark, Airflow, or data warehouse infrastructure often fail to connect their experience to what the company actually builds.

Results without any evidence: Saying 'performance improved' is weak. Even rough estimates like 'deployment time dropped from about an hour to under ten minutes' are far more convincing. If you cannot recall exact figures, say so honestly: 'I do not remember the precise number, but based on our incident logs, time to resolve dropped noticeably.'

Using 'we' throughout your STAR answers: Interviewers need to assess your individual contribution. Over-relying on 'we' makes it difficult for them to judge what you specifically brought to the outcome.

Asking no questions at the end: Candidates who have nothing to ask signal low interest. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions about the team's current infrastructure challenges, on-call culture, or how platform work connects to product development at Acceldata.

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Acceldata typically have for Platform Engineer roles?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter or HR screening call, one or two technical rounds covering platform problem-solving and system design, and a final discussion with a senior engineer or hiring manager. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and level. It is always fine to ask the recruiter at the start how many rounds to expect and what each one covers.

Is there a coding or DSA round for Platform Engineer interviews?

Candidates for Platform Engineer roles at infrastructure-focused companies typically do not face competitive programming or algorithm-heavy DSA rounds. Technical rounds more commonly focus on system design, infrastructure problem-solving, and scenario-based questions around tools like Kubernetes and Terraform. Some interviews include a light scripting or debugging exercise, so be comfortable reading and writing Python or Bash confidently.

What salary can I expect as a Platform Engineer at Acceldata?

Acceldata does not publish salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and levels.fyi listings for Platform Engineer roles at data observability and data infrastructure companies in India commonly cite a range that varies by years of experience and city. Bangalore roles typically attract stronger packages given the local market. Check current Glassdoor and levels.fyi entries for the most recent figures before entering any salary discussion.

How long does the full process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report the full process spanning two to four weeks from the screening call to a final decision, though timelines can vary by team and hiring urgency. If you have not heard back within a week of completing a round, a polite follow-up email to the recruiter is entirely appropriate. Having other active offers can sometimes accelerate the timeline.

What is the best way to prepare for the system design round?

Focus on designs that involve distributed systems, observability, and data infrastructure, since these align directly with what Acceldata builds. Practise thinking out loud as you work through a design: start with requirements, then cover components, reliability, and monitoring. Reading Acceldata's public product pages and engineering blog will give you useful vocabulary and signal that you have done your homework before the interview.
