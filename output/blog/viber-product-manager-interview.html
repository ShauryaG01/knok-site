01 Overview

Overview

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

  1. Viber's daily active users have been flat for two consecutive quarters. How do you diagnose and fix this?
  2. How would you improve Viber's community feature to compete more effectively with Telegram channels and WhatsApp groups?
  3. Walk me through how you would prioritize the next three features for Viber for Business.
  4. Design a monetization model for Viber that generates revenue without degrading user experience.

  11. Describe a product you shipped from zero to one. What was the hardest call you made along the way?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioral and experience questions. Candidates report Viber interviewers push hardest on the Result step, so prepare concrete outcomes wherever possible.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder to protect the product roadmap.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the sales team was pushing hard to add a bulk-broadcast feature to our messaging product before a major enterprise deal closed. The feature was not on the roadmap and had unreviewed privacy implications.

*Task:* I needed to balance short-term commercial pressure against long-term product integrity and user trust.

*Action:* I organized a working session with the sales lead, legal, and engineering. I acknowledged the deal's importance, then walked through three risks: user trust erosion, regulatory exposure under data privacy rules, and technical debt from rushing. I proposed a limited pilot using existing features, buying us one quarter to build the right version properly.

*Result:* The enterprise deal closed using the pilot approach. We shipped the full broadcast feature six weeks later with proper privacy controls, and it became one of our top B2B features by usage.

---

Q: Viber's daily active users have been flat for two quarters. How do you diagnose this?

*Situation:* This is a product analytics case. Flat engagement in a messaging app typically means acquisition has stalled, churn has risen, or both, and the aggregate number is masking the real story.

*Task:* My job is to isolate the root cause before prescribing any fix.

*Action:* I would segment the data by new vs. returning users, geography, device type, and feature usage. I would check whether the flatness is a mix shift where one growing segment offsets decline elsewhere. Then I would map the activation and retention funnel to find where users are dropping off. I would run user interviews in two or three key markets and review app store reviews for recent sentiment changes.

*Result:* In a similar situation at a previous role, this segmentation revealed that desktop retention was falling while mobile was growing. We identified and fixed a specific notification bug on desktop, and retention recovered within two months.

---

Q: Describe a product you shipped from zero to one.

*Situation:* At a fintech startup, I was asked to build a peer-to-peer payment feature inside our existing chat app, with a team of four engineers and one designer, in three months.

*Task:* I had to define the MVP scope, navigate payment compliance requirements, and ship something users would trust with their money.

*Action:* I ran a two-week discovery sprint with user interviews, mapped the core jobs-to-be-done, and cut scope to three flows: send money, request money, and view history. I worked with our legal team on the compliance checklist and held weekly demos with the founders to keep alignment tight.

*Result:* We launched on schedule. Publicly reported user feedback was positive, and our internal analytics showed a meaningful lift in retention in the three months after launch.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product improvement and design questions: Start with the user (who are they, what is their core job-to-be-done), define the specific problem, brainstorm a range of solutions, prioritize by impact vs. effort, and close with how you would measure success. Do not skip the user definition step.

For metric and analytics questions: Work in layers. Define the north star metric first. Identify the input metrics that drive it. When something drops, segment by user type, platform, geography, and time to find the anomaly. Always ask whether this is a data issue, a product issue, or an external event.

For strategy and competitive questions: Use a three-part structure: where is the market going, what is Viber's unique position, and what is the single bet you would make? Viber's Rakuten connection is a real differentiator. Do not ignore it when you frame strategic options.

For stakeholder and cross-functional questions: Show that you understand each team's incentives. Sales wants to close deals. Engineering wants clean scope. Design wants user trust. A strong PM translates between these and finds the path that works for everyone.

For 'first 90 days' questions: Structure your answer in three phases: listen and learn (weeks 1-4), identify the highest-leverage problem (weeks 5-8), and begin shipping (weeks 9-12). This signals that you will not make decisions before you understand the context.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Viber competes in a mature messaging market where most users already have a preferred app. Interviewers are looking for PMs who understand that growth here requires precision, not just feature additions.

Deep product empathy. Can you articulate why a user in Eastern Europe or South Asia would choose Viber over a well-funded competitor? Interviewers want to see that you have used the product and thought critically about it before walking into the room.

Comfort with ambiguity. Viber sits at the intersection of consumer social, B2B messaging, and Rakuten's commerce ecosystem. There is no single playbook. Candidates who ask sharp clarifying questions and structure messy problems clearly stand out.

Ownership and accountability. Candidates report that interviewers push hard on the Result part of STAR answers. They want to know: did it actually work, and how do you know?

Cross-functional fluency. Messaging products touch trust and safety, legal, engineering, design, and business development simultaneously. Show that you have navigated multi-team complexity before.

A global mindset. Viber's user base is not India-first. Interviewers appreciate candidates who can reason about product decisions for users in markets they have not personally lived in.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product cold. Download Viber and use it for a week. Try communities, sticker packs, Viber for Business, and the Rakuten integration if available in your region. Write down three things you would change and why before you do anything else.

Week 2: Study the competitive landscape. Map how Viber is positioned against WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, and Line. Focus on where Viber wins today and where it is genuinely weakest. This framing will anchor your answers to strategy questions.

Week 3: Practice your frameworks out loud. Do not just read frameworks. Apply them to actual Viber-specific questions and record yourself. The goal is to sound natural and structured at the same time, not to recite a template.

Week 4: Build your behavioral story bank. Write out five to seven stories from your career that each demonstrate ownership, cross-functional leadership, data-driven decisions, or handling failure. Map each story to multiple question types so you are never caught without a relevant example.

Ongoing: Stay current. Read Viber's official blog and Rakuten's investor communications. Being able to reference a recent Viber product launch or partnership during your interview is a strong signal of genuine interest.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Viber like WhatsApp. Many candidates default to WhatsApp analogies because both are messaging apps. Viber has a different user base, a different monetization model, and a parent company with distinct strategic priorities. Generic answers signal a lack of preparation.

Skipping the motivation question. Candidates report that 'why Viber?' carries real weight in interviews. A vague answer about 'exciting product challenges' will not land. Be specific: mention a feature, a market, or a Rakuten integration angle that genuinely interests you.

Jumping to solutions before diagnosing. In analytical and product design questions, interviewers are watching how you think, not just what you conclude. Rushing to the answer skips the part they actually care about.

Weak results in STAR answers. Saying 'the team was happy' or 'the launch went well' is not a result. Quantify where you can, and if you cannot share numbers, describe the business impact in concrete terms.

Ignoring the Rakuten angle. Viber is not a standalone startup. Its parent company's loyalty, e-commerce, and financial services ecosystem is a real strategic lever. Not referencing this in a strategy question is a missed opportunity.

Sounding scripted. Candidates who have memorized answers lose the interviewer's trust quickly. Prepare your stories and structures, but stay flexible enough to adapt when the conversation moves in a new direction.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Viber PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically runs three to five rounds, though this varies by level and team. You should expect at least one product sense round, one behavioral or leadership round, and one session focused on your past work. Some candidates report a final conversation with a senior leader before an offer is made.

Does Viber ask technical questions in PM interviews?

Typically, Viber PM interviews do not require you to write code. However, candidates report questions about how messaging systems work at a high level, such as message delivery guarantees, latency trade-offs, or API design for third-party integrations. A working understanding of how consumer communication apps are built is helpful, but a deep engineering background is not required.

What products should I study before a Viber PM interview?

Use Viber itself, covering communities, Viber for Business, and the sticker marketplace. Also review Rakuten's broader product ecosystem to understand the parent company's strategic direction. Spending time with competitor products like Telegram and Line will help you articulate where Viber's differentiation is real and where the gaps are.

How should I approach salary negotiation at Viber?

Research current market ranges on Glassdoor and levels.fyi before your first compensation conversation. Salary bands for PM roles in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level up to 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PMs, based on industry-wide data. Come in with a specific number anchored to your experience and current market data rather than a wide range.

Is Viber a good company for PMs looking to grow their career?

Viber offers exposure to a global product at scale, with the added complexity of operating inside Rakuten's ecosystem. Candidates who have joined typically cite the international user base and the mix of consumer and B2B product work as strong learning environments. Growth opportunities depend on the specific team and the level of investment Rakuten is directing toward that product area when you join.

How long does the Viber hiring process take from first contact to offer?

Candidates report the full process typically takes three to six weeks, though timelines can vary significantly by team and location. Following up politely after each round is standard and generally appreciated. If you have a competing offer, communicate this to your recruiter early so they can expedite the process where possible.
