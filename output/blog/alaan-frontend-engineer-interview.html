01 Overview

Overview

Alaan is a corporate spend management platform serving businesses in the Middle East, with product and engineering teams based in India. As of mid-2026, Alaan has 22 open roles, with frontend engineering being an active hiring area. Candidates report a process that typically spans three to four rounds: a recruiter screening call, a technical round (take-home task or live coding), one or two deeper technical rounds, and a final round with a senior engineer or product stakeholder.

The technical bar focuses on React, JavaScript depth, and building UI for complex financial workflows. Alaan's product handles expense cards, approvals, and real-time spend dashboards, so interviewers pay close attention to how you think about state management, performance, and data accuracy in a fintech context.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates interviewing at Alaan for frontend roles report questions across three areas: React and JavaScript depth, component and system design, and product thinking for fintech scenarios.

  1. Walk me through how React reconciliation works and where it can cause performance problems in a large UI.
  2. How would you design a real-time expense dashboard that handles frequent data updates without unnecessary re-renders?
  3. Explain the difference between controlled and uncontrolled components. When would you choose one over the other in a form-heavy product?
  4. How do you manage global state in a large React app? What trade-offs have you seen between Redux, Zustand, and React Context?
  5. Describe a time you improved the performance of a frontend feature. What did you measure, what did you change, and what was the outcome?
  6. How would you build a multi-step approval flow (such as an expense approval) that is both accessible and easy to test?
  7. What is your approach to handling API errors and loading states without degrading the user experience?
  8. How do you keep a component library consistent when multiple engineers are contributing to it?
  9. Alaan displays financial data that must be accurate. How do you think about number formatting, rounding, and edge cases like null or zero-value transactions?
  10. Describe a difficult debugging session in a React app. What tools did you use and how did you isolate the problem?
  11. How do you approach writing tests for frontend code? What do you prioritise and what do you skip?
  12. If a product manager asks you to cut accessibility to ship faster, how do you respond?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you improved the performance of a frontend feature.

*Situation:* At my previous company, our transaction history table was slow to render when a user applied a date filter, causing visible lag on the page.

*Task:* I was asked to reduce render time without a full rewrite, since the table component was shared across three product areas.

*Action:* I profiled the component using React DevTools and found the entire list re-rendered on every keystroke because the filter input was triggering a broad parent state update. I moved the filter state down, added useMemo on the filtered dataset, and replaced the custom renderer with a virtualised list for large datasets.

*Result:* Render time dropped noticeably on a commonly cited benchmark of 500 rows. The fix was merged without changing the shared component API, so other teams were unaffected.

---

Q: How would you design a real-time expense dashboard?

*Situation:* In a side project, I built a spend-tracking dashboard for a freelance client who wanted live updates as transactions came in.

*Task:* I needed to keep the UI current without polling every second and adding unnecessary backend load.

*Action:* I used WebSockets to push updates only when new transactions arrived. On the frontend, I kept a normalised store keyed by transaction ID so incoming updates patched individual rows rather than replacing the entire list. I also added a 'last updated' indicator so users always knew the data was live.

*Result:* The client found the dashboard fast and responsive, and backend load stayed low. The pattern applies directly to a product like Alaan's spend management view, where real-time accuracy matters to finance admins.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you pushed back on cutting accessibility.

*Situation:* A PM at my last company wanted to skip keyboard navigation on a modal dialog to ship a feature by end of sprint.

*Task:* I needed to communicate the risk clearly without blocking the release.

*Action:* I showed the PM that the modal was part of our expense approval flow, used by finance admins who relied on keyboard navigation because of company IT restrictions. I estimated the fix was a small, contained piece of work. I also flagged that WCAG compliance was part of our enterprise contract terms.

*Result:* The PM agreed to keep the fix in scope. We shipped on time, and the accessibility work prevented a compliance flag during an enterprise customer audit two months later.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Every story needs a Situation (context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did), and Result (a concrete outcome). Keep Situation and Task short so you spend most time on Action and Result. Use 'I' for actions you personally took.

The 'why before how' approach for design questions. State your constraints and goals before jumping to implementation. Say something like: 'I would first clarify whether this needs real-time updates and how many concurrent users, because that changes whether I use polling or WebSockets.' Interviewers at product-focused companies like Alaan reward engineers who ask the right questions before writing code.

The breadth-then-depth pattern for technical questions. Give a one-sentence answer first, then go deeper. If asked about reconciliation, start with 'React compares the new and old virtual DOM trees and only updates what changed,' then expand with specifics. This shows clarity of thinking before diving into detail.

The trade-off frame for library and architecture questions. Avoid saying one tool is simply better. Instead say: 'Redux gives you a predictable single source of truth and strong devtools, but adds boilerplate. Zustand is lighter and easier to set up for medium-complexity apps. Context is fine for low-frequency updates like theme or auth, but causes performance issues for high-frequency state.' This signals senior-level thinking and shows real experience with each option.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Product intuition alongside technical skill. Alaan builds for finance and operations teams, not developers. Interviewers want to see that you think about the end user, not just the implementation. Mention loading states, error feedback, and data accuracy without being prompted.

Ownership. Candidates report that Alaan interviewers ask follow-up questions to find out whether you drove a solution or just participated. Be specific about what you personally built, decided, or changed rather than speaking only about what the team did.

Clean, readable code over clever code. Fintech codebases are maintained by teams under pressure. Interviewers typically value components that a new engineer can understand quickly over highly abstracted solutions that are hard to trace.

Comfort with financial data. Numbers on a spend dashboard must be correct. Interviewers pay attention to whether you mention formatting, rounding, currency handling, and edge cases like null or zero-value transactions without being asked.

Clear communication. Because Alaan's product and engineering teams work closely together, interviewers look for candidates who explain their reasoning, ask clarifying questions, and flag trade-offs rather than just delivering a solution in silence.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: JavaScript and React fundamentals.
Revisit closures, the event loop, prototypal inheritance, and how the browser renders a page. For React, be ready to explain reconciliation, hooks rules, and the difference between useEffect and useLayoutEffect. Practice explaining these out loud, not just writing them.

Week 2: State management and performance.
Pick one state management library you know well and prepare a clear explanation of why you would choose it and where it falls short. Practice profiling a React app with React DevTools. Understand virtualisation (react-window or react-virtual) and when it is necessary.

Week 3: Component and system design.
Design two or three components from scratch: a multi-step form, a data table with filtering, and a notification system. Focus on the component API, state shape, and how you would test each one. Think about how these map onto a spend management product.

Week 4: Behavioural prep and product research.
Prepare STAR stories for: a performance improvement, a hard technical trade-off, a disagreement with a stakeholder, and a bug you found difficult to debug. Research Alaan's product by reading their public blog and LinkedIn updates. Understand what expense cards, approval workflows, and spend controls mean from a user perspective.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to code before clarifying requirements. Interviewers at product companies like Alaan often leave design questions intentionally vague. Candidates who ask about scale, edge cases, and success criteria before writing code score higher than those who open a code editor immediately.

Memorising definitions instead of understanding concepts. If you can recite the reconciliation algorithm but cannot explain why a specific component re-renders unexpectedly in a real app, interviewers will notice. Practice applying concepts to scenarios, not just defining them.

Ignoring the fintech context. Generic React answers are a missed opportunity. If you are discussing a table component, mention currency formatting. If you are discussing state, mention what happens when an approval action fails mid-flow. Contextual answers show genuine product interest.

Underplaying your role in STAR answers. Candidates often say 'we did X' throughout behavioural answers. Use 'I' for actions you personally took. 'We decided' is fine for team decisions, but 'I wrote the hook,' 'I proposed the approach,' and 'I presented the outcome' are what interviewers listen for.

Skipping tests in take-home assignments. Candidates report that Alaan reviewers check for tests in take-home submissions. Even basic unit tests on a utility function signal engineering discipline.

Not asking questions at the end. Alaan is an early-to-mid stage company. Asking about the deployment pipeline, how the frontend team is structured, or what the biggest current technical challenge is shows genuine interest and helps you evaluate the role honestly.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Alaan frontend interview typically have?

Candidates report three to four rounds: a recruiter screening call, a technical round with a take-home task or live coding, a deeper technical and system design round, and a final round with a senior engineer or product stakeholder. The exact structure can vary based on the seniority of the role and team availability.

Is there a take-home assignment?

Many candidates report receiving a take-home task, typically a small React project or component-building exercise scoped to a few hours. Reviewers typically look at code quality, component design, and whether you included tests, not just whether the feature works. Skipping tests is one of the most commonly cited reasons for not advancing past this stage.

What React topics come up most often in the technical rounds?

Candidates consistently mention reconciliation, hooks (especially useEffect, useMemo, and useCallback), state management trade-offs, and performance optimisation. Questions about building UI for data-heavy or real-time scenarios are also common, which fits directly with Alaan's spend dashboard product.

How should I approach salary negotiation with Alaan?

Use the salary bands in this guide as a market benchmark by experience level. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for any Alaan-specific data points before your offer discussion. Early-to-mid stage companies sometimes have flexibility on equity or role scope even when base salary is fixed, so it is worth asking about the full package.

Do I need fintech experience to interview at Alaan?

You do not need deep fintech experience, but showing familiarity with Alaan's product helps. Spend time understanding what corporate expense cards, approval workflows, and spend controls mean from a user perspective. Interviewers respond well to candidates who connect their technical answers to the product context rather than treating Alaan like a generic SaaS company.

How long does the Alaan hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report the process typically takes two to four weeks from first contact to offer, though this varies based on team availability and how many rounds are scheduled. Following up with your recruiter after each round is a straightforward way to stay informed without appearing pushy.
