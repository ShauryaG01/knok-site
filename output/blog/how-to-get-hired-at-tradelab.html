03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Here is how Tradelab's interview process typically unfolds, based on publicly available candidate accounts. The exact stages can vary by role, so treat this as a general guide.

Application and Resume Screen
Your resume is reviewed by the talent team for relevant experience. For technical roles, keywords like programmatic advertising, RTB, demand-side platforms (DSP), or data pipelines help your profile stand out. A focused resume that maps your experience directly to the role description is ideal.

Recruiter Call
If your profile is shortlisted, a recruiter or HR partner reaches out for an initial call. This covers your current role, total experience, notice period, and salary expectations. Be honest and clear about your availability and current CTC.

Technical or Domain Assessment
For engineering and data roles, expect a take-home coding challenge or an online assessment. Topics commonly include data structures, SQL, or a short analytics case. For marketing and commercial roles, you may be asked to prepare a short campaign strategy or a market analysis. This stage is where focused preparation pays off most.

Manager or Panel Interview
This round goes deeper. A hiring manager (and sometimes a peer from the team) will ask about your past projects, your approach to problem-solving, and your understanding of the ad-tech space. Expect a mix of technical questions and scenario-based questions.

Final Discussion and Offer
If all rounds go well, HR reconnects with a compensation discussion. Be ready to share your current CTC and expected CTC clearly. Tradelab typically moves quickly at this stage once a decision is made.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Tradelab looks for people who combine technical sharpness with genuine curiosity about digital advertising. Here are the traits and skills that tend to matter most.

Technical Depth
For engineering roles, solid fundamentals in software development, distributed systems, and real-time data processing are valued. For data roles, proficiency in SQL, Python, and the ability to derive actionable insights from campaign data matters a great deal.

Ad-Tech Domain Knowledge
You do not need prior ad-tech experience, but candidates who understand how programmatic advertising works (RTB, DSPs, SSPs, header bidding) stand out clearly. Even self-taught knowledge from reading industry blogs and documentation counts in your favour.

Analytical Mindset
Tradelab's core business is about making better decisions with data. Across all roles, they value people who ask 'why' before acting and who back their thinking with evidence rather than assumptions.

Communication and Collaboration
Because teams work across functions (tech, product, sales, and client success), clear communication is valued. Being able to explain a technical idea to a non-technical stakeholder is a practical skill that interviewers notice and remember.

Ownership Mindset
Smaller ad-tech teams expect people to take initiative. If you have examples of projects you drove from idea to outcome, bring those stories to the interview with specific context and concrete results.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparation for Tradelab interviews can be broken into three areas: domain knowledge, technical skills, and storytelling.

Learn the Basics of Programmatic Advertising
If you are new to ad tech, spend time understanding the programmatic supply chain: how advertisers buy inventory through DSPs, how publishers sell through SSPs, and what real-time bidding (RTB) means in practice. This context will help you answer 'why Tradelab' in a way that feels genuine rather than scripted.

Polish Your Technical Skills
For engineering candidates, practise data structures, algorithms, and system design. For data candidates, sharpen your SQL and Python skills, and practise interpreting campaign metrics like CPM, CTR, and ROAS (these are commonly cited performance benchmarks across the digital advertising industry). For business roles, prepare a short case study on how you would approach a client acquisition or retention problem.

Prepare Strong Stories
Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to structure examples from your past work. Pick stories that show ownership, impact, and cross-functional collaboration. If you can reference results with numbers drawn from your own work experience, those stories land much stronger in a panel setting.

Research Tradelab Specifically
Read their blog, case studies, and any press coverage from 2024 to 2026. Know their product positioning and who their clients are. Showing that you have done your homework goes a long way in the manager interview, and it signals genuine interest beyond just wanting any job.

Ask Smart Questions
Prepare two or three questions for your interviewers: about the team's current priorities, the biggest technical challenges they are solving, or how success is measured in the first few months. Interviewers remember candidates who are curious and engaged.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Tradelab operates with the energy of a technology-first company in a fast-moving industry. Programmatic advertising changes quickly (new formats, new privacy regulations, new platforms), and the culture reflects that pace.

Data-Driven Thinking
Decisions at Tradelab tend to be grounded in data rather than gut feel. If you enjoy measuring outcomes and iterating based on what the numbers say, you will feel right at home.

Cross-Functional Collaboration
Ad tech products require tight coordination between engineering, data science, product, and commercial teams. Tradelab teams work closely across these functions, which means your work will touch multiple stakeholders and strong cross-discipline communication matters.

Ownership and Autonomy
Publicly available reviews commonly cite a culture where individuals are trusted to own their work with relatively little hand-holding. This is a strong fit if you are self-directed, and a tougher environment if you prefer highly structured daily guidance.

Learning Culture
The ad-tech landscape is evolving quickly, especially with changes to cookie tracking, AI-driven bidding, and new attribution models. Tradelab tends to attract people who are genuinely interested in staying current with the industry rather than just executing tasks on a checklist.
