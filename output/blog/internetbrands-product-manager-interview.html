01 Overview

Overview

Internetbrands runs a large portfolio of vertical websites across healthcare, legal, automotive, and home services. The company's PM team works in complex, multi-product environments where user intent, monetisation, and cross-functional coordination all matter. As of mid-2026, knok's jobradar shows Internetbrands has 56 open Product Manager roles across experience levels, from Associate PM to Senior and Group PM.

Candidates report that the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, and a panel or loop of interviews covering product sense, analytics, and behavioural questions. Some candidates also report a product case or take-home exercise, though this varies by team. Knowing the company's business model (lead generation and advertising on vertical sites) before you walk in will set you apart from most other candidates.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Interviewers at Internetbrands typically focus on product thinking within a multi-vertical, data-driven environment. These are the questions candidates report coming up most frequently.

  1. Internetbrands runs dozens of vertical websites. How would you decide which product area to invest in next?
  2. Describe a time you used data to change a product decision you had already committed to.
  3. How would you improve one of Internetbrands' healthcare or legal portals for its end users?
  4. Walk me through how you would prioritise a backlog with competing requests from sales, engineering, and customers.
  5. Tell me about a product you launched that did not perform as expected. What did you learn?
  6. How do you define success for a content-discovery feature on a vertical website?
  7. Describe a time you had to influence a team without direct authority.
  8. How would you think about monetisation for a free directory product?
  9. A major competitor launches a feature your users are asking for. How do you respond?
  10. How do you balance short-term revenue goals with long-term user experience?
  11. Tell me about a time you managed a stakeholder who disagreed with your roadmap.
  12. How would you measure whether a redesign of a lead-generation form improved user outcomes?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural questions. Below are three worked examples.

Q: Tell me about a product you launched that did not perform as expected.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we added a salary-range filter to a job board to help candidates find more relevant listings.

*Task:* My goal was to increase click-through on job listings by surfacing better matches faster.

*Action:* I led discovery, wrote the spec, and worked with design and engineering to ship the feature. After launch, I tracked engagement daily and found that fewer users were applying the filter than projected, and those who did were not converting at a higher rate.

*Result:* A quick usability test showed users did not trust the salary figures. We added a label clarifying that figures were employer-reported, and engagement recovered. The takeaway: trust signals matter as much as the feature itself.

---

Q: How do you prioritise a backlog with competing requests?

*Situation:* I inherited a backlog with requests from multiple teams, all marked urgent, with no clear ranking.

*Task:* I needed a defensible priority order before sprint planning.

*Action:* I scored each item on user impact, estimated engineering effort, and alignment to the quarterly OKR. I shared the scoring sheet with each stakeholder and held a short review call so everyone could see the reasoning.

*Result:* The team shipped the top items on the list without mid-sprint re-prioritisation. One stakeholder who had initially disagreed later adopted the same framework for her own team.

---

Q: Describe a time you influenced a team without direct authority.

*Situation:* I needed the data engineering team to instrument a new event on our website, but I had no reporting line to their manager.

*Task:* I had to make a compelling case that this work was worth their sprint time.

*Action:* I wrote a one-page brief showing how the missing data was causing us to mis-attribute conversions to the wrong channel, and what decisions we could unlock once we had it. I shared it in their team channel and offered to join their next planning call.

*Result:* They picked it up in the following sprint. The data later informed a roadmap decision that improved lead quality across multiple portals.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product improvement and design questions, use this structure: identify the user segment, state their core job-to-be-done, list pain points ranked by severity, propose solutions, and define how you would measure success. For a company like Internetbrands, always tie back to the business model (lead generation, ad revenue, or subscriptions) when you reach the metrics step.

For prioritisation questions, a simple scoring grid works well. Score each candidate feature on user impact, business impact, and estimated effort. Interviewers want to see a repeatable, transparent method, not just gut feel.

For analytical and metrics questions, start by clarifying what the metric is meant to measure, break it into a component tree (for example, leads equal visitors multiplied by conversion rate), identify which component is the problem, then propose a diagnosis. This shows structured thinking without requiring you to invent numbers.

For strategy questions (such as 'which vertical should we invest in next?'), use a market-sizing and opportunity-scoring approach: addressable users, current product gaps, monetisation potential, and competitive intensity. Acknowledge assumptions out loud. Interviewers at Internetbrands are listening for intellectual honesty as much as for the answer itself.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Interviewers at Internetbrands typically look for a few key traits.

Business model fluency. Internetbrands makes money through lead generation, advertising, and SaaS subscriptions across vertical sites. Candidates who understand this model and frame product decisions in terms of it stand out. If you have worked on marketplace, lead-gen, or media products before, highlight that directly.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. PM roles here span many verticals, so interviewers probe whether you can apply a consistent thinking process to unfamiliar domains. Walk through your reasoning step by step, even when the answer feels obvious.

Data fluency without data dependency. You should be comfortable reading metrics and designing experiments, but interviewers also want to see that you can make a reasoned call when data is thin. Saying 'I would run an A/B test' is a starting point, not a complete answer.

Cross-functional collaboration. Internetbrands has large engineering, sales, and content teams. Behavioural questions will test whether you can work with and influence people you do not manage. Prepare specific stories for this.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: company and domain research. Spend time using Internetbrands' key products, such as Healthgrades, FindLaw, or Autobytel. Map out how each site makes money. Read recent news about the company's strategy. This background will make your answers feel grounded rather than generic.

Week two: question practice. Work through each question in the section above with a written STAR answer. Then practise saying each answer aloud, keeping yourself concise. For product case questions, practise structuring your thinking step by step without rushing to a solution, so your reasoning is visible throughout.

Week three: mock sessions. Do multiple mock interviews with someone who will give honest feedback, ideally a PM or someone who has recently gone through a similar process. Focus on cutting filler phrases and sharpening your results with specific outcomes wherever you can.

Throughout: track the role. Internetbrands currently has 56 open PM roles on knok's jobradar. Check the specific job description for the role you are applying to and look for keywords around the vertical (healthcare, legal, auto) or the experience level. Tailor your most relevant stories to match that context.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Internetbrands as a generic tech company. Candidates who pitch consumer-app ideas or quote big-tech PM playbooks without connecting to the lead-generation and vertical-media context tend to lose the room quickly. Research the business model first.

Giving vague results in STAR answers. 'The team was happy' or 'the product improved' are not results. Tie outcomes to user behaviour, business metrics, or team process changes. If you genuinely do not have a number, describe the qualitative signal and explain why it mattered.

Skipping the 'why' in prioritisation. Listing a priority order is not enough. Interviewers want to hear the criteria you used. If you cannot explain why item A beats item B, the answer falls apart.

Over-indexing on frameworks. Dropping framework names (RICE, CIRCLES, and so on) without actually applying them reads as surface-level preparation. Use the framework as a tool, not as the answer itself.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask no questions at the end of rounds are often seen as less curious or less prepared. Prepare a few questions per interviewer, ideally tied to something they mentioned during the conversation.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Internetbrands PM interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager round, and a panel or loop with several interviewers covering product sense, behavioural questions, and sometimes an analytical case. Some teams also include a take-home exercise or a live case discussion, though this varies by team and level. Always confirm the format with your recruiter so you can prepare accordingly.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Internetbrands?

Based on knok's jobradar data, Associate PM roles in the market broadly range from 12-20 LPA, mid-level PM roles (with 3-6 years of experience) range from 24-40 LPA, and Senior PM roles range from 40-60 LPA. For Group or Principal PM levels, publicly reported figures from Glassdoor and levels.fyi suggest ranges starting above 55 LPA. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific team, and negotiation.

Does Internetbrands ask product design or case questions?

Candidates report that product case questions do come up, particularly for mid-level and senior roles. These are typically open-ended improvement questions tied to one of Internetbrands' verticals, such as healthcare or legal. The best preparation is to practise a structured approach out loud: identify the user, the problem, solution options, and success metrics, so your thinking is visible to the interviewer throughout.

Should I know SQL or analytics tools for the PM interview?

You do not need to write production SQL, but candidates report that interviewers do ask how you would approach an analytical problem, such as diagnosing a drop in form submissions. Being able to describe how you would break a metric into components and what data you would look for is usually sufficient. If your target role involves a heavy data component, brush up on the basics of funnel analysis and A/B test interpretation.

How important is domain knowledge in healthcare or legal?

Prior domain experience is a plus but not always required. What matters more is showing that you can quickly understand a vertical's users and business model. Spend time using the relevant Internetbrands product before your interview, note what feels broken or could be improved, and be ready to discuss your observations. This signals curiosity and preparation, which interviewers value highly.
