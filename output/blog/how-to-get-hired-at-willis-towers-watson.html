01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Willis Towers Watson (WTW) is a global advisory, broking, and solutions company with a strong presence across India, including Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, and Gurugram. The firm works across risk management, insurance broking, benefits consulting, and people analytics. As of mid-2026, WTW has 273 open roles across its global and India operations, spanning actuarial science, technology, data analytics, HR consulting, finance, and client management.

WTW hires at multiple experience levels. Fresh graduates can enter through analyst programs and campus recruitment pipelines. Experienced professionals are sought for specialist roles in actuarial, risk consulting, and benefits administration. The hiring process is structured and multi-stage, designed to assess both technical capability and cultural alignment. Whether you apply directly through WTW's careers portal or discover a role through a job platform, expect a thorough evaluation process.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

WTW's hiring process typically runs through several stages, though the exact structure varies by role, business unit, and seniority. Here is what candidates commonly report.

Stage 1: Application and resume screening
The talent acquisition team reviews your resume against the role requirements. For specialist functions like actuarial or data science, they look for specific qualifications and relevant certifications at this early stage.

Stage 2: Recruiter screening call
If your profile is shortlisted, a recruiter typically reaches out for an initial conversation. This call covers your background, availability, salary expectations, and interest in the role. It is conversational in nature and not a deep technical assessment.

Stage 3: Hiring manager or technical interview
This is usually the most substantive round. Depending on the role, expect case-study questions, technical problem-solving, or domain-specific scenarios. Actuarial candidates may be asked about exam progress and core technical concepts. Technology candidates often face coding exercises or system design questions. Consulting candidates typically work through structured problem-solving scenarios.

Stage 4: Behavioral or competency interview
WTW places significant weight on values and professional behaviors. This round often follows a structured competency format, asking you to share specific examples using a situation-action-result structure. Common themes include collaboration, client focus, navigating ambiguity, and delivering results under pressure.

Stage 5: Final or panel round
Senior stakeholders or cross-functional leaders may join for a final discussion. This round is as much about cultural fit as it is about capability, and you will usually get time to ask your own questions about the team and the role.

Offer and background check
Successful candidates receive a verbal offer followed by a written one. A standard background verification check happens before your joining date.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

WTW looks for a combination of technical depth and interpersonal effectiveness. The balance shifts depending on the function, but certain qualities appear consistently across roles.

Analytical and quantitative ability. WTW's work in risk, actuarial science, and people analytics demands comfort with structured reasoning and data. Even non-technical consulting roles benefit from a quantitative mindset.

Client orientation. A large share of WTW roles involve advising clients or managing relationships. Interviewers assess whether you can explain complex ideas clearly, listen actively, and adapt your communication style to different audiences.

Domain expertise. For specialist roles, WTW expects genuine depth. Actuarial candidates are assessed on exam progress and technical mastery. HR consulting candidates should understand compensation structures, benefits design, or workforce strategy in the Indian market context.

Collaboration and adaptability. WTW operates across global teams and time zones. They look for professionals who can work across cultures, manage ambiguity, and contribute constructively in a matrixed environment without waiting to be told what to do.

Integrity and professional standards. As an advisory firm, WTW's reputation rests on the trustworthiness of its advice. Candidates who demonstrate ethical grounding, attention to detail, and accountability tend to stand out in the process.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research WTW's business lines before your interview. WTW operates across risk and broking, benefits delivery and administration, and health and benefits consulting. Know which segment you are applying to and understand its key challenges in the Indian market.

Prepare strong behavioral examples. WTW's process is heavily competency-based. Use a situation-action-result format and prepare examples that show client impact, leadership in ambiguous situations, and collaboration across teams. Vague or generic answers work against you here.

Brush up on domain knowledge. If you are in actuarial, review your exam syllabus and be ready to discuss how concepts apply in practice. If you are in technology, practice coding and system design relevant to the job description. If you are in consulting, think through current industry trends and how WTW's services address them.

Read WTW's published values and recent announcements. WTW talks openly about its culture of inclusion, client focus, and innovation. Their annual report and press releases give you material to reference in interviews, and doing so signals genuine interest rather than a spray-and-pray application.

Prepare thoughtful questions for your interviewers. WTW hiring teams appreciate candidates who ask specific, informed questions about the team's work and growth expectations. Questions about current challenges, what success looks like in the role, or how the team collaborates demonstrate that you have done your homework.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

WTW positions itself as a firm where rigorous analytical thinking meets genuine care for people and clients. Its culture is often described by employees as collegial and intellectually demanding, with clear career tracks and real investment in professional development.

In India, WTW offices are known for a structured environment where performance expectations are high but support systems are in place. The firm actively encourages employees to pursue professional certifications relevant to their domains. This is a particularly strong draw for actuarial and HR consulting professionals who want employer backing for their exams and coursework.

WTW has made public commitments around diversity and inclusion, with visible initiatives focused on gender equity and inclusive leadership. These are not just external messaging points. Candidates who demonstrate inclusive behaviors and perspectives in interviews tend to resonate well with WTW's hiring teams.

The firm's global footprint means cross-cultural collaboration is a genuine daily reality, not a buzzword. If you are comfortable working across time zones and communicating with colleagues in different regional offices, you will find WTW's environment a natural fit.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds of interviews does WTW typically have?

The number of rounds varies by role, seniority level, and business unit. Candidates commonly go through a recruiter screening call, one or more technical or functional interviews, a behavioral competency round, and sometimes a final panel discussion. Senior roles and specialist positions like actuarial tend to have more stages than entry-level analyst roles.

How long does the WTW hiring process usually take?

The timeline depends on the role and location. Experienced hires in specialized domains like actuarial or consulting typically go through multiple rounds spread across several weeks. Campus hiring tends to move faster, with structured timelines tied to the recruitment calendar. Staying in regular contact with your recruiter is the most reliable way to track your status.

Does WTW hire freshers in India?

Yes, WTW runs campus hiring programs and graduate intake for roles in actuarial science, technology, and analyst tracks. Check their careers portal during campus recruitment season, which typically runs in the final quarter of the calendar year and into the first quarter of the following year. Actuarial graduate intakes sometimes have their own separate timelines, so it is worth monitoring those specifically.

How important are actuarial exam passes for WTW actuarial roles?

Exam progress is a key signal for actuarial hiring at WTW. Candidates who have cleared several papers from the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries or the Institute of Actuaries of India are viewed more favorably than those who are just starting out. WTW also supports exam preparation for employees already on board, which makes it an attractive employer for professionals mid-way through their qualification journey.

What salary can I expect at WTW India?

Compensation varies widely by role, level, and city. Glassdoor and LinkedIn Salary have crowd-sourced data from WTW India employees that can give you a realistic reference range for your specific function and experience level. Use those figures as a benchmark before your salary discussion with the recruiter, and come prepared with a specific expectation rather than leaving it open-ended.

Can I apply to multiple WTW roles at the same time?

Generally yes, but it pays to be strategic. WTW's recruiters can see multiple applications from the same candidate, so applying to roles that genuinely match your skills makes a stronger impression than mass-applying. Prioritize the positions where your background is the strongest fit, and tailor your resume accordingly for each one.
