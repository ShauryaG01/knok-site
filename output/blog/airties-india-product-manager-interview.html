01 Overview

Overview

Airties India: What to Expect from the PM Interview

Airties is a global managed WiFi company that partners with telecom operators and ISPs to deliver smart home networking solutions to subscribers. Their India team contributes to cloud platform development, firmware features, and operator-facing tools that run in homes across multiple countries.

Candidates report that the Airties PM interview blends product thinking, light technical depth, and behavioral questions. The central challenge the role deals with is a two-sided market: the ISP or telecom operator is your direct buyer, while the home subscriber is your actual end user. Interviewers typically look for PMs who can hold credible conversations with firmware engineers, articulate operator economics, and still stay focused on what the subscriber experiences at home.

As of July 2026, Airties India has 1 open PM role, within a national market of 2,009 PM openings. The interview process typically runs across a few rounds, though the exact structure varies by team and role. Hedge any specific expectations accordingly.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you prioritize features when the ISP partner and the end consumer have competing needs?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built a B2B2C SaaS product where enterprise clients configured the platform and their employees were the actual end users. The sales team pushed for features the enterprise wanted, while support data showed end users were struggling with onboarding.

*Task:* I had to create a prioritization framework that respected both sets of needs without alienating either stakeholder.

*Action:* I mapped each feature request into one of three buckets: 'operator control' (buyer-facing), 'subscriber experience' (end user-facing), and 'platform health' (benefits both). I scored each by impact and effort, then ran a quarterly review with our top two operator partners to validate the roadmap. I made sure every sprint included at least one consumer-facing improvement alongside operator requests.

*Result:* End user drop-off in the first week improved noticeably within two quarters, and our operator renewal rate held steady. The framework became the team's standard prioritization approach.

---

Q: Describe a product you shipped where hardware constraints changed your original plan.

*Situation:* I was the PM for a connected device feature that relied on continuous background data sync. Mid-development, the hardware team flagged that the chip's memory budget was significantly tighter than originally scoped.

*Task:* I had to rethink feature scope without killing the core user value or slipping the launch date by a full cycle.

*Action:* I ran a trade-off session with engineering to separate essential from 'nice to have' components. We cut the real-time sync layer and shipped an optimized batch-sync approach instead. I updated the roadmap to flag the full real-time version for the next hardware revision, and communicated the change early to our operator partner so there were no surprises.

*Result:* We launched on time. The batch-sync version received positive feedback from operators because it was more predictable on their network. The real-time version shipped in the following hardware cycle.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to influence an engineering team on a priority call without direct authority.

*Situation:* Our engineering team was deep in a platform refactor they considered critical. At the same time, a key operator partner escalated a subscriber-facing bug affecting a large portion of their user base.

*Task:* I needed to get engineering time on the bug fix without making the engineering manager feel the refactor was being hijacked.

*Action:* I came to the conversation with data: support ticket volume, the operator's SLA terms, and an estimate of the commercial risk if the bug persisted through another sprint. I proposed a specific, time-boxed plan: two engineers for one sprint, then back to the refactor. I framed it as protecting the refactor from a larger fire, not competing with it.

*Result:* The manager agreed. The bug was resolved within the sprint, the operator escalation closed, and the refactor resumed on the original schedule. The engineering manager later said the data-first framing made the call easy to approve.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For Product Sense and Prioritization Questions

Use a two-layer stakeholder map before jumping into any framework. At Airties, every prioritization decision has a B2B layer (the ISP operator, who controls procurement and SLAs) and a B2C layer (the subscriber, who experiences the product at home). A feature that improves both layers is almost always higher priority than one that helps only one side. Once you have the stakeholder map, apply an impact-effort scoring and bring it to a notional quarterly review with your top operator partners.

For Metrics and Success Definition Questions

Start with the user goal, not the product output. For a managed WiFi product, operational metrics typically look like: connection drop rate per session, self-service resolution rate (can the subscriber fix issues without calling support?), and support ticket rate per subscriber. Tie your metrics to a north star before listing secondary indicators. Candidates who lead with 'user satisfaction' without operationalizing it typically get pushed hard in follow-up questions.

For Hardware-Constrained Prioritization

Use a 'must-have vs. nice-to-have vs. next-cycle' bucketing approach when hardware or firmware constraints come up. Separate launch scope from roadmap commitments clearly. Interviewers at hardware-adjacent companies like Airties know that firmware release cycles are long and reversing a shipped decision is expensive. Show that you scope conservatively and communicate changes early.

For Influence Without Authority Questions

Lead with data and frame your ask as time-boxed and reversible. Show that you understand what the other team cares about: their roadmap integrity, their SLAs, their credibility with leadership. Position your request in those terms, not just your own priorities.

For India Market Localization Questions

Ground your answer in real operator dynamics. Jio, Airtel, and BSNL each have different subscriber profiles and support models. Show awareness of price sensitivity in the Indian market, the prevalence of shared WiFi in multi-household buildings, and the value of vernacular language support in onboarding flows for first-time broadband users.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical Credibility Without Engineering Depth

Airties builds firmware and cloud software for WiFi hardware. Interviewers typically want to see that you can hold a conversation with an RF engineer or a firmware developer. Know the basics: what mesh networking is, how band steering works, why a 5 GHz connection drops further from the router than a 2.4 GHz one. Candidates who cannot demonstrate this baseline often do not progress past the technical cross-functional rounds.

Two-Sided Market Thinking

The hardest part of a managed WiFi PM role is that your direct customer (the ISP) is not the person using the product (the home subscriber). Interviewers look for candidates who instinctively ask 'which customer are we solving for here?' and who can articulate trade-offs clearly without defaulting to one side. Show that you have internalized this tension before walking in.

Operator Economics Fluency

ISPs have long procurement cycles, strict SLA requirements, and large support organizations. A PM who treats the ISP as a simple feature requester will not land well. Interviewers want to see that you understand operator economics: support cost per subscriber, churn driven by poor WiFi experience, and the commercial value of self-service tools that reduce call center load.

Execution Discipline

Because hardware and firmware have hard release cycles, Airties PMs need strong execution discipline. Interviewers look for evidence that you scope carefully, communicate changes early, and do not over-promise on roadmaps. Bring examples where you actively managed scope, not just where you eventually shipped a feature.

Data-Driven Decision Making

Candidates report being asked how they have used data to make product calls. Prepare examples where you defined a metric, measured it, and made a decision based on the result, including cases where the data told you something you did not expect or want to hear.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Company and Domain Research

Read Airties' public product pages and any operator case studies they have published. Understand their core product: cloud-managed WiFi for ISPs, with device management, analytics, and subscriber self-service apps. Note the operators they partner with globally and think about what those partners care about from a commercial and operational standpoint.

Brush up on managed WiFi fundamentals: mesh networking, band steering, WiFi 6 and WiFi 6E, and cloud-based device management. You do not need deep engineering knowledge, but you should be able to discuss these topics without asking the interviewer to explain them to you.

Week 2: Product Thinking Practice

Practice the 12 questions in the 'Most Asked Questions' section above. For each one, draft a two-minute verbal answer and check it against the frameworks in the 'Answer Frameworks' section. Focus especially on two-sided market questions, since these are consistently reported as the core test in Airties PM interviews.

Prepare three to four STAR stories from your own experience. Aim for at least one that involves a hardware-adjacent or technically constrained product, and at least one that involves cross-functional influence with an engineering team. If you lack hardware experience, draw a clear parallel to any product with long release cycles or hard external dependencies.

Week 3: Mock Interviews and Gap Closing

Do at least two mock interviews with someone who can give honest feedback. Evaluate yourself on three fronts: do you explain the two-sided market clearly? Do you lead with data in behavioral answers? Do you use basic networking vocabulary accurately without over-claiming expertise?

As context, as of July 2026 there are 2,009 PM openings across India, with Bangalore leading at 271 roles. Industry surveys commonly cite PM salaries in the 3-6 year experience range in the 24-40 LPA band. Use this to calibrate your broader job search if you are applying beyond Airties.

In the final days before your interview, run through your STAR stories once more and prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer about Airties' product direction and their model for working with operator partners.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating the ISP as Just Another Stakeholder

Many PM candidates apply a generic 'user research and prioritization' playbook without recognizing that at Airties, the ISP operator is not simply a stakeholder to be managed. They are a sophisticated B2B buyer with contractual leverage, procurement timelines, and support cost economics. Candidates who miss this dynamic typically struggle in the product sense rounds.

Over-Claiming Technical Expertise

Some candidates oversell their networking knowledge and get caught when interviewers probe deeper. It is better to say 'I understand managed WiFi at a product level and ramp up quickly with engineering support' than to claim deep RF or firmware expertise you do not have. Interviewers at hardware companies tend to probe technical claims carefully.

Vague Metrics Answers

Saying 'I would measure user satisfaction' is not sufficient for a metrics question in this context. Interviewers typically push for operational specifics: self-service resolution rate, WiFi drop frequency per session, support ticket rate per subscriber. Practice translating high-level goals into concrete, measurable indicators before your interview.

Ignoring the Subscriber Layer

Because Airties sells to ISPs, some candidates focus entirely on operator needs and forget the subscriber at home. Interviewers flag this quickly. Always show that you are thinking about what the end user actually experiences, even when the immediate buyer is the operator.

Weak Cross-Functional Stories

Airties PMs collaborate with firmware engineers, hardware teams, and operator integration teams. Candidates who cannot tell a specific, clear story about working through a technical constraint or disagreement with engineering often do not progress. Prepare at least one story where you navigated a hardware or technical dependency.

Generic Questions for the Interviewer

Candidates who ask generic closing questions miss a real signal opportunity. Ask about how the India team interfaces with the global product organization, how operator-specific feature requests are handled at the roadmap level, or what the biggest subscriber experience challenge looks like right now. This signals that you have thought about the role specifically, not just PM interviews in general.

How many interview rounds does Airties India typically have for PM roles?

Candidates report that the process typically spans multiple rounds, though the exact number varies by role and team. Commonly reported stages include an initial screening call, one or two product or case-based discussions, and a behavioral or leadership round. Some candidates also report a technical conversation with an engineering stakeholder. Treat any specific round count you hear as a general guide, not a guarantee.

Do I need a background in networking or telecom to apply for a PM role at Airties India?

A networking background is helpful but not a strict requirement, based on what candidates typically report. Airties does expect PMs to ramp up on the domain quickly and to hold credible conversations with firmware and hardware engineers. Spending a week on managed WiFi basics, mesh networking fundamentals, and ISP operator economics will meaningfully improve your positioning in the interview.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Airties India?

Airties does not publish salary ranges publicly, so specific numbers are difficult to verify independently. Based on publicly reported industry surveys, PM roles in India at the 3-6 year experience level commonly fall in the 24-40 LPA range, and senior PM roles are commonly cited in the 40-60 LPA range. Is the Airties India PM interview more product-focused or technical?

Candidates report a blend of both, with product thinking as the primary focus and a layer of technical awareness expected on top. You are unlikely to be asked to write code or solve algorithmic problems. However, you will be expected to discuss WiFi concepts, firmware release constraints, and cloud platform trade-offs in a product context. Think of it as 'technically informed product thinking' rather than a pure engineering test.
