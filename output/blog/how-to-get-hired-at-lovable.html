01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Lovable builds AI-powered tools that let anyone create web applications through a chat interface, without needing to write code from scratch. Originally launched as GPT Engineer, the company rebranded to Lovable as it sharpened its focus on making software creation feel as natural as having a conversation. The product sits at the intersection of developer tools and consumer software, which shapes the kind of people they hire.

With 74 open roles right now, Lovable is in an active growth phase. Positions span engineering, product, design, marketing, and operations. Most roles are remote-friendly, and the company hires internationally, so Indian professionals can absolutely compete for these positions.

The core pitch of working at Lovable is the chance to build tools that genuinely change who gets to create software. If that idea excites you more than the perks list, you are already thinking like a Lovable hire.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Lovable does not publish a detailed breakdown of their interview stages, so the outline below reflects patterns commonly seen at similar early-stage AI product startups. Treat it as a reasonable guide, not a guarantee.

Application and initial screen: You apply through their jobs page, typically with a CV and sometimes a short cover note. A recruiter will review and, if your profile fits, reach out within a week or two. For some roles there may be a brief async video question or a written prompt to complete before any live conversation.

Hiring manager or recruiter call: This is usually a conversation of around half an hour about your background, your motivation for joining Lovable specifically, and how you work. Expect questions like 'what have you shipped that you are proud of?' or 'how do you operate when priorities shift fast?'

Skills assessment: For engineering roles, this commonly takes the form of a take-home task or a live coding session focused on real-world problem-solving rather than algorithmic puzzles. For product, growth, or marketing roles, a case study or short presentation is more typical. The format varies by team, so it is worth asking the recruiter what to expect.

Team or leadership round: Final-stage interviews at startups often include a conversation with a senior leader or a co-founder. This round is partly about skills and partly about whether your values align with where the company is going.

Offer: Startup hiring can move quickly. If rounds go well, you may receive an offer within days of the final interview. Have your questions about compensation, equity, and role scope ready so you can move decisively.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Lovable is building in a space where the technology shifts faster than most job descriptions can capture. Across roles, a few qualities come up consistently at companies like this.

Builder mentality: They want people who have actually made things, not just planned or reviewed them. For engineers, this means shipping code. For product and growth hires, it means running experiments and iterating on real users. Side projects, open-source contributions, or indie products carry real weight here.

Comfort with ambiguity: Early-stage AI companies change direction often. Candidates who need detailed specs and stable roadmaps to do their best work tend to struggle. Lovable values people who can figure out what 'good' looks like when no one has defined it yet.

Genuine curiosity about AI: This is not a company where you can treat AI as a buzzword. The product is the AI. Hiring managers will notice quickly whether you use tools like theirs in your own work and whether you have genuine opinions about where the space is going.

Clear communication: Remote-first companies depend on writing. Whether you are in engineering, design, or operations, being able to explain your thinking in a message, a doc, or a design review matters as much as the work itself.

Speed over perfection: Lovable operates in a market where moving fast is a competitive advantage. They tend to favor candidates who can ship something imperfect and iterate over candidates who wait for the perfect solution.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product first. Before any interview at Lovable, spend time with their app builder. Build something small, notice what works, notice what feels rough. You do not need to have all the answers, but showing up with real product experience signals that you care about the mission, not just the job.

Tailor your application to the role. Generic CVs get filtered out fast at startups. Highlight the work most relevant to what Lovable is building. If you have built tools, worked with AI APIs, or shipped something used by real users, lead with that.

Prepare your 'why Lovable' answer. Every good startup interview includes this question in some form. A weak answer references the company's growth or funding. A strong answer explains what specific problem they are solving and why you want to work on that problem. Be honest and specific.

Have examples ready for ambiguous situations. Expect behavioral questions about times you have operated without clear direction, changed course mid-project, or pushed something out with incomplete information. Use real examples and be specific about what you did and what you learned.

Brush up on AI product thinking. Even if you are applying for a non-engineering role, understanding how large language models behave, where they fail, and how users experience AI tools will help you stand out. You do not need to be an ML researcher, just genuinely informed.

Ask good questions. In the final rounds, the questions you ask signal how you think. Ask about how decisions get made, what the team is most uncertain about right now, or what success looks like in the first few months. These show you are thinking about the work, not just the offer.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Lovable is a startup built around a genuinely optimistic idea: that anyone, regardless of technical background, should be able to create software. That belief shows up in the culture in a few concrete ways.

Speed is a value, not just a preference. The team ships fast and expects everyone to operate that way. This is not about working longer hours. It is about making decisions with incomplete information, cutting scope when necessary, and learning from what is in front of users rather than from what is in a planning doc.

Remote-first, async by default. Lovable operates across time zones, which means written communication is central to how the team functions. Decisions get documented, context gets shared in writing, and meetings are used for things that actually need real-time discussion.

Mission-driven without being preachy. The company cares deeply about democratising software creation, but the culture, from what is publicly visible, is more 'show through the product' than 'talk about the vision.' People are expected to care about impact and demonstrate it through their work.

Small team, high ownership. With a focused headcount relative to its ambition, Lovable tends toward giving people wide scope. You are unlikely to find a role where someone else is handling all the hard decisions in your area.

If you thrive on autonomy, care about the product you are building, and want to work somewhere that is still figuring things out in real time, Lovable's culture is likely a good fit.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Lovable hire from India?

Lovable hires internationally and many of their roles are listed as remote. Indian professionals can and do apply for engineering, product, and operations positions. That said, some roles may require working in or relocating to Stockholm, so check the specific job listing for location requirements. If the role says 'remote,' you are eligible to apply from India.

What roles does Lovable currently have open?

As of early July 2026, Lovable has 74 open roles listed across their teams. These span software engineering, product management, design, growth, marketing, and operations. The mix of roles reflects a company that is scaling both its product and its go-to-market functions at the same time. Check their careers page directly for the current list since roles open and close quickly at fast-growing startups.

Is prior AI experience required to work at Lovable?

For most roles, deep AI or machine learning expertise is not a hard requirement. What matters more is that you genuinely use AI tools in your work and have real opinions about how they perform. Engineers do not need to have trained models, but they should be comfortable building with AI APIs and understand how language models behave in production. For non-technical roles, being an informed and curious user of AI products is usually enough.

How long does the Lovable hiring process take?

Startup hiring timelines vary, but early-stage companies like Lovable often move faster than large tech firms. If you are a strong fit, it is not unusual to go from application to offer in two to three weeks. Delays typically happen when a role is being redefined or when the hiring team is stretched across many open positions. Staying responsive and asking the recruiter about timelines after your first call helps you stay on track.

What should I include in my Lovable job application?

Lead with what you have built and shipped, since Lovable values tangible output over credentials. A CV that shows real projects, side products, or measurable results will stand out more than a list of companies you have worked at. A short, specific cover note explaining why you want to work on this particular problem, not just at a well-funded startup, is worth writing. Link to anything you have published, built, or contributed to that is publicly visible.
