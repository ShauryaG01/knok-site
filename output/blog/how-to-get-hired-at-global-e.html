01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Global-e is a cross-border e-commerce technology company that helps major brands sell to shoppers worldwide. The platform handles localised checkout, local payment methods, duties, taxes, and last-mile logistics, so brands can reach international customers without rebuilding their entire tech stack. Listed on NASDAQ and growing fast, Global-e has built partnerships with premium fashion labels, electronics brands, and specialty retailers across the globe.

As of early July 2026, Global-e has 74 open roles spanning engineering, product, sales, customer success, finance, and operations. This level of hiring activity points to continued expansion across both product lines and geographies. Whether you are a software engineer, a commercial leader, or an operations specialist, there is likely a relevant opening worth targeting.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Global-e typically runs through four or five stages, though the exact number depends on the role and seniority. Here is how it usually looks:

Application and resume screen: A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume for relevant experience. Tailoring your resume to the specific role, especially if you can highlight e-commerce, payments, or SaaS experience, improves your chance of moving forward.

Recruiter call: If your profile clears the screen, a recruiter will reach out for a short conversation about your background, salary expectations, and interest in the role. This is also your chance to ask basic questions about the team and work location.

Hiring manager interview: A deeper conversation about your experience and how it maps to what the role needs. For technical roles this may include a short problem-solving or system design discussion. For commercial roles, expect questions about past deals, clients you have managed, or metrics you have owned.

Technical or functional rounds: Depending on the function, this stage could be a coding challenge, a case study, a product walkthrough, or a data exercise. Engineering candidates typically see algorithmic questions and system design. Product candidates may be asked to critique a feature or sketch a roadmap.

Final panel or leadership interview: A conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholders. Expect questions about how you work with others, handle ambiguity, and think about global markets.

An offer and background check follow if all rounds go well. Candidates commonly report the end-to-end process taking a few weeks, though urgent roles sometimes move faster.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Global-e sits at the intersection of e-commerce, fintech, and logistics. The traits and skills that tend to matter most:

Domain relevance: Experience in e-commerce, payments, SaaS, or international business is a strong signal. You do not need all four, but familiarity with at least one area helps you speak the company's language from day one.

Data orientation: Global-e's value to clients is measurable, including conversion rates, localisation uplift, and checkout performance. Candidates who can talk about outcomes in numbers and who are comfortable with dashboards or analytics stand out across functions.

Collaborative working style: The company works across time zones and cultures. Examples of working effectively with remote or international colleagues make a strong impression on interviewers.

Ownership mindset: Interviewers commonly ask about situations where you took initiative, moved a project forward without being asked, or fixed something that was not your direct responsibility. Prepare two or three concrete stories.

Communication clarity: Because the product is technically complex, the ability to explain things simply, whether to a client, a colleague, or leadership, is valued in almost every function.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Study the product first. Visit the Global-e website and read how the platform describes its value to brands and shoppers. Understanding terms like 'localised checkout,' 'guaranteed duty and tax,' and 'local payment methods' will help you speak naturally in interviews rather than sounding like you just discovered the company.

Research their client base. Global-e works with premium and enterprise brands. Being able to name a few clients and explain what problem the platform solves for them shows genuine preparation and interest, which interviewers notice.

Prepare metric-driven stories. For every significant role or project on your resume, know the outcome in numbers. What did you ship, how many users did it affect, what revenue or efficiency did you support? Even rough figures are better than vague descriptions.

Practice cross-border e-commerce basics. You do not need deep expertise, but knowing why international shoppers often abandon checkouts (unfamiliar currencies, unexpected duties on delivery, no local payment option) gives you useful context for almost any conversation at Global-e.

Ask sharp questions. Interviewers at growth-stage companies notice candidates who have done genuine research. Ask about the team's roadmap, how success is measured in the role, or how the team coordinates across geographies.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Global-e operates as a high-growth technology company with a strong commercial focus. The culture tends to be fast-paced, with teams expected to own outcomes rather than just complete tasks. A few patterns that come up consistently in candidate feedback and public reviews:

Global by default: With clients and colleagues spread across continents, the working day often involves calls across time zones. Flexibility and strong async communication matter here more than at a typical domestic employer.

Client obsession: The business model depends on brands seeing measurable results. Teams across functions, not just customer success, are expected to understand what clients need and to respond quickly when something is not working.

Low hierarchy, high accountability: Global-e has grown quickly, and like many scale-ups, the structure is relatively flat. This means more autonomy than you would find at a large enterprise, but also an expectation that you will figure things out without detailed instruction.

Diversity of perspective: With offices across multiple countries and a global client base, the company brings together people from varied backgrounds. Candidates who are curious about different markets and comfortable with cultural nuance tend to fit well here.

How many jobs is Global-e currently hiring for?

As of early July 2026, Global-e has 74 open roles across functions including engineering, product, sales, customer success, and operations. The mix reflects growth across both product and go-to-market teams. Checking the Global-e careers page directly gives you the most current list, as roles open and close frequently at a company of this size.

Does Global-e hire remotely, or only from specific cities?

Global-e has offices across Israel, the UK, the US, and other locations, and some roles are listed as remote or hybrid. The availability of remote work depends on the specific team and the role. Check each job listing carefully for the location requirement before applying, as policies can vary across departments.

What salary can I expect at Global-e?

Compensation varies widely by role, level, and location. Figures publicly reported on Glassdoor and levels.fyi give a rough sense for engineering and product roles, but sample sizes are often small and skewed toward certain geographies. Asking the recruiter directly in the first call is the most reliable approach, as many Global-e recruiters share a salary band early in the process.

Is prior e-commerce experience required to get hired?

It is not always required, but it is a real advantage, especially for commercial and product roles. Engineering candidates with strong technical fundamentals and SaaS experience have moved through the process successfully without a dedicated e-commerce background. The key is being able to learn the domain quickly and showing genuine interest in the problem Global-e solves.

How long does the Global-e interview process take?

Candidates commonly report the process taking a few weeks from application to offer, though this varies by role and how urgently the team needs to fill the position. Technical roles with multiple rounds tend to run longer than commercial or operations roles. A polite follow-up to your recruiter after each stage is perfectly reasonable if you have not heard back.

What is the best way to stand out in my application?

Tailor your resume to highlight any experience with e-commerce platforms, international markets, payments, or SaaS metrics. A short, specific cover note that references a Global-e client or describes a problem the platform solves tends to get attention. A referral from a current employee also carries weight, so check if anyone in your network works there before applying cold.
