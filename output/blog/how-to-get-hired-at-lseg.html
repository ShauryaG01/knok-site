01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

LSEG (London Stock Exchange Group) is one of the world's leading financial markets infrastructure providers, with a strong footprint across data, analytics, trading, and risk management. Following its acquisition of Refinitiv, the company grew into a global data and technology powerhouse with a large workforce spread across major financial centres.

For Indian professionals, LSEG is an attractive destination. The company has major technology and operations centres in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Chennai, actively recruiting talent across engineering, data science, product, compliance, and finance roles.

As of July 2026, LSEG has 129 open roles across its hiring platforms. Positions span software engineering, data engineering, quantitative analysis, risk, product management, and business analysis, so whether you come from a technology or a finance background, there is likely a fit worth exploring.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

LSEG's hiring process typically runs across several stages, and the exact structure can vary depending on the role, the team, and the seniority level. Here is what candidates commonly report.

Stage 1: Online Application

You apply through LSEG's careers portal or via LinkedIn. The application asks for your resume, academic background, and sometimes a short cover note. Initial screening is often handled by an applicant tracking system, so make sure your resume mirrors the language in the job description.

Stage 2: Online Assessment

For many roles, particularly in technology and finance, candidates are invited to complete an online test. This typically covers aptitude, numerical reasoning, or coding challenges depending on the function. Engineering roles commonly use timed coding platforms, while finance roles lean toward numerical and logical reasoning tests.

Stage 3: HR or Recruiter Screen

A short call with an HR recruiter. Expect questions about your background, why you want to join LSEG, and logistics like notice period and preferred location.

Stage 4: Technical or Functional Interview

This is the core evaluation round. For engineering roles, expect coding problems, system design discussions, and questions about your past projects. For finance or data roles, expect domain questions, case-style problems, and a discussion of your analytical approach.

Stage 5: Final Round

This often involves a panel interview or a meeting with senior stakeholders. Behavioural questions are central here, covering topics like handling ambiguity, cross-team collaboration, and working under pressure. Some roles include a short presentation or case study at this stage.

The full process can span a few weeks, so stay responsive and keep close track of your communications with the recruiter.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

LSEG looks for professionals who combine strong domain knowledge with the ability to work in a fast-moving, globally connected environment. Here is what tends to matter across most roles.

Technical depth for technology roles. Engineers and data professionals should be comfortable with languages like Python, Java, or Scala, and have working knowledge of cloud platforms. Familiarity with financial data formats, messaging systems, or trading infrastructure is a strong advantage.

Financial markets literacy. Even on the technology side, LSEG values people who understand the business context of what they build. Knowing how capital markets work, what a trade lifecycle looks like, or how risk is managed gives you an edge over equally technical candidates.

Analytical thinking. Across functions, LSEG prizes people who can break down complex problems, back up their thinking with data, and communicate findings clearly to non-technical audiences.

Collaboration and communication. LSEG operates across time zones and cultures. The ability to work with distributed teams, manage stakeholders, and adapt your communication style is consistently valued.

Ownership and initiative. Hiring managers look for candidates who take accountability for outcomes, not just tasks. Examples of proactively solving problems or driving improvements tend to land well in interviews.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Getting an offer from LSEG takes preparation across both technical and behavioural dimensions. Here is how to approach it.

Understand LSEG's business. Spend time on their investor materials and product pages to understand how their data, analytics, and trading divisions fit together. Knowing how their post-acquisition data products work, or how their clearing and settlement infrastructure operates, signals genuine interest to interviewers.

Brush up on financial markets basics. Even for tech roles, you should be able to explain what an order book is, how equities and derivatives differ, and what market data means in a trading context. This is not deep quant knowledge, just basic fluency.

Practise coding and aptitude assessments. For engineering roles, practise on competitive coding platforms. Focus on data structures, algorithms, and SQL, as these appear most often. For finance roles, numerical reasoning and logical reasoning practice goes a long way.

Prepare your STAR stories. LSEG's behavioural rounds lean on structured answers. Prepare five or six clear stories covering topics like leading under pressure, resolving a conflict, delivering despite ambiguity, and owning a mistake.

Tailor your resume to each job description. LSEG uses applicant tracking systems and job descriptions are detailed. Mirror the language in the description while keeping your resume honest and specific.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

LSEG describes its culture around openness, partnership, and long-term thinking. These values show up in how teams are structured and how decisions get made.

Inclusion and diversity. LSEG is vocal about its inclusion commitments, running employee resource groups across gender, ethnicity, and mental health. Interview processes are designed to reduce bias, and structured development programmes exist for underrepresented groups.

Collaboration over hierarchy. Despite being a large organisation, LSEG encourages cross-functional collaboration. Teams often work across London, New York, Bengaluru, and Hong Kong simultaneously, so a flat communication style is valued over formal top-down structures.

Purpose-driven work. LSEG positions itself as infrastructure for the global economy. Employees often cite the satisfaction of knowing their work, whether in data pipelines, trade matching, or risk analytics, underpins real financial activity for institutions around the world.

Continuous learning. The company invests in professional development, including support for certifications, internal mobility, and structured learning platforms. For Indian professionals looking to build a global career in financial markets, this is worth factoring into your decision.

Overall, LSEG suits professionals who are comfortable with complexity, take initiative, and want a global stage without giving up depth of work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What kinds of roles does LSEG typically hire for in India?

LSEG's India offices, primarily in Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Hyderabad, hire across software engineering, data engineering, quantitative analysis, product management, compliance, and operations. Technology roles make up a large share of openings, particularly around cloud infrastructure, data platforms, and financial software development. Finance and business roles also exist, covering areas like risk, regulatory reporting, and client services. With 129 open roles as of July 2026, there is a broad range worth browsing.

Does LSEG hire fresh graduates or mainly experienced professionals?

LSEG hires both. For experienced professionals, most roles require at least a few years of relevant experience and the interview process reflects that depth. For freshers, LSEG runs graduate programmes and campus hiring initiatives, particularly targeting premier engineering and management institutions. If you are a recent graduate, look specifically for 'graduate' or 'analyst' level postings and apply through the campus hiring channel if your college is eligible.

How long does the LSEG interview process usually take from application to offer?

Candidates commonly report that the full process, from online assessment to final round, takes anywhere from a few weeks to a couple of months depending on the role and team. Roles in high-demand areas like technology tend to move faster than niche finance or compliance positions. Staying responsive to recruiter messages and completing assessments promptly are the best ways to keep things moving on your end.

What salary can I expect from LSEG in India?

Exact figures vary widely by role, city, and seniority. Based on publicly reported data on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, LSEG is considered a competitive payer within the financial data and fintech segment in India, and industry surveys consistently place it on par with other large financial markets infrastructure firms. Total compensation typically includes a base salary and an annual performance bonus, and senior or specialised roles sometimes carry additional long-term incentives. Cross-referencing Glassdoor listings for your specific role title and city gives the most accurate current picture.

What is the work culture like for Indian employees at LSEG?

Indian employees at LSEG generally describe a professional, globally connected environment where working across time zones is common. For technology and product teams, this can mean occasional overlap with UK or US hours. The company has a stated focus on inclusion and internal mobility, and the pace is steady rather than startup-frantic, which suits professionals who prefer structured, impact-driven work.

How should I follow up after an LSEG interview?

Send a brief thank-you note to your recruiter within a day or two of each interview stage. Keep it short, confirm your interest in the role, and mention one specific thing from the conversation that reinforced that interest. If you have not heard back within the timeline the recruiter mentioned, a single polite follow-up email is appropriate. Avoid multiple follow-ups in a short window, as this tends to read as pressure rather than enthusiasm.
