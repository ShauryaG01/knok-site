01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Twin Health is a digital health company building what it calls the 'Whole Body Digital Twin', a personalised AI model that tracks metabolism, sleep, and lifestyle to help reverse chronic conditions like Type 2 diabetes and obesity. The company works with employers and health plans in the US, while its engineering, data science, and clinical teams are largely based in India.

As of mid-2026, Twin Health has 33 open roles listed across software engineering, data science, machine learning, clinical operations, and product. The company is in an active hiring phase, making this a good window to apply.

Roles span junior to senior levels. Most technical positions expect strong fundamentals in Python, cloud infrastructure, or health data systems. Non-technical roles in clinical coordination and customer success value healthcare domain knowledge and clear communication.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Twin Health's hiring process typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact number varies by role and team.

Stage 1: Application and resume screening
The recruiting team reviews applications for role fit. A tailored resume that mirrors the job description language improves your chances of moving forward.

Stage 2: Recruiter call
A short introductory call with an HR or talent team member. Expect questions about your background, current role, notice period, and salary expectations. This is also your chance to ask basic questions about the team and work setup.

Stage 3: Technical or functional screening
For engineering and data roles, this is usually a coding or system design round conducted over a video call. For clinical or operations roles, it may be a case discussion or a short written task. The difficulty is calibrated to the seniority of the role.

Stage 4: Panel or loop interviews
A set of back-to-back interviews with team members and cross-functional stakeholders. Technical candidates can expect a mix of coding, system design, and behavioural questions. Non-technical candidates typically go through scenario-based and competency questions.

Stage 5: Leadership or final round
Senior hires usually have a conversation with a director or VP. This round focuses on cultural alignment, long-term thinking, and your understanding of the health-tech space.

Offer and background check
If selected, HR shares a verbal offer followed by a formal letter. Background verification is standard before joining.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Twin Health sits at the intersection of AI and healthcare, so they value people who can hold both technical rigour and a genuine interest in patient outcomes.

For engineering and data roles, strong Python skills are a baseline expectation. Experience with ML pipelines, time-series data, or cloud platforms such as AWS or GCP gives you an edge. The company builds real-time metabolic models, so candidates comfortable with health data standards or wearable device data stand out.

For clinical and operations roles, prior experience in digital health, insurance, or hospital operations is valued. Clear written communication matters because many client interactions happen asynchronously across time zones.

Across all roles, Twin Health looks for:
- Ownership mindset: the ability to drive a problem end-to-end without waiting to be directed
- Comfort with ambiguity: early-stage health tech means requirements change fast
- Mission alignment: interviewers consistently probe whether candidates care about chronic disease outcomes, not just the product
- Collaboration across functions: clinical, engineering, and data teams work closely, so comfort working across disciplines is common

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product before your first call. Twin Health's Whole Body Digital Twin is specific enough that interviewers notice when candidates have not looked it up. Understand what metrics the platform tracks and which chronic conditions it targets.

Tailor your resume to the role. With 33 open roles across functions, the same resume will not serve all of them equally. Highlight the parts of your experience that match the job description language, especially terms like 'health data', 'metabolic modelling', or 'patient engagement' where relevant.

Prepare STAR answers for mission questions. You will almost certainly be asked why you want to work in health tech or why this problem matters to you. Have a specific, genuine answer ready. Generic answers about 'impact' do not land well here.

For technical roles, practise system design with a health-tech angle. Think about how you would design a data pipeline for wearable sensor data or a real-time alert system for metabolic changes. These are closer to the actual problems the team solves than generic e-commerce or social-feed design questions.

Ask good questions. Interviewers at mission-driven companies pay attention to the quality of your questions. Ask about how the team measures clinical outcomes, how engineering and clinical teams collaborate, or what the biggest technical challenge looks like for the role right now.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Twin Health describes itself as a mission-first company, and this shapes how the team operates day to day. Employees who thrive here tend to be comfortable working across clinical, data, and product functions without sharp handoff boundaries.

Work setup: Most India-based roles operate in a hybrid or remote-friendly model, with some in-office presence for teams in Bengaluru. US-facing roles may require overlap hours in the evening.

Pace: The company is in a growth phase, which means the roadmap evolves quickly. People who prefer clearly defined scope and stable requirements often find the pace challenging. People who like building from scratch and influencing decisions early tend to find it energising.

Collaboration style: Decision-making is relatively flat for a health-tech company of this size. Individual contributors are expected to raise concerns and propose solutions rather than waiting for top-down direction.

Mission weight: Unlike many product companies where 'mission' is a talking point, at Twin Health the clinical outcomes data is part of how teams measure success. Engineers regularly interact with clinical data and patient metrics, which most team members cite as a motivating factor.
