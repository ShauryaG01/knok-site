01 Overview

Overview

outsidecapital is a capital-markets technology platform that helps private credit funds, family offices, and institutional investors manage deal flow, reporting, and investor relations. A Solutions Engineer at outsidecapital is a technical commercial role: you demo the product, guide clients through onboarding, build or review integrations, and act as the bridge between sales and engineering.

outsidecapital currently has 92 open roles across functions, signalling active hiring. Across India, the Solutions Engineer category has 1,270 live openings as of mid-2026, with Bangalore leading at 55, followed by Mumbai (23) and Delhi (20).

Candidates report the interview process typically runs two to four rounds. It usually begins with a recruiter or hiring-manager screening call, moves into a technical product round (API familiarity, integration concepts, live demo exercise), and ends with a stakeholder or culture discussion. Some candidates also report a written or take-home case study. Rounds are not always formally named, so treat each call as potentially covering both technical and behavioural territory.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from reported candidate experiences and the typical scope of a Solutions Engineer role at a fintech platform like outsidecapital.

  1. Walk us through how you would run a discovery call with a new prospect who has never used a capital-markets platform before.
  2. A client's API integration breaks in production the evening before their go-live. What do you do?
  3. How do you explain a complex data model or API structure to a CFO or investment director who is not technical?
  4. Describe a time you had to tell a client that their request was out of scope. How did you handle the pushback?

  10. What metrics would you track to decide whether a client onboarding was successful?
  11. Describe your experience with REST APIs, webhooks, or data integrations in a client-facing context.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would run a discovery call with a new prospect.

*Situation:* In my previous role at a fintech SaaS company, a large asset manager was evaluating our platform to replace a manual reporting workflow spread across multiple spreadsheets.

*Task:* I had to run the discovery call solo, surface the client's actual pain points, and map those to our product's capabilities without jumping into a demo too early.

*Action:* I prepared a structured agenda: opened with broad questions about their current process, asked which teams were involved and where handoffs broke down, then narrowed to specific friction around data consolidation and reporting timelines. I summarised what I had heard back to them before the call ended and sent a written follow-up the same day.

*Result:* The client described it as 'the most organised discovery call they had been part of.' We moved to a paid pilot within two weeks.

---

Q: A client's integration breaks in production the evening before go-live. What do you do?

*Situation:* A mid-size fund was scheduled to go live the following morning and their data team flagged a field-mapping issue in our API integration late the same evening.

*Task:* As the Solutions Engineer on the account, I had to coordinate a fix without delaying the launch date.

*Action:* I called the client right away to acknowledge the issue and set clear expectations. I looped in our on-call engineer, isolated the mismatched fields using a minimal test payload, and stayed on a shared call with both sides until the mapping was corrected. I sent a short status update each time we cleared a blocker.

*Result:* They went live on schedule the next morning, three days ahead of their internal target. Their ops lead sent a written note calling it 'the smoothest go-live they had managed.'

---

Q: How do you explain a complex data model to a non-technical stakeholder?

*Situation:* A CFO at a family office was unsure whether our platform could replace their fund administrator's reporting. She had no background in APIs or data modelling.

*Task:* I needed to connect our technical architecture to the specific outcomes she cared about, not walk through a schema diagram.

*Action:* I used a concrete analogy: our platform acted like a single dashboard that pulled data from all their existing sources, so her team would stop copying figures from one spreadsheet to another. I then ran a screen-share of a sample report, pointing only to the line items she recognised from her current workflow.

*Result:* She approved the pilot that same week. The account grew to become one of our largest by annual contract value that year.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions is the most reliable structure: Situation (one or two sentences of context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, step by step), Result (a concrete outcome, ideally with a client or business impact). outsidecapital interviewers typically probe the Action section hardest, so spend more time there than on setup.

PREP for product or opinion questions works well when you are asked something like 'how would you demo this' or 'what do you think of our pricing model.' Lead with your Point, give a Reason, offer an Example from your own experience, then restate the Point. This keeps answers tight and avoids rambling.

The 'Client First' filter is worth applying before any answer: ask yourself whether your response shows that you understood what the client needed, not just what you did technically. outsidecapital is a commercial role, and every answer benefits from showing client impact.

Clarify before you answer on case-style or scenario questions. Saying 'before I answer, can I ask one clarifying question?' signals consultative thinking, which is core to the role.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical credibility without arrogance. You do not need to be a software engineer, but you must be comfortable reading API documentation, understanding data flows, and troubleshooting integration issues on a call with a client's tech team. Demonstrate that you can hold your own without pretending to know everything.

Client empathy and communication. outsidecapital sells to sophisticated financial buyers: fund managers, CFOs, compliance officers. Interviewers want to see that you can read the room, adjust your language, and build trust quickly. Stories that show you calmed a stressed client or translated jargon into plain language score well.

Ownership under pressure. Solutions Engineers often own problems that cross team boundaries. Interviewers look for candidates who stay calm, communicate proactively, and drive issues to resolution rather than escalating and waiting.

Product curiosity. Candidates who have researched outsidecapital's product, understand the private credit market, and arrive with genuine questions about the platform's roadmap consistently stand out in reported feedback.

Commercial awareness. You are part of the revenue cycle. Showing that you understand how your role affects renewal, expansion, and referral signals maturity beyond a purely technical mindset.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: product and market research. Read everything publicly available about outsidecapital: their website, any press coverage, LinkedIn posts from the team. Understand the private credit lifecycle (fundraising, deal origination, reporting, investor relations) well enough to speak to it without notes. Study one or two competitor platforms so you can frame outsidecapital's positioning.

Week two: technical refresh. Review REST API concepts, common data integration patterns (webhooks, polling, field mapping), and how you would debug a failed API call step by step. If you have a portfolio project or past integration work, prepare a short walkthrough of it.

Week three: behavioural story preparation. Write out five or six stories from your experience that cover: a difficult client, a technical crisis, a cross-team coordination challenge, a time you learned something fast, and a time you pushed back. Use the STAR structure for each.

The final two days: mock and logistics. Run one full mock interview out loud, not just in your head. Review your stories and prepare two or three thoughtful questions for each interviewer (ask about team structure, onboarding ramp, and how success is measured in the first quarter). Confirm the interview format, time zone, and any tools you need to have ready.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure engineering interview. Solutions Engineering is a commercial role. Candidates who go deep on code but never mention client outcomes miss what the panel is evaluating.

Underresearching the product. outsidecapital operates in a specialist market. Arriving without knowing what private credit is, or not having explored the platform, signals low motivation.

Vague STAR stories. Answers like 'I worked with the team to resolve the issue' do not show individual ownership. Interviewers want to know what you specifically said, built, or decided.

Skipping the clarifying question. On scenario questions, jumping straight to an answer without clarifying assumptions looks reactive. A brief clarifying question shows structured thinking.

Over-promising on timelines or capabilities. Solutions Engineers who say 'yes' to everything in a demo or sales call create support nightmares later. Interviewers often test whether you know when to say 'let me confirm that with the team.'

Not preparing questions. Candidates who have no questions for the interviewer appear disengaged. Thoughtful questions about the team, the client base, or the product roadmap consistently leave a positive impression.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the outsidecapital Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report two to four rounds, though the exact structure varies. A first screening call is almost always followed by a technical or product-knowledge round. Some candidates also describe a live demo exercise or a take-home case study before a final stakeholder discussion. Confirm the format with your recruiter after the first call.

Do I need a finance background to apply for this role?

A formal finance degree is not typically required, but you are expected to understand the basics of private credit, fund administration, and investor reporting well enough to speak credibly with clients. Candidates with a technology or engineering background who have worked in fintech or with financial data report getting through successfully when they invest time in learning the domain before interviews.

What technical skills matter most for this interview?

REST APIs, data integration concepts (field mapping, webhooks, authentication), and basic troubleshooting methodology are the most commonly tested areas. You do not need to write production code, but you should be able to read API documentation, interpret an error response, and explain a data flow to both a technical and a non-technical audience.

Is there a live demo or case study in the process?

Candidates frequently report some form of demo or scenario exercise, either live or as a take-home. Typically you are given a brief about a fictional client and asked to walk through how you would handle discovery, configure a demo, or troubleshoot an issue. Practise speaking out loud through your thought process, not just thinking through it silently.
