01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Roko Labs interview process, based on patterns at startups of similar size and stage, typically moves through four to five stages. Exact steps vary by role, so treat this as a general guide.

Stage 1: Application and resume screening. Your resume goes through an initial review by a recruiter or hiring manager. With 11 open roles, the hiring team is not overwhelmed, which means your application is likely to get a genuine read. A well-tailored resume with clear outcomes will pass this stage faster than a dense list of responsibilities.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR call. If shortlisted, you will receive a short introductory call to confirm your availability, notice period, salary expectations, and basic fit. Have a crisp answer ready for 'why Roko Labs' because this question almost always comes up here.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment. For engineering and product roles, this is commonly a take-home assignment or an online test. For other functions, expect a case study or a work sample. Turnaround windows are usually a few days, so clear your schedule when you receive it.

Stage 4: Technical or domain interview. A deeper conversation with a team lead or senior member of the function you are joining. Engineers can expect problem-solving or project walkthrough questions. Non-technical candidates typically face scenario-based questions tied to their domain.

Stage 5: Culture or founders round. Many early-stage companies end with a conversation involving a co-founder or senior leader. This round is less about skills and more about whether you are someone the team wants to work with closely. Come with thoughtful questions about the company's direction and challenges.

Offers at companies of this size typically follow within a week of the final round if things go well.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Roko Labs, like most growth-stage startups, values a specific combination of skills and attitude over a polished but generic resume.

Ownership mindset. Small teams cannot afford people who wait to be told what to do. Hiring managers look for candidates who have taken initiative in past roles, whether that means leading a project without being asked, finding a fix for a broken process, or building something from scratch with limited resources.

Depth in your core skill. This looks different depending on the role. For engineers, it means solid fundamentals and the ability to reason through problems out loud. For product and design candidates, it means a clear understanding of users and trade-offs. For business roles, it means a track record of results, not just activities.

Clear communication. In a small team, everyone works across functions. You need to explain your thinking to engineers, to non-technical stakeholders, and to founders. Candidates who can write and speak clearly have a significant edge.

Comfort with ambiguity. Startups rarely have well-documented playbooks. If your entire background is in large companies with structured processes, be ready to demonstrate with specific examples that you can figure things out independently.

Genuine interest in the mission. Founders at companies like this are deeply invested in the problem they are solving. Candidates who arrive at interviews having actually thought about the product and the market stand out quickly from those who applied broadly.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Do real research on the company. Before any round, go through Roko Labs' website, any public blog posts or announcements, founder LinkedIn profiles, and press coverage you can find. Know what the product does, who it is for, and what problem it is solving. Interviewers at small companies notice almost immediately when a candidate has done genuine homework versus surface-level prep.

Tailor your resume to the specific role. With 11 open positions available, choose the one that best matches your background and rewrite your resume highlights to mirror the language in that job description. Pull out projects and outcomes that are directly relevant, and put them near the top.

Prepare a specific 'why this company' answer. This is not optional at a startup. A vague answer about 'exciting opportunities' will not land. Mention something real: the product problem, a public statement from the founders, or something specific about how the company approaches its market.

Build a bank of structured examples. For behavioral questions, practice answering with a clear structure: here is the situation, here is what I did, and here is the measurable result. Have four to five strong examples ready covering ownership, problem-solving, handling failure, and cross-functional collaboration.

Brush up on technical fundamentals if relevant. For engineering roles, platforms commonly cited by job seekers such as LeetCode are a standard preparation resource. Focus on problem-solving approach and communication during the solution, not just the answer.

Prepare smart questions to ask. At every stage, have two or three questions ready that show you have thought seriously about the role. Ask about team structure, current technical or business challenges, or how success in the role is measured in the first few months.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Roko Labs sits in a space where speed, quality of thinking, and genuine curiosity about the problem matter every day. Based on what is publicly visible about the company, the culture leans toward high ownership, direct communication, and a strong preference for people who figure things out rather than wait for direction.

Small team, high visibility. At a company of this stage, there is no large org structure to disappear into. Your work is visible, your contributions are noticed, and your gaps are equally apparent. If you thrive in environments where what you build actually ships and makes a difference, this is the right kind of place.

Fast learning is expected. Startups in the AI and technology space evolve quickly. The teams that do well tend to be ones where people are constantly picking up new things, whether through side projects, following developments in the field, or simply being curious enough to ask questions. Showing this trait during interviews helps.

Direct feedback is the norm. Smaller teams rarely have the layers of management that soften hard conversations. Expect feedback to be honest and fairly immediate, and be ready to give the same. Candidates who have worked in flat, direct environments adapt more naturally.

The mission is taken seriously. Founding teams at this stage have usually made real sacrifices to build the company. They hire people who share at least some of that seriousness about the problem. You do not need to perform passion, but genuine curiosity about what Roko Labs is building goes a long way.
