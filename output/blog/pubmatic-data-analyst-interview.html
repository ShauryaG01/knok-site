01 Overview

Overview

PubMatic is a global programmatic advertising technology company with significant engineering and analytics presence in India, particularly in Pune. Data Analysts at PubMatic work with large-scale auction and impression data, supporting publisher success teams, product managers, and demand-side partners. The role is SQL-heavy and requires a grasp of ad tech fundamentals like eCPM, fill rate, and bid dynamics.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracks 62 open Data Analyst roles at PubMatic alone, out of 319 Data Analyst openings across all companies on the same date. Bangalore leads with 41 openings, followed by Delhi (22), Mumbai (19), and Hyderabad (14).

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and are specific to PubMatic's ad tech context. Prepare for all of them, not just the SQL-heavy ones.

  1. Walk us through a time you investigated a sudden or unexpected drop in a key metric for a publisher or product team.
  2. Write a SQL query to identify the top publishers by total revenue last month, broken down by ad format.
  3. How would you explain fill rate and win rate to a non-technical account manager who has never looked at raw data?
  4. PubMatic processes a very high volume of bid requests daily. How would you approach aggregating or sampling this data to produce a weekly trend report?
  5. A publisher reports their revenue dropped significantly last week. Walk through your investigation step by step.
  6. What is header bidding and how would you measure its impact on a publisher's overall yield?
  7. Describe a dashboard you built from scratch. What metrics did you choose to highlight and why?
  8. How do you handle missing or null values when working with impression-level event data?
  9. Given a dataset of bid requests and win events, how would you calculate bid-to-win conversion rate by device type and ad format?
  10. How would you design and evaluate an A/B test for a change in floor price logic?
  11. What Python libraries do you rely on for large dataset analysis, and why those over alternatives?
  12. Tell us about a time you disagreed with a stakeholder's interpretation of data and how you handled the conversation.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a time you investigated a drop in a key metric.

*Situation:* At my previous company, the publisher analytics team flagged a significant dip in eCPM across mobile web inventory over a weekend.

*Task:* I was asked to identify the root cause before a client call the following Monday.

*Action:* I pulled impression and revenue data segmented by ad format, device type, and demand partner. Using SQL window functions, I compared day-on-day deltas and found that a specific demand partner had stopped sending bids on Saturday morning. I cross-checked bid request logs to confirm the issue was on the demand side and not a publisher-side tagging problem.

*Result:* We shared a clear root-cause summary with the account team before the call. The publisher was reassured quickly, and the demand partner resolved their configuration by Monday afternoon. The analysis was later reused as a template for future incident investigations.

---

Q: Describe a dashboard you built for a non-technical audience.

*Situation:* Our publisher success team needed a weekly performance view but found the existing reports too technical to act on without data team support.

*Task:* I was asked to design a self-serve dashboard they could use independently.

*Action:* I ran short interviews with several team members to understand which decisions the dashboard needed to support. I then built a view showing fill rate trends, revenue by ad format, and top-performing placements, with plain-English labels and colour-coded alerts for anomalies falling outside normal ranges.

*Result:* The team adopted it in the first week. Ad-hoc reporting requests from that group dropped noticeably, and account managers said they felt more confident going into client meetings with data they could read themselves.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder's data interpretation.

*Situation:* A product manager wanted to roll out a new bidding feature to all publishers based on a report showing strong eCPM uplift.

*Task:* I was reviewing the analysis before the rollout decision was finalised.

*Action:* I noticed the comparison window was only a few days and happened to overlap with a seasonal traffic spike. I rebuilt the analysis using a longer baseline and controlled for the seasonal effect. The uplift was still positive but considerably smaller than originally reported.

*Result:* The product manager appreciated the corrected view and opted for a phased rollout with proper monitoring. That phased approach caught a secondary issue early that would have been difficult to isolate in a full rollout.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Behavioral questions (STAR format): Keep Situation and Task brief, two to three sentences each. Spend most of your time on Action, since that is what interviewers are actually evaluating. Quantify the Result where you genuinely can; if exact numbers are not available, describe the qualitative impact clearly and honestly.

SQL and technical questions: Think aloud as you write. Start by restating the problem, identify the tables and joins you need, then build the query step by step. Mention edge cases (nulls, duplicate rows, timezone offsets) even if not explicitly asked. PubMatic interviewers value candidates who anticipate data quality issues before writing a single line of SQL.

Case study and business questions: Begin with clarifying questions, state your assumptions before diving in, and end with a concrete recommendation. In ad tech case studies, anchor your answer in publisher or advertiser impact rather than abstract data observations. Saying 'this would increase fill rate and therefore revenue for the publisher' is more compelling than a purely technical point.

Ad tech domain questions: When explaining a concept like header bidding or floor pricing, give the simplest version first and add detail only if the interviewer follows up. Avoid assuming they want the most technical explanation possible.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Strong SQL, specifically for analytics use cases: PubMatic interviewers look for comfort with window functions, CTEs, GROUP BY with HAVING, and multi-table joins. Basic SELECT skills are assumed; the bar is set above that.

Ad tech domain awareness: Candidates who can speak to eCPM, fill rate, bid density, and auction mechanics stand out. You do not need prior ad tech experience, but arriving without even a working understanding of these terms is a common disqualifier at the technical round.

Clear communication with non-technical partners: A core part of the role is translating data findings for account managers, product teams, and occasionally clients. Interviewers pay close attention to how clearly you explain your thinking during technical questions, not just whether your answer is correct.

Structured, hypothesis-driven problem-solving: When given an open-ended investigation or case scenario, PubMatic interviewers look for a methodical approach: form a hypothesis, check the data, iterate. Candidates who jump to conclusions without checking their assumptions tend to get challenged hard.

Python for data manipulation: At mid and senior levels, comfort with pandas and basic data wrangling is commonly expected alongside SQL proficiency.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1. Understand PubMatic's product: Spend time learning how their SSP platform and OpenWrap header bidding solution work. Their public blog and case studies are a solid starting point. Interview questions are grounded in their actual product context, so surface-level research is not enough.

Step 2. Practice SQL for ad tech scenarios: Focus on window functions (LAG, LEAD, RANK), revenue rollups, and time-series comparisons. Practice with datasets that resemble impression-level event logs rather than clean transactional tables.

Step 3. Study core ad tech metrics: Know what eCPM, fill rate, win rate, bid density, and floor price mean and how a change in one affects the others. Practice explaining each in one or two plain sentences, as if speaking to an account manager.

Step 4. Prepare five to six STAR stories: Cover at least one investigation scenario, one stakeholder communication challenge, and one case where you caught or corrected a data quality issue. Have a clear, honest Result for each.

Step 5. Refresh Python for analysis: If your Python skills are rusty, review pandas aggregation, merge operations, and handling of missing values. Senior-level questions often involve a coding component alongside SQL.

Step 6. Practice experiment design: Be ready to discuss how you would design an A/B test, choose metrics, and define success criteria. Case studies at PubMatic often involve evaluating the impact of a product or algorithm change.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it as a generic data interview: PubMatic questions are rooted in ad tech. Candidates who prepare only generic SQL and Python without ad tech context often get caught flat-footed when the first question involves eCPM or fill rate.

Going silent during technical rounds: PubMatic interviewers want to follow your thinking. Candidates who work through SQL queries or case studies in silence miss the chance to demonstrate their process and recover from small errors by explaining their reasoning.

Inventing numbers in STAR answers: If you do not remember the exact impact from a past project, say so and describe the outcome qualitatively. Invented metrics are easy to probe with follow-up questions and can undermine your credibility for the rest of the interview.

Skipping data quality checks: When given a dataset or scenario, jumping straight to analysis without asking about nulls, duplicates, or data freshness signals inexperience with production data environments, which is a real concern in ad tech.

Over-explaining to non-technical audiences: When asked to explain a concept to a non-technical person during the interview, give the simple version first. Piling on jargon without checking for understanding is a real-world red flag that interviewers actively watch for.

Not asking clarifying questions in case studies: Diving into an answer without checking your assumptions suggests you would do the same on the job, where ambiguous requirements and messy data are the norm.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the PubMatic Data Analyst interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds. This usually includes a recruiter screening call, a technical round focused on SQL and analytics, sometimes a take-home case study or live case discussion, and a final round with a hiring manager or business stakeholder. Round structure can vary by team and level, so confirm the format with your recruiter when you schedule your first call.

What SQL topics come up most in PubMatic Data Analyst interviews?

Candidates consistently report questions involving window functions (ROW_NUMBER, RANK, LAG, LEAD for time-series work), multi-table joins, GROUP BY with HAVING, and CTEs for readable query structure. Ad tech-specific scenarios, such as revenue rollups by publisher or fill rate calculations by ad format and device type, are common. Practising with event-style datasets that resemble impression logs will prepare you better than generic SQL exercises alone.

Do I need prior ad tech experience to get a Data Analyst role at PubMatic?

Prior ad tech experience is a plus but not always a strict requirement, particularly at entry and mid levels. Candidates who arrive knowing the basics of eCPM, fill rate, and how programmatic auctions work tend to perform better than those relying entirely on transferable skills from other industries. Spending time studying ad tech fundamentals before your interview is one of the highest-return preparation steps you can take.

Is there a take-home assignment in the PubMatic interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home analytical exercise involving a sample dataset and open-ended business questions. Not all interview tracks include this step, so confirm with your recruiter early. If you do receive one, focus on clean, well-commented SQL or Python code, a concise summary of your findings, and a clear recommendation rather than a raw data dump.

What is the salary range for Data Analysts at PubMatic in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Data Analyst salaries across companies in India range from 5-10 LPA at entry level (0-2 years) to 10-18 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 18-30 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). Lead roles can reach 28-45+ LPA. For PubMatic-specific figures, publicly reported numbers on Glassdoor can give you a more precise benchmark to anchor your salary discussion.

How long does the full PubMatic interview process typically take from application to offer?

Candidates report the end-to-end process typically takes two to four weeks, though this varies with team availability and whether a take-home task is included. Following up with your recruiter after each round is standard practice and usually welcome. If you have a competing offer with a deadline, let the recruiter know early so they can try to align timelines.
