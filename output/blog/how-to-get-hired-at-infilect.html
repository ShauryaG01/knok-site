01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Infilect is a Bengaluru-based AI company building computer vision solutions for the retail industry. Their platform helps FMCG brands and modern trade retailers track shelf availability, planogram compliance, and in-store execution using smartphone or store camera images. If you are looking to work at the intersection of deep tech and real-world retail problems, Infilect is worth your attention.

As of mid-2026, Infilect has 8 open roles across engineering, data science, and business functions. The company is in a growth phase, so hiring tends to focus on people who can wear multiple hats and move fast. Most roles are based in Bengaluru, with occasional remote or hybrid arrangements depending on the function.

The hiring process at Infilect is relatively lean compared to large product companies. You can expect 3 to 4 rounds from application to offer, though the exact number can vary by role and seniority. The process is designed to move quickly, and candidates who are responsive and well-prepared tend to get through it smoothly.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The typical Infilect hiring journey looks like this, based on commonly reported candidate experiences.

Stage 1: Application and initial screening. You apply through their careers page or a job portal. If your profile matches, a recruiter or hiring manager reaches out within a week or two for a brief call. This covers your background, why you are interested in Infilect, and a quick check on salary expectations and notice period.

Stage 2: Technical or domain round. For engineering roles, this is usually a coding or system design interview. Machine learning and data science candidates can expect questions on model building, computer vision concepts, and occasionally a take-home assignment. Business and GTM roles typically face a case study or a discussion of past work. This round is usually conducted over video call.

Stage 3: Deep technical or product round. A more thorough technical discussion with a senior team member or the hiring manager. For tech roles, this often involves architecture discussions, ML system design, or a walkthrough of a project you have built. For non-tech roles, this may include a presentation or a structured case discussion. The expectation here is that you can defend your choices, not just describe what you did.

Stage 4: Culture and leadership fit. A conversation with a founder or a senior leader. The focus is on your thinking process, how you handle ambiguity, and whether you are genuinely motivated by the kind of problems Infilect works on. This round tends to be conversational rather than evaluative in the traditional sense.

After this, successful candidates typically receive a verbal offer followed by a formal offer letter. The full process from first contact to offer can take 2 to 4 weeks, though it can move faster for senior or urgent roles.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Infilect is a deep-tech startup, so they value a few things above all else.

Strong technical fundamentals matter a lot, especially for engineering and ML roles. If you are applying for a computer vision or ML position, a solid grip on convolutional neural networks, object detection, and model optimization is expected. Python proficiency is table stakes. Familiarity with frameworks like PyTorch or TensorFlow, and experience deploying models in production, will set you apart from candidates who have only worked on notebooks.

Problem-first thinking is something the team consistently looks for. Infilect works on real retail environments where data is messy and constraints are real. They want people who start with the problem, not the tool. Be ready to discuss how you have navigated ambiguous or resource-constrained situations in past roles or projects.

Ownership and pace are cultural traits the company values. Startup experience helps, but what they really want to see is evidence that you have driven something end to end, not just contributed a slice of it. Stories where you took responsibility for an outcome, good or bad, resonate well.

For business and sales roles, experience in SaaS or enterprise software, especially in the retail or FMCG sector, is a strong plus. Understanding how FMCG sales cycles and distributor networks work will make you a far more credible candidate than someone coming from a different domain.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

If you have an Infilect interview coming up, here is how to make the most of your preparation time.

Understand what Infilect actually does. Spend time on their website and look up any published case studies or press coverage. Understand what planogram compliance means, how computer vision applies to retail shelf analytics, and who their typical customers are. Candidates who can speak about the technology in business context stand out immediately from those who treat it as a generic AI interview.

For ML and computer vision roles, revisit the fundamentals of image classification, object detection (YOLO, Faster RCNN), and segmentation. Be ready to discuss trade-offs between model accuracy and inference speed, which is a real constraint in retail deployments on edge devices. If you have worked on any project involving real-world image data, prepare to walk through it in detail.

For coding rounds, practice on standard platforms with a focus on arrays, graphs, and dynamic programming. System design questions at Infilect tend to focus on ML pipelines and data engineering, so be ready to discuss how you would design a scalable image processing system from ingestion to inference.

Prepare two or three strong project stories. Use a simple structure: what the problem was, what you built, what trade-offs you made, and what the outcome was. Concrete numbers help, but be honest if your data is limited or your sample size was small.

For the culture round, think about why you want to work on retail AI specifically. Generic startup enthusiasm does not land as well as a specific, genuine reason tied to the problem space.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Infilect has the energy of a focused, mission-driven startup. The team is relatively small, which means decisions happen quickly and individuals have direct visibility into how their work affects the product and customers.

Collaboration over hierarchy is the general working style. Founders and senior leaders are accessible, and the expectation is that people raise problems early rather than waiting for a manager to notice. If you are used to a large-company environment where roles are tightly scoped, the breadth of what you might be asked to do can be a welcome change or a challenge depending on your preference.

Customer outcomes drive the work. Because Infilect's product lives in physical stores, there is a tangible connection between what the team builds and what a brand manager or retail merchandiser sees on the ground. That grounding tends to attract people who want their work to have a visible, real-world impact rather than just moving metrics on a dashboard.

Learning is treated as part of the job. Computer vision and retail AI are fast-moving areas. The team tends to value people who read papers, run experiments, and bring new ideas to the table, not just execute on a defined spec. If you are the kind of engineer or analyst who tracks what is happening in the field, you will fit in naturally.

Work arrangements are primarily from the Bengaluru office for most roles, with the day-to-day flexibility that typically comes in a startup environment.
