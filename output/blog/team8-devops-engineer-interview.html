02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Based on candidate reports and Team8's publicly known focus areas, these are the questions most commonly seen in their DevOps Engineer interviews:

  1. Walk us through how you have secured a CI/CD pipeline in a previous role.
  2. How do you manage secrets and credentials across microservices running in a Kubernetes cluster?
  3. Describe your infrastructure-as-code experience. Which tools have you used, and what drove those choices?
  4. How would you design a zero-downtime deployment strategy for a critical production service?
  5. Tell us about a time you responded to a production incident. What was your process from detection to resolution?
  6. How do you handle container image security scanning and vulnerability management in your build process?
  7. What is your experience with cloud cost optimisation, and how have you reduced infrastructure spend?
  8. How do you prevent and detect configuration drift between dev, staging, and production environments?
  9. Describe how you would set up monitoring and alerting for a brand-new microservice from scratch.
  10. How do you balance developer velocity with security and compliance requirements?
  11. Tell us about a time you had to get a development team to adopt a DevOps practice they were reluctant about.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioral questions. Here are three worked examples relevant to Team8's focus areas.

Q: Walk us through how you secured a CI/CD pipeline in a previous role.

*Situation:* Our team was shipping code using shared credentials stored as plain text in a config file, and we had an upcoming compliance audit.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the pipeline to meet our new security policy before the audit deadline.

*Action:* I moved all secrets into HashiCorp Vault with short-lived dynamic credentials. I rebuilt the pipeline in GitHub Actions so it pulled credentials at runtime, ran SAST scans and container image vulnerability checks on every pull request, and required two reviewer approvals before any merge to main. I also added branch protection rules and enforced signed commits.

*Result:* We passed the compliance audit with no findings on the pipeline. Candidates report that showing both the 'what' and the 'why' behind each decision lands well in Team8 technical screens.

Q: Tell us about a time you responded to a production incident.

*Situation:* Our API gateway began returning connection errors for a subset of users shortly after a config change was pushed on a Friday evening.

*Task:* As the on-call engineer I had to identify the root cause and restore service as quickly as possible.

*Action:* I pulled logs from our centralised logging stack and traced the errors to a misconfigured timeout value in the gateway config. I rolled back the change using our GitOps workflow, confirmed traffic normalised in the dashboards, and wrote a post-mortem. I also added a config validation step to the pipeline to catch that class of error before it could reach production again.

*Result:* Service was restored within the hour. The validation step has since caught similar misconfigurations before they reached production.

Q: How do you balance developer velocity with security requirements?

*Situation:* A product team complained that our mandatory security scans were significantly slowing their build pipeline and blocking releases.

*Task:* I needed to keep the security gates in place without creating friction that would push the team to work around them.

*Action:* I audited which scans ran on every commit versus which ones truly needed to block a merge. I moved heavy dependency audits to a nightly scheduled run and kept only critical-severity checks as hard pipeline gates. I also set up a weekly digest for the team showing scan results so they could fix issues proactively rather than being surprised at merge time.

*Result:* Build times dropped noticeably, the team stopped escalating complaints, and the critical-severity findings backlog was cleared within a quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical design questions (like 'design a monitoring setup from scratch'), use the PDAC structure: Problem, Decisions, Actions, Checks. Open by restating the goal in your own words, walk through the key design decisions and the reasoning behind them, describe what you would implement, and close by explaining how you would verify it works. This shows structured thinking that Team8 interviewers typically value.

For behavioral questions, stick to STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief and spend most of your time on Action, since that is where your actual skills show. Always close with a concrete Result, even if it is qualitative.

For security-specific questions, frame your answers around defence in depth and show you think in layers. Mention threat modelling, least-privilege access, automated scanning, and audit trails. Team8's cybersecurity background means interviewers notice when candidates treat security as an afterthought rather than a built-in concern.

For cost and optimisation questions, lead with the business impact first, then the technical approach. Saying 'we needed to reduce cloud spend to hit our margin target' before explaining autoscaling or spot instances shows commercial awareness alongside technical skill.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Security-first thinking. Team8 builds security companies, so they expect DevOps candidates to treat security as a core engineering concern, not a checklist item. Interviewers typically look for candidates who bring up secrets management, image scanning, and least-privilege access without being prompted.

Infrastructure ownership. They want engineers who treat infrastructure as code, version it, review it, and own its reliability end-to-end. Candidates who describe manual console changes tend to score lower than those who describe Terraform or Pulumi workflows with proper state management.

Incident response depth. Candidates report that Team8 interviewers probe how you behave under pressure. They want to see a clear detection-to-resolution process, a habit of writing post-mortems, and evidence that you close the feedback loop in the pipeline after incidents.

Cross-team communication. DevOps at Team8 involves working closely with security engineers and product developers. Interviewers look for candidates who can explain technical trade-offs in plain language and who have experience getting buy-in from non-DevOps stakeholders.

Curiosity about the threat landscape. Given Team8's domain, candidates who follow security news, understand CVEs in the tools they use, and can speak to recent cloud or supply-chain security incidents tend to stand out in the interview.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Candidates typically get one to two weeks between a recruiter screen and a technical interview. Here is a realistic plan split across two phases.

Phase one: shore up core skills.

Spend the first few days revisiting any gaps in your Kubernetes and cloud knowledge. Focus on areas you use less often: RBAC configuration, network policies, pod security standards, and managed identity setups on AWS or GCP. Write or redeploy a small Terraform module from scratch to refresh your IaC muscle memory.

From mid-week onward, focus on security tooling. Read up on how tools like Trivy, Checkov, or OPA are used in CI pipelines. If you have not used HashiCorp Vault, work through a short hands-on lab. Practise explaining your past security decisions out loud so they feel natural, not rehearsed.

Phase two: interview-ready practice.

Early in the second week, prepare three to five STAR stories from your actual experience. Cover incidents, pipeline improvements, and a cross-team collaboration challenge. Write them out, then practise saying them aloud until each runs under two minutes and feels natural.

Mid-way through, do at least two mock technical interviews. Ask a colleague to pose a live design question such as 'design a secure CI/CD pipeline for a new microservice' and time yourself. Record any knowledge gaps and fill them before the actual interview.

In the final two days before the interview, research Team8's portfolio companies and any recent news about their work. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about their infrastructure challenges and team culture. Review your notes lightly and get good sleep.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating security as optional. Candidates who answer infrastructure design questions without mentioning security controls consistently score lower at Team8. Always weave in access control, secrets management, and audit logging naturally, not as an afterthought at the end.

Vague STAR stories. Saying 'I improved our deployment process' is not enough. Interviewers want to know what was broken, what you specifically did, and what changed as a result. Practise naming the tools, the specific actions you took, and the concrete outcome.

Over-claiming solo work. Team8 values collaboration. Describing everything as 'I built' and 'I fixed' without mentioning teammates or cross-team dynamics can raise flags. Use 'we' where appropriate and explain your specific contribution within the team context.

Skipping the 'why'. Explaining what you did is only half the answer. Interviewers typically probe for your reasoning: why that tool, why that architecture, why that approach over the alternatives. Practise articulating trade-offs out loud before the interview.

Arriving with no questions. Having no questions for the interviewer signals low interest. Prepare specific questions about Team8's tech stack, their incident response culture, or how security requirements flow into their DevOps processes.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does Team8 typically have for a DevOps Engineer role?

Candidates typically report a recruiter screen, a technical interview covering infrastructure and DevOps concepts, and a final round that may include a system design discussion or a culture-fit conversation. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and seniority level. Confirm the current format with your recruiter when you receive the call, as processes change over time.

Does Team8 give a take-home assignment for DevOps roles?

Some candidates report a short take-home or live coding exercise involving infrastructure as code or a pipeline setup. Others report purely interview-based rounds with no assignment at all. Ask your recruiter upfront whether a practical task is part of the process so you can prepare the right way.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer role at Team8?

Team8 does not publish band data publicly. Based on publicly reported market data for India, mid-level DevOps Engineers (3-5 years of experience) typically see offers in the 15-28 LPA range, and senior engineers (6-9 years) in the 30-50 LPA range. Actual Team8 offers may differ. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for specific data points from candidates who have gone through their process recently.

How important is Kubernetes knowledge for a Team8 DevOps interview?

Candidates consistently report that Kubernetes comes up in nearly every technical screen. Be comfortable with deployments, services, RBAC, network policies, and how you handle secrets inside a cluster. Given Team8's security focus, pod security standards and image signing are worth knowing beyond the basics.

Is prior cybersecurity experience required for a DevOps role at Team8?

Dedicated security certifications are not typically required, but a security-minded approach to infrastructure is expected. Candidates who can speak to secrets management, vulnerability scanning in CI pipelines, and least-privilege IAM design tend to do better than those who treat security as someone else's responsibility. A solid grasp of common cloud misconfigurations goes a long way in the interview.
