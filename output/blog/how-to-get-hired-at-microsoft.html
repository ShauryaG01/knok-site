01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Microsoft is one of the most sought-after employers in the tech world, and it actively hires in India across engineering, product, sales, and operations. As of July 2026, knok's job radar shows 71 open roles at Microsoft, spanning software engineering, program management, data science, and more.

Getting hired here is competitive but structured. Microsoft follows a well-defined hiring process that values both technical depth and cultural alignment. The company has large engineering hubs in Hyderabad and Bengaluru, so Indian candidates have genuine on-ground opportunities, not just remote-only openings.

If you are aiming for Microsoft, the good news is that the process is transparent and predictable. You know what to expect at each stage, which means solid preparation goes a long way.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Microsoft's hiring process typically runs through four to five stages, though the exact number varies by team and role.

Application and resume screen: Your resume is reviewed against the job description. Applicants whose experience and skills clearly match the role tend to move forward. Some teams include an online assessment at this point for technical roles.

Online assessment (for tech roles): Candidates for software engineering and related positions commonly receive a coding test with two to three algorithm or data structure problems to solve within a fixed time window.

Recruiter call: A short conversation with an HR recruiter to confirm your interest, discuss your background at a high level, and explain what comes next. Use this time to ask about the team, role scope, and the expected timeline.

Technical interviews: This is the core of the process. You typically go through two to four technical rounds. These cover coding problems, system design (for senior roles), and sometimes domain-specific questions. Microsoft interviewers are known to focus on how you approach and reason through a problem, not just the final answer.

As Appropriate interview: Microsoft uses a final round called the 'As Appropriate' interview, conducted by a senior leader or partner-level employee. This focuses on your overall work approach, how you handle ambiguity, and your cultural fit.

Offer and negotiation: Once you clear all rounds, the recruiter shares a formal offer covering base salary, bonus, and stock. Compensation at Microsoft is publicly reported to be competitive in the Indian tech market.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Microsoft looks for a specific combination of skills and mindset, not just technical ability.

Technical depth: For engineering roles, strong fundamentals in data structures, algorithms, and system design are essential. You should be comfortable writing clean, working code under interview conditions.

Growth mindset: This is not just a buzzword at Microsoft. It is a core part of how the company operates. Interviewers look for candidates who are curious, willing to learn from mistakes, and open to feedback. Saying 'I don't know, but here is how I would find out' is valued more than bluffing.

Collaboration and real impact: Microsoft wants people who work well across teams and can show tangible results from past work. Be ready to discuss specific projects, the problems you solved, and how your contributions affected the team or product.

Clear communication: The ability to explain complex ideas in plain terms matters a lot, especially in program management and senior engineering roles. Practise walking through your thinking out loud during technical rounds.

Customer focus: Across all roles, Microsoft values people who keep the end user in mind. Whether you are writing code or planning a product launch, showing that you think about real-world impact will stand out.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparation for Microsoft works best when done in layers, starting broad and going deeper as your interview date gets closer.

Build your coding foundation first: Focus on arrays, strings, trees, graphs, dynamic programming, and recursion. Consistent daily practice on coding platforms over several weeks is more effective than last-minute cramming.

Study system design separately: If you are applying for a senior or principal-level role, system design is a major part of the interview. Study how large-scale systems such as distributed databases, messaging queues, and APIs are designed. Think about trade-offs, not just architecture diagrams.

Use the STAR method for behavioural questions: Structure your answers as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Microsoft's behavioural questions often tie back to growth mindset, collaboration, and customer impact. Prepare three to five strong stories from your past work.

Research the team and product area: Microsoft has many divisions, from Azure to Xbox to LinkedIn. Understanding which business unit you are interviewing for and what their products do will help you ask sharper questions and give more relevant answers.

Do mock interviews out loud: Practising with a friend or in a structured session helps you get comfortable explaining your thought process as you go, which is exactly what Microsoft interviewers are assessing.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Microsoft's culture has shifted significantly in recent years, moving from a competitive internal ranking environment toward one that genuinely encourages collaboration and continuous learning.

Growth mindset is the foundation: The idea, drawn from psychologist Carol Dweck's research, is that abilities can be developed through effort and learning. Microsoft hires for this and evaluates performance against it. Employees who treat setbacks as learning opportunities tend to thrive here.

One Microsoft, not siloed teams: Microsoft actively encourages employees to collaborate across divisions. You are expected to share knowledge, support colleagues in other teams, and think about company-wide outcomes, not just your own team's targets.

Inclusion and diversity: Microsoft has publicly reported strong commitments to diversity in hiring and promotion. Employee resource groups are active and well-supported across the India offices.

Hybrid work norms: Most roles in India follow a hybrid model, combining in-office and remote days. Specific policies vary by team, so it is worth clarifying during the recruiter call.

Performance culture: Microsoft uses a model called 'Connects' for performance reviews, focusing on your individual contributions, how you enabled others, and results delivered. It is less about ranking against peers and more about your own growth trajectory.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the hiring process at Microsoft take?

The timeline varies by role and team, but candidates commonly report a process that spans several weeks from the recruiter call to the final offer. Online assessments and technical rounds are sometimes scheduled close together to keep momentum. It is perfectly fine to ask your recruiter for an expected timeline at the very start of the process.

Does Microsoft hire freshers from Indian colleges?

Yes, Microsoft recruits from a range of engineering colleges in India, including IITs, NITs, and other top institutions, through campus placement programmes. Off-campus applications for freshers are also accepted through the official careers portal. Internship roles are a common and well-regarded entry point for students in their pre-final or final year.

What is the 'As Appropriate' interview and how should I prepare for it?

The 'As Appropriate' or 'AA' round is a final interview with a senior Microsoft leader, used to make the overall hiring decision holistic. It focuses less on technical depth and more on your values, how you collaborate, and how you handle difficult situations. Preparing strong behavioural stories using the STAR method and having a clear understanding of Microsoft's core principles will serve you well in this round.

Is it better to apply directly on Microsoft's careers site or through a job portal?

Applying directly on Microsoft's official careers portal is generally the most reliable route, as recruiters actively manage applications there. Referrals from current Microsoft employees carry significant weight and can move your application up the queue. LinkedIn is also commonly used by Microsoft recruiters for sourcing, so keeping your profile current and keyword-rich matters.

What coding language should I use in Microsoft interviews?

Microsoft generally allows you to code in the language you are most comfortable with, and Python, Java, C++, and C# are all commonly accepted. The focus is on your logic and approach to the problem, not on language syntax. If the role is tied to a specific stack, such as a .NET position, familiarity with that ecosystem is a useful bonus.

Can I reapply to Microsoft after not clearing the interviews?

Yes, Microsoft allows reapplication after a waiting period that is publicly reported to be around six months to a year, depending on the role and the stage at which you were not selected. Use the time in between to work on the areas where you received feedback. Applying to a different but related role where your skills are now a stronger match is also worth considering.
