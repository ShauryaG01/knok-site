01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Composio is a developer tools startup building the infrastructure layer for AI agents. Their platform helps developers connect large language models to real-world tools, APIs, and services, making it easier to build production-ready AI applications. As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 30 open roles at Composio, a clear signal of active expansion across multiple functions.

If you are an engineer, product thinker, or go-to-market professional with genuine interest in the AI ecosystem, Composio is worth a close look. The company sits at a busy intersection: developer experience, LLM tooling, and API infrastructure. Hiring at this size tends to be selective, because each person added to a small team carries real weight. Getting in early at a company like this can shape your career in meaningful ways through 2027 and beyond.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Based on publicly shared candidate experiences and patterns common at AI-focused startups of this size, here is what the Composio interview process typically looks like. Processes at early-stage companies can shift quickly, so treat each stage as a likely checkpoint rather than a fixed sequence.

Application and Resume Screen
Your resume goes through an initial review by the talent team or a hiring manager. Relevant experience in backend engineering, AI tooling, or developer platforms is what they are scanning for. A strong GitHub profile or public side projects can help you clear this stage.

Recruiter or Hiring Manager Call
If shortlisted, expect a short introductory call to discuss your background, your interest in Composio, and what kind of work you are looking for. This is also your first chance to ask questions about the role and team structure.

Technical Screening
Engineering candidates typically face a coding round focused on data structures, algorithms, or a practical take-home problem. Some candidates publicly report LeetCode-style questions; others describe a problem closer to what the role actually involves, such as designing a small API integration or building a simple tool-calling flow.

Technical Deep Dive or System Design
More senior roles include a system design discussion. You might be asked to walk through how you would build a scalable webhook system, an OAuth integration layer, or a tool-calling framework for LLMs. For product or growth roles, this stage often looks like a case study or a product thinking exercise instead.

Team Fit and Culture Interview
One or more team members assess collaboration style, communication, and how you handle ambiguity. Expect questions around ownership, past failures, and what specifically draws you to working on AI tooling.

Founder or Leadership Round
At startups of Composio's size, many candidates at the final stage speak directly with a founder or a senior leader. They want to understand whether you share the company's vision and whether you can grow alongside the team.

Offer
Offers typically include a base salary, equity (ESOPs are commonly offered for India-based hires), and standard benefits. Use Glassdoor and levels.fyi data for comparable AI startup roles as your benchmark when negotiating.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Composio hires builders. Across roles, a few qualities come up consistently.

AI curiosity paired with strong fundamentals. You do not need a research background in ML, but you should understand how tool calling, function calling, and agent loops work in practice. Candidates who have explored the OpenAI or Anthropic APIs in personal projects tend to stand out.

Ownership over handoffs. At a startup of this size, problems do not get passed along easily. They want people who see a gap, take responsibility, and ship a fix without waiting for permission.

Clear technical communication. Developer tools live by their documentation and developer experience. Being able to explain complex systems simply is as valued as being able to build them.

Relevant technical depth. Depending on the role, this could mean Python and backend development, familiarity with authentication flows like OAuth, experience with distributed systems, or a solid working knowledge of how modern AI pipelines are structured.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product before your first call. Go through the Composio documentation and quickstart guides, then build something small using their platform. Candidates who have actually used the tool during an interview tend to be remembered, because most applicants skip this step entirely.

Refresh your core fundamentals. For engineering roles, practice REST API design, async system patterns, and writing clean and maintainable code. For senior roles, be ready to discuss system design tradeoffs with concrete examples.

Know the AI agent landscape. Understand why tool calling is a meaningful problem for developers building LLM-powered applications. Being able to answer 'why Composio rather than rolling your own integration layer' signals that you have thought carefully about the space.

Prepare a specific 'why Composio' story. Generic answers about wanting to work in AI do not land well at startups. Be precise: what draws you to developer tooling, what excites you about the agent ecosystem in 2026, and what you would want to build if you joined.

Have a project or portfolio ready. A GitHub repository with a project that uses an LLM API, or builds directly on Composio, is a strong differentiator. Push it before your interview, not after.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Composio operates at the pace typical of an early-stage AI startup. The team is technical and product-focused, and the culture rewards people who move fast, ship often, and take responsibility for outcomes rather than just tasks.

Builder culture. The team tends to be made up of people who enjoy making things work, whether that is a clean API design, a well-documented integration, or solving a tricky edge case in an agent loop. If you enjoy tinkering and shipping, the environment fits.

Direct feedback culture. At a company of this size, feedback tends to be immediate and candid. Expect straightforward conversations about what is working and what is not, both about the product and about your own growth.

Mission-driven work. Composio is building infrastructure that is becoming part of how developers globally build AI products. For many on the team, being early to that wave is a motivating factor that goes well beyond the role itself.
