01 Overview

Overview

Tesco Technology India builds the digital backbone for one of the world's largest retailers, running systems that serve millions of customers across grocery, payments, logistics, and supply chain. Their primary India engineering hub is in Bangalore, where teams work on full-stack, data, platform, and mobile products used across multiple markets.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and Tesco's publicly known engineering values. Exact questions vary by team and level.

  1. Walk us through a system you built that handled high traffic or large data volumes. What trade-offs did you make?
  2. Tesco promotions can spike traffic significantly overnight. How would you design a promotions or pricing service to stay reliable under that load?
  3. Tell me about a time you improved the reliability or performance of a production system. What was the measurable impact?
  4. How do you write code that other engineers will maintain comfortably two or three years from now?
  5. Describe a situation where you disagreed with a technical decision made by your team. How did you handle it without damaging the relationship?
  6. Tesco's platforms serve customers across multiple countries and currencies. How have you handled localisation or multi-tenancy in your past work?
  7. Walk me through how you would debug a latency spike in a microservice that appears only under load, not in staging.
  8. How do you decide when to refactor existing code versus shipping the next feature?
  9. Tell me about a time you had to deliver under tight deadline pressure. What did you cut, and how did you justify that call?
  10. How do you protect customer data in the systems you build, beyond just following a checklist?
  11. Describe your experience with CI/CD pipelines and how you improved deployment reliability or speed on a past team.
  12. Tesco's values include putting customers first. Give an example of a time you made a technical decision specifically to improve the end-user experience.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you improved the reliability or performance of a production system.

*Situation:* Our checkout service was timing out for a small subset of users during peak evening hours, causing failed orders.

*Task:* I owned the investigation and fix while the rest of the team was mid-sprint on a new feature.

*Action:* I added distributed tracing to isolate the slow path, found a synchronous call to a third-party address validation API with no timeout set, and replaced it with an async pattern with a hard timeout and a graceful fallback. I also wired up an alert so we would catch similar regressions early.

*Result:* Timeouts dropped to near zero after the fix went live. The alert has fired twice since, and both times we caught the issue before it reached users.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you disagreed with a technical decision made by your team.

*Situation:* My team decided to move a critical batch job to a serverless function to reduce infrastructure cost.

*Task:* I believed cold-start latency would cause the job to miss its SLA window during high-load periods, but I was the only one raising the concern.

*Action:* Instead of just objecting, I built a quick load test that simulated the worst-case invocation pattern and shared the results in our design review. I proposed a hybrid approach: keep the warm container for the SLA-critical run and use serverless for the lower-priority backfill.

*Result:* The team adopted the hybrid approach. The SLA-critical run has not missed its window since, and we still reduced infrastructure cost on the backfill portion.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to deliver under tight deadline pressure. What did you cut?

*Situation:* We had a hard go-live date for a new loyalty points feature tied to a marketing campaign.

*Task:* A couple of weeks before launch we were behind on three sub-features, and I had to decide what shipped and what did not.

*Action:* I mapped each sub-feature to its customer-facing value and backend risk. One feature, bulk points upload for partners, had no users at launch since partner onboarding was not yet complete. I flagged that to the product manager, got agreement to defer it, and redirected that engineer to stabilise the core earn-and-redeem flow.

*Result:* We launched on time with the core flow stable. The deferred feature shipped a few weeks later without incident. The product manager said it was the clearest scope call they had seen on the team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions, structure your answer in four steps: clarify scale and constraints first (ask about expected load, data size, latency targets), then sketch the high-level components, then call out the one or two hardest trade-offs, and finally explain how you would monitor and operate the system in production. Tesco interviewers care about that last part more than most companies do.

For coding questions, think out loud before writing a single line. State the brute-force approach and its complexity, then work toward the optimal solution. If you get stuck, say what you know and what you are trying to figure out. Silence reads as confusion; narration reads as a structured thinker.

For behavioural questions, use a tight STAR format. Keep Situation and Task short (two to three sentences combined) so you spend most of your time on Action and Result. Quantify the Result whenever possible, but if you do not have a precise figure, say something like 'latency dropped noticeably and we had no repeat incidents' rather than inventing a number.

For values questions, pick examples where your technical decision had a direct, visible impact on a real user, not just an internal metric. Tesco interviewers respond well to answers that name the specific person or user group who benefited.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Customer-first thinking. Tesco is a retailer first, a tech company second. Interviewers want engineers who link their technical decisions back to the customer experience. Saying 'this reduced latency' lands better when you add 'which meant checkout completed before the user lost patience on a slow mobile connection.'

Ownership over process. Candidates who say 'I raised the issue in stand-up' score lower than candidates who say 'I owned the fix end to end, including the post-mortem and the runbook update.' Show that you follow through.

Comfort with scale and ambiguity. Tesco's systems handle millions of transactions. Interviewers probe whether you have thought about failure modes, back-pressure, and degraded-mode behaviour, not just the happy path.

Collaboration without ego. Multiple rounds will surface how you handle disagreement and feedback. Being right matters less than being someone the team can work with under pressure.

Alignment with Tesco values. Tesco publicly states values around customer focus and respecting people. Candidates report that at least one round tests this directly, so prepare a values-aligned example in advance.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Foundations
Revisit core data structures and algorithms at the level expected for your target band. For mid and senior roles, candidates report that graph traversal, dynamic programming, and sliding window problems come up most often. Practice on a timed basis, not just for correctness.

Week 2: System design
Study distributed systems concepts relevant to retail at scale: inventory management, cart and checkout flows, search and recommendation pipelines, and payment processing. For each, practice drawing a high-level design and then stress-testing it with failure scenarios.

Week 3: Behavioural prep
Write out six to eight STAR stories from your own experience covering: delivering under pressure, handling disagreement, improving reliability, putting users first, and mentoring or being mentored. Tesco's values rounds are structured, so rehearsed stories make a real difference.

Week 4: Tesco-specific research
Read Tesco Technology's public engineering blog posts and any publicly available talks from their engineers. Understand the scale they operate at and the problems they have written about publicly. Tailor one or two of your STAR stories to reflect those themes.

Ongoing: Track applications carefully. Tesco currently has 3 open Software Engineer roles in India, and new roles can appear and fill quickly. Staying on top of openings across multiple sites takes time you could spend preparing.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping straight to code. Candidates who start typing without clarifying requirements or stating their approach are regularly flagged in feedback. Spend a couple of minutes clarifying before you write anything.

Generic behavioural answers. Saying 'I am a team player who loves collaboration' without a specific story is the fastest way to lose points in a values round. Every claim needs an example.

Ignoring failure modes in design. Many candidates design the happy path beautifully and cannot answer 'what happens if the payment service is down?' Tesco operates at a scale where partial failures are normal. Design for them.

Overselling salary expectations without market data. Candidates sometimes quote figures they cannot back up. Know what Glassdoor and levels.fyi publicly report for your level before the compensation conversation.

Not asking any questions. Every round ends with 'do you have questions for us?' Candidates who say 'no, I think I am good' leave a weak impression. Prepare two or three specific, thoughtful questions per round, ideally about the team's technical challenges or how they measure engineering quality.

Treating the values round as less important. Candidates who prepare thoroughly for coding and system design but wing the behavioural round often get rejected at the final stage. Tesco is known for taking values alignment seriously.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does Tesco's Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: a recruiter screening call, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final behavioural or values round. The exact number varies by level and team. Senior and lead roles commonly include an additional system design deep-dive.

What salary can I expect as a Software Engineer at Tesco India?

Based on knok data for Software Engineer roles across India, entry-level engineers (0-2 years) fall in the 6-12 LPA range, mid-level (3-5 years) 15-25 LPA, senior (6-9 years) 28-45 LPA, and lead or staff engineers 40-65+ LPA. These are market-wide ranges, not Tesco-specific. Use Glassdoor and levels.fyi for additional data points specific to Tesco India before your compensation conversation.

Does Tesco ask competitive programming style questions or more practical coding problems?

Candidates report a mix: some rounds use standard DSA problems such as arrays, graphs, and dynamic programming, while others lean toward practical problems like designing a rate limiter or writing a caching layer. Senior roles tend to weight system design more heavily than pure algorithmic questions. Prepare for both styles to avoid surprises.

How important are Tesco's values in the interview process?

Very important, candidates report. Tesco publicly emphasises customer focus and treating people well, and at least one round is typically structured around these values. Interviewers are looking for specific examples from your past work, not generic statements. Prepare two or three STAR stories that directly connect a technical decision to a customer or colleague outcome.

Is there a take-home assignment in Tesco's interview process?

Some candidates report a take-home coding task, particularly at mid and senior levels, though this is not universal. The task typically asks you to build a small feature or review and improve existing code. If you get one, treat code quality, test coverage, and a clear README as equally important as a working solution.
