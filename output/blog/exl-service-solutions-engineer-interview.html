01 Overview

Overview

EXL Service is a data analytics and digital operations company that partners with clients in insurance, banking, healthcare, and utilities. A Solutions Engineer here sits at the crossroads of technical depth and client-facing communication: you understand a client's pain points, design a data or process solution, and work with delivery teams to bring it to life.

As of July 2026, EXL had 398 open roles listed across India. The Solutions Engineer role had 1,270 openings nationwide, with Bangalore leading at 55 postings, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5).

The interview process typically runs three to four rounds. Candidates report a recruiter screen first, followed by one or two rounds focused on technical knowledge or case studies, and a final conversation with a senior leader. EXL panels consistently test both domain knowledge and your ability to explain complex solutions clearly to non-technical audiences.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

The questions below reflect patterns candidates report across EXL Solutions Engineer interviews. Expect a blend of behavioural, technical, and case-based questions.

  1. Tell me about a time you turned a vague client problem into a concrete technical proposal. EXL values the ability to bring structure to ambiguity, especially in pre-sales contexts.
  1. How do you decide when to recommend a rules-based automation versus a machine learning model? Interviewers want to see that you match the solution to the problem, not just default to AI for everything.
  1. Walk us through how you would approach solutioning for an insurance client that wants to reduce claims processing time. Insurance is one of EXL's core verticals, and domain-specific thinking impresses panels.
  1. Describe a situation where you had conflicting requirements from a technical team and a business stakeholder. How did you resolve it? Solutions Engineers are bridges between delivery and the client, so this comes up often.
  1. How do you build a proof-of-concept for a client in a data-sensitive industry like healthcare or banking? Expect follow-up questions on data governance and compliance.
  1. EXL competes with other analytics and BPO vendors. How would you differentiate EXL's offering to a skeptical client? Know EXL's positioning: analytics-led operations, not just cost arbitrage.
  1. Tell me about a time you identified an upsell or expansion opportunity with an existing client. EXL grows through land-and-expand. They want to see commercial awareness.
  1. How do you structure a solution presentation for a C-suite audience that is not deeply technical? Storytelling and business-value framing matter here.
  1. Many of EXL's clients have legacy systems. How do you design solutions that integrate with older infrastructure? Practical solutioning, not just greenfield thinking.
  1. Describe a time you did not win a deal or lost a client's trust. What did you learn? Self-awareness and a growth mindset are valued at EXL.
  1. How do you stay current with AI and analytics trends relevant to insurance, banking, or healthcare? Mention specific sources: industry reports, vendor blogs, certifications.
  1. Walk us through how you would scope a digital transformation engagement for a mid-sized bank, from first conversation to proposal. This tests your end-to-end solutioning process.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you turned a vague client problem into a concrete technical proposal.

*Situation:* A regional insurance company approached my team saying they wanted to 'improve operational efficiency' but could not articulate specifics. The stakeholders were business leaders, not technical people.

*Task:* My job was to run a discovery process, identify measurable pain points, and return with a scoped proposal within two weeks.

*Action:* I scheduled structured interviews with their operations head, IT lead, and frontline claims supervisors separately. I mapped their claims journey end-to-end, identified three bottlenecks with measurable cycle times, and proposed a phased solution: first an RPA layer for data entry tasks, then a predictive model for fraud flagging. I wrote the proposal in plain business language with a one-page executive summary for the sponsors.

*Result:* The client approved Phase 1 within a month. The proposal structure later became an internal template my team used for similar insurance engagements.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you had conflicting requirements from a technical team and a business stakeholder.

*Situation:* During a healthcare analytics project, the delivery team said a real-time dashboard would take three months to build. The client's VP wanted it live in six weeks for a board presentation.

*Task:* I had to manage both sides without overpromising to the client or demoralising the delivery team.

*Action:* I proposed a 'fast-track lite' version: a static but visually polished report ready in four weeks, with a clear commitment to move to live dashboards after the board meeting. I documented this in a scope amendment so there were no surprises later. I also joined the delivery team's sprint planning to flag which features were truly board-critical.

*Result:* The client presented on time and was satisfied. The live dashboard shipped six weeks later and the account expanded the following quarter.

---

Q: How do you build a proof-of-concept for a client in a data-sensitive industry?

*Situation:* A banking client wanted to see an AI-based loan default prediction model before committing to a full contract. They were cautious about sharing real customer data.

*Task:* I needed to demonstrate value without putting their data at risk or creating compliance concerns.

*Action:* I proposed using a synthetic dataset generated from publicly available lending data to build the POC. I walked their legal and IT security teams through the data-handling approach upfront, shared a draft data-processing agreement, and ran the POC inside their own sandbox environment. I kept the demo focused on model accuracy and explainability, not just the underlying technology stack.

*Result:* Their security team approved the approach quickly. The POC showed strong results, the client moved to a paid pilot, and I documented the process so future banking POCs at our firm could follow the same playbook.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use the STAR format for all behavioural questions. Every answer needs a Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined) and spend most of your time on Action and Result. EXL interviewers specifically want to hear what YOU did, not what the team did collectively.

For case or solutioning questions, follow this structure:

  1. Clarify the business objective first. Ask one or two scoping questions before diving in.
  2. Map the current state. Show you understand the client's existing process or pain point.
  3. Identify the root cause or gap. Do not jump straight to a solution.
  4. Propose a phased approach. EXL clients typically want quick wins followed by deeper transformation.
  5. Quantify the value. Even directional estimates are better than none, as long as you frame them clearly as approximate.
  6. Acknowledge risks and how you would mitigate them.

For technical questions about AI or analytics, lead with the business context before the technology. Explain why you chose an approach, not just what it does.

For competitor differentiation questions, avoid speaking negatively about rivals. Focus on EXL's specific strengths: analytics depth in regulated industries, domain-trained models, and the combination of operations and technology on a single platform.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

EXL Solutions Engineer panels are typically looking for four things above all else.

Business and domain fluency. EXL's clients are in insurance, banking, healthcare, and utilities. If you can speak their language and show genuine familiarity with claims workflows, underwriting, patient journeys, or utility billing, you will stand out immediately. Generic analytics knowledge is table stakes.

Client-handling maturity. Solutions Engineers at EXL are often the face of the company during pre-sales and early delivery. Panels want evidence that you stay calm under pressure, manage expectations honestly, and can hold a room with senior client stakeholders.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Many questions are deliberately open-ended. Interviewers want to see you ask clarifying questions, organise your thoughts before answering, and arrive at a logical recommendation. Jumping straight to answers signals weak consulting instincts.

Commercial awareness. EXL expects Solutions Engineers to contribute to revenue growth, not just delivery. Be ready to discuss upsells, expansions, or moments when you helped grow an account.

Candidates also report that EXL panels pay close attention to communication style. Being able to explain a machine learning model in plain language to a non-technical client is valued as much as knowing how the model actually works.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Phase 1: Company and domain foundation

Read EXL's recent annual report and investor presentations. Note which industries they highlight and any recent partnerships or acquisitions. Study one EXL industry vertical in depth, ideally the one closest to your own background. Understand the key operational pain points in that vertical so you can reference them naturally in your answers.

Phase 2: Role-specific question practice

Work through the 12 questions listed above out loud or with a peer. Record yourself once and listen back for filler words and for whether your STAR answers have a clear, specific Result. Prepare two to three strong stories from your own experience that can flex across multiple question types.

Phase 3: Case and solutioning drills

Practice walking through a full solutioning scenario from client brief to proposal outline. If you work with analytics platforms, RPA tools, or cloud data stacks, refresh your talking points so you can handle technical follow-ups confidently.

Before the interview

Research the specific team and, if possible, the hiring manager. Check recent EXL news on financial portals. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for the panel about what success looks like in the role and how the team is structured. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure tech interview. Solutions Engineering at EXL is a client-facing, commercial role. Candidates who focus entirely on algorithms or system design often underperform on the case and behavioural rounds.

Being vague about results. 'The project went well' is not a result. Use directional estimates or publicly reported benchmarks (clearly framed as approximate) when you cannot share exact figures from a previous employer.

Not knowing EXL's industries. Saying 'I can learn the domain quickly' instead of demonstrating even basic familiarity with insurance or healthcare operations is a common miss.

Overloading the technical detail. When asked to explain a solution to a hypothetical C-suite audience, some candidates still answer as if they are talking to engineers. Adapt your language to the audience, even within the interview itself.

Skipping the clarifying questions. For open-ended or case questions, jumping straight to an answer without asking at least one scoping question signals poor consulting instincts.

Underselling commercial awareness. If you have ever identified an upsell, retained an at-risk account, or grown an engagement, make sure those stories are ready. EXL expects Solutions Engineers to contribute to revenue growth.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the EXL Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report three to four rounds in most cases. The process typically starts with a recruiter call to check basic fit, followed by one or two rounds focused on technical knowledge or case studies, and ends with a final conversation with a senior leader. Some candidates report a written assignment or pre-sales scenario exercise as an additional step, though this varies by team and seniority level.

Does EXL ask coding questions for the Solutions Engineer role?

Candidates report that coding tests are uncommon for Solutions Engineer roles at EXL, though this can vary by sub-team. The focus is typically on analytical thinking, client solutioning, and domain knowledge. You may be asked about SQL or how you would approach a data problem, but deep coding rounds are more common for engineering-track roles at the company.

Which EXL industry vertical should I focus on in my preparation?

Insurance and banking are EXL's largest and most consistently mentioned verticals, so preparing for either gives you broad coverage. If your background is in healthcare or utilities, lean into that since EXL has significant practices there as well. The goal is to show genuine familiarity with operational workflows in at least one vertical, not just surface-level terminology.

What salary can I expect for a Solutions Engineer at EXL?

EXL has not published official salary bands for this role, and our current data does not include verified compensation figures for it. For current ranges, check platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi where candidates share self-reported offers. Compensation typically varies by years of experience, city, and the specific team or business unit within EXL.

How long does the EXL hiring process usually take from application to offer?

Candidates report that timelines vary depending on team urgency and interviewer availability. Following up politely with the recruiter after each round is advisable. Getting a referral from someone inside EXL can speed up the process significantly compared to applying cold through a job portal.

Is there a pre-sales or case assignment in the EXL Solutions Engineer interview?

Some candidates report a take-home or live solutioning exercise where they are asked to prepare a proposal or presentation for a hypothetical client scenario. This is more common for senior roles. If given a case assignment, focus on clear business value framing, a phased approach, and realistic scope rather than trying to impress with technical complexity alone.
