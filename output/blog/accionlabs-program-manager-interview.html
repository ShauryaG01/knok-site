01 Overview

Overview

Accion Labs is a digital engineering and technology services company that partners with enterprise clients on product engineering, platform modernisation, and digital transformation. A Technical Program Manager (TPM) at Accion Labs sits at the intersection of client delivery, engineering execution, and stakeholder coordination, typically owning complex, multi-team programmes end-to-end.

The company currently has 149 open roles across functions. Across the broader TPM market, Bangalore leads with 41 openings, followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), and Hyderabad (12), out of 313 total TPM roles tracked as of July 2026.

Candidates report the Accion Labs TPM interview process typically runs 3-4 rounds, covering programme management depth, client delivery scenarios, technical communication, and leadership behaviours. Expect practical questions drawn from real client-facing situations rather than abstract puzzles.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly, based on what candidates report and Accion Labs' focus on client delivery and cross-functional programme ownership:

  1. Walk us through the largest programme you have managed end-to-end. What was your specific role, and what was the outcome?
  2. How do you handle scope creep when a client keeps adding requirements mid-programme?
  3. Describe how you set up governance for a multi-vendor or multi-team engagement.
  4. How do you track and manage dependencies across parallel workstreams?
  5. Tell me about a time you had to escalate a risk to senior leadership. How did you decide when to escalate versus handle it yourself?
  6. Accion Labs works closely with enterprise clients. How do you manage a situation where the client's priorities conflict with your engineering team's technical recommendations?
  7. Describe your experience with Agile at scale (SAFe, LeSS, or similar). How have you run PI planning or large-team ceremonies?
  8. How do you measure programme health beyond a simple RAG status? What metrics do you rely on?
  9. Tell me about a time a programme went seriously off-track. What did you do to recover it?
  10. How do you align and onboard new team members who join a programme mid-flight?
  11. Describe a time you influenced a critical decision without having direct authority over the people involved.
  12. Two senior stakeholders disagree on priority. How do you resolve that without escalating every time?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time a programme went off-track and how you recovered it.

*Situation:* I was leading a platform migration for a large retail client. Midway through the programme, a key third-party integration vendor missed several consecutive milestones, putting our go-live date at serious risk.

*Task:* I needed to protect the client relationship, keep the engineering team motivated, and find a path to delivery without simply pushing the date out by a significant margin.

*Action:* I ran an immediate dependency audit to identify which downstream workstreams were actually blocked versus which could proceed independently. I re-sequenced the plan so the team could keep delivering value on the unblocked tracks. In parallel, I set up weekly escalation calls with the vendor's delivery director (not just the account manager), created a shared risk log visible to both sides, and prepared a revised roadmap with two scenarios: one with the vendor recovering quickly, and one contingency plan that excluded them from the critical path entirely.

*Result:* The vendor recovered ahead of the contingency deadline. We absorbed a minor slip rather than a major delay, and the client later cited the transparent communication and contingency planning as a reason they extended the engagement.

---

Q: How do you handle scope creep when a client keeps adding requirements?

*Situation:* On a cloud transformation programme, the client's product team started requesting 'small additions' every sprint. After several sprints, the accumulated requests had grown well beyond what the original scope could absorb.

*Task:* I needed to protect delivery commitments while keeping the client satisfied and not damaging the relationship.

*Action:* I introduced a formal change control process: every new request went through a one-page impact assessment covering effort, cost, and schedule effect. I presented this to the client steering committee and framed it not as 'saying no' but as 'making trade-offs visible.' I also created a backlog parking lot so no request was lost, just prioritised transparently.

*Result:* Scope additions slowed considerably in the following sprints. The client appreciated seeing trade-offs clearly. We delivered the original committed scope on time, and two of the parked items were scoped into a follow-on phase.

---

Q: Describe a time you influenced a decision without direct authority.

*Situation:* Two engineering leads on my programme disagreed about the architecture for a key integration layer. Both were senior in their own organisations and neither reported to me.

*Task:* I needed a decision within a week or the design phase would stall and we would miss a planning milestone.

*Action:* Instead of picking a side, I facilitated a structured trade-off session. I prepared a comparison table covering performance, maintainability, team skill fit, and timeline impact for both options. I invited both leads plus one external architect from our centre of excellence as a neutral voice, and kept the focus on programme goals rather than technical preferences.

*Result:* The team reached consensus in a single session. The chosen architecture went into production successfully, and one of the engineering leads later said the structured format made it easy to leave personal preferences aside.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For behavioural questions (the majority of a TPM interview), use STAR:

Structure every story as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action, since that is where interviewers assess your judgement. Always close with a concrete Result, ideally a business outcome, not just 'the project was delivered.'

For 'how would you approach' questions, use a three-layer structure:

First, clarify the goal, the constraint, and who the stakeholders are. Second, break the problem into workstreams or phases and explain your sequencing logic. Third, define what success looks like and how you will track progress toward it.

For client conflict or stakeholder questions, anchor on interests, not positions:

Identify what each party actually needs (speed, quality, cost, compliance) rather than what they are demanding on the surface. Then explain how you found or negotiated a path that addressed the real underlying interests.

For Agile or process questions, always connect process choices to outcomes:

Do not just describe a ceremony or framework. Explain why you chose that approach for that specific context and what improved as a result.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Accion Labs TPM interviewers are typically looking for five things:

Client-centricity. Accion Labs is a services company. Interviewers want to see that you treat the client as a critical stakeholder, not just an end user. Show that you have managed client relationships directly, handled difficult conversations, and built trust with client-side counterparts.

Ownership without authority. TPMs at Accion Labs often do not have direct line management over the engineers they coordinate. The most valued stories involve influencing peers, senior stakeholders, or external vendors to move in the right direction without relying on hierarchy.

Technical grounding. You do not need to write code, but you need to understand what is being built well enough to spot risks, ask the right questions, and translate between technical and business language. Expect at least one question that probes this directly.

Structured thinking under pressure. Interviewers notice when a candidate thinks out loud in an organised way versus when they ramble. Bring a clear framework to every ambiguous question.

Outcome focus. Every story you tell should end with a measurable or observable result. Process descriptions without results are one of the most common weak spots in TPM interviews.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Know the company and your own portfolio.

Read Accion Labs' published case studies and service line descriptions so you can reference their actual domains (cloud, data engineering, product modernisation) naturally in your answers. Then list every significant programme you have managed and write one STAR story for each. Identify your three strongest stories covering: delivery recovery, stakeholder conflict, and cross-team alignment without formal authority.

Step 2: Practise out loud.

Record yourself answering the questions listed above. Play it back and check: Do you reach the Action quickly? Do you close with a concrete result? Are you using jargon the interviewer may not share?

Step 3: Sharpen your technical communication.

Prepare to explain one complex technical concept you dealt with recently in plain language. Also refresh your knowledge of the Agile at scale frameworks you have used (SAFe, LeSS, or similar), since Accion Labs delivery teams commonly use these.

Before every round:

Prepare two or three questions for the interviewer that show you have thought about the role. For example, ask about programme sizes in the current pipeline, how the TPM function interacts with client delivery managers, or what the typical ramp-up looks like when a new TPM joins an active account.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Describing process instead of impact. Saying 'I set up a RAID log and ran weekly standups' tells the interviewer nothing about your judgement. Always follow any process description with what changed or improved as a result.

Generic answers that could apply to any company. Accion Labs is a client-services business. If your answers could have come from a product company interview, you are missing the point. Bring in client relationship management, commercial awareness, and accountability against client commitments.

Underselling the influence-without-authority muscle. Many candidates describe situations where they had formal power over the team. The more compelling stories, and the ones Accion Labs probes for specifically, involve influencing peers, senior stakeholders, or external vendors.

Not preparing questions for the interviewer. Candidates who ask nothing at the end of a round can come across as unengaged. Prepare at least two questions per round.

Hedging on results. Phrases like 'it went okay' or 'it was mostly successful' weaken an otherwise strong story. Be specific about what improved, even if the metric is qualitative (for example, the client renewed the contract or the team avoided a significant delay).

Spending too long on context. Set the Situation in a few sentences and move on to what you did and why. Interviewers tend to disengage when they feel stuck in backstory.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What does the Accion Labs TPM interview process typically look like?

Candidates report a process of roughly 3-4 rounds, though this varies by team and seniority level. Rounds typically cover an initial HR or recruiter screen, one or two technical and programme management interviews with hiring managers or senior TPMs, and a final round that may include a leadership or culture discussion. Some candidates also report a scenario or case study exercise, so it is worth asking the recruiter what to expect at each stage before you prepare.

What salary can I expect as a TPM at Accion Labs in India?

Accion Labs does not publish salary bands publicly, and this guide does not have compensation data for this role. Based on publicly reported figures and Glassdoor data for TPM roles at comparable IT services companies in India, compensation varies considerably by years of experience, city, and the specific client account you are aligned to. Ask the recruiter for the band upfront and cross-check against current Glassdoor or levels.fyi listings for the TPM title in your city.

How important is domain knowledge for a TPM role at Accion Labs?

Accion Labs works across industries including retail, healthcare, financial services, and manufacturing. Candidates with domain experience in the client vertical they will serve have an advantage, but it is not always a hard requirement. What interviewers consistently prioritise is the ability to ramp up quickly on a new domain and communicate credibly with both technical teams and business stakeholders. Highlighting past examples where you got up to speed on an unfamiliar domain quickly will strengthen your case.

Is Accion Labs a good company for TPMs looking to grow?

Accion Labs is a growing digital engineering firm with a broad client portfolio, which means TPMs are typically exposed to varied programmes across industries and technology stacks. The services model also means you build strong client-facing skills that transfer well across roles and companies. Growth pace and internal opportunity will depend heavily on the team and account you join, so asking your interviewer directly about career paths for TPMs in that specific business unit is the most reliable way to get an honest answer.

What tools and frameworks should I know before interviewing?

You should be comfortable with at least one Agile at scale approach (SAFe is commonly cited in IT services firms), standard programme tracking tools such as Jira or Azure DevOps, and risk and dependency management practices. Familiarity with change control processes and governance reporting (steering committee decks, RAG dashboards) is also expected at the TPM level. You do not need to be an expert in all of these, but you should be able to speak concretely about how you have applied them on past programmes.

How long does the Accion Labs hiring process take from first round to offer?

Candidates report timelines ranging from a few weeks to over a month, depending on the urgency of the role and scheduling availability on both sides. Client services firms sometimes move faster when they have an active programme that urgently needs a TPM. Following up with the recruiter after each round to confirm next steps is a good habit, and if you are interviewing at other companies simultaneously, letting the recruiter know can sometimes prompt them to align timelines.
