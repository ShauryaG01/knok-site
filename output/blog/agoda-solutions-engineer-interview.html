01 Overview

Overview

Agoda is one of Asia's largest online travel platforms, known for fast iteration and a strong engineering culture. A Solutions Engineer at agoda acts as the technical partner for suppliers, travel agencies, and enterprise clients, helping them integrate with agoda's systems, troubleshoot issues, and scale their use of the platform's APIs and booking tools. This is not a pure coding role: it blends technical depth with clear communication and a genuine interest in solving partner problems.

As of July 2026, there are 1,270 Solutions Engineer openings tracked across India, with Bangalore leading at 55 postings, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). Agoda itself has 299 open roles across all functions, making it one of the more active travel-tech hirers right now.

Candidates typically go through several rounds: an initial screening, a technical or take-home assessment, and panel interviews covering both technical and behavioural areas. This guide covers what gets asked, how to answer, and where most candidates fall short.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Here are the questions candidates report most frequently in agoda Solutions Engineer interviews.

Technical and Integration Knowledge

  1. Walk me through how you would onboard a new travel supplier onto an API platform like agoda's.
  2. A partner reports that their booking confirmation rate has dropped sharply. How do you diagnose and fix this?
  3. Explain the difference between REST and SOAP APIs to a partner who has only worked with XML feeds.
  4. How have you used logging, monitoring, or alerting tools to catch integration issues before they reach partners?

Customer-Facing and Communication

  1. How would you explain a complex technical failure to a non-technical business stakeholder?
  2. A partner is resistant to migrating to a new API version even though the old one is being deprecated. How do you handle this?
  3. Describe a time you had to manage expectations with a partner who was unhappy with a delayed implementation.

Problem-Solving and Prioritisation

  1. You have multiple partners with urgent issues at the same time. How do you decide who gets your attention first?
  2. Walk me through a time you turned a stalled or failing implementation into a success.
  3. How do you stay current with API design trends and travel industry developments, and how does that help you in this role?

Behavioural and Culture Fit

  1. Agoda moves fast and ships frequently. Describe a time you helped a partner stay confident and informed through a major platform change.
  2. What draws you to the travel-tech space specifically, and how does that connect to the Solutions Engineer role?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a time you turned a stalled or failing implementation into a success.

*Situation:* I was supporting a mid-size hotel chain that had been in the integration pipeline for several months with no go-live in sight. Their technical team was small and the project had stalled because of unclear documentation and repeated back-and-forth on data field mappings.

*Task:* My goal was to get this partner live within a defined sprint cycle. Beyond fixing the technical blockers, I also needed to rebuild trust with their business team, who were frustrated by the delays.

*Action:* I set up a weekly sync with both the technical lead and the business sponsor on the partner side. I rewrote the relevant sections of the integration guide in plain language, created a mapping spreadsheet to replace the ambiguous documentation, and ran several live walkthroughs of the API flow. I also flagged bugs in our validation logic to our internal team that were silently rejecting the partner's test calls.

*Result:* The partner went live within the next sprint. The mapping guide I created was later adopted as a standard template by the team for similar partner types.

---

Q: How would you explain a complex technical failure to a non-technical business stakeholder?

*Situation:* A key distribution partner experienced a spike in failed bookings over a weekend, affecting their revenue directly. The root cause was a race condition in how our system handled concurrent requests during high traffic.

*Task:* I needed to communicate clearly with their VP of Partnerships, who had no engineering background, while our technical team worked on a fix.

*Action:* I skipped the technical jargon entirely. I explained it using an analogy: imagine a busy hotel reception desk with only one phone line, and a pair of guests trying to book the last room at exactly the same moment. The system gets confused about who confirmed first. I gave them a timeline of what happened, what we were doing to fix it, and a clear expected resolution window. I sent updates at regular intervals so they were never left guessing.

*Result:* The partner appreciated the communication style so much that they mentioned it specifically in their quarterly review. The fix was deployed the following day and the issue did not recur.

---

Q: You have multiple partners with urgent issues at the same time. How do you decide who gets your attention first?

*Situation:* On one particularly busy day I had a new partner mid-integration who had hit a blocker, an existing high-volume partner reporting intermittent failures, and a smaller partner asking for routine documentation help.

*Task:* I had to triage all of them and communicate realistic timelines to each stakeholder without dropping any of them.

*Action:* I triaged by business impact first. The high-volume partner's intermittent failures were affecting live bookings, so I escalated that to our on-call engineering team immediately while keeping the partner updated. I gave the new partner a specific time window when I would unblock them, which was enough for them to continue with other parts of the setup in the meantime. I told the smaller partner I would address their request the following morning and sent them a relevant resource from our docs.

*Result:* All issues were resolved within the same working day. The high-volume partner's problem was fixed without breaching any service commitment, and neither of the other partners escalated.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Solutions Engineer interviews at agoda typically test both structured thinking and genuine empathy for partners. Here are the frameworks most useful for this role.

STAR for Behavioural Questions
Use Situation, Task, Action, Result for any 'tell me about a time' question. Keep the Situation brief (a sentence or two). Spend most of your time on Action, since that is what the interviewer is evaluating. End with a concrete Result you can connect to business impact.

The Partner-First Frame for Technical Questions
When asked about a technical concept or process, structure your answer as: what the problem is from the partner's perspective, what technical solution addresses it, and how you would communicate the solution. This shows the dual focus agoda looks for in a Solutions Engineer.

The Triage Lens for Prioritisation Questions
For any 'how do you prioritise' question, use three layers: business impact (what breaks or costs money if this is not solved), urgency (how fast is this getting worse), and relationship (is this a critical partner that needs visible care). State your triage logic out loud rather than just giving the conclusion.

Acknowledge-Simplify-Confirm for Communication Questions
When asked how you would explain something complex: first acknowledge what the listener already knows, then simplify using an analogy or plain-language summary, then confirm understanding by asking if it lands. This shows interviewers you actually think about the person you are talking to.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Agoda Solutions Engineers are trusted technical advisors to partners, which means interviewers are looking for a specific combination of qualities.

Technical depth without gatekeeping. You should be comfortable with REST APIs, webhooks, JSON/XML, and basic debugging. But what interviewers watch for is whether you can translate that depth into partner-friendly explanations. Pure technical fluency is table stakes. Communication of it is what differentiates candidates.

Customer obsession with commercial awareness. Every story you share should show that you understood the business stakes, not just the technical problem. Did you know which partner was highest priority? Did you flag the revenue impact? Agoda operates at scale, and Solutions Engineers are expected to think like owners.

Comfort with ambiguity. Travel-tech changes fast, and partners often bring unusual or undocumented use cases. Interviewers want to see that you can work confidently with incomplete information, escalate intelligently, and still keep the partner moving forward.

Cross-functional collaboration. You will work with product, engineering, sales, and partner teams. Candidates who show examples of influencing without authority (such as getting an internal team to prioritise a partner bug) score well here.

Genuine curiosity about travel. This is not just a filler question. Agoda's culture rewards people who care about the product. Be ready to speak about what interests you in travel technology and why you find the sector compelling.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A practical, phased approach to preparing for an agoda Solutions Engineer interview.

Build your technical foundation first
Review REST API fundamentals: HTTP methods, status codes, authentication patterns (OAuth, API keys), and JSON/XML structure. If you have not worked with API debugging tools recently, practise with a free public API to refresh your workflow. Read agoda's publicly available developer or partner documentation if accessible, to get a feel for how they structure integrations.

Build your story bank next
Write out several specific examples from your past work: a complex implementation, a difficult partner conversation, a time you escalated something effectively, a time you had to prioritise under pressure, and a time you explained something technical to a non-technical audience. Use the STAR format for each. Practise saying them out loud, not just writing them.

Research agoda specifically
Read recent news about agoda's product and partner initiatives. Understand how their platform fits into the broader online travel agency space. Think about how Solutions Engineers specifically contribute to partner success at a platform of this scale. Prepare several thoughtful questions for your interviewers that show you have done this research.

Simulate in the final stretch
Do mock interviews with a friend or colleague, especially for the technical triage and 'explain to a non-technical person' questions. These are easier to practise in advance than to improvise under pressure. Time your STAR answers to make sure they stay focused and do not run too long.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Going too deep technically without connecting to the partner. Many candidates with strong engineering backgrounds give thorough technical answers but forget to mention how they communicated the situation to the partner or what the business impact was. Interviewers notice this immediately.

Vague STAR answers. 'I helped improve the process' is not a result. 'The partner went live ahead of schedule and their booking volume improved, which we tracked through their reporting' is a result. Be specific about what changed and who cared.

Treating prioritisation as a simple queue. If you say 'I do first in, first out', that signals you have not worked in a partner-facing role at scale. Show that you think about business impact, urgency, and partner relationship health as separate inputs.

Not preparing questions to ask. Solutions Engineers are evaluated on curiosity and commercial thinking. Walking in with no questions signals that you have not thought deeply about the role. Prepare questions about how success is measured in the early months, how the SE team collaborates with product, or what the most common partner integration challenges look like right now.

Underselling cross-functional wins. If you got an internal engineering team to reprioritise a partner bug, that is a significant achievement. Many candidates tell the story but do not name the influence skills that made it work. Be explicit about what you did to build internal buy-in.

Rehearsed answers that do not adapt. If an interviewer asks a follow-up that takes the question in a different direction, go with it. Sticking rigidly to a prepared script makes you sound like you are reciting rather than thinking.

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does agoda typically have for Solutions Engineer roles?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a technical assessment or case study, and one or two panel interviews covering behavioural and technical areas. The exact structure can vary by team and location. It is worth asking your recruiter at the start what the full process looks like so you can plan your preparation accordingly.

Does agoda ask coding questions in the Solutions Engineer interview?

Candidates report that SE interviews at agoda are not typically heavy on algorithmic coding. The technical focus is more likely to be on API concepts, integration scenarios, and debugging case studies. You should be comfortable reading and writing basic scripts or pseudocode to describe a technical approach, as some interviews include a technical walkthrough or take-home exercise.

What does the onboarding period look like for new Solutions Engineers at agoda?

Candidates and employees report that agoda's onboarding is typically structured around learning the platform's partner ecosystem, internal tools, and integration workflows. New SEs are usually paired with existing team members and expected to shadow partner calls before taking the lead. The pace is generally fast given the company's culture, so being proactive about asking questions early is valued.

Is travel industry experience required to get a Solutions Engineer role at agoda?

Direct travel experience is not a listed requirement for most SE roles at agoda. What matters more is your ability to learn domain context quickly and apply it in partner conversations. Candidates who demonstrate genuine curiosity about the travel industry and can speak to why it interests them tend to perform better than those who treat it as just another tech company.
