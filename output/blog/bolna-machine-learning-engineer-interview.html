01 Overview

Overview

Bolna builds real-time AI voice agent infrastructure, letting developers create and deploy conversational voice bots at scale. Their ML Engineers work at the crossroads of automatic speech recognition (ASR), large language model (LLM) orchestration, and text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis. The core challenge is making voice interactions feel fast and natural, which means the team obsesses over latency, model accuracy, and production reliability.

As of July 2026, Bolna has 11 open roles listed across their hiring pages, signalling an active growth phase. The ML Engineer position typically sits within a team handling model training, fine-tuning, inference optimization, and pipeline monitoring. Expect interviews to lean heavily on practical, production-focused problems rather than abstract theory.

For broader market context, knok jobradar currently tracks 803 Machine Learning Engineer openings across India. Bangalore leads with 165 openings, followed by Delhi at 50 and Hyderabad at 27. Bolna operates as a remote-friendly startup, so your city matters less than your depth in voice AI tech stacks.

The interview process typically spans three to four stages: an initial recruiter or hiring manager screening, one or two technical rounds covering ML concepts and system design, and a closing stage that may include a take-home task or a discussion with senior leadership. Candidates report that Bolna values depth in speech or NLP systems and want to hear about real deployment experience, not just model-building in notebooks.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you would design a low-latency ASR-to-LLM-to-TTS pipeline for a real-time voice bot.

*Situation:* At my previous company we were building a customer support voice bot and the end-to-end latency was unacceptably high, which users found frustrating in testing.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the redesign of the inference pipeline to bring perceived latency to a target the product team had defined, without degrading accuracy.

*Action:* I broke the pipeline into three stages and tackled each separately. For ASR, I switched from a batch model to a streaming setup so we could start processing partial transcripts as the user spoke. For the LLM, I implemented speculative decoding and streamed tokens to the TTS layer as soon as the first sentence boundary appeared, rather than waiting for the full response. For TTS, I used a model that synthesises audio chunk-by-chunk and begins playback immediately. I also added a lightweight intent classifier before the LLM to handle common queries through a fast rule-based path.

*Result:* Time-to-first-audio dropped significantly in our A/B test and user satisfaction scores improved. The streaming architecture also made the system more resilient to LLM timeouts.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a production ML model degraded in quality. How did you detect and fix it?

*Situation:* We had a named entity recognition model in production for an HR tech platform. About three months after launch, users started reporting missed extractions for job titles.

*Task:* I owned the ML pipeline and needed to diagnose the issue and ship a fix quickly.

*Action:* I first checked monitoring dashboards and noticed the distribution of input text had shifted. Users were now pasting job descriptions from a new job board whose formatting differed sharply from our training data. I pulled a sample of recent failures, annotated them, and confirmed the data drift hypothesis. I retrained with the new samples added to the training set and set up a distribution-monitoring alert using KL divergence on token frequency to catch similar drift earlier in future.

*Result:* Model performance returned to baseline within a week of retraining. The drift alert caught two smaller issues in the following quarter before they reached users.

---

Q: Describe your experience fine-tuning or adapting an LLM for a specific domain.

*Situation:* I was working on a legal document summarisation product. The base LLM performed well on general text but struggled with Indian legal terminology and citation formats.

*Task:* My goal was to improve domain accuracy without increasing inference cost.

*Action:* I first tried few-shot prompting with curated examples, which gave moderate improvement. I then collected a dataset of legal documents and summaries from our internal team and public sources, and ran LoRA fine-tuning on the base model. I held out a validation set that matched real production queries to avoid overfitting to clean examples. I also set up automated evals using a rubric our legal team had defined, so we could iterate quickly between training runs.

*Result:* The fine-tuned model improved accuracy on our internal benchmark and reduced hallucinated citations. Inference cost stayed flat because LoRA adds minimal parameters to the base model.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for behavioural questions. Most 'tell me about a time' questions at Bolna follow a behavioural format. Structure your answer as Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you did, step by step), and Result (measurable outcome or learning). Keep Situation and Task short. Spend most of your time on Action.

Use a top-down approach for ML system design. Start with the goal (latency target, accuracy requirement, scale), then describe the architecture (data pipeline, model choice, serving layer), then discuss tradeoffs (accuracy vs speed, cost vs quality). Interviewers want to see that you think about constraints before jumping to a model choice.

For debugging questions, use Diagnose-Hypothesize-Verify. State what symptoms you observed, list your hypotheses from most to least likely, describe how you verified each one, and explain the fix. This shows systematic thinking rather than 'I just tried things until it worked.'

Quantify when you can, hedge when you must. Bolna interviewers value engineers who have measured outcomes. If you have real numbers from your own work, use them. If the result was qualitative, say so honestly rather than inventing a metric.

Signal product awareness. Bolna builds developer tooling for voice agents. When answering system design questions, connect your technical choices to the developer or end-user experience. A model that is slightly less accurate but returns results in half the time may be the better product decision, and saying so shows you think beyond benchmarks.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hands-on production experience. Bolna is a startup where ML Engineers ship to real users. Interviewers want to see that you have moved models from experiments into production and dealt with the messy realities: data drift, latency spikes, and rollback decisions. Notebook-only experience will not carry the interview.

Depth in at least one voice or NLP domain. You do not need to be an expert in ASR, TTS, and LLMs equally. But you should have gone deep on at least one area. Candidates who have fine-tuned models, profiled inference, or built custom evaluation pipelines stand out.

Systems thinking across the pipeline. Voice agents involve multiple models chained together. Interviewers want to see that you understand how a decision in the ASR layer affects the LLM and TTS layers downstream. Think in pipelines, not isolated models.

Clear communication. Because Bolna's product is developer-facing, ML Engineers often interact with external users and partners. Interviewers notice whether you can explain a complex model choice in plain terms without losing accuracy.

Ownership mindset. Candidates report that Bolna values people who identify problems proactively rather than waiting to be told. Come prepared with examples of times you flagged an issue, proposed a fix, and followed it through to production.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1, part one: Review ML and NLP fundamentals. Refresh transformer architectures, attention mechanisms, and common fine-tuning approaches such as LoRA and PEFT. Also review classic ML concepts: bias-variance tradeoff, regularisation, and evaluation metrics for both classification and generation tasks.

Week 1, part two: Study voice AI basics. If your background is not in speech ML, read about ASR pipeline components (acoustic model, language model, decoder), TTS architectures (neural vocoders, duration predictors), and how streaming inference works. Public engineering blogs from major ASR and TTS providers are a solid starting point.

Week 2, part one: Practise ML system design. Work through at least three design problems: a real-time voice bot pipeline, a retrieval-augmented generation system, and a model monitoring setup. Focus on stating requirements before designing, and practise explaining tradeoffs out loud, not just on paper.

Week 2, part two: Prepare behavioural stories. Write out five to six STAR stories covering a production incident you solved, a model you improved, a time you disagreed with a technical decision, and a time you learned something new quickly. Bolna typically values people who own outcomes end to end.

Week 3: Mock interviews and Bolna research. Do at least two mock technical interviews with a peer or on a practice platform. Read Bolna's public documentation, blog, and developer community posts to understand what their users care about. This context helps you frame answers in terms of real product impact.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Describing models without mentioning deployment. Many candidates explain how a model works but cannot describe how they served it, monitored it, or rolled it back. At a startup like Bolna, the full ML lifecycle matters more than model architecture knowledge alone.

Ignoring latency in system design. Voice is a real-time medium. A system that is accurate but slow is a product failure. Always mention latency constraints when answering design questions, and describe how you would measure and reduce it in your proposed architecture.

Overclaiming results. Candidates sometimes state improvement numbers without context: sample size, baseline, or how the metric was measured. Interviewers with strong ML backgrounds will probe these claims. Be honest about the scale and conditions of your past work.

Being vague about your individual role. When you say 'we improved the model,' interviewers want to know what you specifically did. Practise answering the follow-up question 'what was your individual contribution?' before you go into the interview.

Skipping clarifying questions in system design. Jumping straight into an architecture before establishing requirements (scale, latency budget, accuracy target, team constraints) signals shallow systems thinking. Start every design question by asking or stating your assumptions explicitly.

Dropping the result in STAR answers. Behavioural answers that end at Action without a clear Result leave interviewers guessing about your impact. Always close with what changed, what you learned, or what you would do differently next time.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Bolna ML Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four stages: an initial screening call with a recruiter or hiring manager, one or two technical rounds covering ML concepts and system design, and a final round that may include a take-home task or a conversation with leadership. The exact structure can vary by role level and team. It is worth asking your recruiter to confirm the format before you start preparing.

Does Bolna focus more on LLMs or speech ML in their interviews?

Based on the nature of Bolna's product, candidates report questions spanning both areas: LLM fine-tuning, RAG pipelines, and inference optimization on one hand, and ASR, TTS, and streaming pipelines on the other. You do not need to be equally strong in both, but you should be able to speak to how they connect in a voice agent system. Depth in one area combined with awareness of the other tends to perform well.

Is a take-home assignment common in Bolna's interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a short take-home task, typically a small ML or system design problem related to voice or NLP. Not all candidates receive one, and it may depend on the specific role or team. If you do get a take-home, focus on code clarity, your reasoning in comments or a short write-up, and how you handle edge cases. Submit something clean and working rather than something ambitious but incomplete.

What is the salary range for an ML Engineer at Bolna?

Bolna has not publicly disclosed salary bands for this role. Glassdoor and levels.fyi list ML Engineer compensation at early-stage AI startups in India across a wide range depending on experience level and equity structure. For the most accurate picture, ask the recruiter directly during your first screening call. Knowing the band early helps both sides avoid wasting time if expectations are far apart.

I come from a data science background, not ML engineering. Can I still apply?

It depends on how close your work has been to production systems. If your experience is primarily analysis and model building in notebooks, the gap will likely show in system design and deployment questions. However, if you have shipped models to production, worked on inference pipelines, or built evaluation frameworks, your background can translate well. Emphasise production work in your resume and interviews, and be honest about areas where you are still building skills.

How important is open-source contribution for a role at Bolna?

Open-source work is a plus but not a hard requirement, based on what candidates report from similar startups. What matters more is demonstrating real depth through any channel: a strong GitHub project, a detailed write-up of a past system, or concrete production examples from your job history. If you have contributed to relevant projects in ASR, LLMs, or voice AI, mention it because it signals genuine interest in the domain beyond your day job.
