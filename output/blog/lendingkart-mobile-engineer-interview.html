01 Overview

Overview

Lendingkart is a fintech company focused on working capital loans for small and medium businesses across India. As a Mobile Engineer there, you would build and maintain the apps that borrowers and field agents use to apply for loans, check disbursement status, and manage repayments.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows Lendingkart with 72 open roles across functions, signaling active hiring. The Mobile Engineer interview process typically spans 3-4 rounds, candidates report, covering a coding screen, a technical deep-dive, a system design session, and a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager.

Lendingkart's apps handle sensitive financial data for borrowers who are often in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities with patchy internet. Interviewers pay close attention to your grasp of security, offline-first patterns, and performance on budget Android devices.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Technical questions candidates report seeing:

  1. How do you handle offline data sync in a mobile app where users have patchy internet connectivity?
  2. Walk me through how you would design the architecture for a loan application flow, from form entry to document upload to status tracking.
  3. How do you secure sensitive data (like Aadhaar numbers or PAN details) stored locally on a device?
  4. What is the difference between a ViewModel and a UseCase in a clean architecture setup? How do you decide what logic lives where?
  5. How have you improved app performance for users on slow or unreliable connections?
  6. Explain how you would implement a retry mechanism for failed API calls in a fintech app where double submissions could cause real financial harm.
  7. How do you handle deep linking in your app, and how do you pass state through a deep link safely?
  8. What testing strategy do you follow for UI-heavy screens? How do you balance unit tests, integration tests, and UI tests?
  9. You notice the app crashes for a segment of users on older Android versions. How do you diagnose and fix this without access to those devices?
  10. How would you implement biometric authentication and fall back gracefully if the device does not support it?
  11. Describe a time you had to reduce APK size or improve app startup time. What tools and techniques did you use?
  12. How do you manage feature flags in a mobile app so that you can roll out new loan products to a subset of users first?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you handle offline data sync in a mobile app?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built a field agent app used in rural areas where agents often had no internet while visiting borrowers.

*Task:* I needed to make sure agents could fill in loan application forms offline and have the data sync reliably when connectivity returned, without duplicating or losing records on the server.

*Action:* I implemented a local Room database as the single source of truth. Every form submission was written locally first with a 'pending' status. A WorkManager job ran whenever network became available, picked up pending records, posted them to the API, and updated the status to 'synced'. I added idempotency keys on every request so re-sending a failed call would not create a duplicate loan application.

*Result:* Support tickets about 'lost applications' dropped to near zero within the first month after release, and field agent productivity in low-connectivity areas improved noticeably.

---

Q: How would you secure sensitive data like Aadhaar or PAN stored locally on a device?

*Situation:* Our app pre-filled KYC fields from a cached profile to speed up repeat applications, which meant PAN and Aadhaar numbers lived temporarily on the device.

*Task:* A security audit flagged that this data was stored in plain SharedPreferences, readable on rooted devices. I had to fix this before the next release.

*Action:* I replaced SharedPreferences with Jetpack EncryptedSharedPreferences backed by the Android Keystore. For document images, I moved them to the app's internal storage with mode PRIVATE and added a flag to clear the cache on logout. I also added a check that blocks the app from running on rooted devices for any flow that handles KYC data.

*Result:* The app passed the re-audit with no findings in the local storage category. The root detection check also gave the compliance team confidence when presenting to banking partners.

---

Q: Describe a time you reduced APK size or improved app startup time.

*Situation:* Our loan app's download size had grown over multiple releases and cold start time felt noticeably slow on the budget Android devices common among our target users.

*Task:* Product wanted the install and first-launch experience to feel lighter so we did not lose users at the Play Store step.

*Action:* I ran an APK analysis in Android Studio and found that image libraries we no longer used were still bundled in. I removed them and replaced remaining large PNGs with WebP. I switched to App Bundles so the Play Store delivered only resources matching the user's device configuration. For startup, I profiled with Perfetto and found we were initialising an analytics SDK on the main thread, which I moved to a background coroutine.

*Result:* Download size and cold start time both improved measurably. Our internal benchmark moved from 'slow' to 'acceptable' on a mid-range test device, and the product team noticed fewer drop-offs at install.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task brief together. Spend most of your answer on Action, with specific technology choices explained and justified. End with a concrete Result, even if you say 'support tickets dropped' rather than an exact number you cannot recall.

For system design questions, start by clarifying the user and scale: 'Is this for a borrower on a budget Android phone or a loan officer on a tablet?' Lendingkart serves Tier 2 and Tier 3 India, so assumptions about connectivity and device tier matter a great deal. Then walk through: data layer, sync strategy, UI architecture, and security considerations in that order.

For debugging questions, show your diagnostic process rather than jumping straight to a solution. Mention tools: Android Studio Profiler, Firebase Crashlytics, Perfetto, LeakCanary. Interviewers want to see a methodical approach, not a lucky guess.

For 'how do you choose between X and Y' questions (Jetpack Compose vs Views, Retrofit vs Ktor, etc.), give your default choice, explain one trade-off, and say clearly when you would pick the other. Avoid strong opinions without justification.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Lendingkart's mobile team, candidates report, is primarily Android-focused given the platform's dominance among small business borrowers in India. Cross-platform experience is a bonus as they expand reach.

Fintech-specific thinking matters a lot. Can you explain why a double API call in a payment flow is dangerous? Do you think about what happens when the user kills the app mid-transaction? Interviewers want engineers who treat money movement as a different category from typical CRUD apps.

Empathy for the actual user base stands out. Engineers who have thought about low-RAM devices, intermittent connectivity, and users who may not be tech-savvy tend to ask better clarifying questions in design rounds and propose more realistic solutions.

Ownership and follow-through are valued. Lendingkart is a scale-up, not a large enterprise, so they want people who can take a feature from design to release without heavy hand-holding.

Communication clarity in system design and debugging rounds is important. Thinking out loud and checking alignment with the interviewer is seen as a positive, not a sign of uncertainty.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core Android and architecture
Revise Jetpack components including ViewModel, LiveData or StateFlow, Room, and WorkManager. Make sure you can explain the lifecycle of each and when to use one over another. Practice drawing a clean architecture diagram from memory.

Week 2: Fintech-specific patterns
Study offline-first architecture, idempotency in API design, and local data encryption with Android Keystore. Read about KYC flows and think through edge cases such as partial uploads and network drops mid-document-scan.

Week 3: Performance and security
Practice an APK analysis walkthrough using Android Studio. Learn to use the Profiler to spot memory leaks and slow frames. Understand biometric API, root detection, and certificate pinning at a conceptual level.

Week 4: Mock interviews and behavioural prep
Prepare 5-6 STAR stories covering: a performance improvement, a hard debugging session, a time you pushed back on a product decision, and a feature you shipped end-to-end. Run at least two timed system design sessions with a peer or on a practice platform.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'why' on technology choices. Saying 'I used Retrofit' is not enough. Explain why you chose it over alternatives in that specific context.

Ignoring offline and low-connectivity scenarios. For a company whose borrowers are often in Tier 2 or Tier 3 India, treating network issues as edge cases is a clear red flag.

Being vague about results in STAR answers. 'The app got faster' is weaker than describing the before-and-after on a benchmark device. If you do not remember exact numbers from your own experience, say so and give a relative description.

Treating the system design round as a lecture. Not checking in with the interviewer, not asking clarifying questions about scale, and not pausing to confirm alignment are common failure modes.

Over-engineering small problems. Proposing a complex distributed sync engine for a simple settings screen signals poor product judgement. Match the solution to the stated scale.

Not mentioning security at all when designing a financial flow. Even if the interviewer does not ask explicitly, raising data encryption and API integrity shows maturity for a fintech role.
