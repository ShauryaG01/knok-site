03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Popclub typically unfolds in a few stages, though the exact number of rounds can vary depending on the role and team. Here is what candidates commonly report:

Application and screening: After you apply, a recruiter or someone from HR usually reaches out for a brief initial call. This conversation covers your background, what you are looking for, notice period, and basic expectations. It is your first chance to make a good impression, so be ready to speak clearly about your experience and why Popclub interests you.

Technical or functional round: Depending on the role, this could be a coding exercise for engineering candidates, a case study for business or strategy roles, or a portfolio review for design and marketing. Expect questions that test practical, hands-on skills directly tied to the job description.

Hiring manager round: This is a deeper conversation with the person you would likely report to. Expect role-specific questions alongside behavioural questions about how you have handled real situations in the past. Be specific with your examples.

Culture or leadership round: Some roles include a final round with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder. This round tends to focus on your thinking style, how you handle ambiguity, and whether you fit the way the team works.

Offer stage: If rounds go well, HR follows up with a verbal offer before sending the formal letter. If you have not heard back, a polite follow-up after a week is perfectly acceptable.

Engineering roles sometimes include a separate system design or live coding round. Sales and operations roles may involve a practical task or role-play scenario. Always confirm the full process with HR at the start so you know what to expect.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Popclub looks for people who take ownership, communicate clearly, and can move fast without needing every decision handed to them. A few qualities stand out across roles:

Ownership mindset: Candidates who show end-to-end responsibility for past projects, not just tasks they were assigned, tend to do well. Frame your experience in terms of outcomes you drove, not just duties you performed.

Comfort with ambiguity: As a growing company, Popclub does not always have rigid processes in place. They want people who can figure things out, adapt on the fly, and help build structure as they go.

Consumer empathy: Because Popclub builds products for everyday shoppers and brand partners, a genuine curiosity about consumer behaviour and user experience is valued across all functions.

Cross-functional collaboration: Most roles involve working with people from different teams. Showing that you can align with others, handle disagreement constructively, and move projects forward together matters a great deal.

Clear, simple communication: Whether you are in engineering, marketing, or operations, being able to explain your thinking in plain language is something interviewers notice and value. Avoid jargon in your answers.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product before anything else: Spend time with the Popclub app or platform before your interview. Understand how it works, who it is built for, and what problem it solves. Being able to reference specific features or user flows shows genuine interest and sets you apart from candidates who only skimmed the website.

Prepare your stories using STAR: For each behavioural question, structure your answer around Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Prepare examples that show ownership, problem-solving under pressure, and cross-team collaboration. The more specific you are, the more credible you sound.

Brush up on role-specific skills: For tech roles, practise data structures, algorithms, and system design. For business or product roles, review how you think about metrics, user journeys, and go-to-market decisions. Use the job description as a checklist for what to revise.

Prepare thoughtful questions for each round: Asking a good question signals that you are thinking seriously about the role. Try asking about how the team makes decisions, what success looks like in the first few months, or what the hardest challenge in the role currently is. Avoid questions whose answers are already on the company website.

Follow up after each round: A short, genuine message to HR or the interviewer after each stage keeps you top of mind and shows professionalism. Keep it brief and specific to what you discussed.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Popclub has the energy of a consumer-tech startup, which means the culture rewards initiative, speed, and a bias toward getting things done. Teams are lean, so each person's contribution is visible and individual impact is real.

Collaboration over hierarchy: Decisions tend to be made by the people closest to the problem, not purely by seniority. In practice, this means a junior team member who has the data and the argument can influence outcomes in ways that would be unusual at a large corporation.

Customer obsession: Because the product directly serves consumers and brand partners, teams talk about the customer constantly. This shows up in how features are prioritised, how success is measured, and how problems are framed in meetings.

High learning curve, high reward: Employees at growth-stage companies like Popclub often develop faster than at larger firms because they handle multiple responsibilities and are close to decisions that matter. If you are early in your career and want to build skills quickly, that trade-off is worth thinking about.

The culture may not suit everyone. If you prefer a highly structured environment with clear processes and defined boundaries, a startup can feel chaotic at first. But if you thrive on ownership, variety, and visible impact, Popclub can be a strong step forward.
