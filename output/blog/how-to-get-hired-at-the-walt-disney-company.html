01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

The Walt Disney Company is one of the most recognized entertainment brands in the world, spanning streaming services (including Disney+ and Hotstar in India), film studios, theme parks, media networks, and consumer products. For Indian professionals, Disney is a compelling target both for roles within its India operations and for global positions in technology, data, content, finance, and marketing.

As of July 2026, knok is tracking 24 open roles at Disney across these functions. The mix typically covers engineering and product, data and analytics, finance, creative, and business operations. Given Disney's global scale, some of these roles may offer hybrid or remote flexibility.

Getting hired at Disney is competitive. The company receives a high volume of applications for every opening. What separates shortlisted candidates is usually a combination of sharp, relevant skills and a genuine, demonstrable connection to what Disney does: creating stories and experiences that people remember for a lifetime.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Disney's hiring process typically runs through five to six stages, though the exact structure varies by role and seniority. Here is what you can broadly expect.

Stage 1: Application and resume screening. Disney's talent team reviews resumes against the job description. A resume tailored to the specific role, using language from the posting itself, performs better than a generic one. Volume is high, so the screening bar is real.

Stage 2: Recruiter phone screen. Shortlisted candidates receive a call from a Disney recruiter. The recruiter will ask about your background, your interest in Disney specifically, and basic logistics like availability and location.

Stage 3: Hiring manager round. This is a deeper conversation with the person who would directly manage you. Expect questions about your past projects, how you handle specific challenges in your domain, and what draws you to this particular role.

Stage 4: Panel interviews. For mid-to-senior roles, Disney commonly runs a panel of two to four interviewers from different teams. These rounds combine technical or functional assessments with behavioral questions. Expect 'tell me about a time when...' style questions drawn from their core competencies.

Stage 5: Case study or work sample (select roles). Strategy, data, and senior creative roles often require a presentation or take-home assignment. This gives the team a clear view of how you structure your thinking and communicate ideas, not just what your resume says.

Stage 6: Offer and background verification. Disney runs a standard background check before issuing a final offer. The overall timeline from application to offer can range from a few weeks to a couple of months depending on role seniority.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Brand passion, not just brand awareness. Disney consistently looks for candidates who genuinely connect with what the company does. This does not mean memorizing every Marvel film in order. It means being able to speak clearly about why Disney's work matters and how your specific role contributes to it.

Storytelling ability across all functions. From engineering to marketing to finance, Disney values people who can frame their work as a narrative. When describing your experience, think in terms of problem, action, and impact rather than just listing responsibilities.

Collaboration and cross-functional thinking. Disney's projects routinely involve large, diverse teams across geographies. Candidates who show they have worked well with people outside their immediate function consistently score higher.

Domain expertise paired with curiosity. Disney expects strong functional skills, whether in data engineering, content strategy, or financial planning. But they also want people who ask questions beyond their lane and stay curious about how the broader business works.

Audience-first mindset. Disney's internal language centers on the guest or the audience experience. Candidates who frame their work in terms of the end experience, rather than internal processes alone, tend to resonate better with interviewers.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the specific division, not just Disney overall. Disney operates across very different businesses: parks and experiences, studio entertainment, streaming, and media networks. Know which division you are applying to and what its current priorities are. Interviewers notice when candidates speak specifically versus generically.

Prepare behavioral stories using the STAR format. Disney's interviews lean heavily on behavioral questions. For each key competency in the job description, prepare a specific story covering Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Have four to six stories ready.

Know the products. If you are applying to a streaming or content role, be current on Disney+ and Hotstar. If it is a parks role, know what Disney is doing in the experiential space. Product knowledge signals genuine interest.

Study Disney's values and language. Disney talks about 'magic,' 'storytelling,' and 'guest experience' in a specific way. Read their careers page and any public interviews with Disney leaders. Mirror this language naturally in your answers without sounding scripted.

Prepare sharp questions for each interviewer. Disney interviewers appreciate candidates who ask thoughtful questions about team dynamics, upcoming challenges, and what success looks like in the role. Avoid asking things easily found on their website.

For tech roles, brush up on system design and data fundamentals. Disney's engineering and data teams run technical rounds comparable to other large technology companies. Practice system design, SQL, and relevant algorithms depending on your track.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

The 'magic' standard is a real operating principle. Disney's culture is built around the idea that every product, experience, or service should feel extraordinary to the person on the receiving end. This shows up in how teams approach their work, even in back-office functions. The bar for quality and presentation is genuinely high.

Structure exists, but cross-functional work is expected. Disney is a large, structured organization with clear reporting lines and approval processes. At the same time, cross-team collaboration is the norm, and employees at all levels are expected to build relationships outside their immediate function.

Inclusion is a stated priority. Disney has publicly invested in diversity and inclusion across its workforce. For Indian candidates, this generally means a workplace familiar with diverse backgrounds, though as with any large organization, actual culture varies by team and manager.

Creative and commercial instincts coexist. Disney is an entertainment company at heart, but it is also a large commercial operation. Employees who thrive here tend to respect both sides: they care about the creative mission and can also work with targets, data, and business outcomes without losing sight of the audience.

Long-term brand loyalty runs deep. Many Disney employees have strong personal histories with the brand, often from childhood. This creates a culture with real emotional investment in the work. Coming in with that same sense of connection, expressed in your own way, tends to land well in interviews and on the job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many jobs is Disney currently hiring for?

As of July 2026, knok is tracking 24 open roles at The Walt Disney Company. This reflects what knok's job radar is actively monitoring and may not cover every internal or unlisted opening. Roles span technology, content, finance, marketing, and operations across Disney's various divisions.

Does Disney hire freshers or entry-level candidates from India?

Disney does post entry-level and associate-level roles, though these are less common than mid-to-senior openings. Freshers often have the best shot through Disney's internship programs, which the company runs globally and in India. Converting an internship into a full-time offer is a well-known path into the organization.

How long does Disney's hiring process take from application to offer?

The timeline varies by role and level. Individual contributor roles can move from application to offer in a few weeks, while senior or leadership roles often take longer due to more interview rounds and additional stakeholder approvals. Building in extra time and staying active on other applications while you wait is a practical approach.

What salary can I expect at Disney in India?

Publicly reported salary data for Disney India roles on platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi varies widely by function and seniority. It is worth checking those platforms directly for current, role-specific numbers. Disney is broadly seen as a competitive employer, with the brand value and work environment being part of the overall offer.

Does a Disney referral help, and how do I get one?

A referral from a current Disney employee can help your application surface faster in a high-volume applicant pool. It does not guarantee an interview, but it often means a recruiter gives your resume a closer look. Connecting with Disney employees on LinkedIn before you apply, and being genuine about your interest, is a practical first step.

What are the most common mistakes candidates make in Disney interviews?

The most common mistake is treating Disney like any other corporate employer and failing to connect your experience to the brand's specific mission. A second frequent error is under-preparing for behavioral questions, which Disney uses extensively at every level. Candidates who prepare specific stories rather than vague answers consistently perform better.
