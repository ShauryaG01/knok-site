01 Overview

Overview

Brex is a US-headquartered fintech company focused on corporate spend management, and it currently has 279 open roles across the company (as of July 2026). The Technical Program Manager role at Brex sits at the centre of engineering execution: you coordinate large, cross-functional programs spanning product, engineering, and business teams in a fast-moving fintech environment.

Candidates report that the interview process typically involves a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, and then a series of behavioural and technical depth sessions. Some candidates also report a final round with cross-functional stakeholders. Brex values ownership and speed, so interviewers tend to probe for concrete examples of how you drove programs forward under ambiguity, not just how you tracked status.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have interviewed at Brex for TPM roles report questions that test both execution rigour and business awareness. Here are commonly reported questions:

  1. Tell me about a large, multi-team program you owned end to end. How did you structure it and what was your biggest challenge?
  2. How do you manage dependencies between teams that have conflicting priorities?
  3. Brex operates in financial services. Describe a time you managed a program with compliance or regulatory constraints.
  4. Walk me through how you track program health. What are your early-warning signals that something is off track?
  5. Tell me about a time a program you were running was at serious risk of failing. What did you do?
  6. How do you influence a technical decision when you do not have direct authority over the engineering team?
  7. Describe a time you had to push back on scope or cut features to protect a deadline.
  8. How do you ramp up quickly in a new technical domain you have not worked in before?
  9. Brex values speed. How do you balance moving fast with managing risk in a regulated product area?
  10. Describe a difficult stakeholder situation. How did you bring them on board with the program direction?
  11. Walk me through a post-mortem or retrospective you facilitated. What changed as a result?
  12. How do you decide what to escalate to leadership versus what to resolve yourself?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for these. Keep each story under two minutes when spoken aloud.

Q: Tell me about a large, multi-team program you owned end to end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were migrating a core payments processing module to new infrastructure, involving multiple engineering teams and several external vendors simultaneously.

*Task:* I was the sole TPM responsible for ensuring all teams delivered on time for a regulatory-driven cutover date, with no option to extend.

*Action:* I set up a single source of truth in our project tracker, ran weekly dependency review calls across all teams, and created a red-amber-green health tracker visible to all leads and senior leadership. When one vendor slipped on an API integration, I negotiated a phased rollout that unblocked the dependent teams while that vendor caught up.

*Result:* The migration completed on schedule with no customer-facing incidents during cutover. The phased rollout approach became a reusable template the team adopted for later programs.

---

Q: Describe a time you managed a program with compliance or regulatory constraints.

*Situation:* My team was building a new lending feature and needed sign-off from legal and compliance before we could launch.

*Task:* I had to sequence engineering milestones around legal review windows, which were slow and unpredictable, without letting the engineering team go idle.

*Action:* I ran a risk-mapping session with legal early in the program to identify which components needed review and in what order. I built review cycles into the timeline as explicit milestones, not hidden dependencies, so they were visible during planning. I also wrote a plain-language summary of each component so legal reviewers could move faster.

*Result:* We got legal sign-off without a single unplanned delay, and the feature launched on the original date. The plain-language summary approach was later adopted by other product teams in the organisation.

---

Q: Tell me about a time a program you were running was at serious risk.

*Situation:* A few weeks before a major product launch, our infrastructure team flagged that load testing had revealed performance issues that could cause failures at peak traffic.

*Task:* I had to replan the program in real time while keeping stakeholders calm and maintaining team momentum.

*Action:* I immediately convened a working session with engineering leads to assess the blast radius. We identified the root causes, agreed on a fix for the critical one, and put a monitoring-plus-throttling workaround in place for the secondary issue. I updated stakeholder communications to reflect a brief delay, framing it as a quality decision rather than a failure.

*Result:* The launch went ahead shortly after with no incidents. The stakeholder communication approach (framing delay as a quality decision) received positive feedback from senior leadership and became a template for future launch communications.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use these structures to shape your answers during the Brex TPM interview.

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result): The most versatile framework. Keep the situation brief, clarify your specific responsibility, describe what YOU did (not what the team did), and close with a concrete outcome. Brex interviewers typically want to hear your individual contribution, so use 'I' rather than 'we' when describing actions.

Influence without authority: For questions about cross-team collaboration, structure your answer around how you built credibility with the other team, how you aligned on shared goals, and how you negotiated a path forward. Avoid framing it as 'I convinced them I was right'; frame it as 'we found an approach that worked for both sides'.

Risk and escalation: When asked about managing risk, cover how you identified the risk early, what your assessment criteria were (likelihood and impact), what you tried to resolve yourself, and when and how you escalated. Brex values judgment, so show you know when NOT to escalate as much as when to escalate.

Technical depth: You do not need to write code, but you should be able to discuss system architecture concepts, API dependencies, infrastructure constraints, and why certain technical decisions affect program timelines. For fintech-specific programs, know how compliance review cycles and audit trails intersect with engineering delivery.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Brex TPM interviewers typically look for a specific mix of traits, based on what candidates report and what the role demands in fintech.

Ownership over coordination: Brex does not want a status-tracker. They want someone who feels personally accountable for program outcomes. Your answers should show that you proactively removed blockers, made judgment calls, and held the program together, not just that you ran meetings and updated dashboards.

Technical fluency: You should be comfortable discussing APIs, data pipelines, infrastructure dependencies, and system design at a conceptual level. You do not need deep coding expertise, but you should be able to have a real conversation with engineers about trade-offs without getting lost.

Speed with rigour: Brex moves fast and operates in financial services, which creates an interesting tension. Interviewers want to see that you can move quickly while still thinking clearly about compliance, security, and reliability. Show examples where you did both.

Clear, direct communication: Brex values directness. In your answers, get to the point quickly. Interviewers should know where your story is going within the first minute. Do not bury the result at the end of a long setup.

Data-informed decisions: Where possible, show that your program decisions were grounded in data, not just intuition. Metrics, dashboards, and explicit success criteria signal maturity in a TPM candidate.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Follow this plan in the weeks before your Brex TPM interview.

Step 1: Study Brex's products and domain. Spend time on Brex's public resources, engineering blog, and product pages. Understand what corporate spend management means, how their card and banking products work, and the regulatory environment (PCI-DSS, banking compliance) that shapes their engineering decisions.

Step 2: Build your story bank. Write out your top programs using the STAR format. Prepare ready stories for: a large cross-functional program, a missed deadline or near-miss recovery, a stakeholder conflict, a technical trade-off decision, and a compliance or risk-related program. Practise saying each story aloud and keep it under two minutes.

Step 3: Prepare your technical depth. Review concepts like API dependency management, incident response, capacity planning, and phased rollouts. If your background is not in fintech, read a few articles on how payment rails and compliance cycles work. You do not need to be an expert, but you should not be caught off guard by these topics.

Step 4: Prepare thoughtful questions for each interviewer. Ask about program scale, team structure, current challenges, and how success is measured for TPMs at Brex. Specific questions signal genuine interest.

Step 5: Do a mock interview. Ask a peer or mentor to run a behavioural session. Focus on keeping answers concise and structured, avoiding tangents, and leading with results.

Step 6: Follow up after each round. Send a short, professional note to your recruiter promptly after each conversation. Keep it brief and genuine.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about your individual contribution. Saying 'we launched the product' tells Brex nothing. They want to know what YOU did. Be specific about your decisions and actions.

Describing coordination without impact. Listing activities ('I ran weekly syncs, I updated the tracker') without connecting them to outcomes reads as a coordinator, not a program owner. Always close with what changed because of your work.

Ignoring the fintech context. Generic TPM stories are less compelling at a company like Brex. If you have relevant experience in fintech, payments, compliance, or regulated industries, lead with it.

Talking only about successes. Brex interviewers typically dig into failures or near-misses. If you do not have a ready story about a program that went badly and what you learned from it, you will be caught off guard.

Overloading answers with process jargon. Brex values clear communication. Using too much acronym-heavy or process-heavy language without explaining how it drove outcomes misses the point.

Not preparing questions to ask. Showing up without thoughtful questions about the role, the team, or the company's technical challenges signals low engagement. Prepare at least a couple of genuine questions for each round.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does the Brex TPM process typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves a recruiter screen, a hiring manager call, and then a series of behavioural and technical depth sessions. Some candidates also report a final round with cross-functional stakeholders. The exact structure can vary by team, so ask your recruiter what to expect after your first conversation.

Do I need a technical background to be a TPM at Brex?

You do not need to write code, but Brex operates in a technically complex environment. Candidates report that interviewers test whether you can have a real conversation with engineers about system design, dependencies, and trade-offs. A background in software engineering or a related technical field is common, but strong technical fluency built through experience can compensate for a non-engineering degree.

What salary can I expect for a TPM role at Brex in India?

Brex does not publicly publish India-specific salary bands for TPM roles. Publicly reported figures on Glassdoor and levels.fyi for senior TPM roles at similar US-headquartered fintechs in India can vary widely based on level, location, and experience. Ask your recruiter directly about the band for the specific level you are interviewing for.

How important is fintech or payments experience for this role?

It is helpful but not always required. Candidates report that Brex values general program management excellence and technical depth over domain-specific experience. If you lack fintech experience, showing that you have worked in a regulated or compliance-driven environment (healthcare, enterprise SaaS, or similar) can compensate. Do your homework on payment rails and compliance basics before the interview.

How long does the full Brex TPM interview process take from application to offer?

Candidates report the process can take anywhere from a few weeks to a couple of months, depending on the role and team. Delays are often on the recruiter or approvals side rather than the candidate side. Stay responsive and follow up politely if you have not heard back in a reasonable time.

Does Brex offer remote or hybrid roles for TPMs in India?

Brex has historically supported distributed teams. Candidates report that some roles are remote-friendly while others have specific location requirements. Check the job listing carefully and ask your recruiter to clarify the work arrangement before investing significant time in the process.
