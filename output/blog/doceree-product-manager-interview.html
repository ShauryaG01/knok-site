01 Overview

Overview

Doceree builds programmatic advertising technology for the healthcare industry. Their core asset is a physician identity graph that links verified, licensed doctors to their digital behaviour across medical portals, tele-consultation apps, and health content sites. Pharma brands use Doceree's platform to run targeted, compliant campaigns that reach the right specialist at the right moment.

For a Product Manager, this means working across a genuinely multi-sided marketplace. On one side sit pharma and biotech clients who want reach, engagement, and attribution for their HCP campaigns. On the other sit medical publishers who monetize their audiences through Doceree's ad stack. PMs own features like campaign setup workflows, audience segmentation tools, analytics dashboards, publisher yield tools, and identity verification pipelines.

The regulated nature of pharma advertising (India's UCPMP guidelines, strict claims rules, and data privacy requirements) means every product decision carries a compliance dimension. Candidates who have worked in health-tech, ad-tech, or B2B SaaS with enterprise clients tend to transition well.

Doceree currently has 7 open roles tracked by knok's jobradar (as of July 2026). India-wide, there are 2,009 active Product Manager openings, with Bangalore leading at 271 roles and Delhi at 177.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates at Doceree typically face a mix of product sense, analytical, and domain questions. Here are the questions most commonly reported:

  1. How would you define and measure success for a new feature on Doceree's physician-facing ad experience?
  2. Walk us through how you would prioritize the roadmap for a pharma client's campaign analytics dashboard when sales, clients, and engineering all want different things.
  3. Doceree's physician identity graph is its core moat. How would you extend or strengthen that capability as a product initiative?
  4. How do you handle a product decision when healthcare compliance rules out your preferred solution?
  5. A key publisher partner reports a sharp, sudden drop in fill rate on their portal. How do you investigate and respond?
  6. How would you design an A/B test to improve HCP engagement rates on a banner ad format, given that the verified-doctor sample is smaller than a typical consumer audience?
  7. Describe how you would work with a pharma brand that is asking for broad reach metrics but your data shows engagement quality is poor.
  8. How would you approach entering a new therapeutic category (say, oncology) with Doceree's platform?
  9. What would a north star metric look like for Doceree's demand-side product, and why?
  10. Tell us about a time you managed a product with competing stakeholders on both sides of a marketplace.
  11. How would you write the product spec for an HCP segmentation feature that must comply with data anonymisation requirements?
  12. If Doceree decided to launch a self-serve campaign tool for smaller pharma brands, how would you scope the MVP?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioural questions. Here are three worked examples:

---

Q: Tell us about a time you improved a B2B analytics product based on user feedback.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we had a reporting dashboard used by marketing managers at healthcare clients. Feedback calls revealed that users were spending most of their session time exporting data to spreadsheets just to do basic trend comparisons.

*Task:* I needed to close this gap without a full dashboard rebuild, given a two-sprint constraint agreed with the engineering lead.

*Action:* I ran five user interviews to map the three most common export workflows. I then worked with design to add an inline 'compare periods' toggle and a pre-filter on CSV exports, and wrote the spec prioritising those two flows. I coordinated with the data engineering team to ensure the backend aggregation could support the new query without adding latency.

*Result:* After release, 'how do I export' support queries dropped noticeably and time-on-dashboard increased in the following two sprints. The team adopted the same interview-led process for the next two feature cycles.

---

Q: Describe a situation where compliance constraints slowed your product velocity. How did you manage it?

*Situation:* We were building a new targeting feature that used specialty codes to segment physicians. The legal team flagged that combining specialty with geography could make individual doctors identifiable under our data agreement.

*Task:* I had to redesign the feature to satisfy both the client's targeting needs and the privacy requirement, without slipping the launch date by more than one sprint.

*Action:* I brought legal, data science, and design into a single working session. We agreed on a minimum-cohort rule (no segment below a set doctor count would be targetable) and I rewrote the spec accordingly. I communicated the trade-off to the client directly, framing the minimum-cohort rule as a trust signal rather than a limitation.

*Result:* The feature launched one sprint late. The client accepted the cohort floor without escalation, and the same rule was later adopted as a standard privacy guardrail across all our segmentation products.

---

Q: Walk us through a time you balanced competing stakeholder demands on a roadmap.

*Situation:* Sales wanted a bulk-upload feature for campaign creatives. Engineering wanted to refactor the ad-serving layer. A key client wanted better frequency capping controls. All three were flagged as urgent in the same planning cycle.

*Task:* My job was to sequence the roadmap fairly, keep morale intact on all sides, and still ship something meaningful in the quarter.

*Action:* I mapped each request to a business outcome metric and an effort estimate. Frequency capping had the clearest revenue impact (it was blocking an upsell conversation) and the lowest engineering effort, so it went first. The refactor was non-negotiable for long-term velocity, so I ring-fenced two engineers for it as a parallel track. Bulk upload was deferred one quarter with a written rationale shared to sales leadership.

*Result:* Frequency capping shipped in week four. The refactor reduced ad-serving error rates the team had been tracking. Sales accepted the deferral because the written rationale was specific and tied to capacity, not preference.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions respond well to a simple structure: define the user (which HCP segment or pharma client type), identify their core job-to-be-done, list two or three solutions, pick one and explain the trade-off, then name the metric that tells you it worked.

Prioritisation questions at a healthcare ad-tech company typically involve compliance, client revenue impact, and engineering cost as the three axes. Show that you weight these explicitly rather than going purely by effort-vs-impact scoring.

Metrics questions at Doceree tend to focus on supply-side health (fill rate, publisher yield) and demand-side outcomes (verified HCP reach, campaign completion rate, cost per verified engagement). Know both sides of the marketplace, not just the advertiser side.

Root cause or investigation questions follow a funnel logic: start with the top-level metric, break it into components (impressions, fill, engagement), isolate where the drop occurs, then look at external changes (publisher code update, seasonality, data feed issue) before assuming a product bug.

The north star framing works well for Doceree: argue that the north star for the platform is 'verified HCP engagements delivered per campaign cycle,' because it captures both sides of the marketplace in a single number without conflating raw reach with quality.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain fluency. Doceree interviewers want to hear that you understand the pharma advertising ecosystem: who the buyers are (medical affairs and marketing teams at pharma brands), who the users are (HCPs on the other side), and what compliance looks like in practice. You do not need a pharma background, but you should have read up on how HCP targeting differs from consumer advertising.

Comfort with a two-sided marketplace. The platform serves publishers and advertisers simultaneously. Interviewers probe whether you instinctively think about supply and demand together, not just one side at a time.

Data-driven thinking with honest uncertainty. Healthcare data is messier than consumer data. Interviewers respond well to candidates who name the limits of their metrics (small verified-doctor cohorts, consent constraints) rather than projecting false precision.

Stakeholder management under pressure. Pharma clients often have fast campaign cycles and strict brand safety requirements. Showing that you can manage client expectations without overpromising is valued at every seniority level.

Ownership without over-engineering. Doceree is a growth-stage company. They want PMs who will ship and iterate, not PMs who write exhaustive specs for a two-week build.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand the company and product.
Use Doceree's public website, blog, and any available case studies to map their product lines. Know what a DSP is, what a publisher network looks like, and what 'verified physician identity' means commercially. Read one or two public articles on UCPMP guidelines for pharma digital marketing in India.

Week 2: Practice domain-specific product sense.
Pick two features you noticed were missing or weak from their public product descriptions and structure them as product teardowns: who is the user, what is the job-to-be-done, what would you build, how would you measure it. Practice explaining both out loud, not just in writing.

Week 3: Sharpen behavioural answers.
Map three or four stories from your past work to the STAR format. Make sure at least one story covers a compliance or ethical trade-off, one covers a marketplace or B2B context, and one covers a data-driven decision made with incomplete information.

Week 4: Mock interviews and logistics.
Do at least two timed mock interviews with a peer or mentor. Review basic ad-tech vocabulary (CPM, fill rate, frequency capping, programmatic, header bidding at a high level). Prepare two or three questions to ask the interviewer about Doceree's roadmap priorities and how PM success is measured internally.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a consumer product interview. Doceree is B2B and operates in a regulated space. Defaulting to consumer analogies (Instagram features, e-commerce funnels) without adapting to healthcare signals poor preparation to a domain-aware interviewer.

Ignoring the supply side. Many candidates focus entirely on the pharma-brand (demand) side and forget that publishers are an equally important customer. If you only talk about campaign analytics and never mention fill rate, yield, or publisher tools, interviewers notice.

Overstating certainty on healthcare data. Saying 'we can track individual doctor behaviour across sessions' without acknowledging consent and anonymisation requirements will raise red flags with a compliance-aware interviewer.

Skipping the 'why Doceree' question. Candidates who cannot articulate what makes Doceree's physician identity graph defensible come across as unfocused. Have a specific, considered answer for why the category interests you.

Under-preparing on metrics. Doceree is a data company. If you cannot name at least three meaningful KPIs for an HCP engagement product off the top of your head, spend more time on the metrics section of your prep before you walk in.
