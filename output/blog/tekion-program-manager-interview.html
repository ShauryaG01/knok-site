01 Overview

Overview

Tekion builds cloud-native software for automotive retail, helping car dealerships manage inventory, sales, and service operations on a single platform. A Technical Program Manager at Tekion typically owns large cross-team programs, drives delivery across engineering and product, and translates complex dealership workflows into structured execution plans.

As of July 2026, knok's jobradar shows 313 open TPM roles across India. Bangalore leads with 41 openings, followed by Delhi with 14 and Pune with 13. Tekion currently has 16 open roles across its India teams. Candidates report the interview process typically spans multiple rounds covering program execution, technical depth, and cross-functional leadership.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates report most frequently from Tekion TPM interviews. They reflect Tekion's focus on automotive domain fluency, delivery execution, and stakeholder management.

  1. Walk us through a program you owned end-to-end. How did you define scope and drive delivery?
  2. Tekion serves automotive dealerships, a domain most engineers have not worked in. How do you get up to speed on an unfamiliar business domain quickly?
  3. Describe a time when engineering and product had conflicting priorities. How did you resolve it?
  4. How do you track dependencies across multiple teams working toward a shared release?
  5. Tell us about a program that slipped its timeline. What caused it and what did you personally do to recover?
  6. How do you handle a situation where an engineering lead's estimate is far larger than what leadership expects?
  7. Tekion's platform serves dealerships at scale. How have you ensured reliability or quality in a program you managed?
  8. Describe how you run a weekly program review. What do you track and what decisions come out of it?
  9. Give an example of when you had to push back on a senior stakeholder request. How did you frame it?
  10. How do you manage delivery across a distributed or remote engineering team?
  11. Walk us through your approach to risk management across a multi-team program.
  12. How do you balance technical debt reduction with feature delivery when planning a roadmap?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a program that slipped its timeline. What caused it and what did you personally do to recover?

*Situation:* I was managing a platform migration for a B2B SaaS product, coordinating several engineering teams with a committed go-live date for a major enterprise customer.

*Task:* Midway through execution, we discovered a data schema conflict that had not been caught in planning and that threatened the entire delivery.

*Action:* I immediately called a war-room with the architects and tech leads, mapped which features were blocked and which could proceed independently, and built a revised delivery plan quickly. I then negotiated with the customer to deliver a partial feature set on the original date, preserving trust, while taking a brief extension for the remainder. I also added a schema review checkpoint to our standard program template.

*Result:* The critical path shipped on the original date with no customer-facing outage. The schema checkpoint has since caught similar conflicts in later programs before they became delivery risks.

---

Q: Describe a time when engineering and product had conflicting priorities. How did you resolve it?

*Situation:* In a previous role, the engineering team wanted to refactor a core API before building the next major feature, while the product team had already committed that feature to a key client.

*Task:* I needed to help both sides reach a decision that protected the delivery commitment without creating unacceptable long-term technical risk.

*Action:* I facilitated a structured session where engineering quantified the risk of skipping the refactor in terms of future rework and potential incidents, and product shared the client commitment and its business consequences. Together we agreed on a 'thin refactor' covering the highest-risk components within the current sprint, with the full refactor planned for the following quarter.

*Result:* The feature shipped on the committed date, the client relationship was preserved, and engineering leadership confirmed the partial refactor reduced risk to an acceptable level.

---

Q: Give an example of when you had to push back on a senior stakeholder request. How did you frame it?

*Situation:* A senior business stakeholder asked to add a major reporting module to a program that was already in final integration testing.

*Task:* I needed to protect the program timeline and team focus without damaging a key relationship.

*Action:* I prepared a one-page impact brief showing the effort involved, the risk to committed features, and two alternatives: a lightweight version of the report within the current scope, or the full module as a fast-follow next quarter with dedicated focus. I presented both options and let the stakeholder choose.

*Result:* The stakeholder chose the fast-follow option. The core program shipped on time, and the reporting module launched the following quarter with better quality than a rushed in-scope version would have achieved.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Most Tekion TPM questions fall into a few categories. Knowing which structure to reach for makes your answers clearer and faster to deliver.

For delivery and execution stories, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief so you spend the bulk of your answer on Action and Result. Interviewers are judging your personal judgment and contribution, not just whether the program succeeded.

For 'how do you approach X' questions (risk tracking, dependency management, roadmap planning), open with your approach in two or three sentences, walk through the key steps, then anchor it with a real example from your experience. This shows both knowledge and practical application.

For conflict and stakeholder questions, use a three-part structure: acknowledge both sides fairly, describe how you structured the conversation or decision, and close with the outcome and what you learned. Avoid framing either side as the villain.

For technical discussions, you do not need to write code, but you should be comfortable explaining architecture trade-offs at a conceptual level. If a question goes beyond your depth, say so clearly and explain how you would involve the right engineers to reach a good answer.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report from Tekion TPM interviews, interviewers typically evaluate across four dimensions.

Domain learning speed. Tekion's core business is automotive retail, which most candidates have not worked in. Interviewers want to see that you can absorb an unfamiliar domain quickly, ask the right questions, and translate dealer workflows into program requirements. Genuine curiosity about the automotive space is a positive signal.

Cross-team influence without authority. Tekion TPMs work across engineering, product, dealer operations, and business stakeholders. Interviewers want clear evidence that you can align people and drive delivery without being a direct line manager. Stories where you influenced without authority carry more weight here than stories where you managed a team you owned.

Clarity under ambiguity. Programs at a fast-growing product company are rarely fully specified at the start. Interviewers want to hear how you define scope, surface risks early, and make structured decisions with incomplete information. Candidates who show discomfort with ambiguity tend to score lower.

Metrics and accountability. Tekion interviewers want to see that you track leading indicators (signals that a program is drifting before it misses a date), escalate early, and own outcomes rather than just facilitate meetings. Be ready to describe the specific metrics you watch in a weekly program review.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the company and the domain first

Read Tekion's public materials about their Automotive Retail Cloud. Understand how dealerships use software for vehicle inventory, F&I (finance and insurance), and service operations. Review the specific job description and map your experience to each requirement explicitly. You will be asked how you ramp up on new domains, so have a clear and honest answer ready.

Build your story bank

List several programs you have owned. For each, note the scope, the teams involved, the key risks you managed, and the outcomes. Prepare stories covering: a program that slipped and what you did, a cross-team conflict you resolved, a stakeholder pushback you navigated, and a risk you caught early. Make sure each story has a clear personal contribution rather than a team narrative.

Practise out loud

Candidates report that Tekion typically runs multiple rounds including a technical or systems discussion, a program management deep-dive, and a leadership or behavioural round. Practise each story out loud with a timer. If an answer runs long, cut the Situation and Task sections first. Your goal is to leave room for the interviewer's follow-up questions.

Prepare questions to ask

Have at least three questions ready for each round. Good questions for Tekion include: how the TPM and product manager divide ownership on a program, what the biggest delivery challenges have been in the past year, and how engineering teams are structured relative to programs.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Vague outcomes. Saying 'the program went well' without describing what changed is a red flag. Even when exact figures are confidential, you can say the program shipped on time, the team's velocity improved, or the customer renewed the contract.

Skipping your personal contribution. Many candidates describe what the team accomplished but not what they personally drove. Interviewers are checking your judgment and initiative, not the team's output. Use 'I' deliberately when describing the actions you took.

Assuming the interviewer knows your context. Do not assume the interviewer understands your previous company's org structure, technology stack, or internal terminology. Explain enough context that someone outside your company can follow the story.

Overclaiming technical depth. Tekion TPMs need enough technical fluency to work credibly with engineers. Overclaiming and then getting stuck on a follow-up is worse than saying 'I partner with architects on deep design decisions.' Be honest about where your judgment ends and your engineers' judgment begins.

Showing no curiosity about automotive retail. Candidates who ask no questions about Tekion's domain tend to score lower in culture assessments. A brief, genuine mention of what interests you about automotive software signals that you did your homework and are not just interviewing at every company in the market.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Tekion typically have for TPM roles?

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two program management or technical rounds, and a final cross-functional or leadership round. The exact structure can vary by team, level, and hiring manager. Confirm the format with your recruiter at the start of the process so you can prepare accordingly.

Does Tekion ask coding questions in TPM interviews?

Typically not deep coding, but candidates report some technical discussion covering systems design concepts, API trade-offs, or architecture decisions at a high level. You should be comfortable explaining how distributed systems work conceptually, discussing database choices, and asking sharp technical questions of engineering teams. You are not expected to write code on the spot.

Is the Tekion TPM interview more product-focused or engineering-focused?

Candidates report it leans toward program execution and cross-functional delivery rather than product strategy or feature design. Expect detailed questions about how you track progress, manage dependencies, handle delays, and resolve conflicts between engineering and business stakeholders. Pure product roadmap questions are less common in the rounds candidates describe.

What salary can I expect for a TPM role at Tekion in India?

Tekion does not publicly list salary bands for India-based TPM roles. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have some community-submitted data points for this role type, but sample sizes are small so treat those figures as rough reference only. Your actual offer will depend on your seniority level, total years of experience, and the specific team.

Where are most Tekion TPM openings located in India?

Based on knok's jobradar data as of July 2026, Tekion has 16 open roles across India. Across all TPM openings tracked in India, Bangalore leads with 41 openings, followed by Delhi with 14 and Pune with 13. If you are open to relocation, Bangalore is the strongest market for this role type.

How should I follow up after each Tekion interview round?

Send a brief, specific thank-you note to your recruiter within a day of each round, referencing something concrete from the conversation. Candidates report that response timelines vary across teams. If you have not heard back after a week, one polite follow-up email is appropriate. Avoid following up more than once per week, as it can read as pressure rather than enthusiasm.
