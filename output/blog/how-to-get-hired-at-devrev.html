01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

DevRev builds a platform that brings together product engineering and customer support in one place, helping software companies connect dev teams directly with customer feedback and real-world usage. With 40 open roles as of mid-2026, hiring is active across engineering, product, design, sales, and go-to-market functions. The company is known for a high hiring bar: expect a thorough, multi-stage process that evaluates both technical depth and how you think about customers and products.

Roles are available across locations including Bangalore and select remote-friendly positions, making DevRev one of the more active product-led SaaS employers in the Indian tech market right now. If you are targeting a role here, preparation and genuine product curiosity matter more than simply having the right keywords on your resume.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process typically runs four to six rounds, though this varies by role and team. Here is what candidates commonly report:

Round 1: Recruiter or HR screen. A short call covering your background, why you want to join DevRev, and basics like notice period and location preference. This round sets expectations on both sides.

Round 2: Technical or domain screen. For engineers, this is usually a coding round focused on data structures and algorithms. For product or GTM roles, expect a short case study or a conversation about past work and domain knowledge.

Round 3: Technical deep-dive. Engineers typically face one or two rounds covering system design and problem-solving. Interviewers want to understand how you approach ambiguous, large-scale problems, not just whether you can write correct code.

Round 4: Cross-functional or bar-raiser round. A senior team member, sometimes from a different function, evaluates how you collaborate, communicate, and handle pushback. This round is heavily focused on judgment and ownership rather than technical skill alone.

Round 5: Hiring manager or leadership. A conversation about your career goals, how you handle failure, and your depth of interest in the problem DevRev is solving. Culture alignment carries significant weight here.

Offer and background check. If all rounds go well, the recruiter shares a verbal offer followed by a formal letter. Background verification is standard.

Total calendar time from first call to offer is commonly reported as two to four weeks, though urgent or senior roles can move faster.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

DevRev hires for a specific kind of person: someone who genuinely cares about the end customer, not just the code or the product spec.

Customer obsession. DevRev's product sits at the junction of engineering and customer support. They want people who naturally ask 'how does this affect the user?' at every step, not just at the end of a sprint.

First-principles thinking. Interviewers push hard on the 'why' behind your decisions. Being able to break a problem down from scratch, rather than pattern-matching to something you have seen before, stands out clearly.

Ownership. DevRev runs lean teams. They look for people who take end-to-end responsibility for outcomes, not just their assigned slice of a task. If something breaks outside your area, they want you to care.

Cross-functional communication. Because their product bridges dev and support functions, they value people who can talk to engineers and non-technical stakeholders equally well, switching registers without losing precision.

Relevant technical depth. For engineering roles, strong fundamentals in data structures, distributed systems, and scalable backend design are expected. For product roles, experience with B2B SaaS products, especially in CRM or dev tooling, is a clear advantage.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand what DevRev actually does. Before any round, spend real time in their product or watch their demos. Interviewers notice when a candidate speaks about their platform in concrete terms versus vague generalities like 'it connects teams.'

Practice system design with a customer-centric lens. When solving a design problem in your interview, think aloud about how the system affects the end user. DevRev values this perspective more than most companies at this stage.

Prepare STAR stories around ownership and failure. Pick two or three examples from your career where you took end-to-end ownership of a messy problem. Be ready to talk about what went wrong, what you learned, and what you would do differently.

Brush up on DSA fundamentals. For engineering roles, coding rounds are standard. Focus on arrays, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. LeetCode medium difficulty is a fair benchmark based on what candidates commonly report.

Research the B2B SaaS landscape. Understand the competitive space DevRev operates in. Being able to speak intelligently about why their approach is different from others in the market shows genuine interest, not just job-hunting desperation.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

DevRev describes itself as a 'builder's company,' meaning the culture rewards people who create things, not just manage or coordinate them. A few traits that come through consistently in what people report about working there:

Fast-paced and high-ownership. Teams are small, so individuals carry real weight. If you prefer a clearly defined scope and a steady, predictable pace, this environment may feel intense at first.

Customer proximity. Unlike many B2B software companies where engineering teams are insulated from customer feedback, DevRev builds this connection into how teams work. Engineers are expected to understand customer pain directly, not just through a product manager's summary.

Transparent and direct communication. The culture values saying what you think clearly, including disagreeing with seniors when you have a strong reason. Candid feedback is seen as a sign of respect, not conflict.

Learning-heavy environment. Because the product is technically ambitious, the bar for staying current is high. People who thrive here tend to be genuinely curious about hard problems and uncomfortable with stagnation.

Global team, Indian roots. DevRev has a significant presence in India, with Bangalore as a key engineering hub, alongside offices in the US and other locations. This gives Indian hires real exposure to global product and customer contexts, which is a meaningful career advantage.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does DevRev have right now?

As of mid-2026, DevRev has 40 open roles across engineering, product, sales, and go-to-market functions. The mix skews toward technical roles, but there are positions at multiple seniority levels. Checking their careers page directly gives the most current picture since roles open and close frequently.

Is DevRev's interview process harder than a typical startup?

Candidates commonly report that DevRev's bar is closer to a mid-size product company than an early-stage startup. The technical rounds are rigorous, and the culture-fit evaluation is unusually thorough compared to many SaaS companies of similar size. If you prepare seriously across both technical and behavioral dimensions, you are in a reasonable position to clear the rounds.

Do I need prior B2B SaaS experience to get hired at DevRev?

Not strictly, but it helps for non-engineering roles like product management or sales. For engineering roles, strong fundamentals and a customer-focused mindset matter more than your specific industry background. Highlighting any past experience where you worked close to end users is a plus regardless of which domain you come from.

What is the work culture like for engineers at DevRev?

Engineers commonly describe a high-ownership, fast-moving environment where individuals work across the stack and interact directly with customer problems rather than being shielded from them. It suits people who want breadth and meaningful impact over a narrowly defined role. The tradeoff is that the pace and expectations are higher than at larger, more structured companies.

How long does the DevRev hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates commonly report the full process takes two to four weeks once interviews begin, though initial response times after applying can vary quite a bit depending on role urgency. Applying through a referral or a direct channel tends to get faster recruiter attention than applying cold through a job aggregator.

What is the best way to stand out in a DevRev interview?

Candidates who stand out tend to combine solid technical preparation with genuine interest in the DevRev product and problem space. Knowing specifically what the company is building and why their approach differs, asking sharp questions, and showing clear end-to-end ownership in your past work stories makes a real difference. Generic answers about being a 'team player' or 'fast learner' rarely land well here.
