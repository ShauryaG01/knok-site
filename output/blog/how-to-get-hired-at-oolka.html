03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Stage 1: Application and Resume Screening
Your resume goes through an initial filter, either by an ATS (applicant tracking system) or a recruiter. Make sure your resume is clean, keyword-rich for the role, and shows results rather than just responsibilities.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR Call
If your profile clears screening, a recruiter or HR person typically reaches out for a short call. These calls are commonly cited as lasting around 20-30 minutes. They want to confirm your notice period, salary expectations, and get a sense of why you want to join Oolka specifically. Be honest about your current CTC and have a clear answer ready for 'why this company.'

Stage 3: Technical or Functional Round
This is where role-specific skills are tested. For tech roles, expect coding problems or system design questions. For business or product roles, expect case-based discussions or portfolio walkthroughs. This round is typically done over a video call and may include a take-home assignment.

Stage 4: Practical Assessment (if applicable)
Many growth-stage companies ask for a short task, such as a code submission, a short product brief, or a data analysis exercise. Industry surveys suggest these are usually meant to be completed in a few hours, not days. Ask the recruiter upfront how much time is expected.

Stage 5: Final Round with Leadership or Hiring Manager
This is a deeper conversation about fit, your thinking process, and longer-term goals. The hiring manager wants to know how you solve problems and whether you will work well with the existing team. Questions here tend to be behavioral, so prepare stories using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Offer and Negotiation
If you clear all rounds, HR will call with a verbal offer before sending a formal letter. Negotiate here, not after the letter arrives. Do your homework on salary benchmarks using Glassdoor or levels.fyi before the call.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Ownership mindset. Growing companies like Oolka look for people who treat their work like it belongs to them, not people who wait to be told what to do next. In your interviews, show examples of times you went beyond your job description to solve a problem.

Relevant technical or domain skills. Depending on the role, this could mean coding ability, product thinking, growth or marketing expertise, or operational execution. Make sure your resume and portfolio reflect what the job description actually asks for.

Speed of learning. Startups and growth-stage companies move fast. Interviewers will often ask how you handled a situation where you had to learn something quickly or make a call with incomplete information. Have at least a couple of strong examples ready.

Communication clarity. The ability to explain your thinking simply, whether in writing or on a call, is valued highly. If you tend to over-explain or go in circles when answering, practice giving structured, concise answers.

Cultural alignment. Most fast-growing companies reward people who are direct, collaborative, and comfortable with ambiguity. Avoid coming across as someone who needs a lot of hand-holding.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the company deeply before any call. Go through Oolka's website, any press coverage, LinkedIn posts from their team, and product reviews if the product is public-facing. Know what the company does, who their customers are, and what problem they are solving. A specific, informed answer to 'why Oolka?' can set you apart from other candidates.

Tailor your resume to the job description. Do not send a generic resume. Pick out keywords from the job description and make sure your resume uses the same language where it is truthful. Highlight outcomes and numbers, not just duties.

Prepare for behavioral questions using STAR format. For each likely question (tell me about a failure, a time you led without authority, a time you handled conflict), write out a STAR answer in advance. Practice saying it out loud so it sounds natural, not rehearsed.

Have your salary and notice period answers ready. Know your current CTC, your expected CTC, and whether you can negotiate your notice period. Fumbling on these basics can signal poor preparation to a recruiter.

Prepare smart questions to ask. Asking about team structure, what success looks like in your first few months, and how decisions get made shows genuine interest. Avoid questions whose answers are on the website.

Do a mock interview. Practice with a friend or using an AI tool so you get comfortable with the format. Confidence and clarity in delivery matter almost as much as the content of your answers.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Oolka, like many companies in the current growth phase (2025-2026), is likely building a culture that rewards initiative, speed, and cross-functional collaboration. Based on publicly reported signals from similar-stage companies, a few patterns tend to hold.

Work is outcome-driven, not clock-driven. You are judged by what you deliver, not how many hours you log. This is great for self-starters but can be hard for those who prefer clear boundaries.

Teams are lean. With 12 open roles currently being filled, the team is still at a size where each person has real visibility and impact. That also means less hand-holding and more expectation that you will figure things out.

Feedback is direct. Most fast-growing companies have moved away from annual review cycles toward more frequent, informal feedback. Be prepared to give and receive direct input without taking it personally.

Growth is a shared goal. You will likely be expected to care about the company's growth metrics, not just your own function. Whether you are in engineering, product, or operations, understanding how your work ties to business outcomes is an asset.
