01 Overview

Overview

Hevodata is a no-code data pipeline platform that helps data teams move and transform data across hundreds of sources and destinations with minimal setup. A Solutions Engineer (SE) at Hevodata sits at the intersection of sales and engineering: you help prospects understand whether Hevodata fits their data stack, guide customers through onboarding, and relay real-world product requirements back to the team.

As of July 2026, Hevodata has 40 open roles, making it one of the more active hirers for technical, customer-facing positions in the Indian SaaS space. The SE role typically requires hands-on familiarity with data pipelines, SQL, cloud data warehouses, and the ability to run confident product demos.

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds: an initial screening call, a technical or product demo round, a case study or practical exercise, and a final conversation with the hiring team. The exact structure can vary by team and location, so confirm the details with your recruiter after your first call.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up frequently in Hevodata SE interviews, based on what candidates publicly report:

  1. Walk me through how you would set up a pipeline from a PostgreSQL source to a Snowflake destination using Hevodata.
  2. A customer's pipeline is failing with a schema drift error. How do you diagnose and resolve it?
  3. Explain the difference between ETL and ELT. When would you recommend each approach?
  4. How do you handle replication from a SaaS source like Salesforce or HubSpot to a data warehouse?
  5. A prospect's data team has just 2-3 engineers. How do you position Hevodata's value versus building custom pipelines in-house?
  6. Tell me about a time you had to explain a complex technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder.
  7. A customer needs a feature that Hevodata does not currently support. How do you handle that conversation?
  8. Describe your hands-on experience with any cloud data warehouse (BigQuery, Snowflake, or Redshift).
  9. A prospect is comparing Hevodata with Fivetran and Airbyte. How do you approach that evaluation conversation?
  10. How do you prioritize when multiple customers are escalating issues at the same time?
  11. What is Change Data Capture (CDC), and when would you recommend it to a customer?
  12. Describe a time you turned a struggling customer relationship into a positive outcome.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A customer's pipeline is failing with a schema drift error. How do you diagnose and resolve it?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a mid-size e-commerce client reported that their nightly sync from MySQL to BigQuery had stopped loading data, with error logs showing a schema mismatch.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause quickly, communicate a clear fix to the customer, and prevent the issue from recurring.

*Action:* I checked the source table and found that the client's engineering team had added two new columns without notifying the data team. I walked the customer through Hevodata's schema management settings, enabled auto-schema detection, and tested a manual re-run. I also shared a short guide on setting up alerts for future schema changes.

*Result:* The pipeline resumed within the hour. The customer mentioned they had not realized the auto-schema feature existed, and later expanded their usage to three additional connectors.

---

Q: A prospect is comparing Hevodata with a competitor. How do you approach that conversation?

*Situation:* A fintech startup was evaluating multiple data integration tools and had already completed a trial with a competitor before speaking to me.

*Task:* My goal was to understand their actual use case and show how Hevodata addressed it, not just list features.

*Action:* I asked them to walk me through their top three pain points with their current setup. Two of the three mapped directly to Hevodata's strengths: ease of setup and transparent pricing. For the third (a niche source connector they needed), I checked with the product team and confirmed it was on the roadmap. I then ran a live demo focused only on their specific use case, not a generic product tour.

*Result:* They chose Hevodata. The focused demo and honest answer about the connector timeline built more trust than a polished pitch alone would have.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you turned a struggling customer relationship into a positive outcome.

*Situation:* A SaaS company was unhappy because their pipeline latency was higher than expected, and they had escalated to my manager.

*Task:* I took ownership of the account and had to rebuild trust while also resolving the technical issue.

*Action:* I scheduled a call within the same day, acknowledged the problem without making excuses, and shared a clear action plan with timelines. Working with the engineering team, I found that the customer was on a shared connector tier not suited for their data volume. I helped them upgrade and reconfigure their pipeline settings.

*Result:* Latency dropped to an acceptable level within a week. The customer renewed their contract and later became a reference account for Hevodata.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions. Keep the Situation brief (one or two sentences), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did), and always close with a concrete Result. Interviewers want to hear what you personally did, not what the team did collectively.

Discovery before demo for technical or case rounds. Start by asking the 'customer' what problem they are trying to solve, then position the relevant feature or workflow, then show it live or walk through it step by step, and finally tie it back to their stated goal. Candidates report that interviewers respond much better to questions asked before a demo than to a one-way presentation that assumes the problem.

For product gap questions. When a customer needs something Hevodata does not currently support, the right move is to acknowledge the gap honestly, explain any available workaround or roadmap timeline, and then ask what the customer would do if that feature were available. This approach shows you can hold a difficult conversation without overselling or shutting the discussion down.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hevodata SEs work closely with sales, product, and engineering teams, so interviewers typically look for three things.

Technical credibility without being a pure engineer. You should be comfortable talking about SQL, data warehouses, and API-based connectors. You do not need to write production code, but you should be able to read an error log and explain what went wrong in plain language that a business stakeholder can follow.

Customer empathy and communication. The role involves explaining data concepts to non-technical buyers and explaining business constraints to technical teams. Candidates who can shift their language depending on who they are talking to tend to stand out in both the demo and behavioral rounds.

Ownership and follow-through. At a growing company like Hevodata, SEs often handle issues end-to-end rather than just escalating upward. Interviewers want to see that you take a problem all the way to resolution and circle back to the customer with an update.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Product and technical foundations

Set up a free trial of Hevodata and connect at least one source to a destination. Go through the documentation on schema management, CDC, and supported connectors. Review the basics of ETL vs. ELT and when each approach makes sense. Practice explaining these concepts out loud in plain language, as if talking to a non-technical stakeholder.

Week 2: Customer scenario practice

Write out STAR answers for all 12 questions listed above. Record yourself doing a mock demo of a data pipeline as if presenting to a prospect, then review where you lost momentum or went too deep into technical detail. Ask a friend or colleague to play the role of a skeptical customer and push back on your answers.

Before your interview

Read Hevodata's blog and recent product announcements to understand what has shipped recently. Prepare two or three questions for the interviewer about the team's biggest customer challenges and how success is measured in the SE role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving a generic demo. Candidates who walk through every feature without connecting it to a specific use case lose the interviewer's attention quickly. Always tie what you are showing to a stated problem from the 'customer.'

Overselling or hiding product gaps. If a feature does not exist, saying so clearly and explaining what does exist or what the roadmap looks like builds far more credibility than a vague or evasive answer.

Treating behavioral questions as optional. Some candidates prepare only for technical questions and then struggle with 'tell me about a time' prompts. Hevodata SEs work with customers daily, so behavioral rounds carry real weight in the hiring decision.

Not asking discovery questions. Jumping straight into a presentation in the demo or case round without first asking the 'customer' about their situation is a red flag. It signals that you would do the same with a real customer.

Underestimating the SQL component. Candidates report that some interview loops include a practical SQL or data modeling exercise. Brush up on window functions, joins, and reading query plans before your technical round.

How many rounds does the Hevodata Solutions Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs 3-4 rounds, though this can vary by team and hiring manager. You can expect some combination of a screening call, a technical or product demo round, a case study or take-home exercise, and a final culture or hiring-manager conversation. Always confirm the exact structure with your recruiter after your first call.

Do I need to know how to code for the Hevodata SE role?

You do not need to write production-level code, but technical fluency matters. Candidates report being asked to read error logs, write basic SQL queries, and walk through data pipeline configurations. Being comfortable with SQL, REST APIs, and cloud data warehouses like Snowflake or BigQuery will put you in a strong position for the technical round.
