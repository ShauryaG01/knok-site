01 Overview

Overview

Sardine is a fraud prevention and compliance platform used by fintechs, crypto exchanges, and digital banks to stop payment fraud, detect synthetic identities, and manage KYC/AML risk in real time. As of mid-2026, knok jobradar tracked 35 open roles at Sardine, reflecting active growth across product and engineering.

The PM role at Sardine sits at the intersection of financial crime, machine learning infrastructure, and developer-facing API tooling. Unlike a typical consumer or SaaS PM role, you are expected to reason about false positive tradeoffs, model explainability, regulatory timelines, and the developer experience of a risk SDK. Candidates typically report that interviewers probe both product sense and domain depth in payments, fraud, or compliance.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report questions across four broad themes at Sardine: product strategy and roadmap, fraud domain knowledge, cross-functional collaboration, and metrics design. Here are the most commonly seen questions.

  1. How would you prioritize the roadmap for Sardine's fraud signals product? Interviewers want to see how you weigh customer impact against model complexity and compliance requirements.
  1. How do you measure the success of a fraud detection feature? They test whether you can define metrics beyond 'fewer fraud cases' and reason about precision, recall, and user friction together.
  1. Walk me through a product you built that relied on ML or data science models. A standard but important check on your ability to work with technical uncertainty and ship probabilistic products.
  1. Sardine sells to both lean fintechs and large enterprise banks. How do you handle conflicting requirements from these two segments?
  1. A key customer says Sardine is blocking too many legitimate users. How do you diagnose and respond? This tests systematic thinking and customer communication under pressure.
  1. How would you design the onboarding experience for a new fintech integrating Sardine's SDK? This probes developer empathy and time-to-value thinking.
  1. How do you explain a model-based risk decision to a non-technical compliance officer? Communication across technical and non-technical stakeholders is core to the role.
  1. If Sardine wanted to expand from payment fraud into account takeover detection, how would you scope the initial product? A market entry and scoping question specific to Sardine's domain.
  1. How do you balance shipping quickly with compliance and audit requirements in a financial crime product?
  1. Tell me about a time you had to kill a feature or change direction mid-development. What led to that decision?
  1. How would you build a feedback loop so that fraud analysts can improve model performance over time? This tests your understanding of the human-in-the-loop model improvement cycle.
  1. What metrics would you use to evaluate Sardine's developer experience, and how would you improve them?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three STAR-format sample answers for high-frequency Sardine PM questions. Adapt these to your own experience. Candidates report that specificity and a clear business result matter more than polished delivery.

---

Q: Walk me through a product you built that relied on ML models.

*Situation:* Our payments platform was seeing a rise in chargebacks from a specific merchant category, and the existing rule-based system was not catching the pattern reliably.

*Task:* I was the PM responsible for working with data science to ship an improved risk scoring layer before the next quarterly compliance review.

*Action:* I ran discovery sessions with our fraud analysts to document the patterns they were flagging manually. I translated those into feature requests for the model team, agreed on a champion-challenger evaluation framework so we could test the new model against the incumbent safely, and worked with engineering to build an analyst override UI so edge cases could feed back into the training pipeline.

*Result:* The new scoring layer went live on schedule. Fraud analysts reported a measurable reduction in manual reviews per week, and the compliance team cleared the quarterly review with no escalations.

---

Q: A key customer says Sardine is blocking too many legitimate users. What do you do?

*Situation:* A fintech client flagged that their user activation rate had dropped after we tightened fraud thresholds following a broader fraud spike in their market.

*Task:* My job was to diagnose whether this was a model issue, a rule misconfiguration, or an intentional tradeoff, and recommend a path forward without exposing the client to new fraud risk.

*Action:* I pulled the decision logs for the affected user cohort with the data team, segmented by device type and transaction value, and found that a new device fingerprinting rule was over-indexing on a mobile OS version common in that customer's user base. I proposed a time-bound threshold relaxation for that segment with daily monitoring, and looped in the customer's risk lead to agree on acceptance criteria before any change went live.

*Result:* The customer's activation rate recovered within two weeks. The adjusted rule was later promoted to production after we confirmed no uplift in actual fraud for that cohort.

---

Q: How do you balance shipping quickly with compliance requirements?

*Situation:* We were building a new KYC feature for a banking client with a hard deadline tied to a central bank directive.

*Task:* I needed to ship a compliant, working feature in time for the regulatory audit while managing existing technical debt in the identity verification module.

*Action:* I worked with legal and compliance early to document the minimum viable controls the audit required. I then separated 'audit-required' scope from 'improvement' scope and ran two parallel tracks: one team focused on the compliant core, another continued tech debt work at a slower pace. I set up a weekly sync with the compliance officer to surface new requirements before they became late scope surprises.

*Result:* We passed the audit on the first submission, and the tech debt track caught up in the following quarter without any compliance risk accumulating.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline. Keep Situation and Task brief, invest the most detail in Action, and anchor Result to a business or user outcome rather than just 'it worked.'

Product sense structure for design questions. Start with who the user is (fraud analyst, developer integrating the SDK, compliance officer), what job they are trying to do, then move to solutions and tradeoffs. Sardine interviewers typically want you to name the tension between fraud catch rate and user friction early in your answer, not at the end.

Precision-recall framing for metrics questions. When asked about success metrics or tradeoffs, show you understand that every threshold change shifts false positives and false negatives in opposite directions. Frame your metric choices around who bears the cost of each error type: the end user, the merchant, or the platform. This signals fluency in fraud PM thinking that generic frameworks miss.

Stakeholder mapping for cross-functional questions. At Sardine, PMs typically work simultaneously with fraud analysts, data scientists, compliance lawyers, and external developer-customers. When answering 'tell me about a complex stakeholder situation,' name each party's incentive explicitly before describing your action. This shows structured thinking rather than storytelling.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain fluency, not fraud expertise. Sardine interviewers are not expecting you to know every fraud vector on day one. They want evidence that you have engaged seriously with financial crime, payments, or risk products. Vague answers about 'reducing risk' without showing you understand the operational mechanics are a common red flag.

Probabilistic thinking. Fraud detection is inherently uncertain. Interviewers want to see you reason about confidence levels, model errors, and threshold tradeoffs rather than looking for binary right-or-wrong answers. Show comfort with 'good enough with monitoring' over 'perfect before shipping.'

Developer empathy. A significant part of Sardine's product is an API and SDK consumed by developers at other companies. Candidates who can articulate what good developer experience means, including clear documentation, low time-to-first-call, and useful error messages, stand out from those who only think about the fraud analyst persona.

Structured communication. Fraud and compliance products require PMs to explain risk decisions to executives, legal teams, customers, and engineers in very different ways. Interviewers typically watch for candidates who visibly adapt their communication style during the interview itself.

Data-first instincts. Candidates who reach for data early in their answers, even to say 'the first thing I would check is X metric,' signal that they will not ship on intuition alone in a domain where errors have direct financial consequences.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Build domain context first.
Read Sardine's public documentation and engineering blog to understand how they describe device intelligence, behavioral biometrics, and rules-based fraud engines to developers and customers. Review public materials on payment fraud patterns, including RBI advisories if you have a background in Indian fintech. You do not need to become a fraud expert; you need to speak the language.

Practice product sense with fraud scenarios.
Work through 4-5 product sense questions with fraud or compliance contexts. For each, practice naming the user persona, the core tradeoff, and at least two metrics before jumping to solutions. Record yourself and check whether you are using domain language naturally or falling back on generic frameworks.

Prepare your STAR stories.
Map your past experience to the themes interviewers focus on: ML model collaboration, cross-functional stakeholder management, compliance or regulatory constraints, and customer escalations. Prepare at least five STAR stories and check that each one ends with a concrete, business-level result.

Prepare sharp questions for each round.
Candidates report that Sardine interviewers appreciate specific questions over generic ones. Prepare two questions per interviewer based on their role. A fraud analyst PM might ask how false positive budgets are set per customer. A data science PM might ask about model retraining cadence and who owns that decision.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating a fraud PM interview like a standard SaaS PM interview. Generic product sense frameworks without fraud context will not impress interviewers with deep domain expertise. Adapt your tradeoff language to risk products before you walk in.

Ignoring the developer persona. Many candidates focus only on the fraud analyst or compliance officer as the user. Sardine's API-first model means the developer integrating the SDK is an equally important user, and missing that persona signals a product thinking gap.

Presenting metrics in isolation. Saying 'I would measure fraud rate' without discussing the tradeoff with false positives reads as shallow. Always pair a metric with what you are willing to accept getting worse in order to improve it.

Over-claiming ML expertise. Candidates sometimes oversell their data science knowledge and get caught in follow-up questions. It is better to say 'I do not build models, but I know how to partner with the team to define features, set up evaluations, and interpret results' than to imply you can do the data science work yourself.

Skipping the 'why Sardine' preparation. Interviewers typically ask why you want to work in fraud or compliance specifically. A vague answer about fintech excitement is weaker than a specific answer tied to why the problem of financial crime matters to you personally or professionally.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Sardine typically have for PM roles?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, one or two product or domain-focused interviews, a cross-functional or leadership interview, and sometimes a case study round. The exact number of rounds can vary by level and team. It is worth asking your recruiter upfront what the structure looks like so you can prepare the right material for each round.

Does Sardine give a take-home case study for PM interviews?

Some candidates report receiving a short written or verbal case exercise, though this is not universal across all PM roles at Sardine. The case, when given, typically involves a product or metrics scenario relevant to fraud or compliance. Prepare for both a live case discussion and a take-home format so you are not caught off guard either way.

What background do successful Sardine PM candidates typically have?

Candidates who land PM roles at Sardine typically come from payments, fintech, fraud, compliance, or developer-tooling backgrounds. A track record of shipping API products or working closely with data science teams is commonly cited as a strong signal. Domain knowledge is valued, but strong product sense and communication skills matter equally in the interview process.

How important is knowledge of Indian payments like UPI or NEFT for this role?

Sardine operates globally, so deep knowledge of Indian payment rails is not a strict requirement for most PM roles. However, if you are based in India and the role covers the Indian market, familiarity with UPI fraud patterns and RBI compliance requirements is a genuine differentiator. Check the job description carefully to see which markets and product areas the role is focused on.

What is the salary range for PM roles at Sardine in India?

Based on knok jobradar data for mid-2026, PM salaries in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level up to 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level. Mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience typically fall in the 24-40 LPA band. Actual compensation at Sardine also includes equity, which can be meaningful at a growth-stage company.

How long does the Sardine PM hiring process usually take from application to offer?

Candidates report the full process typically spans a few weeks from first recruiter contact to offer, though timelines can stretch depending on team bandwidth and how many interviewers are involved. Following up politely after each round is standard practice. If you have a competing offer with a deadline, let the recruiter know early so they can try to align timelines on their end.
