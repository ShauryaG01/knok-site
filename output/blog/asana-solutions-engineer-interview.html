01 Overview

Overview

Asana, the work-management platform trusted by enterprise teams worldwide, hires Solutions Engineers to be the technical backbone of its go-to-market motion. As an SE at Asana, you run product demos, handle technical objections, support proofs of concept, and partner closely with Account Executives to close mid-market and enterprise deals.

Asana currently lists 164 open roles globally, signalling active hiring across its sales teams. The Solutions Engineer position is among the most competitive because it sits at the crossroads of product knowledge, consultative selling, and customer empathy.

Candidates typically report a multi-stage process: a recruiter screen to check the basics, a hiring manager conversation focused on your pre-sales or solutions experience, a demo or presentation round where you show the product to a simulated prospect, and a values or panel interview. Some candidates also report a take-home case study. The process is built to test both your technical fluency and your ability to make complex ideas feel simple to a business audience.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in Asana SE interviews, based on what candidates report:

  1. Walk me through how you would run a discovery call with a net-new enterprise prospect.
  2. How do you handle a situation where a prospect asks for a feature Asana does not currently offer?
  3. Describe your process for building a customised product demo for a specific industry or use case.
  4. Tell me about a time you partnered with an Account Executive to navigate a complex, multi-stakeholder deal.
  5. How have you explained a technical concept to a non-technical executive who was the final decision-maker?
  6. How would you position Asana against a competitor such as Monday.com, Smartsheet, or Microsoft Project?
  7. Describe a proof-of-concept or pilot that did not go as planned. What did you do, and what happened?
  8. How do you manage your time when multiple active deals each demand your full attention?
  9. Asana places strong emphasis on clarity, mindfulness, and purposeful work. How does that align with how you approach your role?
  10. How do you stay current with product updates and turn them into compelling customer value stories?
  11. Tell me about a time you converted a sceptical stakeholder into a strong internal advocate for your solution.
  12. What is your approach to post-demo follow-up to maintain momentum without being pushy?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you handle it when a prospect asks for a feature Asana does not have?

*Situation:* At my previous company I was mid-demo with a large logistics firm when the VP of Operations asked whether the platform could auto-generate reports directly from a third-party ERP system, a capability we did not natively support at the time.

*Task:* I needed to be honest without losing the deal, and find a credible path forward that kept the conversation alive.

*Action:* I paused the demo and told the prospect I wanted to give them an accurate picture rather than overpromise. I acknowledged the gap, then opened the API documentation live to show the integration surface, and highlighted a certified partner integration that covered the core requirement. I also connected the product manager with the prospect directly so the requirement could be logged as formal product feedback.

*Result:* The prospect said the transparency was refreshing. They agreed to a pilot using the partner integration, which addressed the bulk of their requirement. The deal progressed and closed within that quarter.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you explained a technical concept to a non-technical executive.

*Situation:* A CFO at a professional-services firm wanted to understand why migrating from spreadsheets to a work-management platform would reduce project overruns, but she had no appetite for technical detail.

*Task:* I had to translate a platform capability into a financial outcome she could defend to her board.

*Action:* I asked her to describe one recent project that had overrun, then mapped each delay back to a specific information gap, such as not knowing a task was blocked until it was too late. I then showed only the relevant Asana features (task dependencies and status roll-ups) using her own project as example data. I avoided acronyms entirely and framed every feature as an answer to 'who owns this, and is it on track?'

*Result:* She told the Account Executive after the meeting that the demo 'finally made sense'. She became the internal sponsor and championed the deal through legal and procurement with minimal follow-up needed from our side.

---

Q: Describe a pilot that did not go as planned and what you did.

*Situation:* I was running a pilot for a manufacturing company when, halfway through, the primary champion left the organisation and the new contact had no context about why the pilot had been started.

*Task:* I needed to re-establish value quickly with someone who had no buy-in and a full workload of her own.

*Action:* Rather than resending the original pitch deck, I scheduled a short working session with the new contact to understand her own priorities first. I rebuilt the success criteria around her goals, not the previous champion's. I created a one-page summary of the pilot results so far in plain language, tied to the metrics she cared about.

*Result:* She became genuinely interested and extended the pilot to a second phase. The deal ultimately closed, and she later referred us to another division within the same company.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Most Asana SE interviews include several 'tell me about a time' questions. Structure every answer as: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you did, step by step), Result (what changed because of your actions). Keep Situation and Task short. Spend most of your time on Action.

Demo structure for the presentation round. Candidates report that Asana typically provides a fictional prospect brief and asks you to demo the product. A reliable structure: open with a discovery question to confirm the prospect's pain, then show only the features that map to that pain, and close by connecting what you showed back to a business outcome. Avoid feature-touring. Asana interviewers watch for candidates who lead with customer problems, not product capabilities.

Objection handling: acknowledge, pivot, prove. When a prospect (or an interviewer playing a prospect) raises a concern, do three things in order. First, acknowledge the concern without being defensive. Second, pivot to the closest relevant capability or workaround. Third, prove it by showing something live or citing a customer story. This structure works especially well for gap-feature and competitive-positioning questions.

Competitive positioning: lead with differentiation, not criticism. For questions about Monday.com, Smartsheet, or Microsoft Project, frame your answer around what Asana does distinctly well (workflow automation, goal-setting with Portfolios, native reporting) rather than criticising competitors. Interviewers at Asana value candidates who can make a positive case.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Asana SE interviewers look for a specific combination of skills and cultural alignment.

Customer-first thinking. Every answer should show that you start with what the customer needs, not with what the product can do. Interviewers notice quickly when a candidate defaults to feature-listing instead of problem-solving.

Technical credibility without jargon. You do not need to be a software engineer, but you should understand APIs, integrations, and data flows well enough to hold a conversation with a technical buyer. More importantly, you should be able to translate that knowledge into plain language for a business audience.

Comfort with ambiguity. Enterprise deals are messy. Stakeholders change, requirements shift, timelines slip. Interviewers want to see that you stay calm and resourceful when things do not go to plan. The pilot-gone-wrong question is specifically designed to test this.

Asana values in practice. Asana publicly emphasises clarity, mindfulness, and doing less to achieve more. Candidates who reflect these values naturally, rather than reciting them, tend to progress further. Think about moments in your career where you simplified a complex situation or created genuine clarity for a team.

Strong AE collaboration. The SE and Account Executive relationship is central to this role. Be ready to discuss how you have worked with sales counterparts, divided responsibilities, and handled disagreements professionally.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: learn the product deeply. Sign up for an Asana free trial and spend time in every major feature area including Projects, Tasks, Portfolios, Goals, Automations, and Reporting. Read Asana's own customer case studies (available on their website) to build a library of real examples you can reference during the demo round.

Week two: practise discovery and demo. Record yourself running a mock discovery call and a demo using the fictional-brief format. Watch the recording and cut any moment where you talk about a feature before establishing a customer problem. Ask a friend or colleague to play a sceptical prospect and raise objections.

Week three: prepare your stories. Map your past experience to the questions listed above. Write out a STAR answer for each one. Practise saying them aloud until they sound natural, not rehearsed. Prepare one strong example each for: a gap-feature situation, a technical-to-non-technical translation, a failed pilot or deal, and a stakeholder you turned around.

Before the interview: Research Asana's recent product announcements and any publicly reported customer wins in your target industry. Prepare two or three sharp questions for the hiring manager that show you understand the SE role specifically, not just the company in general.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping discovery in the demo round. Many candidates dive straight into showing features. Asana interviewers specifically watch for this. Always open with a question that surfaces the prospect's pain before you touch the product.

Overpromising on gaps. If an interviewer playing a prospect asks for something Asana cannot do, saying 'yes we can handle that' is an immediate red flag. Transparency and creative problem-solving around a gap is far more impressive than a hollow promise.

Generic answers without Asana context. Saying 'I am great at demos' without showing you understand what makes an Asana demo different (goal-to-work alignment, portfolio visibility, cross-team dependencies) signals you have not done your homework.

Ignoring the values round. Treating the cultural-fit interview as less important than the technical round is a common mistake. Asana places significant weight on values alignment. Candidates who treat the values interview as a formality often do not advance.

Talking too much. Solutions Engineers need to listen. If your answers consistently run long without pausing to check for understanding, that behaviour will register as a concern during the process. Practise giving concise answers and inviting dialogue.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

What is the Asana Solutions Engineer role actually like day to day?

An SE at Asana spends most of their time on customer-facing activities: discovery calls, product demos, technical deep-dives, and managing proofs of concept. You work closely with Account Executives on active deals and may also support marketing events or customer advisory sessions. The role is heavily cross-functional and requires constant coordination with product, customer success, and sales leadership. It suits people who enjoy variety and can context-switch quickly across different industries and buyer personas.

How should I prepare for the demo round if I have never used Asana before?

Sign up for the free version and spend at least a week working through every major feature area. Focus especially on Portfolios, Goals, and Automations, as these tend to differentiate Asana in enterprise conversations. Read the customer stories on Asana's website so you have real examples to reference. Practise running the demo out loud, not just clicking through the product silently, because how you narrate matters as much as what you show.

Does Asana ask coding or SQL questions in Solutions Engineer interviews?

Candidates typically report that coding tests are not a standard part of the Asana SE process. However, you may be asked about APIs, webhook concepts, or how you would approach a custom integration for a prospect. Being conversational about technical concepts matters more than writing code. Reviewing Asana's developer documentation before your interview is a practical way to prepare for these questions.

How competitive is it to get an SE role at Asana?

Asana currently lists 164 open roles globally, and Solutions Engineer positions attract strong interest from candidates with SaaS and enterprise sales backgrounds. The process is competitive because the role requires a blend of technical depth, consultative selling, and cultural fit. Thorough product knowledge and a strong demo performance are the most commonly cited differentiators by candidates who have received offers, based on publicly reported interview experiences.

What salary can I expect as a Solutions Engineer at Asana in India?

Asana's India-based compensation figures are commonly reported on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, though sample sizes are small and figures vary significantly by city, experience level, and seniority. Check those platforms directly for the most current publicly reported ranges. It is worth verifying any figure you find against the offer you receive, since publicly reported data can lag actual market rates by several months.

How long does the Asana interview process typically take from application to offer?

Candidates report that timelines vary, but a typical process from first recruiter screen to final decision spans several weeks. Timelines can stretch when scheduling across multiple interviewers is complex or when the hiring team is evaluating several finalists simultaneously. Staying responsive to interview requests and following up politely after each stage is commonly cited advice from candidates who have successfully navigated the process.
