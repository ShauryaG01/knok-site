01 Overview

Overview

Hyreo is a recruitment technology company that builds hiring automation and candidate experience products. Recruiters use hyreo to manage pipelines, automate communication, and track applicants. Job seekers get visibility into where they stand in the hiring process. As of July 2026, hyreo has 96 open roles, signaling active product investment and real design needs across its platform.

Product Designers at hyreo typically work on applicant-facing flows, recruiter dashboards, interview scheduling tools, and notification systems. Because the product sits in HR-tech, good design here means making complex, multi-step hiring workflows feel intuitive for two very different audiences: tech-savvy recruiters and first-time job applicants.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect what candidates report hearing in Product Designer interviews at companies like hyreo, given the nature of HR-tech product work. Expect a mix of portfolio walkthroughs, behavioral questions, and product thinking scenarios.

  1. Walk me through a product you owned end-to-end. What was the problem and how did you know your solution worked?
  2. Hyreo serves both recruiters and job seekers. How do you make design decisions when these two groups have conflicting needs?
  3. Describe a flow you redesigned. What triggered the redesign and what changed as a result?
  4. How do you approach designing for users with low digital literacy, such as first-time job applicants on a mobile device?
  5. Tell me about a feature you shipped that did not land as expected. What happened and what did you do next?
  6. How do you work with product managers when requirements are vague or keep changing mid-sprint?
  7. Walk me through how you would build a design system component from scratch. What decisions would you make along the way?
  8. How do you decide which user pain point to fix first when engineering bandwidth is limited?
  9. Describe a time you used data, such as analytics or usability test findings, to change a design decision you had already made.
  10. How do you handle a stakeholder who wants to keep adding features to an already complex screen?
  11. If you joined hyreo tomorrow, what is one thing you would improve in the candidate-facing experience? Walk me through your thinking.
  12. How do you stay updated on design trends and translate them into practical improvements for a B2B SaaS product?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format for all behavioral questions. Here are three worked examples you can adapt for your own experience.

Q: Walk me through a product you designed end-to-end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, job applicants were dropping off midway through a multi-step application form. Analytics showed a steep completion drop at step 3 of 5, but we did not know the reason.

*Task:* I was asked to redesign the application flow to improve completion. I owned the project from discovery to launch, working with one PM and two engineers.

*Action:* I ran five user interviews with recent applicants and found that step 3 asked for a work history going back ten years, which many junior applicants simply did not have. I proposed splitting the form into two paths: one for freshers and one for experienced candidates. I built low-fidelity prototypes, tested them with eight users, iterated twice, and handed off a Figma spec with interaction notes.

*Result:* After launch, the team observed a meaningful improvement in form completion. The initial data came from a small sample, so the final picture took a few weeks to solidify, but the direction was positive.

---

Q: Describe a time data changed a design decision you had already made.

*Situation:* I had designed a recruiter dashboard that used cards to display candidate profiles. It looked clean in Figma and stakeholders approved it.

*Task:* Before shipping, the team ran a quick usability test to validate the layout with actual users.

*Action:* Three of five testers said they wanted to see more candidates at once without scrolling. They were used to spreadsheet-style views and found cards 'too slow to scan.' I switched to a dense table layout, preserved the card view as an optional toggle, and re-tested with four of the same users.

*Result:* All four preferred the table as the default. The PM agreed to ship it that way. It was a useful reminder that what looks polished in a design tool may not match how power users actually work.

---

Q: Tell me about a feature that did not perform as expected after launch.

*Situation:* I designed an in-app notification centre intended to reduce email overload for recruiters.

*Task:* The goal was to move a large share of email notifications into the app within the first two months after launch.

*Action:* I designed the centre, collaborated on the copy, and the team shipped on schedule. After launch, I monitored engagement in our analytics tool and found that recruiters were still relying on email and largely ignoring in-app notifications.

*Result:* We did not hit our adoption target. Follow-up interviews revealed that recruiters often work across many tabs and rarely keep the app in the foreground. We added a browser notification option in the next sprint, which improved adoption. The lesson was that 'in-app' assumes the app is always open, and for this user type, it was not.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The Double Diamond for design thinking questions. When asked 'how would you approach X,' walk the interviewer through Discover (research, user interviews, analytics review), Define (problem framing, 'How might we' questions), Develop (ideation, prototyping, testing), and Deliver (handoff, launch, measurement). This shows structured thinking without overcomplicating the answer.

Jobs-to-be-Done for user empathy questions. Instead of describing users by demographics, describe what they are trying to accomplish. For hyreo's context, a recruiter's job is 'find the right candidate before my competitor does,' and a job seeker's job is 'know where I stand so I can plan my next steps.' Framing answers this way signals product maturity.

STAR for all behavioral questions. Situation (context), Task (what you were responsible for), Action (specifically what you did, not 'we'), Result (what changed, and be honest if results were mixed or the sample was small). Keep each component to two or three sentences.

Why-What-How for portfolio walkthroughs. Start with Why (the business or user problem), then What (the design solution), then How (your specific process). Many candidates jump straight to How, skipping the problem context that makes the interviewer care about the solution.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Process, not just polish. Interviewers at product companies like hyreo typically care more about how you think than how your final screens look. Bring annotated Figma files or process slides that show your messy early iterations alongside the final design.

Comfort with two-sided products. Hyreo's platform serves both recruiters and job seekers. Interviewers want to see that you understand how decisions made for one group affect the other, and that you can articulate those tradeoffs clearly.

Data-informed design thinking. You do not need to be a data analyst, but you should be able to talk about how analytics, usability test findings, or user feedback shaped your decisions. Vague answers like 'users liked it' are a common red flag.

Collaboration stories. Product Designers at SaaS companies work closely with PMs and engineers. Expect questions about how you handle disagreements, unclear requirements, and scope changes. Interviewers want to see that you can work in ambiguity without stalling.

Genuine curiosity about hyreo's product. Candidates who have explored hyreo's actual product before the interview tend to stand out. Prepare one specific observation about the candidate experience and a direction you would explore to improve it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the problem space.
Sign up for hyreo or explore any publicly available demo. Map out the key flows for both a recruiter and a job seeker. Write down three things that work well and three that feel confusing. This research becomes direct material for your 'what would you improve' answer.

Week 2: Prepare your portfolio story.
Select two or three projects that best fit hyreo's context: SaaS products, two-sided platforms, or tools with complex workflows. For each project, prepare a five-minute walkthrough using the Why-What-How structure. Practise it out loud until you can do it without reading from notes.

Week 3: Practise STAR answers.
Write out STAR answers for at least six behavioral questions from the list above. Focus on specificity: name the actual tool you used, the number of users you tested with (even if small), and what you learned. Practise with a friend or record yourself and review.

Week 4: Simulate the interview.
Do a full mock interview covering one portfolio walkthrough, two behavioral questions, and one product thinking question. Candidates report rounds typically run roughly an hour, and pacing matters. Ask for feedback on clarity of reasoning, not just delivery style.

On the day: Have your Figma or portfolio open in a browser tab before the call. Jot down the interviewer's exact question before answering. That small pause prevents you from answering a question that was not actually asked.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the problem statement. Many candidates open their portfolio by showing screens before explaining the problem they were solving. If the interviewer does not understand why the design mattered, the screens have no context.

Saying 'we' instead of 'I.' Interviewers want to know what you specifically did. 'We ran user interviews' becomes 'I recruited participants, wrote the discussion guide, and led five sessions.' Own your contribution clearly.

Overstating results. Claiming a design improved key metrics when the sample was small or the test ran for only a couple of weeks is a credibility risk. Saying 'early data was positive but the sample was small' is a stronger answer than an inflated claim.

Ignoring the recruiter user. Hyreo's primary paying customer is typically the recruiter, not the job seeker. Candidates who focus only on the candidate-facing experience in their answers may signal a mismatch with the product's business reality.

Not having questions ready. Ending an interview with 'I think you covered everything' is a missed opportunity. Prepare two or three genuine questions about the design team's structure, how success is measured for design work, or what the biggest unsolved UX challenge is right now.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the hyreo Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four rounds. This commonly includes an initial screening call, a portfolio presentation, a design exercise or case study, and a final discussion with a senior stakeholder or team lead. Round structure and order can vary, so confirm the format with your recruiter contact after your first call.

Is there a design assignment or take-home task?

Many product companies at hyreo's stage include a design exercise as part of the process. Candidates report being given a brief related to a hiring or candidate experience problem and asked to present their thinking within a few days. Focus on showing your process clearly, including your problem framing and any assumptions you made, rather than producing overly polished visuals.

What salary can a Product Designer expect at hyreo?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, Product Designer roles in India range from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years) to 14-24 LPA at mid-level (3-5 years), 26-40 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 36-55+ LPA at lead or principal level. Actual offers depend on your experience, interview performance, and the specific role. It is reasonable to ask your recruiter for the salary band before your final round.

Does hyreo prefer candidates with HR-tech experience specifically?

HR-tech experience is a plus but not a strict requirement, based on what candidates report from hiring conversations. What matters more is experience with two-sided SaaS products, complex workflows, or enterprise tools where multiple user types interact with the same system. If you have designed for marketplaces, scheduling tools, or communication platforms, make those parallels explicit in your interview.

How important is the portfolio versus the interview conversation?

Both matter, but candidates report that the reasoning behind design decisions carries as much weight as the visual quality of the portfolio. Interviewers typically pause the portfolio walkthrough to ask 'why did you make this choice?' or 'what else did you consider?' Come prepared to speak to the tradeoffs in every design decision, not just the final output.

Are hyreo's Product Designer roles remote or office-based?

Hyreo has the most openings in Bangalore, with roles also in Delhi and Mumbai based on current job data. Whether specific roles are hybrid or fully in-office varies by team. Confirm the work model with your recruiter early in the process so there are no surprises at the offer stage.
