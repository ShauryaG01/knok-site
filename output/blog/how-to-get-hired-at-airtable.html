01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Airtable is a San Francisco-based platform that lets teams build custom apps, workflows, and databases without writing code. Its product sits at the intersection of spreadsheets, databases, and no-code automation, and it is widely used by product, operations, marketing, and data teams across mid-size and enterprise companies.

As of July 2026, Airtable has 39 open roles, spread across engineering, product, sales, marketing, and customer success. After going through a period of focused restructuring in recent years, the company has returned to selective hiring. Roles tend to be senior-leaning, the interview bar is high, and Airtable moves at a measured pace. If you are applying, expect a thorough process and budget a few weeks from first call to offer.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Airtable's process typically runs across several stages. Details can vary by team and role, so treat this as a general map.

Application and resume screen. Airtable's recruiters look closely at your resume for relevant domain experience and signal that you understand the no-code or workflow-tools space. A tailored resume with measurable impact does better than a generic one.

Recruiter screening call. If your resume clears the screen, a recruiter reaches out for a brief introductory call. They will ask about your background, what you are looking for, and why Airtable. Be ready to explain your interest in the product specifically, not just the company name.

Hiring manager conversation. This is a deeper discussion about your past work, how you operate, and how your experience maps to the role. Expect questions about scope, ownership, and cross-functional collaboration. The manager wants to see how you think, not just what you have done.

Skills assessment or take-home. For technical and product roles, there is usually a practical exercise. Engineers get a coding or system design challenge. Product managers often get a product case. These are meant to be realistic work samples, so read the prompt carefully and show your reasoning throughout.

Final panel interviews. The last stage involves several conversations with future peers and cross-functional stakeholders. Interviewers look at both skill depth and how you would work with the team day to day. Expect at least one behavioral round focused on values and working style.

Feedback and offer decisions usually come within a week or two of the final panel, though timelines vary by team.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Airtable hires generalists who think like owners. A few traits stand out across roles.

Deep craft with broad context. Engineers are expected to write great code and understand the product impact of their work. PMs are expected to have strong product instincts and be able to work closely with technical teams. Airtable does not want people who stay strictly in their lane.

Customer empathy. Airtable's users range from non-technical operations managers to developers building internal tools. The company values candidates who can hold both perspectives and design or build for real-world use cases, not just ideal ones.

Clear, structured communication. Airtable has a strong writing culture. In interviews, answers that are concise and well-structured leave a better impression than long, meandering ones.

Ownership over execution. They want people who identify problems without being asked, propose solutions, and follow through. In behavioral interviews, stories where you waited to be told what to do tend to score poorly.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know the product deeply. Sign up for Airtable if you have not already. Build something with it, even a simple project tracker or CRM. Interviewers notice candidates who speak about the product from real use rather than from a marketing page.

Prepare structured stories. For behavioral rounds, prepare several stories using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Pick stories that show ownership, cross-functional impact, and dealing with ambiguity. Include at least one story about a failure or setback and what you learned.

Study the role description carefully. Airtable writes detailed job descriptions. Map your experience to the specific responsibilities and requirements listed. If a skill is mentioned more than once, it matters a great deal.

Practice product thinking (even for non-PM roles). For engineering and design candidates, Airtable often asks product-flavored questions. Practice articulating why a feature exists, who it is for, and how you would improve it.

Prepare smart questions. At each stage, asking specific questions about the team's roadmap, working norms, or recent challenges signals genuine interest. Generic questions are acceptable, but specific ones stand out.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Airtable operates with a builder's mentality. The people who thrive there are curious, structured in how they communicate, and motivated by seeing their work reach real users.

Transparency and directness. Airtable has a reputation for candid internal communication. Employees are expected to share opinions clearly, flag problems early, and give direct feedback. If you prefer a more hierarchical or deferential culture, it may feel uncomfortable at first.

Thoughtful, not fast. Unlike companies that prize shipping at all costs, Airtable tends to value careful product decisions. This shows up in interviews too: they would rather you pause and think than rush to an answer.

Cross-functional by default. Engineers talk to customers. Salespeople sit in product reviews. Boundaries between roles are soft, and the expectation is that everyone contributes to the company's direction, not just their own function.

Remote-friendly but connection-aware. Airtable has distributed teams, but it invests in in-person time for key moments. If you are based in India and applying for a remote role, be prepared to discuss time zone overlap and your habits around async collaboration.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Airtable hire remotely, and can candidates based in India apply?

Airtable does have remote roles, but most require significant overlap with US time zones, typically the Pacific or Eastern working day. Candidates based in India should check each job description carefully for location requirements. Some roles are listed as 'remote US only,' which means they are not open to international candidates regardless of remote status. Always confirm with the recruiter early in the process.

How long does Airtable's hiring process take from application to offer?

The full process, from application to offer, commonly takes a few weeks to over a month, depending on the role and how quickly both sides can schedule. The recruiter screen and hiring manager call can happen within the first couple of weeks if there is mutual interest. The final panel and debrief add more time after that, so keep your schedule flexible once you reach that stage.

What is Airtable's compensation like?

Airtable's compensation figures for India-based or remote roles are not publicly standardised, so check platforms like Glassdoor or levels.fyi for reported ranges by role and level. Compensation typically includes base salary, equity (RSUs), and benefits. Equity vesting schedules and refresh grants vary, so ask the recruiter for specifics during the offer discussion rather than assuming a standard structure.

How competitive is it to get an interview at Airtable?

Airtable receives a large volume of applications relative to its headcount, and the recruiter screen is selective. A resume that clearly shows relevant domain experience and measurable impact improves your chances of passing that initial filter. A warm referral from a current Airtable employee significantly increases your visibility with the recruiting team and is worth pursuing if you have the connection.

Does Airtable do background checks or reference checks before making an offer?

Reference checks are common at Airtable, usually at or after the final panel stage. Prepare a few professional references who can speak to your work directly, ideally a former manager and a cross-functional colleague. Background checks depend on the role and are standard for positions that involve access to sensitive systems or customer data.

What is the best way to get noticed by Airtable's recruiting team?

A referral from a current Airtable employee is the most reliable way to get your resume reviewed quickly. Beyond that, a tailored resume that speaks directly to the job description and shows genuine familiarity with the no-code or workflow-tools space will stand out from generic applications. Engaging with Airtable's user community, writing publicly about relevant topics, or contributing to the broader no-code ecosystem can also build credibility over time.
