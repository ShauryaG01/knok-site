03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Stage 1: Application and resume screening

Every role at aspora starts with a recruiter reviewing your resume against the job description. The basics matter here: a clear, well-formatted resume that uses the same language as the job post, and work experience that maps to what the role requires. Gaps or mismatches are fine if you can explain them, but a generic or poorly formatted resume is likely to be filtered out early.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR screening call

Shortlisted candidates typically receive a call from the HR or talent team. This is a brief conversation covering your professional background, why you are interested in aspora, your notice period, and your salary expectations. Be honest and concise. This is also your first chance to ask clarifying questions about the role and team structure, so come prepared.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment

For technical roles, candidates commonly report a coding challenge or a problem-solving session, either async (sent over email with a deadline) or live (on a video call with a screenshare). For product, business, or operations roles, this stage may involve a case study, a written exercise, or a short presentation. The format and difficulty reflect the specific role, so read the job description carefully and ask the recruiter what to expect before this round.

Stage 4: Managerial or panel interview

This round typically involves the hiring manager and sometimes a few team members. Expect questions that go deeper into your past work, how you approach problems, and how you collaborate with others. Behavioral questions in the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) are common. Interviewers look for clarity, honesty, and genuine ownership of the examples you share.

Stage 5: Final discussion and offer

Some candidates report a closing conversation with a senior leader or a culture-fit discussion before the formal offer is made. This round is often less evaluative and more about mutual alignment on expectations. Once an offer is extended, you will usually have a few days to review it and respond.

The number and nature of stages can differ across teams and roles. The process above reflects what candidates commonly describe, but your experience may vary based on the team and seniority level.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Practical, role-specific skills

aspora's job descriptions typically focus on skills that apply directly to the work. For engineers, this means clean code, thoughtful design decisions, and comfort with the tools and languages listed in the job post. For business and operations roles, this means structured thinking, clear communication, and a track record of delivering results.

Comfort with ambiguity

Growing companies move fast and do not always have a playbook for every situation. Interviewers tend to value candidates who can take an unclear problem, make reasonable assumptions, and work toward a solution without needing constant direction. Show this by walking through how you have handled open-ended situations in past roles.

Ownership and initiative

Candidates who have gone beyond the scope of their job title, whether by fixing a problem nobody assigned them, building a tool to save the team time, or taking responsibility when something went wrong, stand out consistently. Use concrete examples with clear outcomes, not vague claims.

Clear, concise communication

The ability to explain your work simply matters across all roles. Practice describing what you built, why it mattered, and what you learned from it. Interviewers value candidates who can adjust their explanation depending on who is in the room.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research aspora before every round

Spend time on aspora's website, any public blog posts or media mentions, and the specific job description you applied for. Understand what they build, who uses it, and what problems they are solving. Interviewers notice genuine curiosity, and being well-informed helps you ask sharper questions and connect your experience to their actual needs.

Tailor your resume for the role

With 29 open roles across different functions, a generic resume is unlikely to work well. Highlight the experience and skills that directly match the job post you are applying for. Use the same terminology the post uses, where it honestly reflects your background.

Prepare concrete examples from your past work

Before each round, write down several examples of projects or situations that show impact, problem-solving, collaboration, and handling of failure. Practice saying them out loud in the STAR format so they come out naturally in the interview, not like a script you memorized.

Revisit technical fundamentals

For technical roles, go back to the core concepts in your domain. If the job post mentions specific tools, frameworks, or methodologies, be ready to discuss your experience with them honestly, including where your knowledge has limits. Interviewers appreciate honesty over bluffing.

Prepare questions to ask

At the end of each round, asking thoughtful questions about the team, the work, and how success is measured signals genuine interest. 'What does a strong first few months look like in this role?' and 'What is the biggest challenge the team is working through right now?' are good starting points.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

aspora has the feel of a company that values people who take initiative and want their work to have a visible impact. Based on how the company presents itself and what candidates describe, a few themes come through consistently.

Ownership without waiting to be asked

People who do well at aspora tend to be self-directed. They notice what needs doing, take it on, and see it through. If you come from an environment where you waited for detailed instructions before acting, it is worth practising how to frame your own initiative in interviews with specific examples.

Directness and open communication

The culture appears to favour candid conversations over formal hierarchies. Feedback flows in both directions, and team members are expected to speak up when they disagree or when something is not working. Candidates who can show they are comfortable with direct feedback, both giving and receiving, tend to resonate well.

Iteration over perfection

Like most growing companies, aspora likely values shipping, learning, and improving quickly over waiting for a perfect solution. If you have examples of launching something imperfect, gathering real feedback, and making it meaningfully better, those stories are worth preparing.

Genuine user focus

The team appears to care about the people they are building for, not just internal metrics. In interviews, connecting your past work to real user outcomes rather than just output volume tends to land well.
