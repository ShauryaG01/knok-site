03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three STAR-format sample answers for question types you are likely to face at Lybrate.

Q: 'Design a feature to improve patient retention after the first consultation.'

*Situation:* At my previous role on a consumer health platform, we noticed that a large share of users who completed one consultation never returned, which hurt the platform's long-term value.

*Task:* My goal was to understand why users were not coming back and to design an intervention that would meaningfully improve repeat visits.

*Action:* I ran user interviews and exit surveys to find the core reason: patients did not know what to do after the call ended. There was no summary of the doctor's advice, no medicine reminder, and no nudge to rebook. I designed a 'post-consultation care plan' feature: an auto-generated summary of the doctor's advice, a medicine reminder flow, and a contextual rebooking prompt timed to the nature of the condition. I worked with the design team to keep the experience lightweight and with doctors to make the summary template quick to fill.

*Result:* We piloted with a small cohort and tracked rebooking rate, medicine adherence via in-app logging, and post-consultation satisfaction scores. Rebooking improved meaningfully. Industry surveys on post-care nudges in telehealth commonly cite significant retention gains when follow-up is structured.

---

Q: 'A key metric has dropped. How do you diagnose it?'

*Situation:* At a previous company, completed bookings dropped noticeably over a weekend and leadership wanted answers quickly.

*Task:* I needed to determine whether this was a data issue, a product bug, an external factor, or a behaviour shift, and communicate findings clearly.

*Action:* I followed a structured funnel analysis: first I checked whether the data pipeline itself had an error. Once I confirmed the data was clean, I broke the funnel into steps: impression to click, click to booking page, booking page to payment confirmation. The drop was concentrated at the confirmation step. Cross-referencing with the release log, a payment gateway update had gone live that morning. I escalated to engineering, who confirmed a bug in the payment retry flow.

*Result:* The fix went live the same day. I also put in place a dashboard alert for the confirmation step so future drops would be caught automatically rather than discovered by chance.

---

Q: 'How do you prioritise when two teams both need engineering bandwidth?'

*Situation:* I was PM for a two-sided marketplace where both the supply team and the demand team had high-priority requests in the same sprint.

*Task:* I had to make a prioritisation call with limited engineering capacity and no easy way to do both.

*Action:* I used an impact vs. effort matrix combined with business goal alignment. The patient-side request (faster search filters) had high reach and low effort. The doctor-side request (bulk availability management) had lower reach but high strategic importance because doctor churn was a tracked risk. I presented both options to leadership with supporting data, and we agreed to ship the patient feature first and schedule the doctor tool for the following sprint, with a lightweight manual workaround in the meantime.

*Result:* Both features shipped in successive sprints. Doctor churn for the affected segment stabilised in the period that followed, according to our internal tracking.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

A few frameworks that map well to Lybrate's interview style:

CIRCLES (for product design questions). Comprehend the situation, Identify the customer, Report the customer's needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise. This is especially effective for 'design a feature for X user' questions. State the user segment clearly before jumping to solutions.

MECE funnel analysis (for metric drop questions). Break the problem into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive layers: data integrity first, then funnel steps, then segment cuts by platform, geography, or cohort. Lybrate interviewers typically value structured thinking here. Resist the urge to jump to a hypothesis before eliminating upstream causes.

Jobs-to-be-done (for retention and engagement questions). Frame the user's need not as a feature request but as a job they are trying to accomplish. A patient is not 'booking a consultation' but rather 'getting clarity on a worrying symptom quickly and affordably.' This framing leads to features that address real behaviour rather than surface-level requests.

Impact vs. Effort matrix (for prioritisation). Simple, visual, and effective. Combine it with business objective alignment: a feature that scores medium on impact but directly supports a stated company goal can outrank a high-effort feature that does not map to current priorities.

Candidates report that Lybrate interviewers appreciate when you acknowledge trade-offs and push back on your own initial assumptions mid-answer. Self-correction signals strong product instinct.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates typically report from health-tech PM interviews, Lybrate interviewers look for a specific combination of qualities.

Healthcare empathy without being naïve. You should understand that the stakes in a health product are higher than in a typical consumer app. At the same time, they want PMs who can ship practical features, not candidates paralysed by edge cases. Show that you can hold both realities.

Marketplace thinking. Lybrate has doctors and patients as two distinct user groups. Interviewers want to see that you understand supply-demand dynamics: that improving one side without considering the other can backfire, and that you know how to measure health on both sides of the marketplace.

Data fluency. Expect to define metrics, interpret a drop in a number, and defend your measurement choices. You do not need to be a data scientist, but you should be comfortable with funnels, cohort analysis, and the difference between a vanity metric and an actionable one.

Speed and pragmatism. Health-tech in India is competitive. Candidates report that Lybrate values PMs who can scope a feature down, ship a version that tests the core hypothesis, and iterate based on real signal rather than waiting for a perfect spec.

Clear communication under ambiguity. Several candidates note that the ability to explain a complex health feature to a non-technical stakeholder, or to push back on a feature request diplomatically, comes up in the behavioural portions of the interview.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused preparation plan for the Lybrate PM interview, typically spread over a week or two.

Domain and product knowledge first.

Start by using the Lybrate app deeply as a patient. If possible, explore the doctor-facing side too. Note friction points, missing features, and moments of delight. Read recent publicly reported news about the Indian telehealth market from 2024 to 2026 to understand the regulatory and competitive context. You should be able to speak to at least a couple of specific improvements you would prioritise and why.

Framework practice out loud.

Practise the CIRCLES and MECE frameworks by speaking them aloud, not just writing them down. Record yourself and listen back. Most candidates underestimate how much structure gets lost when speaking versus writing. Focus on clarity in the first sentence of each answer: it signals immediately whether you are structured or not.

Case and behavioural prep.

Work through a handful of metric drop scenarios end to end. For each one, identify which step in the funnel you would check first and why. Separately, prepare a few stories from your own experience covering: a feature you shipped and measured, a prioritisation conflict you resolved, and a time you used data to overturn an assumption. Use the STAR format for each.

Final day.

Review your stories once, check the Lybrate app for anything new, and get enough sleep. Do not cram new frameworks the night before. Familiarity beats novelty in a live case interview.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

The mistakes that tend to knock PM candidates out of Lybrate interviews are predictable and avoidable.

Skipping the user segment. When asked to design a feature, many candidates jump straight to the solution. Always start by stating who the user is and what their specific pain is. Interviewers at product-focused companies notice immediately when this step is missing.

Generic metrics. Saying 'I would track DAU and conversion' is weak for a health-tech interview. Show that you understand what a good consultation experience looks like in numbers: time to find the right doctor, wait time before the call connects, post-consultation satisfaction, and repeat visit rate. Make your metrics specific to the use case.

Ignoring the supply side. Lybrate needs doctors to be engaged and satisfied too. Candidates who optimise only for the patient experience without considering doctor workload, earnings, or scheduling friction tend to get challenged in the interview.

Over-complicated frameworks. Some candidates try to use every framework they know in a single answer. Pick one, apply it cleanly, and move on. Interviewers want to see clear thinking, not a strategy consulting presentation.

Not asking clarifying questions. Health-tech problems are inherently ambiguous. 'Improve retention' could mean first-to-second-visit retention or long-term annual retention. Clarifying the scope before diving in is not a sign of weakness. Candidates report that interviewers typically reward it.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Lybrate PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screening call, one or two product case rounds, a metrics and execution discussion, and a final culture or leadership conversation. The exact number of rounds can vary by level and team. Senior PM roles sometimes include a presentation component where you critique a feature or outline a roadmap.

What is the salary range for PM roles at Lybrate?

Lybrate has not publicly disclosed its salary bands. Based on knok jobradar data for PM roles in India broadly, Associate PMs typically see 12-20 LPA, PMs with 3-6 years of experience fall in the 24-40 LPA range, and Senior PMs are commonly in the 40-60 LPA range. For Lybrate-specific numbers, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for self-reported figures.

Do I need a healthcare background to interview for a PM role at Lybrate?

Candidates report that a healthcare background is appreciated but not mandatory. What matters more is that you have studied the product, understand the two-sided marketplace dynamics, and can speak to the unique constraints of health data and user trust. Consumer app or marketplace PM experience is commonly cited as a strong foundation to build on.

How should I prepare for the metric drop question?

Practise a structured funnel approach: start with data integrity, then break the funnel into discrete steps, then cut by segment such as platform, geography, or user cohort. Avoid jumping to a solution before you have diagnosed the root cause. Interviewers typically want to see calm, systematic thinking and the right clarifying questions before you propose a fix.

Is there a take-home assignment in the Lybrate PM process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home product case or a brief written exercise, though this is not universally reported across all roles and levels. Ask your recruiter upfront so you can plan your time. If you do receive one, focus on clarity and specificity over length. A concise, well-reasoned response typically beats a sprawling one that tries to cover every angle.
