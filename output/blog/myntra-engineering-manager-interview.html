01 Overview

Overview

Myntra, India's leading fashion e-commerce platform and part of the Flipkart group, is actively hiring Engineering Managers in 2026. Knok jobradar data (as of July 2026) shows 8 open EM roles at Myntra, with positions primarily based in Bangalore.

The interview process is thorough and multi-stage. Candidates report that rounds typically cover system design, people leadership, delivery track record, and cultural alignment. Given Myntra's scale (serving millions of customers and running large sale events like EORS), interviewers consistently look for managers who can handle ambiguity, drive execution, and develop engineers under pressure.

Salary bands from knok jobradar data show Engineering Manager roles in the 35-60 LPA range, Senior Manager at 55-90 LPA, and Director-level at 90 LPA and above. Your specific offer will depend on your current CTC, years of experience leading teams, and depth of technical ownership.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Myntra's EM interviews blend engineering rigour with people leadership depth. These are the questions candidates most commonly report across rounds:

  1. Tell me about a time you scaled an engineering team during a high-growth phase. Myntra grows fast around sale events, so this question almost always comes up.
  1. How do you handle an underperforming engineer who is technically strong but misses deadlines consistently?
  1. Walk me through how you run sprint planning and delivery for a team of eight or more engineers.
  1. Describe a system you designed or owned that handled large-scale traffic. Fashion e-commerce demands strong scale thinking across catalogue, search, and order flows.
  1. How do you balance technical debt against feature pressure from product? This tests your stakeholder management and long-term engineering thinking.
  1. Tell me about a conflict between two senior engineers on your team and how you resolved it.
  1. How do you set goals for your team and track progress without micromanaging?
  1. Describe a project where you coordinated across multiple teams (say, backend, mobile, data). Cross-functional delivery is central to how Myntra ships at scale.
  1. How do you keep engineers motivated and retain top talent in a competitive hiring market?
  1. Myntra runs major sale events where engineering reliability is business-critical. How do you prepare your team for peak load?
  1. How do you approach hiring? What do you look for in a senior engineer joining your team?
  1. Tell me about a time your team shipped something that failed in production. What happened next?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you scaled an engineering team during a high-growth phase.

*Situation:* Our checkout and payments team supported a rapidly growing user base with just five engineers handling both feature work and production incidents.

*Task:* I needed to scale to twelve engineers within two quarters while keeping service reliability intact and delivery on track.

*Action:* I first mapped every critical function to a clear owner so nothing depended on a single person. I partnered with recruiting to define a sharp hiring bar and personally ran half the final-round interviews. I also built a structured onboarding plan with buddy pairing and clear milestones for the first few months, so new joiners contributed quickly without pulling senior engineers off their own work.

*Result:* We brought in seven new engineers within a few months. Incident response improved because we now had dedicated on-call rotations. Feature velocity went up measurably in the quarter that followed.

---

Q: How do you handle an underperforming engineer who is technically strong but misses deadlines?

*Situation:* A senior engineer on my team was one of the best system designers we had, but sprint after sprint they missed their committed items and gave optimistic updates until the final day.

*Task:* I needed to fix the delivery problem without losing someone genuinely valuable to the team.

*Action:* I set up weekly one-on-ones focused not just on status but on blockers and workload. I noticed they were picking up unscoped tasks mid-sprint that kept expanding. Together we agreed on a rule: no new work enters a sprint without removing something else. I also asked them to flag 'at risk' items by Wednesday of each sprint week, not Friday.

*Result:* Within two sprints their delivery improved substantially. They later told me the structured check-in actually reduced their stress because they were no longer holding information alone.

---

Q: Tell me about a time your team shipped something that failed in production.

*Situation:* My team shipped a new recommendations engine just before a major sale event. Within two hours of go-live, homepage latency spiked and we had to roll back.

*Task:* I needed to manage the live incident, restore service, and make sure the team came out of it stronger rather than demoralised.

*Action:* I ran the incident bridge myself so the engineers could focus entirely on diagnosing and fixing. After we stabilised, I called a blameless postmortem within two days. We found that load testing had not covered the specific query pattern that sale traffic generated. I then built a 'sale readiness checklist' that every team had to complete before any major event launch.

*Result:* The recommendations engine launched successfully the following week. The checklist was later adopted as a template by other engineering teams across the org.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the core framework for every behavioural question at Myntra. Keep the Situation and Task concise, and spend the bulk of your answer on the Action you took and the concrete Result you drove.

For system design questions, use a structured walk-through: clarify requirements and constraints first, sketch the high-level architecture, then go deep on one or two components the interviewer focuses on. At Myntra, always address how your design handles peak traffic (think sale events) and what your failure modes look like.

For people management questions, show a consistent pattern: observe carefully, understand the root cause, act with both empathy and clarity, then measure the outcome. Myntra interviewers look for managers who diagnose before they react, not managers who jump to PIPs or reassignments on first sight of a problem.

For cross-functional and stakeholder questions, use a three-part structure: how you aligned on goals upfront, how you navigated conflict or trade-offs through the middle, and what the final outcome was. Be specific about which teams were involved and how the key decision was ultimately made.

A common trap is giving answers that are vague and values-heavy without evidence. Name the metric, the tool, the team size. Specificity builds credibility far faster than any framework alone.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Scale mindset. Myntra operates at significant scale, and interviewers want evidence that you have managed teams and systems under real pressure. If you have run services through peak-load events or rapid growth phases, bring that out explicitly rather than leaving it implied.

Genuine people investment. Myntra is known for a culture that values engineer development. Candidates who come across as purely execution-focused without genuine care for their team's growth typically do not clear the people rounds. Be ready to talk about how specific engineers grew under your mentorship, not just what the team delivered as a whole.

Product curiosity. Unlike infrastructure or B2B companies, Myntra sits at the intersection of fashion, data, and consumer experience. Managers who understand why product decisions are made (not just how to build them) stand out clearly. Use the Myntra app regularly in the weeks before your interview and form real opinions about the experience.

Execution discipline. Clear planning, measurable goals, and a solid delivery track record matter. Bring specifics from your own history where you can: team size managed, how reliability changed after you joined, what delivery improvement looked like over a quarter.

Ownership of failure. Myntra values speed, ownership, and learning from mistakes. Stories where you personally owned a failure and then drove a structured fix play very well with interviewers there.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Two to three weeks before:

Read Myntra's public engineering blog and any available talks from their engineering leaders to understand their tech choices and scale challenges. Start using the Myntra app daily to build genuine product intuition.

Build a personal story bank covering these scenarios: team scaling, conflict resolution, delivery under pressure, a system you designed end to end, a technical debt decision, and a time you hired well. Write each story in STAR format before you walk into any round.

One week before:

Practise answering the questions in this guide out loud, not just in your head. Ask a peer to act as interviewer and give you feedback on specificity and clarity. Aim to give complete, grounded answers without rambling.

Refresh distributed systems fundamentals that are relevant to e-commerce: product search and catalogues, recommendation engines, order management systems, and payment flows.

Day before:

Re-read the job description line by line and match each requirement to a specific story from your bank. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer about engineering culture, team structure, or upcoming technical challenges. Avoid asking about salary or leave policy in early rounds.

On the day:

Candidates report that Myntra interviewers appreciate honest, grounded answers over polished but vague ones. If you genuinely do not know something, say so and explain how you would approach finding out.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Staying in IC mode. EM candidates who spend most of their time talking about their own technical contributions rather than how they led others are frequently rejected. Myntra is hiring a people leader, not a senior engineer. Keep your answers weighted toward leadership impact over individual contribution.

Giving vague, values-heavy answers. Saying 'I believe in open communication and trust' is not an answer. You need a real story: a specific situation, a concrete action, and a result you can name. Vague values without evidence do not move the needle at Myntra.

Not knowing the product. Candidates who have never properly used the Myntra app, cannot name any features, and have no view on the customer experience come across as unprepared. Spend real time on the app before your first round.

Claiming individual credit. Myntra culture values collective ownership. Saying 'I built the system' when a team of engineers built it will ring false to experienced interviewers. Use 'my team delivered' and 'I enabled' language, not 'I did it.'

Dropping the result. Many candidates give a strong Situation and Action but end with something like 'it worked out well.' Put a clear outcome on every story: what metric moved, what behaviour changed, what the team or business gained.

Under-preparing for people questions. Some EM candidates over-invest in system design and neglect leadership depth. Candidates report that people management is weighted equally to, or more than, technical depth in Myntra EM rounds.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Myntra EM interview process typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically spans multiple rounds over one to three weeks. These commonly include a recruiter screen, one or two system design or technical discussions, a people leadership round, and a final round with a senior leader. Myntra may adjust the number of rounds based on role seniority, so confirm the exact structure with your recruiter before your first session.

Is there a coding round for Engineering Manager roles at Myntra?

Candidates report that EM roles at Myntra typically do not include a competitive coding round the way IC roles do. You should, however, expect system design questions, architecture trade-off discussions, and technical decision-making scenarios. Some rounds may involve reviewing a design or discussing code-level choices, so do not let your technical fundamentals go cold entirely.

What salary can I expect as an Engineering Manager at Myntra?

Based on knok jobradar data, Engineering Manager roles in India currently range from 35-60 LPA, with Senior Manager roles from 55-90 LPA. Your actual Myntra offer will depend on your current total compensation, years of experience managing teams, and how negotiations go. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have user-reported numbers that can give you additional data points before you negotiate.

How much does product knowledge matter in the Myntra EM interview?

It matters more than most candidates expect. Myntra is a consumer product company and interviewers often test whether you can connect engineering decisions to business and customer outcomes. Spend real time on the app, notice features that seem technically interesting such as search, personalisation, and checkout, and be ready to share your observations. Candidates who demonstrate genuine product curiosity stand out clearly from those treating it as a pure technical screen.

How should I prepare for the people management rounds?

Build a set of real stories covering common EM scenarios: underperformance, conflict between engineers, a project that failed, a time you hired well, and a time you pushed back on leadership. Practise each story in STAR format, out loud. Myntra interviewers typically probe two or three layers deep with follow-up questions like 'what would you do differently?' or 'what did that engineer do next?', so your stories need to hold up under sustained questioning.

Does Myntra prefer hiring EMs internally or from outside?

Myntra does both. With 8 open EM roles currently listed on knok jobradar, they are actively hiring externally as well. External candidates are typically expected to demonstrate clear evidence of managing meaningful team sizes, owning end-to-end delivery, and working in a fast-paced consumer tech or e-commerce environment. If you come from a different domain, be ready with a clear narrative on how your experience translates to the consumer product world.
