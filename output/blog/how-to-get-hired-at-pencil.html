03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Pencil's interview process typically runs across four to five stages. The exact format can vary by role, so treat the stages below as a reliable guide rather than a guarantee.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen. When you apply, your resume goes through an initial review by a recruiter or hiring manager. Pencil looks for clear evidence of relevant skills and measurable impact. Vague descriptions of 'responsibilities' tend to get passed over. Specific outcomes and results stand out.

Stage 2: Introductory call with a recruiter or hiring manager. If your profile stands out, you will typically hear back within a few business days. This is a short, conversational call. Expect questions about your background, what draws you to Pencil, and your availability. Prepare a brief, clear summary of your career story that connects your background to the role. Come ready to ask thoughtful questions: what the team looks like, how success is measured, what the onboarding experience is like.

Stage 3: Skills assessment or portfolio review. Depending on the role, you may be asked to complete a take-home task, share work samples, or walk through past projects. For creative or growth roles, expect to discuss the thinking behind your work, not just the output. For engineering roles, expect a coding exercise or system design prompt. Quality of reasoning matters more than a polished final answer.

Stage 4: Technical or in-depth interview. This is a longer session where interviewers go deeper on your domain. They will probe how you think through problems, handle constraints, and communicate your reasoning. Be ready to talk through your process step by step rather than jumping straight to the conclusion.

Stage 5: Final round. A conversation with senior leadership or a cross-functional panel. This round often focuses on values alignment, long-term goals, and how you handle ambiguity. You may meet people from multiple teams in the same session. Ask questions that show you have thought seriously about the role and the company's direction.

With 11 open roles active right now, Pencil is in an active hiring phase. Moving quickly on applications and responding promptly to recruiter messages will work in your favour.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Pencil hires for a specific combination of curiosity, craft, and comfort with uncertainty. Here is what tends to matter most across roles.

Genuine interest in AI. Pencil builds AI tools for advertisers. They want people who are not just tolerating AI as a trend but are actively curious about what it can and cannot do. If you have experimented with AI tools in your own work, say so clearly and specifically.

Outcome-oriented thinking. Pencil moves fast. They want candidates who default to asking 'what result does this create?' rather than 'what process should I follow?' Use specifics when describing past work, even rough estimates with context, rather than vague claims about impact.

Creative and analytical balance. Many roles at Pencil sit at the junction of performance marketing and generative AI. The strongest candidates can hold both: they understand what makes an ad visually compelling and can also discuss why a certain creative format outperformed another in a campaign.

Clear, direct communication. The team is distributed and fast-moving. The ability to write and speak clearly, especially in async formats, is valued across every function. This is not a soft skill at Pencil: it is a core work requirement.

Ownership and low ego. Pencil is not a large company where you can hand off problems up the chain. They look for people who take full ownership of their work and ask for help early rather than late. Candidates who talk about what 'we' achieved without being able to explain their personal contribution tend not to progress.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Preparation for a Pencil interview is less about memorising frameworks and more about being able to speak clearly about what you have actually done and why.

Before the introductory call. Research Pencil's product: try the platform if you can, look at publicly available case studies, and understand who their customers are. Prepare a focused summary of your career that connects your background to the role. Think of it as a short pitch: what you have done, what you are good at, and why Pencil makes sense as your next step. Keep it crisp and leave room for conversation.

For the skills assessment. Do not rush the take-home task. Read the brief carefully, ask a clarifying question if something is genuinely unclear, and show your thinking throughout. A well-reasoned answer that acknowledges trade-offs will beat a polished answer with no reflection.

For technical rounds. Practice thinking out loud. Interviewers at companies like Pencil often care more about your reasoning process than your final answer. If you get stuck, narrate your approach rather than going silent. Silence reads as uncertainty; narration reads as composure.

For the final round. Prepare specific examples of times you worked through ambiguity, delivered under pressure, or changed your mind based on new information. These conversations tend to go deeper than a standard culture-fit chat.

General tip. Come with questions at every stage. Candidates who ask sharp, specific questions (about the product roadmap, team structure, what success looks like in the first few months) consistently make a stronger impression than those who say 'I think I have everything I need.'

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Pencil operates with a startup mindset: small teams, high ownership, and a genuine belief that AI is changing how the world creates and distributes advertising. The culture rewards people who are proactive and comfortable making decisions without a detailed playbook.

Speed over perfection. Pencil ships and iterates. The expectation is that you will get things done, learn quickly from what does not work, and move forward. If you prefer environments where every decision goes through multiple approval layers, this may not be the right fit.

Data and creativity together. The product sits at the junction of performance marketing and generative AI. The culture reflects this: people are expected to appreciate both the creative and the analytical side of the work, regardless of their job title. Someone in a purely analytical role is still expected to develop an opinion on what makes good creative, and vice versa.

Remote and distributed work norms. Pencil operates across time zones. Written communication is central to how the team functions. Being clear, concise, and responsive in writing is not optional: it is how trust gets built on distributed teams.

Founder-driven energy. Like many companies at Pencil's stage, the founders are close to the product and the hiring process. Expect a culture where company direction can shift in response to market signals, and where your input into that direction is genuinely welcomed once you have earned credibility through your work.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does Pencil currently have?

As of mid-2026, Pencil has 11 open roles. The mix typically spans engineering, product, growth, and creative functions. Check the Pencil careers page directly for the current list, since roles open and close quickly at companies of this size.

How long does the Pencil hiring process usually take?

Based on what candidates commonly report for companies at Pencil's stage, the process from application to offer can take anywhere from a couple of weeks to about a month. The exact timeline depends on the role, how quickly both sides move, and how many rounds the hiring manager includes. Following up politely after each stage is reasonable if you have not heard back within a week or so.

Does Pencil hire remotely, and does location matter?

Pencil has operated as a distributed team and has hired across multiple geographies. Whether a specific role is remote, hybrid, or location-specific will be stated in the job listing. If location flexibility matters to you, clarify this early in the recruiter call so there are no surprises later in the process.

What kind of take-home task should I expect?

The take-home format varies by role. Creative and growth candidates may be asked to critique an existing ad, propose a creative strategy, or analyse a campaign scenario. Engineering candidates are more likely to see a coding exercise or a small system design problem. In either case, showing your reasoning clearly is more important than arriving at a single 'correct' answer.

How do I stand out in a Pencil interview?

The candidates who stand out tend to do a few things well: they speak in specifics rather than generalities when describing past work, they demonstrate genuine curiosity about AI and Pencil's product, and they ask sharp questions at every stage. Saying something like 'I noticed your platform does X, have you considered Y?' signals the kind of proactive thinking Pencil values.

What should I do if I do not hear back after applying?

One follow-up message to the recruiter or hiring manager about a week after applying is completely appropriate. Keep it brief and specific: restate the role you applied for and express continued interest. If you still do not hear back, the role may have been filled or paused, and it is worth applying to other similar companies in parallel rather than waiting on a single application.
