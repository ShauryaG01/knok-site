01 Overview

Overview

Commvault is a US-headquartered data protection and management company known for enterprise backup, recovery, and cloud data management products. Engineers here work on core infrastructure that protects data for large enterprises, so the bar for technical depth is noticeably high.

As of July 2026, Commvault has 110 open Software Engineer roles tracked on knok. The interview process typically runs three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a managerial round focused on ownership and culture fit. Candidates report that Commvault interviewers pay close attention to how you reason about reliability, scale, and data integrity, not just whether your code runs correctly.

Commvault's India engineering centres in Pune and Hyderabad handle significant product development work on the core platform as well as the Metallic SaaS offering. Being familiar with the product landscape before your interview gives you a real edge.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioral question. Here are three worked examples you can adapt to your own experience.

Q: Walk us through a production bug you debugged under pressure.

*Situation:* Our backup service was silently skipping some file-change events on high-traffic file servers, causing missed backups for certain clients.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and had to find the root cause and deploy a fix before the next scheduled backup window.

*Action:* I added structured logging around the event queue, replicated the traffic pattern in staging, and traced the issue to a queue overflow that silently dropped events when concurrent writes exceeded a threshold. I patched the consumer to apply backpressure and added an alert for when queue depth crossed a safe limit.

*Result:* The fix deployed within hours. Missed-backup alerts dropped to zero in the following week. We also added a runbook so the next on-call engineer would not start from scratch.

---

Q: Describe a time you collaborated with a team that had different priorities.

*Situation:* The QA team was not prioritising automated regression tests for a new backup agent because they were stretched across several other projects.

*Task:* I needed test coverage before the release but had no direct authority over the QA team's backlog.

*Action:* I wrote the first set of integration tests myself and shared them with the QA lead, framing it as reducing their review effort rather than adding work. I also put together a short risk summary showing what manual testing would miss, which the QA manager used to reprioritise one sprint.

*Result:* We shipped with full automated coverage. Those tests caught regressions in the next release cycle that would otherwise have reached production.

---

Q: How do you design code so it is easy to test and maintain?

*Situation:* I joined a team whose core module had grown very large with almost no unit tests, making every change risky.

*Task:* Refactor the module incrementally without breaking existing functionality or slowing down ongoing feature work.

*Action:* I introduced interfaces at the boundaries of external dependencies first, then wrote tests against those boundaries. I split the module into smaller single-responsibility classes over several sprints, keeping the public API stable throughout.

*Result:* Test coverage on the module improved substantially (I cannot share internal numbers). The next two feature additions took noticeably less time and introduced no regressions.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For coding questions: Think aloud from the start. State the brute-force solution first, name its time and space complexity, then work toward a better approach. Always ask about constraints (input size, null inputs, duplicates) before writing any code. Commvault interviewers typically care more about your reasoning process than a perfectly optimised first draft.

For system design questions: Follow a four-step flow. First, clarify requirements and scale (read vs. write ratio, expected data volume, consistency needs). Second, sketch a high-level architecture. Third, drill into the component that matters most for reliability. For Commvault, this is usually the storage or scheduling layer. Fourth, discuss trade-offs explicitly: what does your design sacrifice in order to gain reliability or throughput?

For behavioral questions: Use STAR, but keep the Situation and Task sections brief. Spend most of your time on Action and make sure Result has something concrete, even if it is qualitative ('reduced on-call alerts', 'cut review cycle time'). Avoid vague results like 'the team was happier'.

For product questions: Show that you have done some reading about Commvault's offerings before the interview. Metallic (the SaaS platform), the core Command Center, and cloud integrations are good areas to mention. Frame your answer around a real user problem, not just a technical feature.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Commvault interviewers look for a specific combination of qualities. Here is what candidates report being evaluated on.

Deep technical fundamentals. Data structures, algorithms, and systems concepts come up in every coding round. Distributed systems topics (consistency, replication, failure handling) appear frequently in design rounds because Commvault's product is fundamentally about reliable data movement across systems.

Product awareness. Interviewers want to see that you understand what Commvault makes and why it matters. Generic answers about 'building scalable software' land poorly. Answers that connect your skills to backup reliability, data integrity, or cloud data management land well.

Ownership mindset. Multiple candidates report being asked questions that probe whether you follow up after shipping, whether you proactively flag risks, and whether you own failures instead of deflecting them. This is a strong cultural signal at Commvault.

Clear communication. Commvault's India engineering teams collaborate closely with global product and business stakeholders. Interviewers pay attention to how clearly you structure your thoughts and whether you can explain a technical decision to a non-technical audience.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Here is a four-week plan that candidates report finding useful before a Commvault Software Engineer interview.

Week 1: Coding fundamentals. Revisit arrays, linked lists, trees, graphs, and dynamic programming. Focus on problems involving streams or large datasets, as these connect directly to Commvault's domain. Practice explaining your approach out loud as you solve.

Week 2: System design. Study distributed system concepts: replication, consistency models, CAP theorem, and job scheduling. Design a backup system from scratch at least once. Think through how you would handle partial failures, retries, and idempotency.

Week 3: Company and product research. Read Commvault's product pages for Metallic, Command Center, and their cloud integrations. Prepare a few specific observations about the product that you can bring up naturally during the interview.

Week 4: Behavioral prep and mock interviews. Map your past projects to STAR stories. Prepare answers for ownership, conflict resolution, and technical decision-making questions. Do at least a couple of mock interviews with a peer or mentor before your actual rounds.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping edge cases in coding rounds. Commvault's products handle real enterprise data. Interviewers notice when candidates write happy-path code without considering null inputs, empty lists, or concurrent writes.

Treating system design as a whiteboard sketch. Candidates who draw boxes and arrows without discussing trade-offs, failure modes, or consistency requirements typically do not progress. Come prepared to defend your design choices.

Not researching the product. Walking in without knowing what Commvault makes is one of the most common mistakes. Even a basic understanding of backup and recovery, or knowing what Metallic does, signals that you are genuinely interested in the company.

Generic behavioral answers. Answers like 'I am a good team player' or 'I work hard' do not land. Every behavioral answer needs a specific situation and a concrete outcome.

Talking too long on context. Some candidates spend so much time on background that they never reach the actual solution. Interviewers have a fixed time window. Practice keeping your Situation and Task sections to a sentence or two each.

Not asking clarifying questions. Jumping straight into a design or coding problem without asking about constraints signals that you skip requirements gathering on the job. Always ask at least a couple of clarifying questions before you start.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a Commvault Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final managerial round. The exact number varies by level and team, so it is worth asking the recruiter upfront how many rounds to expect and who will be on each panel.

What salary can a Software Engineer expect at Commvault in India?

Knok's job radar data for Software Engineer roles in India shows 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA at mid-level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). For company-specific figures, Glassdoor and levels.fyi often carry Commvault salary reports from recent joiners. Always factor in variable pay and any stock components when comparing offers.

Is the interview more focused on coding or system design?

Candidates report that both areas get serious attention at Commvault. Junior roles lean more toward coding fundamentals, while mid and senior roles typically include at least one dedicated system design round. Given that Commvault builds data protection infrastructure, design questions often centre on distributed reliability, large-scale data movement, and failure recovery.

Do I need prior experience in storage or data protection to apply?

Not necessarily. Most candidates come from general software engineering backgrounds. What interviewers look for is strong fundamentals and genuine curiosity about the domain. Spending a few hours reading about how backup systems work and being able to discuss data integrity and consistency clearly signals that curiosity.

How long does the Commvault hiring process take from first screen to offer?

Candidates report the process can take anywhere from a couple of weeks to over a month, depending on the team and scheduling availability. After your final round, follow up with the recruiter if you have not heard back within a week. Having a competing offer in hand can sometimes help accelerate a decision.

Which Commvault offices in India hire Software Engineers?

Commvault's primary India engineering hub is in Pune, with additional presence in Hyderabad. Most open roles are Pune-based. If location matters to you, confirm with the recruiter whether the role supports hybrid or remote work, as policies vary by team.
