01 Overview

Overview

Hyreo is a talent-tech company that builds AI-powered recruitment software used by enterprises to manage hiring at scale. It currently has 96 open QA Engineer positions, making it one of the more active recruiters for this role right now. The work involves testing web and API-based products used by both recruiters and job seekers, so your output directly affects real hiring decisions.

Across India, knok's jobradar tracked 459 active QA Engineer openings as of July 2026. Bangalore leads with 87 open roles, followed by Delhi with 67, Chennai with 13, Pune with 12, Hyderabad with 8, and Mumbai with 5. Hyreo's volume suggests hiring across multiple teams and experience levels.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up most often in QA Engineer interviews at hyreo and similar product-tech companies. Candidates report seeing a mix of conceptual, behavioral, and hands-on questions:

  1. Walk me through how you would write a test plan for a new feature from scratch.
  2. What is the difference between verification and validation? Give a real example from your work.
  3. How do you decide which tests to automate and which to keep manual?
  4. Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle. What did you do?
  5. What automation frameworks have you worked with, and why did you choose them for your projects?
  6. How do you test a REST API endpoint? What do you check beyond the success case?
  7. What does test coverage mean to you, and how do you ensure you have enough without over-testing?
  8. How would you approach testing a candidate matching or resume-parsing feature?
  9. How do you handle regression testing when the codebase changes frequently in an agile team?
  10. What do you do when a developer disagrees that something you raised is actually a bug?
  11. Describe your experience with performance or load testing and the tools you have used.
  12. How do you keep a test suite maintainable as the product grows and evolves?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a critical bug you found late in the release cycle. What did you do?

*Situation:* During final regression before a major release, I found that confirmation emails were not being sent when users completed a transaction on certain mobile browsers.

*Task:* The release was scheduled for the following morning. I needed to assess severity quickly, communicate clearly, and help the team make a go-or-no-go call.

*Action:* I reproduced the issue across three devices to confirm it was consistent, documented exact reproduction steps with screenshots, and immediately escalated to the dev lead and product manager. I flagged it as high severity because it directly affected user trust. I also helped narrow the root cause by isolating it to a specific browser rendering condition, which made the fix faster to scope.

*Result:* The developer patched it that same evening. I verified the fix before the release window closed and the launch went ahead on schedule. We added a browser-matrix check to our standard regression checklist so this class of issue would be caught earlier in future cycles.

---

Q: How do you decide what to automate versus what to test manually?

*Situation:* At a previous company, the test suite had grown large and the team was spending most of each sprint on repetitive manual checks that slowed down releases.

*Task:* I was asked to propose a smarter testing strategy to reduce manual effort without reducing coverage quality.

*Action:* I mapped all existing test cases into three groups: tests that run every release with stable requirements (strong automation candidates), tests for exploratory or UX-heavy flows where human judgment matters (keep manual), and edge cases tied to third-party integrations where context shifts each cycle (evaluate individually). For each group I weighed the cost of writing and maintaining automation against the frequency and risk of the scenario.

*Result:* We automated the first group, which covered a large portion of our regression suite. Sprint testing time dropped noticeably and the team could redirect manual effort toward areas where it added the most value.

---

Q: How would you test an API endpoint beyond the happy path?

*Situation:* I was the sole QA engineer on a new internal API that handled candidate data for a recruitment workflow, similar in nature to what hyreo builds.

*Task:* I needed to design comprehensive coverage for a single endpoint before the front-end integration went live.

*Action:* Beyond the standard success case, I tested invalid input types, missing required fields, boundary values for numeric inputs, unauthorized access attempts, duplicate request handling, and behavior when a downstream service was unavailable. I used Postman for exploratory testing and wrote automated assertions for the most critical paths.

*Result:* I found two defects that would have caused silent data loss in edge cases. Both were fixed before the integration launched, and my Postman collection became the reference for the team's ongoing API testing.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For 'walk me through your process' questions, use a simple three-part structure: what you assessed first, what you decided and why, and what the outcome was. Hyreo interviewers typically want to see that you think before you act, not just that you know the right terminology.

For bug-related questions, always lead with severity and business impact, then move to communication, then resolution. Show that you think about what a bug means for the user or the business, not just its technical root cause.

For 'how would you test X' questions, a strong answer covers: understanding requirements and acceptance criteria, identifying happy paths and edge cases, deciding what tools and environments you need, and how you would log and track defects. Apply this to hyreo's product context. Relevant examples include testing a candidate search filter, a job application form, or an email notification trigger.

For questions about tools and frameworks, be specific about what you used and why. Avoid listing tools as a stack dump. Mention trade-offs: why you picked one framework over another, or where a tool fell short of what you needed.

For behavioral questions, use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your answer on Action, meaning what you specifically did rather than what 'the team' did. Always close with a concrete Result. Candidates who trail off without stating an outcome consistently score lower.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hyreo's product is used by recruiters and candidates, so bugs have a direct and visible impact on real hiring decisions. Interviewers typically look for these qualities in QA Engineers:

Domain awareness. You do not need prior HR-tech experience, but you should be able to discuss how you would test a feature where data accuracy matters, such as a resume parser, a job matching algorithm, or a notification system.

Clear communication. QA sits between development and product. Show that you can write a crisp bug report, push back constructively on a bad implementation, and keep non-technical stakeholders informed without overwhelming them.

Judgment about automation. Candidates report that hyreo values engineers who know when automation is worth the investment and when it is not. Treating automation as always superior, or avoiding it entirely, both signal poor judgment.

Ownership of quality. Show examples where you improved a process, added coverage proactively, or caught an issue others missed. Quality as a mindset rather than a task list is what product companies look for.

Agile comfort. Hyreo teams typically work in sprints. Be ready to discuss how you fit testing into a sprint, how you handle shifting requirements, and how you balance speed with thoroughness.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Phase 1: Solidify your fundamentals

Revisit core testing concepts: test planning, test case design techniques such as equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, and decision tables, the defect lifecycle, and the distinction between functional and non-functional testing. If you use an automation framework, review it well enough to discuss your past project confidently and explain your design choices.

Phase 2: Hands-on practice and tools

Write test cases from scratch for a feature similar to hyreo's product, such as a job application form, a candidate profile page, or a search-and-filter screen. Review API testing basics using Postman or a similar tool. If you have worked with Selenium, Cypress, Appium, or similar frameworks, refresh yourself on common patterns and be ready to explain why you chose them.

Phase 3: Behavioral prep and mock interviews

Prepare at least three strong STAR stories covering: a critical bug you caught, a process improvement you drove, and a conflict you navigated with a developer or product manager. Say the answers out loud rather than rehearsing them only in your head. Do at least one mock interview with a peer or record yourself and play it back.

Final prep before the interview

Research hyreo's product and read any recent coverage of the company. Prepare two or three specific questions for your interviewer about the team's quality processes, current testing challenges, or how QA interacts with product development. Thoughtful questions consistently leave a strong impression.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Testing only the happy path. Interviewers notice quickly when a candidate cannot see past the expected flow. Always raise edge cases, boundary conditions, and failure scenarios without being prompted.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved our testing process' without specifics gives interviewers nothing to evaluate. Name the tool, describe the change you made, and state a concrete outcome.

Over-relying on automation. Saying you would automate a test without explaining why signals poor judgment. Always connect automation decisions to the cost and frequency trade-off specific to that scenario.

Asking no questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing signal low engagement. A thoughtful question about hyreo's quality challenges or team structure shows you are thinking like a future colleague, not just a candidate trying to get through the round.

Mixing up QA and testing. Quality assurance covers process, standards, and prevention. If an interviewer asks what QA means to you, show that you understand it goes beyond finding bugs after the fact.

Skipping non-functional testing. Performance, security, and accessibility come up more often than candidates expect at product companies. Have at least a basic answer ready on how you approach these areas.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does hyreo typically have for QA Engineers?

Candidates typically report a process of two to three rounds. This usually includes an initial screening call, a technical interview covering testing concepts and sometimes a live exercise, and a final round with a senior engineer or HR. The structure can vary by team and role level, so it is worth asking the recruiter upfront what to expect.

Will I be asked to write test cases or code during the interview?

Candidates commonly report being asked to write test cases on the spot for a given feature or scenario. For automation-focused roles, a short coding or scripting exercise is also possible. Practice writing test cases for features like a login form, a search bar, or a form submission to get comfortable thinking out loud under pressure.

What testing tools should I know for a hyreo interview?

Hyreo has not publicly listed its internal toolchain. Candidates report encountering questions about Selenium, Postman, and general scripting skills. Focus on being genuinely fluent in the tools you have actually used and be ready to explain your reasoning rather than trying to guess and match their stack. Showing real depth in fewer tools is better than shallow knowledge of many.

What salary should I expect as a QA Engineer at hyreo?

Market benchmarks for QA Engineers in India broadly show 4-9 LPA at entry level, 9-17 LPA at mid level, 17-30 LPA at senior level, and 28-45+ LPA for lead roles. Hyreo has not publicly shared its specific pay bands, so use these ranges as context when negotiating. Factor in your experience level, the specific role grade, and any competing offers you have in hand.

How should I prepare if I only have manual testing experience?

Lead with your depth in test planning, exploratory testing, and defect management rather than apologising for a gap in automation. Be honest about your automation skills rather than overstating them. Showing that you understand automation concepts and have a clear plan to build those skills tends to go over well with interviewers. Across the 459 active QA Engineer openings in the current market, many companies hire strong manual testers and invest in upskilling from there.

With 96 open QA Engineer positions, hyreo is hiring at scale across multiple teams and possibly locations, so applying to more than one relevant role is reasonable.
