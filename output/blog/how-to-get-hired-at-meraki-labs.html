03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Based on what candidates commonly report for companies of this type, the Meraki Labs hiring process typically moves through four to five stages. The exact steps can vary by role and team, so treat this as a general map rather than a guarantee.

Stage 1: Application and resume screen
You apply through a job portal or the company website. A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume, often within one to two weeks. Tailoring your resume to the specific role description increases your chances of getting through this stage.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR call
If your profile looks like a fit, you will get a short call with someone from HR or talent acquisition. They will ask about your background, your current situation, your notice period, and your salary expectations. This is also your chance to ask basic questions about the role and team.

Stage 3: Technical or skills round
For engineering and product roles, this is usually a coding test, a case study, or a take-home assignment. Design roles may ask for a portfolio review. Business roles often get a short analytical exercise. The difficulty level is meant to test practical ability, not trick you.

Stage 4: Technical interview or panel
One or two senior people from the team you would be joining will interview you. For tech roles, expect questions on your core domain: data structures, system design, or domain-specific knowledge. For non-tech roles, expect scenario questions about past work and how you handled problems.

Stage 5: Culture fit or final round
Often a conversation with a team lead or a founder-level person. They want to know how you think, how you communicate, and whether you would thrive in their environment. This round is as much about you evaluating them as it is the reverse.

Offer and onboarding
If all rounds go well, HR will share a verbal or written offer. Background verification typically follows before the official letter is issued. Notice periods are usually respected, though some teams move faster if you are immediately available.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Companies like Meraki Labs, especially those in a growth phase, tend to prioritise a specific combination of skills and mindset over a long list of credentials.

Ownership and initiative: They want people who do not wait to be told what to do. If you can show examples from past work where you spotted a problem and fixed it without being asked, highlight those prominently.

Clear communication: Whether you are in a technical or business role, being able to explain your thinking simply is valued. This shows up in interviews through how you walk through your answers, not just what you say.

Domain depth: For engineering roles, strong fundamentals matter more than knowing every framework. For product and business roles, they look for analytical thinking and the ability to back up decisions with clear reasoning.

Adaptability: Smaller and mid-size tech companies move quickly. They value people who can pick up new tools or contexts without a long ramp-up period.

Cultural alignment: A genuine interest in what the company builds, not just the job title, comes through clearly in interviews. Read about the product before you walk in.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Getting ready for Meraki Labs in a focused, structured way will separate you from candidates who apply cold.

Research the product: Spend time actually using or reading about what Meraki Labs builds. Come with a point of view on what works, what could be better, and where you see opportunities. Interviewers notice this immediately.

Tailor your resume: Match the language in the job description. If the role mentions 'backend engineer with API experience,' your resume should clearly show API work. Do not make them hunt for the connection.

Prepare stories using the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Have four or five strong examples ready from your work history that you can adapt to different questions. Behavioural questions are predictable; your answers should not be vague.

Practice your technical fundamentals: For engineering roles, revisit core concepts relevant to the job description. For product roles, practice estimation and prioritisation frameworks. For business roles, brush up on metrics and basic analytics.

Prepare smart questions: Asking nothing makes you look disengaged. Good questions to have ready include: 'What does success look like in the first few months?', 'What is the biggest challenge the team is working through right now?', and 'How do decisions get made here?'

Sort out logistics: Know your current CTC, your expected CTC range, and your notice period before the HR call. Being unclear on these slows things down and can signal a lack of seriousness.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Technology-focused companies like Meraki Labs tend to build cultures around speed, ownership, and continuous learning. While the exact values vary by team and leadership, a few patterns are common across companies in this space.

Flat-ish structure: In most growing tech companies, there are fewer layers between you and decision-makers. This means more visibility and more responsibility earlier in your career, but also more expectation that you can manage yourself.

Feedback culture: Teams that move fast need honest, direct feedback to course-correct quickly. Expect to receive candid input on your work and to give the same to your peers. If you prefer a more formal or hierarchical feedback process, this kind of environment takes some adjustment.

Learning on the job: Meraki Labs, like most companies in their growth stage, will expect you to figure things out that no one has documented yet. A curiosity mindset, where you enjoy learning from ambiguity rather than finding it stressful, fits well here.

Impact-focused: The measure of good work is usually the outcome it drives, not just the effort put in. Showing you can connect your work to a real business or user result will resonate well in interviews and on the job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the Meraki Labs hiring process usually take?

The full process from application to offer commonly takes several weeks, depending on the role and how quickly both sides move. Technical roles with multiple interview rounds tend to take longer than business or support roles. Staying responsive to recruiter messages and being ready with your documents speeds things up on your end.

Does Meraki Labs hire freshers or only experienced candidates?

It depends on the role. Some positions are open to freshers, particularly in engineering, operations, or business development, while senior roles require demonstrated experience. With 8 open positions currently listed, it is worth reading each job description carefully to check the experience requirement before applying.

What should I say in the HR call to make a good impression?

Keep your introduction crisp: who you are, what you have done, and why this role specifically interests you. Avoid vague answers like 'I want growth.' Instead, tie your interest to something specific about the company or the problem they are solving. Also have your CTC and notice period details ready because HR will ask.

Can I negotiate the salary offer at Meraki Labs?

Salary negotiation is standard practice in the Indian job market and most companies expect it. Come prepared with a clear expected range based on Glassdoor data or industry surveys for your role and experience level. Frame your counter as a reasoned ask, not a demand, and keep the conversation collaborative.

What is the best way to follow up after an interview?

Send a short thank-you note to the recruiter within a day of your interview, referencing one specific thing from the conversation. If you have not heard back after the timeline they gave you, one polite follow-up message is appropriate. Avoid following up multiple times in a short window as it can work against you.

Is it helpful to connect with Meraki Labs employees on LinkedIn before applying?

A warm connection can help your application stand out, but approach it carefully. Reaching out with a thoughtful message about the work rather than asking for a referral immediately tends to land better. A genuine conversation about the team or the product can sometimes lead to a referral organically, which does increase your visibility in the process.
