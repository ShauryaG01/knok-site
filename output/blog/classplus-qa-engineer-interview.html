01 Overview

Overview

Classplus is a B2B EdTech platform that helps coaching institutes and independent tutors digitise their courses, collect fees, and engage students online. The QA team carries real weight here: a bug in the payment flow or the live-class feature directly affects the income of thousands of tutors who rely on the product daily. Classplus currently has 13 QA Engineer openings, and candidates report a process that typically spans two to four rounds covering manual testing basics, automation experience, and a practical task.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up most often across Classplus QA Engineer interview rounds, based on what candidates publicly report. Many are shaped by the EdTech product context and the fast-shipping culture at a growth-stage startup.

  1. Walk us through how you would test a new feature on the Classplus tutor app before it goes live. Tests your end-to-end thinking from reading the requirement to giving sign-off.
  1. How do you write test cases for a payment gateway integration? Payments are central to the Classplus business model, so expect this question in almost every round.
  1. Describe your hands-on experience with mobile app testing on Android and/or iOS. Classplus is a mobile-first product, so this matters more here than at many companies.
  1. How do you prioritise bugs when multiple issues appear close to a release deadline? Tests your judgment on severity, business impact, and how you communicate with the team under pressure.
  1. What is your approach to regression testing when the dev team ships updates every two weeks? Agile cadences are the norm at EdTech startups; show you can maintain coverage without slowing things down.
  1. Which automation tools have you used, and how did you wire them into a CI/CD pipeline? Selenium, Appium, and Postman are the tools candidates mention most often.
  1. Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle. What did you do? A classic behavioural question focused on communication and decision-making under pressure.
  1. How do you test REST APIs beyond just checking the response status code? Expect follow-ups on schema validation, edge cases, and response time thresholds.
  1. How would you design a test plan for a feature that lets students pay course fees in instalments? A domain-specific design question combining payment flows with EdTech context.
  1. What QA metrics do you track, and how do you present quality data to the team? Shows whether you think about quality holistically, not just as a bug-count exercise.
  1. How do you handle a situation where a developer says a bug is 'by design'? Tests interpersonal skills and the ability to escalate constructively.
  1. If you were building an automation suite for a mobile tutor app from scratch, where would you start? A senior-level architecture question that candidates report appearing in mid and senior rounds.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all experience-based questions. The three examples below are written for the Classplus product context.

---

Q: Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were two days from shipping a payment module update. During final regression, I found that refund confirmations were not reaching students when the original transaction used a wallet balance.

*Task:* I had to decide quickly whether to block the release, raise a severity-1 ticket, or flag it as a known issue with a documented workaround.

*Action:* I reproduced the bug across three device types, documented exact steps, checked production analytics to gauge how many transactions used wallet top-ups, and escalated to the product manager with a written impact summary. I also suggested a short-term fix: send a manual confirmation email while the dev team patched the underlying API call.

*Result:* The release was delayed by one day. The patch went out cleanly, and the workaround covered all edge cases in the interim. The PM mentioned later that the structured escalation note was what allowed the team to act quickly rather than debate the severity.

---

Q: Describe how you set up API test coverage for a new endpoint.

*Situation:* My team was releasing a new student-enrolment API with no existing automated coverage. Any regression would only surface during slow, manual testing.

*Task:* I was asked to build a Postman collection that could run in CI and flag failures before code was merged.

*Action:* I mapped every endpoint in the API contract, wrote test scripts covering happy paths, boundary values, and common error codes, added schema validation using Postman's built-in assertion library, and wired the collection to run via Newman in the GitHub Actions pipeline.

*Result:* Two regressions were caught before they reached staging within the first sprint. The team adopted the collection as a standard template for all new endpoints going forward.

---

Q: How do you prioritise bugs under release pressure?

*Situation:* During a sprint-end crunch, my bug tracker had several open issues with only a few hours left before code freeze.

*Task:* I needed to triage quickly and agree with the dev lead on what blocked the release versus what could move to the next sprint.

*Action:* I sorted bugs by three criteria: user-facing severity, how often the affected flow was used, and whether a workaround existed. I prepared a short table (severity, affected users, workaround yes/no) and walked the dev lead through it in a brief call. We agreed on the blockers and moved the rest to a hot-fix backlog with documented reproduction steps.

*Result:* The release shipped on time. The blockers were resolved within the deadline, and none of the deferred bugs caused a production incident in the following week.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the most useful framework for experience-based questions and covers roughly half of what you will face at Classplus. Lead with a one-sentence situation, keep the task brief, put most of your time into the action, and close with a concrete result. If the result is qualitative (the team trusted the process more, the release shipped without incident), say so plainly.

Test design structure helps with 'how would you test X' questions. A reliable sequence: (1) understand the requirement and acceptance criteria, (2) identify user personas and critical flows, (3) list positive, negative, and edge-case scenarios, (4) decide what to automate versus test manually, (5) define entry and exit criteria. Walking through these steps shows systematic thinking, not ad-hoc clicking.

Impact first applies when describing bugs or metrics. Lead with the business impact ('this broke the payment confirmation flow for wallet-based transactions') before the technical root cause. Interviewers at product-focused companies respond well to QA candidates who connect quality to user trust and revenue, not just defect counts.

PAR (Problem, Action, Result) works well for tool and automation questions. Name the problem that drove the tool choice, describe the actions you took to set it up or maintain it, and state the outcome the team got. Every tool name on your resume should be attached to a story, not listed in isolation.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain awareness. Classplus serves coaching institutes and tutors, not end consumers directly. Candidates who frame test scenarios around 'a tutor uploading a study material PDF' or 'a student trying to rejoin a dropped live class' signal that they have studied the product. Generic answers framed around 'e-commerce checkout' score lower in this context.

Speed with rigour. Classplus ships frequently. Interviewers want to see that you can maintain test coverage without becoming a bottleneck. Talking about risk-based test selection, smoke versus full regression split, or fast feedback loops in CI shows you understand startup QA realities.

Communication under pressure. Several questions are really testing how you talk to developers and product managers during a disagreement or a deadline crunch. Use calm, evidence-based language. A phrase like 'I documented the impact and brought it to the PM with options' reads far better than 'I insisted the bug had to be fixed.'

Automation mindset, not just automation skills. Writing Selenium scripts is expected at mid level and above. What interviewers want to know is how you decide what to automate, how you keep the suite maintainable as the product evolves quickly, and how you handle flaky tests. Show that automation is a deliberate, reasoned choice in your workflow.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A two-week focused plan works well for most candidates, based on what those who have gone through the process typically report.

Week 1: Product research and manual testing foundations

Spend the first couple of days understanding Classplus as a product. Download the app, try the tutor and student flows, and note where payments, live classes, and content uploads intersect. These are your highest-risk areas and the most likely topics for test-design questions.

Next, revisit your core manual testing skills. Write a sample test plan for a feature you have not tested before, practise identifying boundary values and equivalence partitions, and review how to write a defect report that a developer can act on without needing follow-up clarification.

Then brush up on API testing. If you have not used Postman recently, run through a public REST API and write assertions beyond status codes: check schema, response time, and error handling for unexpected inputs.

Week 2: Automation, mock interviews, and final prep

Review the automation framework you listed on your resume. Be ready to explain your folder structure, how you handle test data, and how flaky tests are managed. If you use Appium for mobile, prepare one story about a real mobile-specific challenge you solved.

Practise your STAR answers out loud for the five most common experience-based questions. Record yourself if possible; aim to stay under three minutes per answer without losing the result.

Do one full mock round covering a test-design question, an automation question, and a conflict-resolution question. Note where you over-explain or lose the thread.

Finally, prepare two or three questions to ask the interviewer, such as: 'How does the QA team collaborate with product during sprint planning?' or 'What does your current mobile automation coverage look like?'

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the 'why' in test design. Many candidates list test cases without explaining why those specific cases matter. Always connect your scenario to a real user risk or a business-critical flow.

Generic tool answers. Saying 'I have used Selenium' without a supporting story reads as resume padding. Have at least one specific project ready for every tool you mention.

Underestimating mobile-specific questions. Classplus is a mobile-first platform. Candidates who only discuss web testing are at a disadvantage. If your experience is mostly web, be upfront about it and show you understand what is different about mobile: device fragmentation, network conditions, and OS version coverage.

Confusing severity with priority. Severity is how badly a bug breaks the system; priority is how urgently it needs fixing. Mixing these up during a triage discussion is a slip that experienced interviewers notice immediately.

Passive answers about developer disagreements. Saying 'I raised the ticket and moved on' signals low ownership. Show that you follow up, document impact, and escalate through the right channels when needed.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who say 'I think you covered everything' leave no signal of curiosity or culture fit. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team or product before walking into the interview.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Classplus QA Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report two to four rounds in most cases. The first is typically a screening call covering your background and basic testing concepts. Subsequent rounds go deeper on test design, automation, and past project experience. A final round with a senior engineer or manager is common for mid and senior roles, and candidates report it sometimes includes a test-architecture or system-design discussion.

Is coding tested in the Classplus QA interview?

It depends on the seniority and focus of the role. Candidates applying for automation-heavy positions often report a coding task, typically writing a small Selenium or Appium script or automating a basic API test. For manual-focused roles, a full coding exercise is less common, but basic scripting knowledge is still valued. Prepare at least one scripting language, Python or Java, to cover your bases.

What salary can I expect as a QA Engineer at Classplus?

Knok jobradar data shows QA Engineers in this segment earn 4-9 LPA at entry level, 9-17 LPA at mid level, and 17-30 LPA at the senior level. Company-specific offer data for Classplus is not publicly reported at a meaningful sample size, so treat these as indicative ranges for the EdTech startup segment. Negotiating on the strength of your automation skills or mobile testing depth can move an offer upward.

Does Classplus hire freshers for QA roles?

Classplus does post early-career QA openings, but most positions favour at least one to two years of hands-on experience. If you are a fresher, focus on demonstrating a structured testing mindset, any internship or project-based testing work, and basic tool familiarity with Postman and JIRA. A take-home assignment is more commonly reported by candidates without prior industry experience.

How long does the hiring process take from first round to offer?

Based on what candidates publicly report, the process typically takes two to four weeks end to end. Timelines can vary based on team bandwidth and how many roles are open at a given time. Following up politely after one week if you have not heard back is considered reasonable and is unlikely to hurt your candidacy.

What tools should I know for a Classplus QA interview?

Candidates report that Selenium, Appium, Postman, JIRA, and basic SQL come up most often. For mobile testing, familiarity with Android Studio and Charles Proxy is useful. CI/CD basics such as Jenkins or GitHub Actions are increasingly expected at mid and senior levels. Focus on tools you have genuinely used in projects rather than listing every tool you have heard of.
