01 Overview

Overview

Vetic is a technology-first pet care chain growing across India's metros. The Technical Program Manager role there sits at the crossroads of engineering, product, and clinic operations. You are expected to own complex cross-functional programmes, drive delivery timelines, and translate clinic and business requirements into clear engineering plans.

With 7 open roles at Vetic right now and 313 TPM openings listed across India as of July 2026, this is an active hiring segment. Bangalore leads demand with 41 openings, followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), and Hyderabad (12). At a growth-stage company like Vetic, the TPM role is hands-on. Candidates who have managed multi-location product rollouts, third-party integrations, or health-tech programmes typically get closer attention from interviewers.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

  1. Tell us about a large cross-functional programme you owned end-to-end. How did you keep it on track?
  2. How do you manage dependencies between engineering teams that work on different release cycles?
  3. Walk us through how you would plan a city-wide rollout of a new clinic feature.
  4. Describe a time you had to reprioritise a roadmap because of a business change midway through a sprint.
  5. How do you handle scope creep without damaging team morale or missing deadlines?
  6. Tell us about a project that failed or was delayed. What was your role and what did you change afterwards?
  7. How do you bridge communication between a technical engineering team and a non-technical operations or business team?
  8. What does a healthy programme look like to you? What signals do you track?
  9. How would you approach integrating a third-party vendor tool into an existing product platform?
  10. Describe a time you pushed back on a stakeholder request. How did you handle that conversation?
  11. How do you identify and manage risk when a programme spans multiple teams and timelines?
  12. How have you worked with data or analytics teams to measure the impact of a programme?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a large cross-functional programme you owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* My company was rolling out a new appointment booking system across clinics in three cities, involving product, backend, frontend, and on-ground operations teams.

*Task:* I was responsible for delivering the rollout on schedule without disrupting existing clinic operations.

*Action:* I mapped all cross-team dependencies in a shared tracker, ran a weekly sync with each team lead, spotted a backend API delay early and renegotiated the timeline with the product head, and coordinated a pilot at a couple of clinics before the full launch.

*Result:* Most clinics went live on the planned date. A small number followed a few weeks later because of a third-party hardware delay outside our control. Post-launch defect rates were low and the operations team reported noticeably smoother patient intake.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to reprioritise a roadmap because of a business change.

*Situation:* Mid-sprint, leadership decided to fast-track a loyalty programme feature after a competitor made a similar announcement.

*Task:* I had to revise the sprint plan without losing momentum on work already in progress.

*Action:* I ran a quick triage session with the product manager and tech lead, identified which in-progress tasks could be paused with low rollback cost, and created a revised plan with clear owner assignments. I communicated the change to all stakeholders the same day, including a short note on what was deprioritised and why.

*Result:* The loyalty feature shipped ahead of the original target. The deprioritised items were picked up in the next sprint with no meaningful delay to the overall quarter plan.

---

Q: Tell us about a project that failed or was delayed.

*Situation:* A third-party payment integration I managed missed its go-live date by several weeks.

*Task:* I owned vendor coordination and internal engineering alignment for this integration.

*Action:* In the retrospective I identified two root causes: I had not built buffer time for vendor API documentation gaps, and I had not escalated a security review blocker early enough. I documented both gaps, proposed a revised vendor onboarding checklist, and created an escalation protocol for cross-team blockers.

*Result:* The integration launched successfully after the delay. The checklist was adopted by the wider team for all future vendor work, and the average time to clear vendor blockers dropped noticeably.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioural questions. Keep Situation and Task brief, and spend most of your speaking time on Action and Result. Interviewers care most about what you personally did and what changed as a result.

For system or process questions, use a three-part structure: state the problem and context, describe your approach step by step, then share the outcome or trade-off you accepted. Growth-stage companies like Vetic want to see that you think in trade-offs, not just best-case plans.

For stakeholder conflict questions, lead with how you listened and gathered information before acting. Show that you separated the person from the problem and focused on shared goals such as timeline, quality, or business outcome.

For programme health and metrics questions, name specific signals you track: milestone completion rate, how long a blocker sits unresolved before escalation, post-launch defect rate, and stakeholder alignment via a quick weekly check-in. Avoid vague answers like 'I keep everyone aligned.'

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Vetic interviewers typically look for three qualities in a TPM candidate.

Cross-functional ownership. Can you drive a programme that spans engineering, product, and non-technical teams like clinic operations or vendor management? They want evidence from your actual work, not just familiarity with frameworks.

Communication clarity. TPMs at Vetic interact with both engineers and clinic staff. Candidates who can translate technical constraints into plain language, and operational needs into engineering specs, stand out clearly. Prepare a story where you bridged a communication gap between two very different teams.

Comfort with ambiguity. Vetic is a growth-stage company. Processes are not always fully defined. Interviewers want to see that you can create structure where none exists, without becoming rigid. Bring examples of programmes you set up from scratch or rescued from unclear or shifting requirements.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Ground yourself in the role and the company. Read everything public about Vetic's product: their app, clinic booking flow, and any recent press. Map out what a TPM would likely own: clinic onboarding, feature rollouts, third-party integrations, data platform programmes. Write down several programme stories from your own experience that connect to these areas.

Sharpen your stories. Pick your strongest experience stories and write each one out in STAR format. Cover at least one failure or delay, one stakeholder conflict, one multi-team rollout, and one time you used data to make a programme decision. Practice telling each story clearly in under two minutes.

Prepare your questions and logistics. Candidates typically go through multiple rounds at companies like Vetic, including a hiring manager screen, a technical or case-based round, and a cross-functional interview. Prepare thoughtful questions for each round. Good questions show you have done your homework: ask about the programmes you would own in your first few months, the biggest cross-team challenge the TPM org is currently solving, or how success is measured for this role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking about coordination instead of ownership. TPM interviews reward candidates who say 'I drove,' 'I decided,' and 'I escalated.' Candidates who describe themselves only as facilitators or connectors, without showing clear ownership, tend to score lower.

Skipping the Result. Many candidates give strong Situation and Action details but forget to close with an outcome. Always finish with a clear result. If you do not have hard numbers, describe the qualitative change: 'the ops team stopped escalating this issue,' or 'we shipped on the original timeline.'

Being vague about the technical layer. A TPM is not expected to write code, but Vetic is a tech company. Candidates who can describe the technical architecture of a programme they managed (APIs, databases, third-party dependencies) come across as far more credible than those who speak only in business terms.

Overpreparing for process theory. Interviewers want your real experience, not textbook agile definitions. If asked about sprint planning or dependency management, answer with a specific story from your work, then name the framework only if it adds clarity.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask nothing at the end of a round signal low interest. Prepare at least two genuine questions per round.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Vetic TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes an initial HR or recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation focused on background and motivation, and one or two deeper rounds covering programme experience and cross-functional scenarios. Some candidates mention a case study or take-home exercise as well. Confirm the exact format with your recruiter after the first call, as processes at growth-stage companies can vary.

What is the salary range for a TPM at Vetic?

Vetic has not publicly disclosed salary bands for this role. Glassdoor and industry surveys for TPM roles at growth-stage health-tech companies in India can give you a starting benchmark. Go into the negotiation with a range you are comfortable defending, based on your research and experience, rather than waiting for the company to anchor first.

Does Vetic ask technical coding questions in the TPM interview?

TPM interviews at most companies do not include live coding rounds. Candidates report that Vetic focuses on programme management experience, stakeholder handling, and cross-functional scenarios. You should still be comfortable discussing technical architecture at a conceptual level, describing how you worked with engineering teams, and reading a basic API spec or system diagram.

How should I prepare for a case study or take-home task?

Structure your response around four things: understanding the problem clearly, listing your assumptions, proposing a phased plan with clear ownership, and identifying risks with mitigation steps. Growth-stage companies like Vetic value practical, actionable thinking over polished presentations. Show that you can make reasonable decisions with incomplete information.

Is prior pet care or health-tech experience required for the Vetic TPM role?

It is not typically listed as a hard requirement. Candidates with strong cross-functional programme management experience from fintech, logistics, health-tech, or consumer apps have been shortlisted. What matters more is your ability to manage complexity, align diverse teams, and deliver on time. Domain knowledge in pet care is a plus, not a gate.
