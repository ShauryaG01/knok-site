01 Overview

Overview

Aveva is a UK-headquartered industrial software company whose products, covering SCADA, HMI, engineering lifecycle management, and asset performance, serve sectors like oil and gas, power generation, and manufacturing. In India, Aveva runs engineering and product delivery centres, making it a meaningful employer for Business Analysts who want to work at the intersection of industrial operations and software.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows 5 open Business Analyst roles at Aveva across India, part of a broader market of 398 BA openings nationwide, with Bangalore (53 roles), Delhi (48), and Mumbai (24) leading the count. Candidates report that Aveva's BA interview process typically unfolds over multiple rounds: an initial HR or recruiter screen, one or more business or domain discussions, and a final conversation with a hiring manager or senior stakeholder. A short written case or take-home exercise appears in some processes, though this varies by team.

Preparation is the lever you control. This guide covers the questions most commonly reported, how to frame strong answers, and the specific steps that give you the best chance of converting an Aveva interview into an offer.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from Aveva BA interviews, covering domain fit, stakeholder management, analytical thinking, and product knowledge. Work through each one with a concrete example before your interview.

  1. Aveva's software serves asset-intensive industries. How do you get up to speed on a new domain quickly?
  2. Walk me through a time you translated a complex technical requirement into a user story a non-technical stakeholder could approve.
  3. How have you handled a situation where two business units had conflicting requirements for the same feature?
  4. Aveva works with global teams. Describe how you have managed stakeholders across different time zones or cultural contexts.
  5. Give an example of a process you mapped end-to-end. What gaps did you find and what did you recommend?
  6. How do you prioritize a backlog when the business keeps adding urgent items?
  7. Describe a time you used data to challenge an assumption held by a senior stakeholder.
  8. How would you gather requirements for a new module when your subject matter experts are engineers rather than product people?
  9. Tell us about a project where you balanced scope, timeline, and quality. What trade-offs did you make?
  10. How do you validate that a delivered solution actually meets the original business need?
  11. What requirements management or project tracking tools have you used, and how do you choose between them?
  12. Aveva is moving more products toward SaaS and cloud delivery. How does that shift change the way you define requirements or measure success?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How have you handled a situation where two business units had conflicting requirements for the same feature?

*Situation:* At my previous company, the operations team and the finance team both wanted a reporting dashboard, but their priorities were opposite. Operations needed real-time drill-down by equipment. Finance wanted a rolled-up monthly view with a clean export option.

*Task:* I needed to define a single set of requirements the development team could build within the agreed sprint cycle, without delivering a half-measure to either side.

*Action:* I held separate workshops with each team to uncover their underlying jobs-to-be-done rather than their stated feature requests. I then brought both teams into a joint session, placed their underlying goals side by side, and proposed a tabbed dashboard where each team got their own default view. I also presented the cost comparison between building two separate solutions versus one configurable one, so the decision had a clear business case behind it.

*Result:* Both teams agreed on the shared design. The product shipped on schedule. Operations adopted it for daily shift handovers and finance used the export feature for monthly reporting packs.

---

Q: Describe a time you used data to challenge an assumption held by a senior stakeholder.

*Situation:* A senior director at my previous firm believed the primary driver of customer churn was a lack of features. This assumption was loading the roadmap with new functionality at the expense of other priorities.

*Task:* I was asked to validate roadmap priorities before the next planning cycle. I had access to support ticket logs, exit survey summaries, and usage analytics.

*Action:* I grouped support tickets by category and overlaid them with exit survey themes. The pattern showed that most churned customers had raised unresolved support tickets in their final weeks with the product, not feature requests. I prepared a concise one-pager with the evidence and requested time in the planning meeting to walk the director through the findings.

*Result:* The director agreed to shift a portion of the roadmap toward support experience improvements. The business tracked the impact through its standard NPS process and saw positive movement in subsequent quarters.

---

Q: Walk me through a time you mapped a process end-to-end. What gaps did you find?

*Situation:* I joined a project mid-stream at a manufacturing client where the purchase order approval process was causing procurement delays. No single person had visibility across the full flow because it crossed several departments.

*Task:* I needed to document the as-is process and identify where delays originated before the team could design a future state.

*Action:* I ran brief one-on-one sessions with representatives from procurement, finance, and operations. I used swim-lane diagrams to capture each handoff point. Those sessions surfaced a key gap: finance reviewers were not notified automatically when a PO crossed a certain value threshold. They depended on manual email forwarding from procurement, which was inconsistent.

*Result:* I recommended an automated notification rule in the ERP system. After implementation, the client reported a meaningful reduction in average PO cycle time, measured through their internal KPIs.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions. Aveva interviewers typically probe for specific past behavior rather than hypothetical answers. Structure every behavioral response as: Situation (brief context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, not what the team did), Result (measurable or observable outcome). Keep the Situation short and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Jobs-to-be-done for requirements questions. When asked how you gather or manage requirements, frame your answer around the underlying business goal rather than the stated feature request. Saying 'I always ask why the user needs this, not just what they want' signals BA maturity that Aveva values in candidates who work with engineering clients who may not articulate needs in product terms.

Trade-off framing for prioritization questions. Prioritization questions have no single right answer. Interviewers want to see that you can weigh business value, technical effort, risk, and stakeholder impact, and communicate trade-offs clearly. Avoid describing one rigid framework. Instead, show how you adapt your approach to the context.

Data-first for analytical questions. For any question about analysis or insight, lead with the data source, then your method, then the finding, then the recommendation. This mirrors how Aveva's BA role typically operates in practice, where evidence-based recommendations carry more weight than intuition.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity, not domain expertise. Aveva operates in industrial verticals that most BA candidates have not worked in directly. Interviewers are not expecting deep knowledge of SCADA or process automation on day one. They are looking for evidence that you can learn a technical domain quickly, ask the right questions of engineers, and translate what you learn into clear requirements.

Stakeholder management at multiple levels. Aveva's BA roles sit between engineering teams, product managers, and external clients, sometimes across geographies. Interviewers look for candidates who can adjust their communication style depending on the audience, whether that is a plant operator, a software architect, or a C-suite client contact.

Comfort with ambiguity. Industrial software projects often involve legacy systems, incomplete documentation, and stakeholders who are domain experts but not product people. Candidates who can structure a problem from scratch, without a ready-made framework handed to them, stand out.

Ownership of outcomes. Aveva values BAs who track whether the solution they helped define actually delivered the intended result. Candidates who can speak to post-launch impact, user adoption, or process improvement signal the kind of end-to-end thinking the role requires.

Tool fluency, not tool dependence. Familiarity with JIRA, Confluence, Azure DevOps, or similar platforms is expected. But interviewers want to see that you understand why you use a tool, not just that you can operate it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Understand Aveva's business first. Spend time reading Aveva's public product pages, recent press releases, and any analyst commentary you can find. The goal is to understand the industries they serve and why their software matters to those customers. You should be able to speak naturally about industrial software, asset lifecycle, and operational technology during the interview.

Prepare one strong story for each theme. Map your past experience to the main question themes: stakeholder conflict, requirements gathering under ambiguity, process analysis, data-driven recommendation, backlog prioritization, and post-delivery validation. One well-prepared STAR story per theme is enough.

Research the specific team. Aveva's BA roles vary by product area, from cloud platforms to SCADA to engineering data management. If you know which team is interviewing you, look up that product line, its target users, and any recent news. Candidates who reference the team's actual product in their questions stand out.

Prepare sharp questions to ask. Interviews at Aveva typically include time for candidate questions. Good options: how the BA role interacts with the product team versus the client, what success looks like in the first few months, and how the team handles requirements changes mid-sprint.

Practice out loud. Reviewing notes is not the same as speaking your answers. Run through your STAR stories aloud at least twice before the interview. Record yourself if you can. Notice where you hesitate or become vague, and tighten those sections.

Check the job description carefully. Aveva's BA postings often name specific tools, methodologies, or industry verticals. Use those exact terms in your answers where you have genuine experience with them.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving generic answers. Aveva interviewers can tell when a candidate is reciting a textbook definition of business analysis rather than drawing on real experience. Every answer should be grounded in a specific situation from your own work history.

Confusing the BA role with a PM or QA role. Business Analysts at Aveva are expected to own the requirements layer, not the product roadmap and not the test cases. Be clear about where your responsibility begins and ends in the examples you give.

Ignoring the industrial context. Candidates who treat the Aveva interview like a generic BA interview miss an opportunity. Referencing industrial software concepts, even at a basic level, shows you have done your homework and take the role seriously.

Weak results in STAR answers. The Result section is where many candidates lose ground. Vague results like 'the project went well' do not signal impact. Even without a specific figure, describe the observable outcome: the stakeholder approved the design, the team shipped on time, the client adopted the feature in their daily workflow.

Talking about what the team did instead of what you did. In behavioral questions, interviewers want to know your personal contribution. Phrases like 'we decided' or 'the team ran' obscure your individual role. Use 'I' deliberately.

Not asking any questions. Candidates who have no questions at the end of an Aveva interview signal low interest or low preparation. Prepare at least two specific questions about the role, the team, or the product area.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Aveva typically have for a BA role?

Candidates report that the process typically involves an initial recruiter or HR call, followed by one or two business or domain discussions, and a final conversation with a hiring manager or senior stakeholder. Some teams include a short written exercise or scenario. The exact structure varies by team and seniority level, so it is worth asking the recruiter to outline the full process at the start.

Does Aveva ask case studies or purely behavioral questions?

Candidates report a mix of both. Behavioral questions using the STAR format are common across all rounds. Some teams also present a short business scenario where you walk through how you would approach requirements gathering or stakeholder alignment. Preparing for both formats is the safest approach.

What domain knowledge does Aveva expect from a BA candidate?

Aveva does not typically require candidates to arrive with deep knowledge of industrial software, SCADA, or process automation. What interviewers look for is the ability to learn a technical domain quickly, ask the right questions of engineers, and translate complex inputs into clear requirements. Showing genuine curiosity about Aveva's industries during the interview matters more than arriving with memorized technical terms.

How important is technical knowledge, such as SQL or data tools, for this role?

The level of technical depth expected varies by team. For most BA roles at Aveva, comfort with data analysis tools and platforms like JIRA or Azure DevOps is expected. SQL or data querying skills are a plus, particularly for roles connected to reporting or data products. Check the specific job description for tools listed and be ready to demonstrate genuine familiarity with them.

What salary can I expect as a Business Analyst at Aveva in India?

Aveva does not publicly publish salary bands for India, so specific figures are not available here. Glassdoor and levels.fyi are the most commonly cited sources for BA compensation data at multinational software companies in India, and they publish ranges by city and experience level. Cross-referencing those sources with the seniority of the specific role gives a reasonable reference point before you negotiate.

How long does the Aveva hiring process typically take from application to offer?

Candidates report varying timelines depending on the team and hiring urgency. Processes can range from a few weeks to over a month. Staying responsive to recruiter communication and following up politely if you have not heard back after a week at each stage is generally good practice.
