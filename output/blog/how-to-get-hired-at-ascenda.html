01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Ascenda is a fintech company headquartered in Singapore that builds loyalty and rewards infrastructure for banks and financial institutions around the world. Their platform powers points programs, travel redemptions, and card benefit ecosystems for some of the largest banks globally. As of mid-2026, Ascenda has 12 open roles across functions including engineering, product, data, and business development.

Getting hired here is competitive. Ascenda sits at the intersection of financial services and product technology, and they expect candidates to be comfortable in both worlds. The company is globally distributed, so strong English communication and the ability to work across time zones are practical requirements, not just nice-to-haves.

For candidates based in India, some roles are remote-eligible while others are tied to the Singapore office. Check the specific job description carefully before applying, since location requirements vary by team and seniority level.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Based on publicly reported candidate experiences, Ascenda's hiring process typically runs through four to five stages. The exact structure can shift depending on the role, seniority, and team.

Application and resume screen. Your resume is reviewed for relevant experience in fintech, B2B SaaS, or loyalty technology. Make sure your resume clearly maps your skills to what the job description asks for. Gaps or mismatches at this stage are the most common reason applications do not move forward.

Recruiter call. A short introductory call where HR covers your background, your interest in Ascenda, and basic logistics. This is your first chance to show you understand what the company does and why this specific role makes sense for you.

Technical or functional round. For engineering roles, expect a coding assessment or a system design conversation, depending on your seniority. Product and data candidates may receive a take-home case study or an analytical exercise. Business and partnerships roles typically involve a scenario discussion where you work through a client problem live.

Panel or cross-functional interview. You will likely meet with two or three people, possibly including the hiring manager and a peer from the team. Expect behavioral questions about ownership, cross-functional collaboration, and how you handle ambiguity under pressure.

Final round. Senior hires often have a closing conversation with a VP or director. This round focuses less on technical depth and more on values alignment, how you think about the business, and how you see yourself growing within the company.

After each round, the recruiter typically gives you a timeline for next steps. If you have not heard back within two weeks, a polite follow-up is reasonable.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Ascenda's products run inside some of the world's largest banks, which means the bar for reliability and precision is high. Here is what strong candidates tend to bring to the table.

Domain awareness. You do not need a loyalty tech background specifically, but understanding how banks think about customer retention, rewards economics, and partner integrations gives you a clear edge. Candidates who can connect their past work to financial services problems tend to move further in the process.

Cross-functional thinking. Ascenda's platform touches engineering, partnerships, compliance, and client success simultaneously. They look for people who can collaborate across teams without needing constant direction or hand-holding.

Ownership mindset. Publicly available reviews on Glassdoor and similar platforms consistently mention that Ascenda values people who take initiative, follow through, and treat their work as if it belongs to them.

Clear communication. The company is globally distributed and works with enterprise clients. Strong written and verbal communication matters for every function, not just client-facing ones.

Technical depth for engineering roles. Backend systems, API design, and data pipelines are core to what Ascenda builds. Candidates who can discuss trade-offs in system architecture, rather than just writing clean code, tend to stand out at the mid-to-senior level.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product before the interview. Read Ascenda's public case studies, blog posts, and any press coverage you can find. If you can walk into the interview and explain how their rewards engine integrates with a bank's card infrastructure, you will immediately stand out from candidates who only read the job description.

Use the STAR format for behavioral stories. Ascenda's interviews include a strong behavioral component. For each competency you expect to be tested on, such as ownership, handling ambiguity, or cross-functional delivery, prepare a specific example: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep examples tight and outcome-focused.

Learn fintech and loyalty basics. Even for non-engineering roles, being familiar with concepts like points float, redemption economics, and API-first product architecture signals genuine interest in the space. You do not need to be an expert. A working understanding goes a long way in interviews.

Practice system design if you are in engineering. Mid-to-senior backend candidates should be ready to discuss how they would design a high-throughput points ledger or a third-party partner integration layer. Think about consistency, fault tolerance, and scale rather than just coding syntax.

Ask sharp questions in every round. Ask about how the team handles production incidents, how roadmap decisions are made, and what success looks like in your first six months. These questions signal that you are thinking like an owner, not just a candidate trying to clear a round.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Ascenda operates like a high-performance product company with the accountability expectations of a financial services firm. The culture is widely described as fast-paced and outcome-driven, with a relatively flat hierarchy compared to legacy banks or large IT services companies.

Global by default. The team spans Singapore, India, the US, and Europe. Remote-first norms mean you are expected to communicate clearly in writing, manage your own calendar, and deliver without close supervision.

Ownership over process. Employees who thrive here consistently describe a culture where you are trusted to own your area completely. For self-starters, this is energising. For people who prefer structured direction, it can feel like a steep adjustment initially.

Impact-driven growth. Promotions and recognition are tied closely to what you ship and how it affects clients. According to publicly available employee feedback, visibility without substance does not go far here.

Demanding but collaborative. The work involves tight coordination with partner banks and enterprise clients, which means deadlines carry real weight. At the same time, teammates are generally described as sharp and willing to support each other.

If you are coming from a large Indian IT services background, expect a meaningful culture shift: less process documentation, more independent judgment, and a direct feedback style that can feel blunt but is generally well-intentioned.

Ascenda does hire from India, and some roles are fully remote-eligible. However, the company is headquartered in Singapore, and many senior or leadership positions are tied to the Singapore office. The recruiter usually shares a timeline after each stage, so ask for one if they do not volunteer it. A polite follow-up after two weeks of silence is completely acceptable.

No, a loyalty tech background is not a strict requirement. Ascenda hires from banking, fintech, SaaS, and general software engineering backgrounds.
