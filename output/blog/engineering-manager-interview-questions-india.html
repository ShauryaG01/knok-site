01 Overview

Overview

Engineering Manager (EM) roles are among the most competitive positions in Indian tech right now. Knok's job radar tracked 975 open EM positions as of July 2026. Bangalore leads with 182 openings, followed by Delhi at 53, Pune and Chennai at 20 each, Hyderabad at 16, and Mumbai at 12.

Salary bands from the same data show 35-60 LPA for Manager-level roles, 55-90 LPA for Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at the Director level. These ranges align with figures commonly cited on Glassdoor for Indian tech companies.

The EM interview is quite different from a software engineer interview. You are tested on people management, technical judgment, cross-functional collaboration, and delivery track record. Most product companies run multiple rounds covering behavioural questions, scenario-based discussions, a technical depth check, and a leadership panel. This guide covers the questions candidates commonly report, worked sample answers, and a realistic preparation plan.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through how you run performance reviews and set goals with your engineers.

I start goal-setting at the beginning of each half by working with each engineer individually to align their personal growth goals with team OKRs. I avoid top-down goal assignment. Instead, I ask each person where they want to grow and then find projects that serve both the team and that growth direction. During the review itself, I never surprise anyone. I give feedback continuously in weekly one-on-ones, so the formal review is a summary of conversations already had. I write the review draft first, share it with the engineer before submitting, and invite them to add their own perspective. Rating calibration then happens with peer managers so leveling stays consistent across the team.

---

Q: Your team has missed the last two sprint commitments. What steps do you take? (Scenario)

First, I resist the urge to push the team to simply work harder. I do a quick diagnostic: were the estimates off, did scope creep in, were there blocking dependencies, or is someone on the team overloaded? I look at the data from both sprints to find the pattern. If it is estimation, I run a retro focused specifically on how we break down work. If it is scope, I have a direct conversation with product about commitment hygiene. If someone is struggling, I support them privately before the issue compounds. I then set a smaller public commitment for the next sprint so the team can build momentum with a clear win. Transparency with stakeholders is critical: I communicate the delay proactively with a revised timeline, never letting it come as a surprise at the deadline.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to deliver difficult news to your team. (Behavioural, STAR)

Situation: At my previous company, a major product line we had worked on for two years was cancelled by leadership due to a shift in business strategy.

Task: I had to tell a team of eight engineers that their work was being shut down, with no clear next assignment yet.

Action: I did not send an email or wait for a town hall. I called an emergency team meeting the same day I found out, shared everything I knew, and was transparent about what I did not yet know. I acknowledged that this was genuinely disappointing and gave the team space to react. Over the following weeks I worked with HR and senior leadership to map each person to a new team that matched their skills and interests, and I advocated for everyone to retain their current level and comp.

Result: All eight engineers moved to new teams within three weeks. Two of them later told me that how the transition was handled was a reason they stayed at the company. The experience taught me that people can handle hard news; what they cannot handle is being kept in the dark.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions: 20% situation, 10% task, 50% action, 20% result with numbers.

CIRCLES for product cases (PM): Comprehend, Identify customer, Report needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise recommendation.

For system design (engineering): clarify scale (DAU, QPS), draw high-level boxes, deep-dive one component, discuss failure modes and monitoring.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Signals that move Engineering Manager candidates forward in India:
- Ownership of outcomes, not tasks
- Comfort with ambiguity and incomplete data
- Collaboration with cross-functional partners
- Understanding of India-specific constraints (UPI, logistics, multilingual users, price sensitivity)
- Realistic salary expectations aligned with level

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Self-audit and story bank

Write down every major project, team challenge, hiring decision, and conflict you have handled in your career. For each one, write a two-sentence STAR summary. Aim for at least eight strong stories covering delivery, people, conflict, failure, and hiring. These stories are the raw material for every behavioural question.

Week 2: Question drilling

Work through the twelve questions in this guide. Answer each one out loud, not just in your head. Record yourself if possible. Time each answer: behavioural answers should land between ninety seconds and two minutes. Scenario answers can run slightly longer but should be structured (diagnose, act, communicate). Get a peer or former colleague to give you feedback on at least three answers.

Week 3: Technical and domain refresh

Revise DORA metrics, OKR frameworks, and common engineering org structures (feature teams vs. platform teams vs. stream-aligned teams). Read one or two publicly available engineering blog posts from the company you are interviewing at to understand how they think about engineering culture. Practise a system design discussion at a high level, focusing on trade-offs rather than low-level implementation.

Week 4: Mock interviews and logistics

Do at least two full mock interviews with someone who can play the interviewer role. Prepare your own questions to ask the panel: good questions signal strategic thinking. Prepare a crisp two-minute 'about me' covering your background, team size managed, and management philosophy. Confirm the interview format, number of rounds, and who you will meet.

08 Common Questions

Common Questions

Questions below are grouped by theme. Candidates report that scenario and behavioural rounds carry the most weight at senior companies.

Fundamentals

  1. How do you define the boundary between an Engineering Manager and a Tech Lead on the same team?
  2. How do you stay technically sharp while spending most of your time in meetings and people work?
  3. Walk me through how you run performance reviews and set goals with your engineers.
  4. How do you approach building and growing an engineering team from scratch?

Scenario-based

  1. Your team has missed the last two sprint commitments. What steps do you take?
  2. A high performer tells you they are thinking of leaving. How do you respond?
  3. Product changes a key requirement two weeks before a major release. How do you handle it?
  4. Two senior engineers disagree strongly on an architectural choice and neither will back down. What do you do?
  5. You inherit a team with low morale and high attrition. What is your plan for the first ninety days?

Behavioural (STAR format)

  1. Tell me about a time you had to deliver difficult news to your team or to stakeholders.
  2. Describe a situation where you pushed back on a product decision. What happened?
  3. Give me an example of a technical call you made that turned out to be wrong. What did you learn?
09 Topics To Master

Topics To Master

People management

Performance frameworks (OKRs, KPIs), running effective one-on-ones, giving and receiving feedback, performance improvement plans, career ladders, and leveling. Practise articulating how you calibrate ratings across a team.

Engineering delivery

Sprint planning, roadmap prioritisation, estimation techniques, managing technical debt, and how to measure engineering health. DORA metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, mean time to restore) are commonly asked about in senior EM interviews.

Technical depth

You will not be asked to code, but you will be expected to discuss system design trade-offs at a high level, evaluate architectural decisions, and know when to involve your team vs. make a call yourself. Brush up on distributed systems concepts and cloud infrastructure basics.

Cross-functional collaboration

How you work with product managers, designers, finance, and business stakeholders. Be ready to talk about a time you influenced a decision without direct authority.

Hiring and team building

Structured interview design, writing good job descriptions, leveling new hires, and building a diverse pipeline. Interviewers often ask how you identify and retain top talent.

Culture and psychological safety

How you build a culture where engineers raise concerns early, experiment without fear, and feel ownership. Google's Project Aristotle findings on psychological safety are a useful reference point to mention.

Metrics and reporting

How you report engineering progress to leadership in business terms, not just ticket counts. Know how to translate engineering work into impact on product and revenue.

10 Mistakes To Avoid

Mistakes To Avoid

Staying too technical

Many engineers who move into EM roles default to talking about code and architecture. Interviewers want to hear about people decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes. If your answers focus on what the system does rather than what the team did and why, recalibrate.

Vague answers without specifics

Saying 'I motivate my team' is not an answer. Say how many engineers were on the team, what the specific challenge was, and what changed as a result. Concrete outcomes make answers credible. Use data from your actual work history.

Taking all the credit

Good EMs build teams that succeed. If every answer starts with 'I built' or 'I decided', interviewers will wonder whether you share credit and delegate well. Use 'we' appropriately and acknowledge the engineers who did the work.

Not preparing questions to ask

Candidates who ask no questions at the end of rounds signal low curiosity or low interest. Prepare four or five questions across topics like engineering culture, how success is measured in this role, team challenges, and how the company supports manager development.

Skipping the failure question

Almost every EM interview includes a 'tell me about a mistake you made' question. Candidates who dodge it or give a non-answer like 'I work too hard' lose credibility fast. Prepare a genuine failure story with a clear lesson learned and do not soften it excessively.

Not knowing the company's engineering context

Read their engineering blog, recent press, and current job postings before the interview. Knowing whether they are scaling rapidly, dealing with tech debt, or rebuilding systems gives you context to make your answers relevant to their actual situation.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does an EM interview typically have in India?

Candidates commonly report four to six rounds for EM roles at product companies. This usually includes a hiring manager screen, a behavioural panel, a scenario or case round, a technical depth discussion, and sometimes a skip-level or executive round. Service companies and startups may run fewer rounds. Always ask the recruiter for the exact format so you can prepare accordingly.

Do I need to code in an Engineering Manager interview?

At most Indian product companies, you will not be asked to write code. However, you will be expected to discuss system design at a high level, evaluate technical trade-offs, and show that you can engage credibly with your engineers on technical decisions. Some early-stage startups or companies hiring player-coach EMs may include a light technical screen, so confirm with the recruiter before assuming either way.

What salary can I expect as an Engineering Manager in India?

Based on knok's job radar data, Manager-level EM roles show a band of 35-60 LPA, Senior Manager roles 55-90 LPA, and Director-level positions 90-150+ LPA. These bands vary significantly by company type: product companies and multinational R&D centres tend to pay at the higher end of each range. Always benchmark against publicly reported figures on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before negotiating your offer.

How do I answer behavioural questions if I am transitioning from Tech Lead to EM?

Focus on situations where you already acted like a manager even without the title. Good examples include mentoring a junior engineer, driving alignment across teams, resolving a conflict between teammates, or making a project decision under pressure. Be honest that you are in a transition and frame your stories around the management behaviours you have already demonstrated. Interviewers value self-awareness about the learning curve ahead.

Which cities have the most Engineering Manager jobs right now?

According to knok's job radar from July 2026, Bangalore has the highest number of open EM roles at 182, followed by Delhi at 53, and Pune and Chennai tied at 20 each. Hyderabad has 16 openings and Mumbai has 12. If you are open to relocating, Bangalore gives you the widest set of options across both product companies and MNC engineering centres.

How should I prepare if I only have two weeks before the interview?

Prioritise your story bank first: write STAR summaries for your top eight to ten career moments covering delivery, people, conflict, and failure. Then drill the twelve questions in this guide out loud, ideally with a peer giving you feedback. In the second week, do two full mock interviews, refresh your knowledge of DORA metrics and OKR frameworks, and prepare five strong questions to ask the panel. Two focused weeks is enough to be well-prepared if you stay consistent each day.
