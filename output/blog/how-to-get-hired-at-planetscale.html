01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

PlanetScale builds one of the most respected MySQL-compatible database platforms in the industry, running on Vitess, the same open-source technology used to scale databases at some of the largest tech companies in the world. The company is fully remote, engineering-driven, and known for setting a high bar on technical craft.

As of July 2026, PlanetScale has 12 open roles across engineering, product, and go-to-market functions. The teams are lean, which means each hire matters a great deal. If you are a backend engineer, database specialist, or infrastructure professional, this is a company worth putting serious preparation into.

PlanetScale went through a significant restructuring in 2024 and emerged with a sharper focus on its core database product. That context matters: the company today is looking for people who can operate with a high degree of ownership and autonomy, not people who need a lot of process or management overhead.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at PlanetScale typically runs through several stages. Keep in mind that specific steps can vary by role and team, so treat this as a general guide rather than a guaranteed sequence.

Application and initial screen. Your resume is reviewed by the recruiting team. If there is a match, a recruiter will reach out for a short introductory call to discuss your background, the role, and logistics. This is a two-way conversation, so come prepared with your own questions.

Technical phone screen. A technical conversation with an engineer on the team. For engineering roles, expect questions around your core domain, whether that is databases, infrastructure, backend systems, or the specific area the team works in. This is usually a discussion of how you think and what you have built, not a live coding exercise.

Take-home or coding challenge. Depending on the role, you may receive a take-home project or a focused coding exercise. PlanetScale tends to favour practical, real-world problems over abstract puzzles. Expect something that reflects actual work you would do on the job.

Technical and system design interviews. This is the most demanding part of the process. You will typically go through one or more rounds covering system design, code review, and deeper technical discussion. For database-adjacent roles, knowledge of MySQL internals, replication, and horizontal scaling approaches will very likely come up.

Behavioural and values interview. A conversation with team leads or cross-functional partners about how you work, communicate, and handle ambiguity. Remote-first companies pay particular attention to async communication skills, so have concrete examples ready.

Offer and reference checks. If all rounds go well, you will receive an offer. Reference checks may happen before or after the offer depending on the team.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

PlanetScale is not a typical enterprise company, and it does not hire like one. A few things consistently matter across roles.

Deep technical fundamentals. For engineering roles, surface-level knowledge is not enough. The team expects you to understand the 'why' behind your tools, not just the 'how'. If you are applying to a database or infrastructure role, knowing how MySQL handles transactions, replication lag, and schema changes at scale will set you apart.

Ownership mindset. Because the teams are small and fully remote, PlanetScale needs people who can take a problem from start to finish without constant check-ins. They look for evidence of this in your past work, so prepare specific stories.

Async communication skills. Working remotely across time zones means most collaboration happens in writing. Strong written communication, clear documentation habits, and the ability to unblock yourself are not nice-to-haves here; they are baseline expectations.

Product intuition. Even for purely technical roles, understanding how developers use databases day to day matters. PlanetScale's product is a developer tool, and the team values people who think about the developer experience, not just the underlying infrastructure.

Curiosity and self-direction. The company moves fast. People who proactively explore problems, read broadly, and push the state of the art tend to thrive here.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Getting a job at PlanetScale takes deliberate preparation, especially if you are coming from a more traditional Indian IT background. Here is where to focus your energy.

Build your MySQL and Vitess knowledge. Start with MySQL internals: indexing, query execution plans, replication, and the MVCC model. Then read about Vitess, PlanetScale's open-source foundation. The Vitess documentation and public talks from the engineering team are excellent starting points. This knowledge is what separates serious candidates from the rest.

Practice system design for data-intensive systems. Be able to walk through designing a globally distributed, horizontally sharded database system. Know the trade-offs between consistency and availability, how connection pooling works, and how schema migrations can be done safely without downtime.

Do open-source work that is visible. PlanetScale is an open-source-adjacent company. Contributing to Vitess, MySQL-related projects, or other Go-based infrastructure tools can genuinely get you noticed. Even a thoughtful issue report or a small pull request demonstrates the right instincts.

Sharpen your async writing. Before you apply, practise writing clear, concise technical explanations. A blog, technical newsletter, or any public writing is a signal the team will notice and appreciate.

Tailor your application carefully. With only 12 open roles right now, every application matters. Read the job description carefully, match your resume to the specific requirements, and write a cover note that shows you understand what PlanetScale actually builds, not just what a generic 'database company' does.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

PlanetScale is a remote-first company, which means remote is not an afterthought added on top of an office culture. It is the default. Decisions are made asynchronously, documentation is taken seriously, and no one expects you to be online at a fixed set of hours as long as the work gets done and communication stays clear.

Engineering quality is a core value. The company has a reputation for caring deeply about how software is built, not just whether it ships. Code reviews tend to be thorough, technical debt is taken seriously, and people are expected to push back when something feels wrong.

The team is small and senior. PlanetScale has historically hired experienced engineers rather than building large junior teams. This means your colleagues are likely to be highly capable, and the expectations placed on you will reflect that reality.

Transparency and directness. Feedback tends to be direct. The company shares context openly with employees about the business, product direction, and priorities. If you prefer structured hierarchy and formal processes, this culture may feel uncomfortable at first.

Developer empathy at the core. PlanetScale builds tools for developers, and that shapes the internal culture. There is a genuine interest in making things simple, elegant, and delightful to use. This shows up in how the team thinks about features, documentation, and the support experience.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need to know MySQL specifically, or will other database experience work?

MySQL knowledge is a significant advantage at PlanetScale because the entire platform is built on MySQL-compatible technology. That said, deep expertise in another relational database like PostgreSQL or CockroachDB, combined with a genuine willingness to go deep on MySQL, can still make you a strong candidate. The key is demonstrating that you understand relational database internals at a level beyond just writing queries.

Is PlanetScale open to hiring engineers based in India?

PlanetScale is a fully remote company and has hired internationally in the past. However, availability for specific countries or regions can change over time, so check individual job postings carefully for any location requirements or time zone preferences. Some roles may list a preferred working time zone, which can affect candidates in India depending on the team.

What kind of salary can I expect if I get an offer from PlanetScale?

PlanetScale is a US-based company and compensates at rates that are publicly reported to be competitive with other well-funded tech companies, based on Glassdoor and levels.fyi community data. For remote roles hired internationally, compensation may be location-adjusted or US-rate depending on how the role is structured. Check levels.fyi for community-reported figures specific to this company before entering negotiations.

How long does the hiring process usually take from application to offer?

The full hiring cycle at companies of this type commonly takes several weeks from first contact to offer, though it varies depending on scheduling and how quickly both sides move. PlanetScale's lean team size can sometimes mean decisions arrive faster than at large enterprises. Follow up politely if you do not hear back after a week at any stage, since remote teams can have delays that are not a signal of rejection.

Does PlanetScale sponsor visas for candidates who want to relocate?

PlanetScale is remote-first, so most roles are not tied to a physical office location and visa sponsorship for relocation is not typically part of the package. If you are specifically interested in eventually relocating to the US, ask the recruiter directly during the initial call rather than assuming. Individual role postings may have specific notes on this.

I come from a service company background in India. Can I still get hired at PlanetScale?

It is possible, but you will need to close some gaps first. PlanetScale looks for people who have worked on product companies, open-source projects, or built things with a high degree of personal ownership. If your experience is mainly in client delivery or maintaining large legacy systems, focus your application on any side projects, open-source contributions, or internal tools you built from scratch. Make those the centrepiece of your application, not your project management or delivery history.
