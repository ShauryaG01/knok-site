01 Overview

Overview

Okta is a US-listed identity and access management company. Its products help enterprises control who can log in, what they can access, and how identities are verified across thousands of apps. As a Product Designer, you will own UX for admin consoles, dashboards, and login flows used by IT teams and end users in regulated industries like finance, healthcare, and government.

Candidates report the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a portfolio presentation, a design exercise (take-home or live), and cross-functional interviews with engineers and product managers. The emphasis is on B2B enterprise thinking, not consumer polish.

Okta currently has 388 open roles across teams, so design positions come up regularly. Based on knok job data, Product Designer salaries in India range from 6-12 LPA at entry level up to 36-55+ LPA at lead or principal level.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are questions candidates report encountering most often across Okta design interviews, covering portfolio, process, and behavioral areas.

  1. Walk me through a B2B or enterprise product you designed. What was the core UX challenge?
  2. Our users include IT admins, security engineers, and end users. How do you design for multiple user types on one product?
  3. Tell me about a time you simplified a complex or technical workflow without losing accuracy or security.
  4. How do you approach designing for accessibility in dashboard-heavy products?
  5. Describe a project where engineering pushed back on your design. How did you handle it?
  6. How do you validate design decisions when you have limited time or access to users?
  7. Walk me through how you use or have contributed to a design system.
  8. Tell me about a design that did not succeed. What happened, and what did you learn?
  9. How would you approach designing a feature that increases user trust in a security product?
  10. Describe a time you had conflicting feedback from two stakeholders. How did you reach a decision?
  11. How do you balance business goals with user needs when they are in direct conflict?
  12. Okta's brand centers on trust and security. How does that influence your visual and interaction design choices?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you simplified a complex workflow without losing accuracy.

*Situation:* I was designing the onboarding flow for a SaaS platform where new IT admins had to configure single sign-on settings for their organization. The existing flow was spread across many screens, requiring admins to jump between tabs to gather information.

*Task:* My goal was to reduce setup time while keeping all required fields and avoiding errors that could lock users out.

*Action:* I ran a task analysis with five admins to map where they got stuck. I then collapsed redundant steps, added inline help text with real examples, and introduced a progress indicator so users always knew how many steps remained. I worked with engineering to identify what data could be pre-filled from the existing account.

*Result:* Usability testing showed fewer errors and faster completion in the new flow. After it shipped, the support team reported fewer onboarding tickets in the weeks that followed.

---

Q: Describe a project where you had conflicting stakeholder feedback.

*Situation:* On a B2B analytics dashboard, the product manager wanted to add five new charts to the overview screen. The engineering lead wanted to reduce the number of components to maintain. Both came to me expecting a design that backed their position.

*Task:* I needed to find a direction that served the actual user need without turning the review into a standoff between two senior colleagues.

*Action:* I proposed a short research sprint. I pulled session recordings and support tickets related to the overview screen and presented findings in the next sync. The data showed users were looking for two specific metrics quickly on arrival, not a broad overview. I designed a customizable widget layout that let users control what they saw, satisfying both the 'more data' ask and the 'fewer components' constraint.

*Result:* Both stakeholders aligned on the widget approach. It shipped in the next release and the product team cited it as a positive signal in their quarterly user satisfaction review.

---

Q: Tell me about a design that did not succeed.

*Situation:* I redesigned the notification center for a SaaS tool. I was confident the new information hierarchy was cleaner, so I moved to development without a second round of usability testing.

*Task:* I owned the full design and was responsible for the quality of the shipped experience.

*Action:* After launch, I monitored analytics and noticed users were clicking the wrong action buttons more often than before. I ran an emergency usability session, identified that my label changes had introduced ambiguity, and worked with engineering to ship a patch within two weeks.

*Result:* The patch corrected the issue. More importantly, I now always run at least one round of testing on any copy or label change, even when the visual design feels straightforward.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Portfolio walkthroughs. Use the Problem, Process, Decision, Outcome structure. Start with the user problem and business context, walk through your process briefly, then spend the most time on the decisions you made and why. Close with what shipped and what you learned. Okta interviewers typically want to hear your reasoning, not just the final screens.

Behavioral questions. Use STAR: Situation (brief), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did), Result (concrete outcome). Keep Situation and Task short. Spend the majority of your answer on Action and Result.

Design critiques. When asked to critique a product, including Okta's own, use 'I like / I wish / what if.' This keeps feedback constructive and shows you can give peer critique professionally without putting people on the defensive.

Prioritization questions. When asked how you prioritize features or design debt, reference an impact versus effort framing. Okta works at scale, so showing you can articulate tradeoffs between user value and implementation cost is valued by interviewers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Security and trust thinking. Okta's products live in high-stakes environments. Interviewers want to see that you think about error states, edge cases, and failure scenarios, not just the happy path. If your case studies only show polished success screens, prepare to speak to what happens when something goes wrong.

Enterprise UX fluency. Consumer app experience alone is not enough. Show that you understand admin personas, complex permission models, and density-heavy interfaces where expert users repeat the same tasks many times a day.

Clear communication across functions. Product Designers at Okta work closely with engineers and product managers who have strong technical opinions. Candidates who explain design decisions in plain terms and hold their ground with evidence tend to move forward in the process.

Data-informed decisions. You do not need a number for every design choice, but interviewers want to see that you use research, analytics, and user feedback to drive direction rather than relying on intuition alone.

Collaboration without ego. Okta is a large organization with many cross-functional dependencies. Candidates report that interviewers pay close attention to how you talk about team contributions, how you receive critique, and whether you credit collaborators alongside your own work.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product. Use Okta's free developer account to explore the admin console, the end-user dashboard, and the login widget. Take notes on what works well and where you see UX friction. This gives you real, specific examples to reference in the interview rather than generic statements about enterprise design.

Week 2: Polish your portfolio. Select 2-3 case studies that show enterprise or complex B2B work. For each, write out your STAR narrative before the interview so you are not improvising on the day. If your portfolio skews consumer, prepare to explain how your skills transfer to admin-facing, multi-user products.

Week 3: Practice out loud. Work through the 12 questions in this guide with a friend or record yourself. Pay attention to answer length and structure. Candidates report that Okta interviewers appreciate concise, structured answers over long storytelling sessions.

Week 4: Prepare your questions. Have 3-4 thoughtful questions ready for each round. Ask about the design team's relationship with engineering, how design decisions get prioritized, and what a successful first few months looks like for a new designer joining the team.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Showing only consumer or visual design work. Okta hires designers for enterprise products. If your portfolio has no B2B, SaaS, or admin-facing work, contextualize your consumer projects in terms of complexity, stakeholders, and constraints rather than aesthetics alone.

Not using the product before the interview. Candidates who have never tried Okta's admin console struggle to connect their answers to specific design challenges at Okta. Spend at least an hour in the product before any screen.

Skipping the 'why' in portfolio walkthroughs. Describing what you built is not enough. Interviewers want to know why you made specific decisions, what alternatives you considered and rejected, and what you would do differently now.

Claiming sole credit for team work. Okta interviewers often probe team dynamics. Saying 'I designed the entire product' for a large project can raise flags. Be specific about what you personally owned versus what the broader team contributed.

Ignoring edge cases and error states. In a security product, what happens when something fails matters as much as the happy path. If your case studies only show the ideal scenario, prepare to speak to how you handled errors, edge cases, and accessibility across the experience.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Okta Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically spans several rounds: a recruiter call, a hiring manager conversation, a portfolio presentation, a design exercise (take-home or live), and a cross-functional panel with engineers or product managers. The exact structure varies by team and level, so ask your recruiter at the start what to expect.

What kind of design exercise does Okta give?

Candidates report seeing both take-home prompts and live whiteboard-style exercises. Prompts typically involve redesigning an existing product flow or solving a hypothetical enterprise UX problem. Interviewers generally care more about your thinking process and the decisions you make than about a polished visual output.

What salary can I expect as a Product Designer at Okta in India?

Based on knok job data, Product Designer salaries in India broadly fall in the ranges of 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 14-24 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 26-40 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 36-55+ LPA at lead or principal level. Specific Okta figures are not publicly reported in India, so treat these as market reference points for the role.

Do I need enterprise design experience to apply at Okta?

Enterprise experience is a strong advantage, especially for mid and senior roles, but it is not always a strict requirement for junior positions. What matters most is showing you can think about complex user types, admin workflows, and edge cases. If your background is consumer-focused, prepare examples that highlight analytical thinking, stakeholder collaboration, and systems thinking.

How important is Figma proficiency for this role?

Candidates report that Figma is the primary design tool at Okta. Proficiency with components, auto-layout, and prototyping is expected. Familiarity with design system principles is also valued because Okta works at scale across many product surfaces, and designers are expected to contribute to and consume shared component libraries.
