01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Internet Brands is a US-based digital media and software company running a broad portfolio of vertical online networks. Their properties span health, legal, automotive, travel, and home categories, making them one of the larger players in category-specific digital content and SaaS products.

As of July 2026, Internet Brands has 56 open roles listed across their business units. Hiring happens across product management, software engineering, marketing, editorial, data, and customer success. Because the company operates many distinct brands under one umbrella, the hiring experience and team culture can vary quite a bit depending on which division you join.

For Indian professionals considering these roles, most positions are US-based. A portion of engineering and technical roles are listed as remote, and some of those may be open to international applicants. Always check the location requirements on each listing, and ask the recruiter early if there is any ambiguity. The company posts openings on their own careers page and on major job platforms.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at Internet Brands typically runs through a few predictable stages. The exact number of rounds and format can shift depending on the role and seniority level, but here is what candidates commonly report.

Application and initial screening: Submit your resume through the Internet Brands careers page or a job board. A recruiter reviews applications and typically reaches out within a couple of weeks. Some teams use applicant tracking software, so aligning your resume keywords with the job description helps you clear that first filter.

Recruiter call: A short conversation with a member of the talent team. They will ask about your background, current situation, and why you are interested in the role. This is also a good moment to ask about team structure, day-to-day responsibilities, and hiring timeline. Come with a clear answer on your expectations and availability.

Skills assessment or take-home task: Many candidates report a practical exercise after the recruiter call. Engineers typically receive a coding challenge; product or marketing roles may get a case study or writing exercise. You usually have a few days to complete this, so take the time to do it carefully rather than rushing.

Hiring manager interview: A detailed conversation with the person you would report to. This round goes deep on your past experience, how you solve problems in your domain, and specific scenarios from your work history. Come with concrete examples that show ownership and results, not just participation.

Panel or cross-functional round: For senior or broad-scope roles, expect a final round with multiple people, sometimes peers, a skip-level manager, or stakeholders from other business units. This round assesses how well you collaborate and whether your working style fits the team.

Offer: If all rounds go well, the recruiter contacts you with an offer. Salary benchmarks for Internet Brands roles are publicly reported on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, so review those before your negotiation conversation.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Internet Brands business units operate with a fair amount of independence. The qualities that come up consistently across teams reflect that structure.

Ownership and follow-through: Interviewers frequently probe for examples where you drove something to completion without waiting for step-by-step direction. They want to see that you can identify a problem, form a plan, and see it through without heavy oversight.

Domain familiarity: Their verticals (health, legal, automotive, travel) are specialised. Candidates who already understand the audience, the regulatory context, or the competitive landscape for that vertical stand out. If you are applying to a health-focused role, knowing how patients and providers search for information online is genuinely valuable.

Comfort with data: Across most roles, from marketing to product to operations, you are expected to use data to inform decisions. You do not need to be a data engineer, but you should be ready to talk about how you have used metrics or analytics to make a call or change direction.

Cross-team collaboration: Internet Brands brands share infrastructure and often cross-sell. Being able to work across divisions and bring in perspectives from other teams is something panel interviewers specifically look for.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Spend time researching the specific business unit or brand you are applying to, not just Internet Brands as a whole. If your role sits within their legal division, understand how that product serves law firms and consumers. If it is in health, look at their patient engagement and provider tools. Showing that you understand the end user for that specific vertical makes a strong impression.

For behavioral rounds, use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and prepare at least three or four stories from your past work. Focus on examples that show ownership, problem-solving under pressure, and working across teams. These themes come up across most Internet Brands interview panels.

For engineering roles, the take-home or online assessment tends to be practical rather than purely algorithmic. Focus on writing clean, working code in the language the team uses. Check the job description for stack hints and practise in that environment before the assessment arrives.

Before any interview, scan recent news or product updates related to the brand you are joining. Mentioning something current, such as a new feature, a recent launch, or a market shift in their vertical, signals genuine interest rather than a generic application.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Internet Brands runs a decentralised structure. Each business unit operates with significant autonomy, which means the culture in one division can feel noticeably different from another. Some teams are fast-moving with minimal process; others are more structured. Knowing which environment suits you best is useful context before you accept an offer.

The company is generally described by employees on review platforms as results-oriented. Output matters more than visibility, which suits people who want to be judged by their work rather than by how often they are seen in meetings.

Work-life balance is commonly cited as reasonable in most business units, though product and engineering teams near a launch cycle may see heavier periods. Remote and hybrid arrangements exist in several divisions, but the specifics depend on the role and the brand.

If you value breadth of exposure, Internet Brands offers an interesting vantage point. Within one employer, you get to see how digital products are built and grown across health, legal, automotive, and several other industries. For someone early in their career or transitioning into a new vertical, that variety has real learning value.
