01 Overview

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate reports and the technical demands of mediasmart's adtech platform. Expect them to appear across multiple rounds.

  1. Walk us through how you would design a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices-based ad-serving platform.
  2. How do you manage Kubernetes clusters in production? Which features, such as HPA, pod disruption budgets, or node affinity, have you used?
  3. Our platform handles real-time bid requests with strict response-time limits. How would you architect infrastructure to absorb sudden traffic spikes during a campaign burst?
  4. Describe your hands-on experience with infrastructure-as-code tools like Terraform or Pulumi. Can you walk through a module you wrote?
  5. How do you set up monitoring and alerting for a high-availability service? Which tools have you used and why did you choose them?
  6. Tell me about a production incident you owned from detection to resolution and post-mortem.
  7. How do you manage secrets, API keys, and credentials securely across staging and production environments?
  8. Which cloud provider have you worked with most, and which services did you rely on for compute, networking, and storage?
  9. How would you reduce cloud infrastructure costs without affecting reliability or latency?
  10. What is your approach to disaster recovery planning, including RTO and RPO targets?
  11. How do you embed security checks into a DevOps workflow, for example at the pipeline or container image level?
  12. Describe a change you made that improved deployment frequency or reduced the rate of failed rollouts.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a production incident you owned from detection to resolution.

*Situation:* At a previous role, our API layer began returning errors for a subset of users shortly after a routine deployment during peak traffic hours.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer responsible for triaging the issue, communicating status to stakeholders, and restoring service.

*Action:* I pulled up our Grafana dashboards first and spotted elevated error rates on two specific pods while the rest of the cluster looked healthy. I rolled back the deployment immediately to isolate whether the new code was the cause, which stopped new errors from appearing. I then diffed pod logs between the rolled-back and previous versions and found that a new environment variable was absent from the Kubernetes config map. I patched the config map, re-deployed to staging to confirm the fix, and then pushed to production. I posted status updates in the incident channel throughout.

*Result:* Service was fully restored within 45 minutes of the first alert. I authored a post-mortem that identified the missing config validation step in our deployment checklist, and we added an automated check to the CI pipeline so the same class of error would be caught before reaching production.

---

Q: How do you manage Kubernetes clusters in production?

*Situation:* At a mid-sized SaaS company, I was responsible for a multi-tenant EKS cluster shared by several product teams with very different workload profiles.

*Task:* My goal was to keep the cluster stable, prevent noisy-neighbour resource contention, and reduce unplanned outages during node upgrades.

*Action:* I introduced namespace-level resource quotas and limit ranges so no single team could starve the others. I configured Horizontal Pod Autoscalers for services with variable traffic and set up Pod Disruption Budgets on critical workloads so rolling upgrades could not take down too many replicas at once. I also moved the node upgrade process to a blue-green node group pattern, draining the old group only after the new one was healthy.

*Result:* Unplanned downtime caused by resource contention dropped significantly over the following two quarters. Teams reported fewer throttling incidents and faster deployments, and node upgrades became a routine task rather than a risk event.

---

Q: How would you reduce cloud infrastructure costs without affecting reliability?

*Situation:* Our AWS bill had grown sharply over 12 months as the team scaled, but there was no formal cost review process in place.

*Task:* I was asked to audit the infrastructure and recommend cost optimizations that carried minimal production risk.

*Action:* I used AWS Cost Explorer to identify the highest-spend areas, which turned out to be oversized EC2 instances and a large number of unattached EBS volumes left over from terminated instances. I right-sized the instances using actual CPU and memory metrics from CloudWatch over a 30-day window, converted steady-state workloads to Reserved Instances after confirming their usage patterns, and wrote a Lambda function to flag and clean up orphaned volumes on a weekly schedule.

*Result:* Monthly cloud spend dropped to a level the finance team acknowledged in the next quarterly review. The audit process was documented and handed to the broader engineering team as a recurring monthly task.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioral question. Situation (1-2 sentences of context), Task (your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, use 'I' not 'we'), Result (observable or measurable outcome). Aim for 2-3 minutes per answer when speaking.

For system design questions, open with requirements: what is the expected scale, what are the latency constraints, what does failure look like? Sketch the components, explain trade-offs, and end with how you would monitor and alert on the system. In an adtech context, interviewers expect you to bring up throughput and latency without being prompted.

For troubleshooting questions, show a methodical process: observe symptoms, form a hypothesis, isolate variables, confirm the fix, then describe what you changed to prevent recurrence. Interviewers want to see calm, structured thinking, not heroics.

For 'why mediasmart' questions, connect your answer to the technical demands of their specific domain. Programmatic advertising infrastructure is genuinely challenging. Showing that you understand why low-latency RTB systems are hard to operate signals preparation rather than generic interest.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hands-on cloud and container experience. mediasmart runs cloud-native infrastructure. Interviewers distinguish between candidates who have managed Kubernetes and cloud services in production and those who know the concepts only from documentation.

Comfort with high-throughput, latency-sensitive systems. Ad platforms process bid requests at scale with strict deadlines. Candidates who have operated similar infrastructure in fintech, ecommerce, or media bring directly transferable context.

Ownership mindset in incidents. They look for engineers who treat on-call as a first-class responsibility, close the loop with post-mortems, and implement process changes so the same incident does not repeat.

Infrastructure-as-code discipline. Candidates who manage infrastructure through code using Terraform, Helm, or similar tools are preferred over those who rely on manual console changes. Come ready with a concrete example from your own work.

Security awareness. With advertising data flowing through the platform, candidates who understand secrets management, container image scanning, and network policies stand out.

Clear communication. Candidates report that interviewers value engineers who can explain a complex system to a non-DevOps colleague. This is treated as a signal of technical depth, not just soft skill.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core technical review
Revisit Kubernetes workloads, networking, storage, and autoscaling. Practice writing Terraform modules for common cloud resources from memory. If you do not have a recent CI/CD example to discuss, build a small personal project with a pipeline using GitHub Actions or GitLab CI.

Week 2: mediasmart-specific research
Read publicly available material on programmatic advertising and real-time bidding architecture to understand the domain context. Think through how DevOps concerns like deployment safety, latency, and cost apply to an adtech platform specifically. Prepare at least 3 STAR stories covering: a production incident you owned, a cost or efficiency improvement, and a system or process you redesigned.

Week 3: Mock interviews and polish
Practice answering the 12 questions listed above out loud. Time your responses. Ask a peer to give feedback on clarity and whether your answers feel concrete or vague. Prepare 3-4 questions for the interviewer focused on on-call culture, current infrastructure challenges, and how the team handles deployments today.

On the day
Have a terminal ready if a hands-on exercise is expected. Bring mental notes on your three strongest STAR stories. Review mediasmart's recent blog posts or LinkedIn updates for any mentions of their stack or team priorities.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Saying 'we' instead of 'I'. Interviewers cannot assess your individual contribution if every answer describes what the team did. Be specific about the decisions and actions you personally owned.

Skipping the result. Many candidates give strong Situation and Action sections but trail off without stating an outcome. Even a qualitative result such as 'the team had fewer rollbacks' or 'management approved the proposal' is far better than leaving the story unfinished.

Listing tools instead of solving problems. Naming Kubernetes, Terraform, and Prometheus in sequence is not the same as explaining why you chose them and what problem they addressed. Lead with the problem, then name the tool.

Not knowing adtech basics. Candidates who cannot explain what a bid request is or why response latency matters in RTB infrastructure look underprepared for a company like mediasmart. A short read on programmatic advertising before the interview is enough to close that gap.

Using vague outcomes. Answers like 'it improved things' carry less weight than 'deployment time dropped from 40 minutes to 8 minutes.' Use real figures from your own work wherever you have them.

Asking no questions. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare thoughtful questions about the team's current pain points, on-call load, and infrastructure roadmap.

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the mediasmart DevOps interview typically have?

Candidates typically report 3-4 rounds. This usually includes an initial HR or recruiter screen, a technical round focused on Kubernetes and cloud infrastructure, a system design or hands-on exercise, and a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager. Round structure can vary, so confirm the exact process with your recruiter after the first call.

What salary can I expect for a DevOps Engineer role at mediasmart?

Based on knok jobradar data for the broader DevOps market in India, mid-level engineers with 3-5 years of experience typically see offers in the 15-28 LPA range, while senior engineers with 6-9 years see 30-50 LPA. Specific mediasmart compensation will depend on your level, experience, and negotiation. Check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for company-specific data points and recent offer reports.

Does mediasmart test Kubernetes hands-on in the interview?

Candidates commonly report that Kubernetes is tested in depth, and some describe a practical exercise. Be ready to write or review a Deployment or Service manifest, explain how HPA and pod scheduling work, and walk through troubleshooting steps for a pod that fails to start. Practice in a local cluster using kind or minikube if you have not worked hands-on with Kubernetes recently.

Is domain knowledge of advertising technology required?

Deep adtech expertise is not required, but understanding the basics of real-time bidding helps you frame your answers in context. Interviewers at platforms like mediasmart appreciate candidates who connect DevOps principles like low latency and high availability to the business reality of processing bid requests within strict time limits. A 30-minute read on RTB architecture before the interview is a worthwhile investment.

How should I prepare for a system design round at mediasmart?

Focus on high-throughput, low-latency system design patterns. Practice designing components like a rate limiter, a message queue for event processing, or a deployment pipeline for a microservices application. Start every design by clarifying requirements and scale constraints, then walk through your architecture, the trade-offs you made, and how you would monitor it in production. mediasmart's adtech context means latency and horizontal scalability should be central to most designs you present.
