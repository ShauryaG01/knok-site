01 Overview

Overview

Clickpost is a logistics intelligence platform that helps e-commerce brands track shipments, manage non-delivery reports, and connect with carrier networks across India and globally. The company currently has 13 open roles, and Product Manager positions are among the most active. As a Clickpost PM, you sit at the crossroads of carrier data, merchant dashboards, and SaaS growth, so interviewers test whether you can simplify complex logistics workflows into products that real operations teams actually use.

The interview process typically involves a short recruiter or hiring manager screen, a product case study (take-home or live), and one or two structured rounds with senior PMs or founders. Candidates report that the case study carries the most weight, so be ready to frame problems using metrics, user segments, and trade-offs specific to the logistics and post-purchase space. Knok jobradar currently tracks 2,009 active Product Manager openings across India, with Bangalore (271), Delhi (177), and Mumbai (56) leading in volume.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates report most often at Clickpost PM interviews, based on the logistics SaaS domain and the company's product focus:

  1. Walk me through a product you owned from discovery to launch.
  2. How would you improve Clickpost's shipment tracking dashboard for a D2C brand doing high volumes?
  3. A carrier integration is failing for a significant share of orders. How do you decide between fixing that and shipping a planned feature?
  4. What metrics would you track for a post-purchase experience product?
  5. Tell me about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder request.
  6. How would you design an NDR (non-delivery report) management tool for mid-market sellers?
  7. A competitor launches real-time tracking. How do you respond as a PM?
  8. How do you handle ambiguous requirements when working with an engineering team?
  9. How would you think about pricing a new analytics module for enterprise logistics clients?
  10. Tell me about a product decision that led to an outcome you did not expect.
  11. How do you balance the needs of small D2C brands and large enterprise accounts on a shared platform?
  12. What does good customer onboarding look like for a logistics SaaS product?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder request.

*Situation:* I was PM for a fulfilment dashboard at a B2B SaaS company. The sales head wanted us to build a custom CSV export feature for a single enterprise client before our quarterly release.

*Task:* My job was to evaluate whether this was the right use of the team's sprint capacity, given we had a core tracking feature already mid-build.

*Action:* I pulled usage data and found that three other mid-market clients had requested a similar but more flexible bulk-export API. I presented both options to the sales head with estimated effort and revenue impact, and proposed building the API instead, which would serve the enterprise client and open a broader use case.

*Result:* The stakeholder agreed. We shipped the API in the same sprint window, the enterprise client adopted it, and two additional clients upgraded their plans within the following quarter.

---

Q: How would you design an NDR management tool for mid-market sellers?

*Situation:* At a previous role, return-to-origin rates were high across mid-market accounts, and sellers had no visibility into why deliveries were failing.

*Task:* I was asked to scope a solution that would reduce failed delivery rates without requiring sellers to monitor carrier portals manually.

*Action:* I ran discovery interviews with five operations managers at mid-market brands to map their current workflow. I found that most NDR actions were delayed because alerts came only via email and no one owned the resolution queue. I proposed a centralised NDR triage view with auto-escalation rules, WhatsApp alerts for high-value orders, and a retry scheduling feature. I prioritised the alert and triage modules first, based on the highest pain scores from the interviews.

*Result:* In a pilot with three brands, re-attempt success rates improved meaningfully, and the tool became a key selling point for the ops team in new client pitches.

---

Q: Tell me about a product decision that led to an unexpected outcome.

*Situation:* I was building a carrier recommendation engine that suggested the lowest-cost carrier for each order based on weight, pincode, and delivery speed.

*Task:* The goal was to reduce shipping costs for clients and position it as an ROI feature for upsell.

*Action:* We launched the engine and set the default to prioritise cost over speed. I had assumed most clients cared primarily about margins.

*Result:* Several mid-market clients saw an uptick in delivery complaints because the lowest-cost carriers had lower on-time rates for certain pincodes. I had not weighted serviceability data heavily enough. We quickly added a configurable priority slider and published clear trade-off data in the UI. The experience taught me to validate assumptions about user priorities with real data before setting any defaults.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Structure every story as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not the team), and close with a concrete Result. If you lack a precise number, describe a directional outcome ('client retention improved', 'the team shipped ahead of schedule').

CIRCLES for product design questions. Comprehend the problem, Identify the user, Report the user's needs, Cut through prioritisation, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarise. At Clickpost, always anchor on the user segment (D2C brand, enterprise logistics head, carrier ops team) before jumping to solutions.

Metrics-first for analytical questions. When asked about a feature or product area, state the North Star metric first, then supporting metrics, then guardrail metrics. For a post-purchase product the North Star might be delivery success rate; supporting metrics might include NDR resolution time and re-attempt rate; guardrail metrics might include carrier API uptime and merchant portal load time.

Trade-off framing for prioritisation questions. Use a simple two-axis view: user impact versus engineering effort. Narrate your reasoning out loud. Interviewers at product-led companies care more about your thinking process than the final answer you land on.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Clickpost interviewers are typically looking for PMs who understand operational complexity without getting lost in it. Based on the company's domain, a few qualities stand out:

Domain curiosity. You do not need a logistics background, but you should know the basics: what NDR means, how carrier SLAs work, and why pincode serviceability matters to a D2C brand. Candidates who have done homework on these basics consistently report a stronger interview experience.

Data-driven decisions. Expect to be asked how you would measure success for almost any feature you describe. Have a clear answer ready, including what you would track, what 'good' looks like, and how you would know if something is going wrong.

Clear communication with engineering. Clickpost is a product-engineering company, so interviewers probe whether you can write clear specs, handle technical ambiguity, and make trade-off calls without micromanaging developers.

User empathy grounded in business reality. The best answers show that you understand what the merchant or operations team is actually trying to accomplish, while also connecting that to revenue, retention, or cost for the business. Pure UX answers without business context tend to score lower.

Ownership mindset. Candidates who say 'we did X' without explaining their personal contribution tend to score lower than those who can articulate exactly what they decided, why they decided it, and what they would do differently now.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: understand the product and domain.
Sign up for a Clickpost free trial or watch their product demo videos to get familiar with the dashboard, tracking pages, and integrations. Read about NDR management and why it matters for e-commerce brands. Look at LinkedIn for recent interview experiences to understand the current process structure.

Week two: build your story bank.
List six to eight situations from your work history that map to common PM themes: prioritisation under pressure, stakeholder conflict, a metric-driven decision, a launch that did not go as planned, and a time you advocated for the user. Write the STAR version of each story and practise telling them concisely.

Week three: practise case studies.
Practise designing or improving at least two logistics SaaS features out loud. Typical prompts at Clickpost-style companies include improving a tracking page, designing an analytics dashboard, or building a carrier selection engine. Get a friend or mentor to give feedback on your structure and how quickly you anchor on user needs versus jumping to solutions.

Before the interview.
Review Clickpost's blog and any publicly reported product updates. Prepare two or three thoughtful questions for your interviewer about the team's current priorities and how PM success is measured. Candidates report that asking specific product questions leaves a much stronger impression than generic 'tell me about the culture' questions.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping user segmentation. A common mistake is jumping to a solution without naming who you are building for. At a B2B logistics company, 'the user' could be a warehouse manager, a D2C brand founder, or an enterprise supply chain head. Each has very different needs.

Vague metrics. Saying 'we improved the user experience' without a number or at least a directional metric is a red flag. Even if you do not have exact data, say what you would have measured and why.

Ignoring trade-offs. Interviewers are not looking for the perfect answer. They want to see that you recognise constraints: engineering bandwidth, carrier API limitations, client contract terms. Candidates who present only the upside of their ideas tend to score lower than those who acknowledge what they gave up.

Talking about 'we' too much. In behavioural questions, interviewers are evaluating your judgment, not your team's. Be specific about what you personally decided or did.

Not asking clarifying questions. In case study rounds, jumping straight into an answer without asking about the user segment, the business goal, or current constraints signals poor PM instincts. Take a moment to ask two or three clarifying questions before you begin.

Under-preparing on logistics basics. You do not need to know carrier rate cards, but not knowing what an NDR is, or why last-mile delivery is expensive, signals that you have not done basic research on the domain.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Clickpost PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a product case study (sometimes take-home, sometimes live), and one or two rounds with senior PMs or a founder. The total number of rounds can vary by seniority level. It is worth confirming the exact structure with your recruiter after the first screen.

Do I need a logistics background to interview for a PM role at Clickpost?

A logistics background is not required, but basic domain familiarity helps significantly. You should know what NDR means, how carrier integrations work at a high level, and why post-purchase experience matters to e-commerce brands. Candidates with SaaS or B2B product experience who have done domain research typically perform well.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Clickpost?

Based on knok jobradar data for Product Manager roles across India, Associate PM roles typically range from 12-20 LPA, PM roles with 3-6 years of experience range from 24-40 LPA, and Senior PM roles range from 40-60 LPA. Actual offers depend on your experience, the specific role level, and negotiation. Glassdoor and levels.fyi may have company-specific data points worth checking.

How important is the product case study in the Clickpost interview process?

Candidates consistently report that the case study carries the most weight in the Clickpost PM process. Interviewers look at how you structure the problem, how quickly you identify the right user, and how you prioritise solutions. Practising out loud and getting feedback on your structure before the interview makes a meaningful difference.

What types of products does a Clickpost PM typically work on?

Clickpost PMs typically work on shipment tracking interfaces, carrier integration management, NDR and returns workflows, and analytics dashboards for logistics operations. The products serve both small D2C brands and large enterprise clients, so PMs need to balance breadth with depth and handle varied stakeholder needs within a single platform.
