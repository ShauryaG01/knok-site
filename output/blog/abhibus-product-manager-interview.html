01 Overview

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions are drawn from candidate-reported experiences and the nature of AbhiBus's product. Expect variations, but these themes come up repeatedly.

  1. Walk me through how you would improve the AbhiBus seat-selection and booking flow to reduce abandonment.
  2. AbhiBus serves two sides of a marketplace: travelers and bus operators. How do you decide whose needs to prioritize when they conflict?
  3. A key booking metric dropped noticeably over the last week. How do you diagnose and respond?
  4. How would you design or improve the cancellation and refund experience to build long-term user trust?
  5. AbhiBus wants to grow in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities where internet connectivity is patchy. What product changes would you propose?
  6. How would you measure whether a new feature, say a 'live bus tracking' screen, is actually successful?
  7. Competitor platforms offer features AbhiBus does not. Walk me through how you would decide whether to build, buy, or ignore those features.
  8. How would you design a loyalty or rewards program that keeps frequent travelers coming back to AbhiBus over alternatives?
  9. What does a healthy operator onboarding funnel look like, and which metrics would you track?
  10. You have limited engineering bandwidth for the next quarter. How do you prioritize your PM roadmap?
  11. How would you approach dynamic pricing display so travelers feel informed rather than surprised by fare changes?
  12. Tell me about a product you admire in the travel or consumer internet space and what AbhiBus could learn from it.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. These are illustrative answers you can adapt with your own real experience.

Q: Tell me about a time you improved a key product metric.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we noticed that a large share of users who reached the payment page did not complete their booking.

*Task:* My task was to identify the root cause and own the solution end-to-end within one quarter.

*Action:* I ran a funnel analysis and found that users on slower connections were timing out at the payment gateway step. I worked with the engineering team to add a lightweight payment retry flow and a clearer loading state so users knew the transaction was still processing. I also ran a small A/B test on the payment page copy to reduce anxiety around failed attempts.

*Result:* Payment completion improved over the following weeks. The retry flow recovered a portion of sessions that previously ended in silent failure. I presented the findings to the leadership team and the approach was rolled out to additional user segments.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you had to choose between two competing feature requests from different stakeholders.

*Situation:* Our sales team wanted a bulk-discount feature for corporate clients, while our growth team was pushing for a referral program to acquire new individual users.

*Task:* Both were on my roadmap and I had bandwidth for only one in the next sprint cycle.

*Action:* I built a simple scoring model using estimated reach, revenue impact, and engineering cost. The corporate discount feature had a clearer and faster revenue path, supported by data from a handful of existing enterprise accounts. I presented the scoring to both teams with full transparency so neither felt dismissed.

*Result:* We shipped the corporate discount feature first, which helped close new accounts within the quarter. The referral program was deferred by one cycle with a committed date, keeping the growth team aligned.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you used data to make a difficult product decision.

*Situation:* Our app had a 'choose your seat' step that user interviews suggested felt slow and confusing, but the design team believed it was a key differentiator.

*Task:* I needed to settle the debate with evidence rather than opinion.

*Action:* I set up session recordings and funnel analytics specifically for the seat-selection step. The data showed a high exit rate at that screen on lower-end Android devices. I ran a prototype test with a simplified single-tap 'best available seat' option for users on slower devices.

*Result:* The simplified flow reduced exits from that screen for the targeted device segment. We kept the full seat map for users who preferred it, turning it into a preference-based experience rather than a one-size-fits-all flow.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product improvement questions (such as 'improve the booking flow'), use a structured approach: start by clarifying the goal (is this about conversion, satisfaction, or retention?), identify the key user segments, map the current journey to find friction points, propose solutions in order of impact and feasibility, and define the metric you would track to call it a success.

For metric-drop questions (such as 'bookings dropped this week'), use a structured diagnosis: first check if the data itself is reliable, then segment by platform, city, route, and user type to isolate the drop. Rule out external factors like a public holiday or a competitor promotion before going to internal causes like a recent release or payment gateway issue.

For prioritization questions, a simple scoring table works well in interviews. List the options, score each on user impact, business value, and effort, and show your reasoning. AbhiBus interviewers typically want to see that you can defend your choices with logic, not just gut feel.

For marketplace balance questions, acknowledge both sides explicitly. Travelers want low prices, reliable information, and easy cancellations. Operators want steady demand, fair visibility, and low operational overhead. A good answer shows you can find solutions that move both sides forward, or honestly explains the trade-off when you cannot.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that AbhiBus interviewers look for a few specific qualities beyond general PM skills.

Domain curiosity. You do not need bus industry experience, but you should be able to talk intelligently about how online travel platforms work, what makes travelers switch apps, and what bus operators care about. Showing that you researched AbhiBus before the interview goes a long way.

Structured thinking under pressure. Interviewers typically present ambiguous problems and watch how you frame them before jumping to solutions. Take a moment to clarify scope, state your assumptions out loud, and organize your answer before diving in.

Data-first instincts. Be ready to talk about metrics at every stage of your answer. If you propose a feature, name the metric that would tell you it worked. If you describe a past project, cite the outcome in numbers (with appropriate context if the numbers are sensitive).

Cross-functional empathy. PMs at AbhiBus work closely with engineering, design, business development (for operator partnerships), and customer support. Candidates who can demonstrate past experience navigating these relationships tend to stand out.

Ownership and follow-through. AbhiBus, like many growth-stage companies, values PMs who take problems from discovery to delivery without needing constant direction. Use your STAR answers to show initiative, not just participation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product. Book a trip on AbhiBus, even if you cancel it. Go through every screen: search, filters, seat selection, payment, confirmation, and cancellation. Note what works well and what feels clunky. Read recent app store reviews to understand real user pain points.

Week 2: Build your framework toolkit. Practice the metric-drop diagnosis framework, a prioritization scoring approach, and the user-journey mapping method until you can apply each one quickly. Use a travel-app scenario each time so your examples feel relevant to the role.

Week 3: Prepare your stories. Map your past experience to the themes above: a time you improved a metric, a time you prioritized under constraint, a time you worked with data, and a time you handled a difficult stakeholder. Write them out in STAR format and trim each to under two minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and research. Do at least two mock interviews with someone who will give honest feedback. Read up on how two-sided marketplace products are managed. Look at how AbhiBus competes in the broader online travel space and form a point of view on where they should go next.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping the clarifying question. Many candidates hear 'improve AbhiBus' and immediately list features. Interviewers want to see you ask: which user segment, which metric, which constraint? One clarifying question at the start changes the quality of the entire answer.

Treating AbhiBus as a generic e-commerce app. Bus ticketing has specific dynamics: routes are fixed, seats are perishable inventory, operators are partners not employees, and many users book for others (family travel). Generic answers miss these nuances.

Vague metrics. Saying 'user satisfaction would improve' is not an answer. Name the metric: booking completion rate, repeat booking rate, refund request rate, operator churn rate. Be specific.

Over-engineering the solution. Candidates sometimes propose a full feature rebuild when a copy change or a loading-state fix would solve the same problem. Start with the smallest intervention that moves the metric, then scale up.

Underselling collaboration. PM interviews reward candidates who show they work well with engineers, designers, and business teams. If your STAR answers sound like you solved everything alone, revisit them and add the cross-functional dimension.

Not knowing basic company facts. Know roughly how many cities AbhiBus operates in, who their main competitors are, and what their primary revenue model is. Candidates who cannot answer basic product questions signal low interest.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the AbhiBus PM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves three to four rounds, covering an initial screening call, one or two product and analytical rounds, and a final round that may include a senior leader or a case study presentation. The exact structure can vary by role level and team. It is worth confirming the format with your recruiter at the start so you can prepare accordingly.

What salary range can a PM expect at AbhiBus?

Exact AbhiBus-specific figures are not publicly confirmed, but based on knok jobradar data for the broader Indian PM market, a PM with three to six years of experience is commonly cited in the 24-40 LPA range, while Senior PMs are commonly cited around 40-60 LPA. Associate PM roles typically sit in the 12-20 LPA band. Actual compensation at AbhiBus will depend on your level, experience, and negotiation.

Do I need prior travel-tech experience to get a PM role at AbhiBus?

Candidates from adjacent domains such as e-commerce, fintech, and consumer apps have reported success in AbhiBus interviews without prior travel-tech experience. What matters more is demonstrating that you understand marketplace dynamics and have done enough product research to speak intelligently about the bus-ticketing space. Using the product yourself before the interview and forming a clear point of view on where it could improve goes a long way.

Is there a case study or take-home assignment in the process?

Some candidates report receiving a product case study, either as a take-home or as a live exercise during the interview. Typical prompts involve improving a specific AbhiBus flow, diagnosing a metric problem, or prioritizing a roadmap. Practicing these scenarios in advance using a structured framework will help you perform well under time pressure.

How competitive is it to get a PM role at AbhiBus right now?

AbhiBus currently has 15 open PM roles, which signals active hiring rather than a single one-off opening. However, PM roles at growth-stage consumer internet companies tend to attract a high volume of applicants, so differentiation matters. A tailored resume that highlights marketplace product experience, clear metrics from past roles, and genuine product thinking about AbhiBus specifically will improve your chances.

What is the best way to research AbhiBus before the interview?

Start by using the AbhiBus app end-to-end, from search to booking to (if possible) cancellation, and take notes on friction points and standout features. Read recent app store reviews on both iOS and Android to get unfiltered user feedback. Look at how AbhiBus positions itself relative to competitors in public press. Forming a clear opinion on one thing AbhiBus does well and one thing you would improve is a strong foundation for the interview conversation.
