01 Overview

Overview

Haptik is a conversational AI company, part of Reliance Jio, that builds enterprise chatbots and virtual assistant products for clients in BFSI, telecom, and retail. A Technical Program Manager at Haptik owns the delivery of complex, cross-functional programs: coordinating engineering, product, and client-facing teams to ship AI-powered features on schedule and within scope.

As of July 2026, Haptik has 16 open roles, which signals active TPM hiring. The interview process typically spans multiple rounds and candidates report it covers program management fundamentals (stakeholder alignment, risk management, delivery tracking), technical depth (APIs, integrations, system design at a program level), and behavioral questions about leadership and client handling.

Haptik interviewers tend to probe how you operate under ambiguity. Conversational AI products evolve fast, clients escalate, and roadmaps shift. The strongest candidates show they have a repeatable system for managing all of this, not just a collection of good stories.

Most Asked Questions

These questions are commonly reported by TPM candidates interviewing at conversational AI companies similar to Haptik. Prepare a concrete answer for each before your interview.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a situation where engineering and product were misaligned on scope. How did you resolve it?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were building a WhatsApp bot integration for a large BFSI client. Shortly before go-live, the product team wanted to add a new document upload flow that engineering said would require an additional sprint.

*Task:* My job was to protect the go-live date while making sure both teams felt heard and the client was not blindsided.

*Action:* I called a joint session with the product lead and engineering lead the same day. I ran a scope-impact exercise: we mapped each requested feature against the original client sign-off criteria. The document upload flow was not in the agreed SOW. I escalated to the delivery head with a recommendation to move it to Phase 2 and drafted a note to the client explaining the phased approach.

*Result:* We launched on schedule. The client accepted the Phase 2 plan and the document upload went live the following sprint without any escalation.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you identified a technical risk before it became a problem.

*Situation:* During a system integration project, I noticed in our weekly status review that the third-party API our chatbot depended on was returning intermittent errors in the sandbox environment.

*Task:* Production cutover was coming up and I needed to validate whether the instability was a sandbox-only issue or a signal of a deeper problem.

*Action:* I worked with the engineering lead to run a structured load test on the sandbox and flagged the vendor for a support call. I also built a contingency plan: a graceful fallback message the bot would show users if the API was unavailable. I updated the risk register and shared it proactively with the client's IT team.

*Result:* The vendor confirmed a known sandbox bug and patched it before production cutover. The client appreciated the transparent communication, which strengthened trust for future phases.

---

Q: How do you handle a client escalation during a live deployment?

*Situation:* We were rolling out a new IVR-to-chat handoff feature for a telecom client. Shortly after go-live, the client's operations head called our account manager saying the handoff was dropping calls.

*Task:* As the TPM on the program, I needed to stabilize the situation, coordinate a fix, and keep the client informed throughout.

*Action:* I immediately pulled the engineering lead and QA lead into a bridge call. We quickly identified that a configuration flag had not been toggled in the production environment. I sent the client regular holding updates so they were never left without information. Once the fix was deployed, I walked the client's team through the root cause and the controls we were adding to prevent a recurrence.

*Result:* The feature was fully stable within a couple of hours of the initial alert. The client acknowledged the swift response in writing and renewed for the next phase of the program.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Two frameworks that work consistently well for TPM interviews at product-led companies like Haptik:

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result): Use this for every behavioral question. Keep your Situation brief (a few sentences), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what the team did), and always describe the Result in concrete terms. If you cannot share exact figures, describe relative impact: 'the client reported fewer escalations' is stronger than 'things improved.'

RACI for dependency and alignment questions: When asked how you manage cross-functional programs, walk through who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for each workstream. Haptik programs typically involve client teams, internal product, engineering, and Jio stakeholders simultaneously. Interviewers appreciate structured thinking about accountability.

Risk register framing for delivery questions: For questions about risk and uncertainty, describe your approach in three steps: identify (what signals you monitor), assess (impact versus likelihood), and mitigate (what you prepared in advance). Candidates who mention proactive risk tracking rather than reactive firefighting consistently receive positive feedback in publicly reported interview reviews for TPM roles.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Haptik TPM interviewers typically look for four qualities across all rounds:

Cross-functional influence without authority. TPMs at Haptik do not always own engineering headcount directly. Interviewers want to see that you can align engineering, product, and client teams through clear communication and structured processes, not by relying on hierarchy.

Comfort with AI and ML product cycles. Haptik ships NLP and conversational AI features where accuracy and model behavior are part of the delivery definition. You do not need to be an ML engineer, but you should be able to discuss how you track and communicate AI feature quality to non-technical stakeholders.

Client-facing ownership. Many Haptik programs involve direct enterprise client interaction. Interviewers probe whether you can manage client expectations, handle escalations calmly, and write clear status updates under pressure.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Conversational AI roadmaps shift frequently. Interviewers look for candidates who have a repeatable approach to scoping, prioritization, and replanning when requirements change mid-program.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week preparation plan for the Haptik TPM interview:

Week 1: Build your foundation. Start by reading Haptik's product pages and any publicly available case studies about their enterprise deployments. Note the types of clients they serve (BFSI, telecom, retail) because interviewers often ask scenario questions framed around their actual client verticals. Refresh your understanding of REST APIs and webhooks at a conceptual level, since Haptik products involve significant third-party integrations. Write out a handful of STAR stories covering: a delivery you owned end to end, a scope conflict you resolved, a risk you caught early, and a client escalation you managed.

Week 2: Practice and refine. Work through each of the questions listed in this guide, keeping your answers focused and concise. Ask a colleague to push back with follow-up questions like 'how do you know that worked?' or 'what would you do differently?' to simulate real interview pressure. Prepare a few thoughtful questions for your interviewers about Haptik's program governance model, how TPMs are embedded in product squads, and what success looks like in the first few months. Review Haptik's recent product announcements to signal genuine interest in their work.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Being too vague about your personal contribution. Candidates often say 'we did X' without explaining what they personally owned. Interviewers at Haptik want to know what you did, not what the team did. Separate your actions from team actions in every STAR story.

Skipping concrete impact. Even if you cannot share exact figures, describe results in specific terms. 'We reduced the client's resolution time' is weak. 'The client reported a drop in agent escalations after the bot went live' is stronger. If you have publicly reported data from your industry, use it.

Treating technical questions as optional. Some TPM candidates assume they can deflect all technical questions to engineers. Haptik interviewers expect you to hold your own on topics like API integrations, deployment pipelines, and how NLP models are evaluated. You do not need to write code, but you need to speak the language.

Not researching Haptik's client verticals. Walking in without knowing that Haptik serves BFSI, telecom, and retail enterprise clients signals low motivation. Spend time on their public case studies before the interview.

Memorising answers word for word. Haptik interviewers follow up and probe. A memorised script falls apart under pressure. Practice the story arc, not a script.
