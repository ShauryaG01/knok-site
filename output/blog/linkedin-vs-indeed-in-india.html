01 Overview

Overview

Two platforms dominate the job search conversation in India: LinkedIn and Indeed. Both are free to use at a basic level, but they work very differently, and picking the wrong one wastes weeks of effort.

LinkedIn is a professional network first and a job board second. Your profile is always on, recruiters search it even when you are not actively looking, and you can message HR directly. Indeed is a pure job board: you search, you apply, you wait. No networking, no profile browsing by recruiters, no direct HR contact.

The right answer depends on your industry, your seniority level, and whether you are hunting actively or just staying open to better offers. This guide compares both honestly so you can stop guessing and start applying in the right place.

03 Best For

Best For

Pick LinkedIn if you are in IT, engineering, product, finance, consulting, or any white-collar sector with more than two years of experience. It works best when recruiters are likely to come to you, not just when you apply outward. LinkedIn is the right choice if you want to cold-message hiring managers directly, build a professional presence that generates inbound interest, or target large companies and MNCs. Professionals in metro cities like Bangalore, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, and Pune will find recruiter density on LinkedIn noticeably higher.

Pick Indeed if you are a fresher or in the first couple of years of your career. It has stronger listings for entry-level roles, BPO, retail, logistics, healthcare, and manufacturing. Indeed also aggregates from smaller job boards, so you often find listings from SMEs and regional companies that never post on LinkedIn. If you are based in a Tier 2 or Tier 3 city, or if you want to apply to many jobs quickly without managing a public profile, Indeed is the more practical starting point.

Use both if you are in IT and actively searching. There is real overlap, but each platform surfaces listings the other misses. Set up job alerts on both with the same search terms so you are not relying on memory to check daily.

04 Pros And Cons

Pros And Cons

LinkedIn: what works
Recruiters search profiles even when you are not applying anywhere. One strong profile works around the clock. Direct messaging means you can bypass the black-hole application portal and reach a real person. Even on the free tier, you can connect, apply, and respond to inbound recruiter messages without paying anything.

LinkedIn: what does not work
The algorithm rewards active content creators. Passive candidates who do not post or engage can become less visible over time. Many listings are managed by third-party recruiters who repost the same role repeatedly, making it hard to judge real demand. Premium pricing is publicly reported as steep relative to the value most Indian mid-level professionals get from the InMail credits.

Indeed: what works
Simple and fast. No profile to maintain, no content strategy, no connections to manage. Aggregation means you cover a lot of ground in one search. Useful for sectors under-represented on LinkedIn, including BPO, retail, logistics, government, and smaller regional employers.

Indeed: what does not work
No recruiter discovery. You are entirely dependent on outbound applying. Many listings come from staffing agencies and consultancies, adding a layer between you and the actual employer. The apply experience is inconsistent: some roles redirect you to third-party portals anyway, and there is no way to differentiate yourself beyond a resume.

05 Recommendation

Recommendation

For most Indian professionals, LinkedIn is the more powerful platform long term, but it requires upfront investment. You need a complete, keyword-rich profile and a habit of staying reasonably active. The payoff from inbound recruiter interest builds over weeks and months, not days.

If you are actively hunting right now and need a role in the next four to six weeks, do not rely on LinkedIn alone. Run searches on Indeed in parallel, especially for roles at smaller companies or in sectors like BPO, retail, or logistics where Indeed has stronger coverage.

If you are a senior professional, industry surveys and publicly reported data consistently point to referrals and recruiter outreach as major sources of senior hires, far more than cold applications. That makes your LinkedIn profile the highest-leverage tool you have. Make sure your headline and about section are written for the recruiter searching for your next role, not for your current employer.

If you are a fresher, start with Indeed to understand what is available in your range, then build your LinkedIn profile in parallel so it is ready when recruiters start noticing you six to twelve months into your first job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Is LinkedIn Premium worth paying for in India?

For most people, no, not right away. The free tier is functional enough to apply, connect, and message people who accept your connection request. Premium is worth considering if you are in a senior role and need InMail credits to reach recruiters who do not accept cold connections. The publicly reported pricing is high relative to what most mid-level Indian professionals actually get from the InMail credits, so try the free tier for a few months before upgrading.

Do Indian recruiters actually use Indeed?

Yes, but the mix differs from LinkedIn. Staffing agencies and consultancies post heavily on Indeed. Large IT companies tend to post on both platforms. SMEs and regional employers often use Indeed more than LinkedIn because it is cheaper and simpler to post on. If you are targeting companies with fewer than a few hundred employees, Indeed is often where they list first.

My applications on both platforms are getting no response. What is going wrong?

The most common reasons are a resume that does not match the job description closely enough, applying to roles where you are missing a key requirement, or applying to jobs that are already filled but still listed. Try filtering by 'recently posted' or 'active' roles on both platforms. On LinkedIn, check how many applicants a role already has. Roles with very high applicant counts, commonly cited as over 200, have low odds regardless of resume quality.

Is it better to apply directly on a company website instead of through LinkedIn or Indeed?

Sometimes, especially for large companies, because it can signal genuine interest. When you apply through a third-party platform, your application enters the same ATS queue anyway and the company sees the source as 'LinkedIn' or 'Indeed'. The bigger factor is always your resume match to the job description, not the application channel. Do not spend extra time on direct applications at the cost of reducing your overall volume.

Can I use LinkedIn and Indeed at the same time without it getting confusing?

Yes, and for active job seekers it is recommended. The two platforms surface different employers and different listings. Set up job alerts on both with the same search terms so you get a daily email instead of having to log in and search manually. Keep a simple spreadsheet to track where you applied and when, so you do not double-apply or forget to follow up.

What about Naukri? Should I use that too?

Naukri is the largest dedicated job board in India by listing volume for corporate roles, and many Indian recruiters, especially in IT, BFSI, and manufacturing, actively search candidate profiles there. The short version is: if you are actively job hunting in India, a Naukri profile is worth having alongside LinkedIn. Indeed aggregates some listings from other boards, but a direct Naukri profile lets recruiters search and contact you directly, similar to how LinkedIn recruiter search works.
