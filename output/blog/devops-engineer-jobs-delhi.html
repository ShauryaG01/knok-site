A few honest caveats. Entry-level roles at 6-12 LPA are standard for engineers transitioning into DevOps from a development or sysadmin background. At the mid level (15-28 LPA), the range is wide because it depends heavily on your cloud and Kubernetes depth. Product companies and well-funded startups tend to pay toward the higher end; IT services firms often sit closer to the lower end.

The senior band (30-50 LPA) applies to engineers who can own infrastructure decisions end-to-end. Lead and Staff roles (45-70+ LPA) are fewer in total count but do exist, particularly at enterprise companies managing complex cloud environments. Note the overlap between the senior and lead bands. Engineers at the higher end of the 6-9 year range with architecture and mentoring experience may be able to negotiate into the lower end of the lead band.

Always clarify the total package. Variable pay, stock options at product companies, and flexibility around remote work can shift the real value of an offer at the senior and lead levels.

05 How To Stand Out

How To Stand Out

DevOps hiring in Delhi NCR is competitive, especially at the mid level. The gap between a resume that gets ignored and one that earns a callback is often smaller than candidates expect.

Cloud certifications clear initial filters. AWS Certified DevOps Engineer, Certified Kubernetes Administrator (CKA), and Google Cloud's Professional DevOps Engineer are consistently sought by hiring managers. They do not guarantee an offer, but they help you clear ATS and recruiter screening, particularly at enterprise companies like Deutsche Telekom Digital Labs and Thales Group.

Specific project work beats generic experience. Saying you 'managed CI/CD pipelines' is common. Walking through a specific migration, for example moving from Jenkins to GitHub Actions and explaining the trade-offs you navigated, is what makes a candidate memorable. A public GitHub repository or open-source contribution serves as proof when client work cannot be discussed freely.

Infrastructure as Code depth is now expected. Terraform is baseline at the mid level. Pulumi or AWS CDK experience stands out as more companies shift toward developer-first infrastructure patterns.

DevSecOps skills open enterprise doors. With companies like Bupa (healthcare) and Thales (defence) hiring in Delhi NCR, security-aware DevOps engineers are in real demand. Integrating SAST/DAST into pipelines and handling secrets management with tools like HashiCorp Vault or AWS Secrets Manager puts you ahead of candidates who treat security as someone else's responsibility.

Communication and cross-team skills matter more than most expect. Many Delhi NCR roles at global captives involve working with distributed teams across time zones. Writing clear runbooks, leading calm incident reviews, and explaining infrastructure decisions to product managers comes up in interviews more than most candidates prepare for.

06 Application Strategy

Application Strategy

Having the right skills is necessary but not sufficient. How you approach applications in Delhi NCR makes a real difference in outcomes.

Mirror the job description's tool names. If the JD says 'ArgoCD', use that exact term, not just 'GitOps tooling'. ATS systems filter on keywords, and so do recruiters scanning quickly. Match their language where it is honest to do so.

Apply early in the posting window. Many Delhi NCR openings at companies like Paytm get strong early interest and slow down after the first week. Being in an early batch of applicants improves your odds of reaching an interview before the role fills informally.

Message the recruiter or hiring manager directly on LinkedIn. Keep it to three or four sentences: who you are, the specific role, and one concrete reason you fit. This is not about being pushy. It is about ensuring your resume actually gets seen instead of sitting in a queue.

Prepare for scenario-based technical screens. Product companies and captives in Delhi NCR tend to run scenario interviews: on-call failures, deployment rollbacks, unexpected cloud cost spikes. Practice talking through these out loud. It is different from writing code on a whiteboard, and most candidates underprepare.

Track your applications actively. With 40 active roles in the market right now, it is easy to lose track of where you applied, what stage each is at, and when to follow up. A simple spreadsheet works well for this.
