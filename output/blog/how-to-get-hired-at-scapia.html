03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Scapia's process follows a pattern common to Indian fintech startups at this stage. The number of rounds and their structure can vary by role and seniority, so treat the stages below as a general guide rather than a confirmed sequence.

Stage 1: Application and initial screening. You apply through the company's careers page or LinkedIn, or through a referral. A recruiter reviews shortlisted applications and reaches out if your profile matches. Referrals tend to surface faster at startups where recruiter bandwidth is limited.

Stage 2: Recruiter or HR call. This is typically a short call covering your background, salary expectations, notice period, and interest in the role. Be direct about your current situation and why Scapia specifically appeals to you. Generic answers about 'wanting to work in fintech' rarely impress startup recruiters.

Stage 3: Technical or functional assessment. For engineering roles, expect a coding round or system design discussion. For product roles, a case study or product teardown is common. Business and growth roles may involve an analytics or go-to-market exercise. At startups, these assessments tend to be practical and tied to real problems the team is actively solving.

Stage 4: Manager or panel interview. You meet the hiring manager and sometimes a cross-functional team member. This round tests depth in your domain, how you think through ambiguous problems, and how you would operate without heavy structure. Expect questions about past ownership, how you have handled failure, and your take on the product itself.

Stage 5: Founder or leadership round. For mid-senior and senior roles, a final conversation with a founder or senior leader is common at startups of this stage. This is less about drilling technical skills and more about culture fit, long-term thinking, and alignment with what the company is building. Come with a clear point of view on the product and the market.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Scapia is building a consumer fintech product, so the qualities they look for cut across technical skill and startup mindset.

Ownership and bias to action. At a startup, you will not have large teams or long approval chains behind you. Candidates who can take a problem, run with it, and close the loop without constant hand-holding stand out. Past examples where you drove something end-to-end matter more than credentials alone.

Comfort with ambiguity. Roadmaps, priorities, and even team structures can shift quickly at a growing startup. If you need a fully scoped brief before you can work, this environment will feel frustrating. Scapia likely values people who can define the scope themselves when it is not handed to them.

Domain relevance. For fintech roles, understanding credit products, payment rails, or consumer lending is a genuine plus. For travel-adjacent roles, knowing how loyalty and rewards programmes work helps. Engineers are expected to write clean, scalable code, given the product handles financial transactions where reliability matters.

Customer obsession. The Scapia card is a consumer product. Understanding what actual users want and translating that into features, copy, or campaigns is valued across functions, not just in product management.

Clear communication. Small teams mean frequent cross-functional work. Clear written and verbal communication, particularly in async settings, is important at any level.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product before anything else. Download the Scapia app, look at the card's reward structure, and form a view on who the target customer is and what job the product is doing for them. Candidates who have actually used the product, or have clearly thought about it, make a noticeably stronger impression in startup interviews.

Research the competitive landscape. Scapia competes in the travel credit card space alongside other fintech players. Knowing what makes a travel card sticky, where Scapia is differentiated, and what gaps exist shows you have done more than skim the website.

Prepare structured examples. Use a format like Situation, Task, Action, Result when answering behavioural questions. Startup interviewers want to know what you specifically did, not what your team did. Be honest about where things did not go as planned. Self-awareness reads as maturity.

Brush up on domain skills. For engineers, practice coding problems and be ready to discuss system design with constraints relevant to fintech (data consistency, idempotency, fraud signal handling). For product and business roles, be ready to size a market, write a prioritisation framework, or walk through a product decision under uncertainty.

Prepare smart questions. Ask about the team's current priorities, what success looks like in the first few months, and how decisions are made. These questions signal that you are thinking about the job as a working environment, not just a title.

Sort your basics early. Know your expected compensation (Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry publicly reported ranges for similar fintech roles in Bengaluru), your notice period, and whether you need relocation support. Startups move quickly once they decide on a candidate, and delays on your end can cost you the offer.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Scapia operates in a space that is genuinely exciting for people who like to travel and build financial products. The culture, based on what is publicly visible about the company and patterns at similar early-stage fintechs, tends to be fast-moving and close-knit.

Small team, high visibility. With a lean team, hierarchy is relatively flat. You are likely to interact directly with founders and senior leaders, which means your work is visible and your voice carries. People who thrive in this kind of environment tend to be comfortable being seen and are motivated by that visibility rather than unsettled by it.

Travel is part of the identity. The product is built around travel, and that enthusiasm tends to extend to the team. If travel is something you genuinely care about, that shared interest makes it easier to connect with the product and with colleagues.

Pace is real. Like most startups at this stage, priorities can shift with market conditions, user feedback, or product pivots. If you value stable, predictable planning horizons, that is worth weighing honestly before you join.

Impact over process, with guardrails. The culture likely rewards people who get things done over those who follow process for its own sake. That said, in fintech, regulatory compliance and data security are areas where process matters a great deal and cannot be cut, so a good balance between speed and rigour is expected.
