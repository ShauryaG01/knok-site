01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Fractal is one of India's best-known AI and analytics firms, serving large global clients across industries like consumer goods, financial services, retail, and insurance. If you want to apply data science and machine learning to problems that actually move business decisions at large companies, Fractal is worth pursuing seriously.

As of mid-2026, Fractal has 55 open roles across functions including data science, data engineering, analytics consulting, product, and business development. Hiring is active across Bengaluru, Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Pune, with some remote-friendly options available in select teams.

The company hires at multiple levels. Fresh graduates can enter through analyst or associate data scientist programmes, while experienced professionals can apply for roles up to director and VP level. Most positions are filled on a rolling basis, so there is no fixed recruitment season to wait for. The earlier you apply once a role goes live, the faster you move through the process.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Fractal's interview process typically runs across three to five rounds, depending on the role and seniority. Here is a stage-by-stage breakdown of what most candidates experience.

HR or Recruiter Screen
This is usually a short call where the recruiter checks your background, current package, notice period, and interest in the role. They will also walk you through the job scope and team. Be ready with a clear summary of your experience and your reasons for wanting to move.

Technical Round
For data and analytics roles, expect questions on SQL, Python, and statistics. Common topics include window functions, joins, probability distributions, A/B testing, and model evaluation metrics. Some interviewers give a live coding task. For consulting-leaning roles, this round may include a mini case study with a dataset to explore.

Case Study or Problem-Solving Round
This is where Fractal differs from many companies. They often present a business scenario tied to an industry they work in, such as retail or financial services, and ask you to walk through your analytical approach. They want to see how you break down a problem, what data you would ask for, and how you would turn findings into a recommendation a business leader can act on.

Managerial Round
A conversation with a senior manager or analytics lead. Expect questions on how you have handled ambiguous projects, worked in cross-functional teams, and communicated technical findings to non-technical stakeholders. Past project walkthroughs are common here.

Leadership or HR Final Round
For senior and leadership roles, a final conversation with a director or business head is standard. This covers career goals, culture alignment, and leadership scenarios. For junior roles, this round may be combined with the managerial round or skipped entirely.

The full process generally takes two to four weeks from first contact to offer, though timelines can vary by team and role.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Fractal looks for people who can connect data to decisions, not just people who can run models.

Technical foundation: Strong SQL and Python skills are expected for most technical roles. For data science positions, you should be comfortable with statistical modelling, machine learning, and experiment design. Familiarity with cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, or GCP) and tools like Spark or Tableau is a plus in many teams.

Business orientation: Because Fractal's work is client-facing, they value people who frame analysis in terms of outcomes. Explaining why your model choice matters to a client's revenue or customer retention is more valuable than perfecting technical jargon.

Communication clarity: You will often present findings to client teams who are not data professionals. The ability to simplify a complex analysis into a clear, actionable recommendation is highly valued.

Ownership and initiative: Fractal projects often require you to manage your own workstream without close supervision. They want people who identify problems proactively and take responsibility for the full outcome, not just their slice of it.

Curiosity: A genuine interest in how businesses work and why data behaves a certain way comes through in interviews. Candidates who ask sharp, specific questions tend to stand out.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Revise the core technical concepts. SQL window functions, joins, and subqueries come up in nearly every technical interview at Fractal. In Python, focus on pandas and scikit-learn workflows. If you are applying for a senior data science role, revise model evaluation, regularisation, and experiment design in depth.

Practise case interviews out loud. Fractal's case rounds are closer to consulting-style interviews than pure coding tests. A useful structure: restate the problem, identify what data you would need, outline your analytical approach, and describe the output you would deliver to the business. Practise narrating your thinking rather than going silent while you work.

Build two or three strong project stories. Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Pick examples where your work influenced a real decision or produced a clear outcome. Be ready to go deep on methodology, data sources, and trade-offs you made along the way.

Read Fractal's published work. They share case studies and thought leadership on topics like AI in insurance, retail analytics, and consumer behaviour modelling. Walking into an interview with specific knowledge of their work signals genuine interest and helps you ask sharper questions.

Prepare a specific answer to 'why Fractal?' A vague answer loses points. Connecting your background to a particular capability or industry vertical they work in makes a strong impression on interviewers who hear the generic version constantly.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Fractal builds its identity around intellectual curiosity and a genuine passion for putting data to work on real business problems. Employees often describe the quality of the work as the biggest draw. You are solving problems that matter to large clients, which keeps the day-to-day engaging over time.

The culture leans collaborative. Data scientists, engineers, and business consultants typically work together in cross-functional project teams. Client interaction is frequent, which means the environment rewards people who can handle feedback gracefully and communicate clearly across levels.

Work pace is real. Publicly reported employee feedback on Glassdoor notes that timelines can get tight when client deliverables are approaching. Work-life balance is commonly cited as varying by team and project phase. It is worth asking about team norms and typical project cycles during your interviews rather than assuming one experience applies everywhere.

Learning and career growth are genuine strengths at Fractal. The company offers internal training, certification support, and exposure to a wide range of industry problems. Early-career professionals in particular commonly cite the pace of skill development as a reason they chose to stay. Internal mobility across teams and geographies is also commonly cited as available and encouraged for strong performers.

Senior leadership is generally described as approachable and engaged. The company has grown significantly while maintaining a culture where good ideas from analysts can reach decision-makers.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does Fractal have right now?

As of mid-2026, Fractal has 55 open roles across functions including data science, engineering, analytics consulting, and business development. Positions are spread across cities like Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Hyderabad. The count changes regularly as roles open and close, so checking their careers page frequently or setting up job alerts is worth doing if you are actively looking.

Does Fractal hire freshers and recent graduates?

Yes. Fractal has analyst and associate-level entry tracks for fresh graduates, particularly from engineering, statistics, and mathematics backgrounds. The selection process for freshers typically includes an analytical or aptitude test, a technical round covering SQL and Python basics, and an HR interview. Campus hiring is active at select engineering colleges, but off-campus applicants are also considered through their careers portal.

What salary can I expect at Fractal?

Exact figures vary by role, seniority, and location. For data science and analytics roles, publicly reported ranges on Glassdoor and levels.fyi give a useful baseline, though these reflect a sample of self-reported figures. Compensation typically includes a fixed component, variable pay, and benefits. It is worth researching role-specific data before your HR discussion and being upfront about your expectations early in the process.

How hard is it to crack the Fractal interview?

The process is moderately challenging. Technical rounds are thorough but not designed to be tricky. The case study round is where most candidates find it hardest, because it requires structured business thinking rather than just technical skill. Candidates who revise SQL and Python fundamentals and practise walking through a business problem out loud generally find the process very manageable. Researching Fractal's industry work helps significantly in the case and managerial rounds.

What are the most common reasons candidates do not clear the Fractal interview?

Based on commonly cited feedback from candidates, the most frequent gaps are weak SQL or Python foundations, unclear case study answers that lack business context, and difficulty explaining past projects in terms of impact rather than just methodology. Interviewers also notice when candidates have not researched what Fractal actually does. Preparing specific project stories and practising case walkthroughs out loud addresses most of these gaps before you walk in.

Is Fractal a good place for long-term career growth?

For data professionals early in their career, Fractal is commonly cited as a strong learning environment. You gain exposure to multiple industries, real client problems, and cross-functional team dynamics in a short span. Internal mobility is available for strong performers. Mid to senior professionals sometimes move on after a few years to client-side or product company roles, using their Fractal experience as a strong credential. How well it works for you long-term often depends on the team and manager you land with.
