03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Embat's hiring process varies by role, but based on commonly reported patterns at European fintech companies at a similar growth stage, here is a typical structure to prepare for.

Application and resume screening. Your resume is matched against the role requirements. Emphasise any fintech, treasury, or B2B SaaS experience clearly, since Embat hires for domain fit alongside technical or functional skill.

Recruiter or HR call. An introductory conversation to confirm your background, notice period, and interest in the role. You may be asked what draws you to a treasury-focused fintech specifically, so prepare a genuine answer.

Technical or domain assessment. For engineering roles, this commonly involves a take-home or live coding task focused on system design and code quality. For product, sales, or finance roles, expect a case study or scenario-based discussion about customer problems or business decisions.

Hiring manager interview. A deeper discussion of your past experience and how you approach complex problems. Specific examples from your previous work, framed around results you delivered, tend to perform well here.

Final round. Usually a panel interview or a conversation with senior leadership. The focus shifts to cultural alignment, long-term thinking, and how you handle ambiguity. Some roles include a short presentation or proposal at this stage.

The full process typically runs across several weeks, so plan your timeline carefully if you have competing offers.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Embat builds financial infrastructure, so they value people who understand both the technical and the business side of what they are building.

Finance or fintech domain knowledge. Understanding treasury management, cash flow, payments, or banking APIs gives you a real edge. You do not need to be an accountant, but knowing why a CFO cares about real-time cash visibility helps you have stronger conversations during interviews.

B2B SaaS experience. Embat sells to finance teams at companies, not to consumers. Product, sales, and engineering candidates who have worked in B2B contexts understand the customer differently, and that shows.

Ownership and self-direction. Growth-stage companies move fast and do not have a process for everything. Candidates who can identify a problem, propose a fix, and follow through without waiting for detailed instructions stand out consistently.

Clear communication. Finance is a domain where precision matters. Whether you are writing a spec, a sales email, or a pull request description, Embat hires people who communicate with care and clarity.

Comfort with ambiguity. Embat is scaling, which means roles evolve and priorities shift. Candidates who frame this as exciting rather than frustrating tend to do well in later interview rounds.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Study the product deeply. Before any interview, use Embat's public materials to understand what their platform does and who it is for. Being able to speak about specific product features or customer problems they solve shows genuine interest and saves interview time.

Understand treasury basics. You do not need a finance degree, but knowing the basics of cash flow forecasting, bank reconciliation, and payment workflows will make you a stronger candidate. Free introductory resources on corporate treasury are widely available online.

Prepare STAR examples. For behavioural interviews, structure your answers as Situation, Task, Action, Result. Focus on examples where you took ownership, navigated uncertainty, or collaborated across teams with different priorities.

Know the competitive landscape. Embat competes in the treasury and cash management software space. Understanding who else operates in this market, and why a customer might choose Embat over an alternative, signals that you have done your homework.

Tailor your resume. Highlight any fintech, B2B, or finance-related experience prominently. If you have worked with payment systems, banking APIs, or enterprise software, call that out specifically rather than burying it in a generic summary.

Prepare smart questions. Interviewers remember candidates who ask thoughtful questions. Ask about team structure, product roadmap priorities, or how success is measured in the role. Avoid questions whose answers are already on their website.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Embat operates with the energy of a growth-stage company that takes its domain seriously. Finance teams rely on their software for critical decisions, so there is a culture of precision and accountability alongside the usual startup pace.

Collaboration across disciplines. Engineering, product, and finance functions work closely together at Embat. People who are comfortable talking to customers, explaining technical constraints in plain terms, or understanding a spreadsheet tend to fit in well.

Customer obsession. Embat's customers are finance professionals with high standards and low tolerance for errors. The team is expected to deeply understand customer pain and build with that context in mind, not just ship features on a schedule.

Speed with quality. Growth-stage fintechs move quickly, but financial software has real consequences when it breaks. The likely balance is: ship reliably, move fast on things that are reversible, and be more careful on things that are not.

Transparency and directness. European fintech companies at this stage often maintain flat hierarchies and open communication. Feedback tends to be direct, and people are expected to raise problems early rather than hoping they resolve themselves.

If you want a real-world sense of the culture before applying, reading public posts from Embat team members on LinkedIn can give you a picture that job descriptions do not always capture.
