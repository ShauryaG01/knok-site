01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

WebMD is one of the most widely recognized names in online health information, serving millions of users who rely on the platform for medical guidance, symptom checks, and doctor-reviewed content. As of mid-2026, WebMD has 86 open roles across its operations, spanning technology, product, content, sales, and corporate functions. This is a healthy number of openings, suggesting the company is in an active hiring phase across several departments.

Roles at WebMD tend to sit at the intersection of healthcare knowledge and digital execution. Whether you are a software engineer building consumer-facing health tools, a content editor ensuring medical accuracy, a product manager driving platform growth, or a data analyst turning user behaviour into insight, there is a broad range of opportunities to explore. Getting hired here is not only about technical skill. Because the company publishes health information that real patients and caregivers rely on, WebMD places a premium on accuracy, responsibility, and clear communication at every level of the organisation.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The hiring process at WebMD typically moves through four to five stages, though the exact format varies by role and team. The outline below reflects what candidates commonly report, so treat it as a guide rather than a guarantee.

Application and initial screen: After you submit your application, a recruiter usually reaches out within a week or two for a short call. This phone screen covers your background, your reason for applying, and basic logistics around location and compensation expectations. Keep it concise and professional.

Skills or technical assessment: For engineering and product roles, expect a take-home assignment or a live coding session covering data structures, algorithms, and sometimes system design. Content and editorial candidates are often asked to complete a writing or editing test. This stage exists to verify that you can actually do the core work the role requires.

Team interviews: This is typically a set of video calls with the hiring manager and two or three teammates. Expect a mix of behavioural questions and role-specific scenarios. Questions often explore how you have handled tight deadlines, cross-functional disagreements, or ambiguous briefs. For senior roles, system design and leadership questions are commonly cited by candidates who have gone through this stage.

Senior stakeholder round: A final conversation with a department head or cross-functional leader. This round focuses less on technical depth and more on how you think, how you communicate, and whether your working style fits the team.

Offer and negotiation: If the process goes well, HR sends a written offer. Salary negotiation is normal and expected at this stage, so do not accept the first figure without at least one conversation about your expectations.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

WebMD is selective about candidates who combine professional skill with a genuine respect for accuracy. A few qualities stand out across roles.

Accuracy and attention to detail: The company publishes health content that people act on. Getting facts wrong has real consequences, so interviewers pay close attention to whether candidates take quality seriously.

Domain awareness: You do not need a clinical background for most roles, but you should be comfortable learning health terminology and understanding why precision in this space matters. Candidates who show genuine curiosity about health topics tend to stand out.

Digital and product thinking: WebMD operates large, high-traffic consumer platforms. Experience with web products, SEO, content management systems, or digital analytics is valued across technology, content, and product teams alike.

Clear communication: Teams collaborate across editorial, engineering, design, and business functions. The ability to explain your work clearly, whether in a document, a message, or a meeting, is something interviewers actively assess.

Ownership and initiative: Employee feedback on Glassdoor commonly describes a preference for candidates who take initiative rather than waiting to be directed. If your past experience shows that you spotted a problem and acted on it without being asked, make sure those stories are in your interview prep.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Use the product before your first interview. Spend real time on WebMD's platforms. Explore the symptom checker, read a few articles, and notice how content is structured. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually engaged with the product rather than only reading about it.

Match your resume to the job description. WebMD posts detailed job descriptions. Use the same language they use for tools, skills, and responsibilities. Applicant tracking systems scan for keyword matches before a human reads your file, so alignment here is not optional.

Prepare three to five STAR stories. Structure each one around a Situation, a Task, an Action you took, and the Result you achieved. Common interview themes include handling competing deadlines, resolving disagreements with a colleague, and delivering work when the requirements were unclear.

For tech roles, revise the core fundamentals. Interviews commonly include questions on data structures, algorithms, and system design. Practice consistently in the weeks before your interview on platforms like LeetCode or similar tools.

Prepare thoughtful questions. At the end of each round, ask something specific about the team's current priorities or a recent challenge they navigated. Generic questions make a weak impression. Specific ones signal that you are already thinking like a potential team member.

Send a follow-up note. A short, professional message to your recruiter after each round keeps you visible in a process that can span several weeks. It is a small habit that many candidates skip, which makes it easy to stand out.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

WebMD's workplace culture is shaped by its mission: helping people get the health information they need to make better decisions. This creates an environment that values editorial standards, thoughtfulness, and process more than speed at all costs. It is not a startup culture. Teams tend to be collaborative rather than competitive, and the company has a history of internal mobility, meaning people do move between roles and functions over time.

Employee feedback on Glassdoor commonly describes the culture as professional and process-oriented, with reasonable expectations around working hours. This makes sense for a company that is a mature, established platform rather than a high-growth early-stage business. If you prefer a structured environment where your daily work has clear social value and where accuracy is treated as a professional standard rather than a suggestion, WebMD tends to appeal strongly to that kind of professional.

The company also places visible emphasis on inclusion and cross-functional collaboration in its hiring materials and public communications. Most roles involve regular coordination between editorial, technology, and business teams, so comfort with working across functions is genuinely valued here, not just listed as a nice-to-have.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many open roles does WebMD currently have?

As of mid-2026, WebMD has 86 open roles across its operations. These span functions including technology, product, content, sales, and corporate support. The number shifts as positions are filled and new ones open, so checking listings regularly gives you the most current picture.

What kinds of roles does WebMD hire for most often?

WebMD hires across a wide range of functions, with consistent demand in software engineering, product management, content and editorial, data and analytics, and sales. Technology and content roles are particularly prominent given that WebMD's core business is a large digital health platform. Corporate functions like HR, finance, and marketing also appear in the listings on a regular basis.

How long does the WebMD hiring process usually take?

Publicly reported candidate experiences commonly suggest a timeline of four to eight weeks from application to offer, depending on the role and the team's urgency. The process can move faster for roles with immediate needs and slower for senior or specialist positions. Following up with your recruiter every week or so is reasonable if you have not heard back after a stage.

Does WebMD hire remotely or is it mostly in-office?

WebMD has offered both remote and hybrid arrangements across different roles and teams. The specific setup depends on the position and the hiring manager's preferences. Job descriptions usually state the location requirement clearly, so check each listing individually rather than assuming all roles follow the same policy.

What salary can I expect at WebMD?

Compensation at WebMD varies by role, level, and location. Glassdoor commonly cites figures for technology and product roles at companies of similar scale, and industry surveys for digital health platforms provide a broader reference point. The best approach is to research the specific role title on Glassdoor or levels.fyi and walk into the offer negotiation with a clear number in mind.

Is it hard to get a job at WebMD without a health industry background?

A clinical or medical background is not required for most roles at WebMD. The company hires generalist engineers, product managers, and business professionals who have no prior health industry experience. What matters more is a genuine interest in the mission, comfort with learning health terminology on the job, and strong fundamentals in your own area of expertise.
