01 Overview

Overview

Alaan is a fintech company that builds corporate card and expense management tools, with its main operations focused on the Middle East market. As of mid-2026, Alaan has 22 open Software Engineer roles, which signals the engineering team is in a meaningful growth phase. Candidates report a structured hiring process that typically includes a coding assessment, a system design round, and one or more behavioral interviews. The full process commonly spans two to three weeks from first contact to offer.

Because Alaan handles real money movement, interviewers look more carefully at how you think about data consistency, fault tolerance, and security than a typical product company might. If you have experience in payments, expense management, or any financial SaaS product, highlight that experience prominently in your resume and answers.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these examples as a template. Replace the specifics with your own experience.

Q: Tell me about a production incident you owned. What did you do?

*Situation:* Our payments reconciliation job started silently failing on weekends, causing mismatches between our ledger and the bank statement.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer that weekend and had to diagnose the issue, limit the blast radius, and restore correctness without losing any transaction records.

*Action:* I first confirmed the scope by querying the database for records where status was 'pending' beyond the expected processing window. I traced the failure to a third-party API that had changed its response schema without notice. I wrote a temporary patch to handle both the old and new schemas, deployed it behind a feature flag, and then backfilled the affected records by replaying the job against a read replica.

*Result:* All records were reconciled by end of day with no data loss. I added a schema validation test and an alerting rule so the same failure mode would page on-call within minutes next time.

---

Q: How would you design a multi-currency expense tracking feature?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we expanded into several new markets and needed to support expenses in local currencies while reporting in a single base currency.

*Task:* I led the backend design for this feature, which needed to handle exchange rate updates, historical rate lookups, and accurate financial reporting.

*Action:* I designed an exchange rate service that polled a reliable rates provider daily and stored snapshots with timestamps. Each expense record stored both the original currency and amount alongside the converted base-currency amount at the time of submission. For reporting, I wrote queries that allowed switching the conversion rate reference point so finance teams could reconcile against end-of-month rates.

*Result:* The feature launched on time and the finance team reported zero reconciliation issues in the first quarter after launch. The schema was later extended by another team to support an additional currency with no changes to existing tables.

---

Q: Describe a time you disagreed with a technical decision. How did you handle it?

*Situation:* My team decided to store all audit logs in the same primary relational database as our transactional data. I believed this would cause performance issues at scale.

*Task:* I needed to raise my concern without slowing down the sprint, and either change the decision or document why we accepted the risk.

*Action:* I wrote a short document outlining the read/write contention risk and estimated our log volume growth rate against the database's observed query times. I proposed two alternatives: a separate append-only table with aggressive archival, or shipping logs to a dedicated store. I shared this asynchronously before the next planning meeting so people had time to think through the options.

*Result:* The team agreed to the separate-table approach with archival. Several months later, when our log volume grew substantially, the primary database was completely unaffected. The tech lead cited this as an example of productive async technical debate in our retrospective.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions, structure your answer in three layers. Start with clarifying questions covering scale, consistency requirements, and failure modes. Then sketch the high-level components and data flow. Finally, go deep on the one or two areas where fintech concerns apply most, such as idempotency keys, double-spend prevention, or audit trails. Interviewers at fintech companies want to see that you default to 'what happens if this fails mid-transaction?' thinking as a first instinct, not an afterthought.

For behavioral questions, use the STAR format: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action, and be specific about what *you* did rather than what 'the team' did. End with a concrete Result, either a measurable outcome or a clear change in system behaviour or process.

For coding rounds, candidates report that Alaan typically favors problems that test data structures, algorithms, and clean code rather than extreme competitive-programming tricks. Talk through your approach before writing code. Name your variables clearly. If you spot an edge case such as a zero-amount transaction or a negative balance, mention it out loud even if you do not write the full guard.

For motivation or culture-fit questions, connect your answer to something specific about Alaan's product domain. Corporate spend management has real complexity: approval workflows, policy enforcement, multi-entity accounting. Mentioning one of these shows you understood the domain before walking into the interview.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fintech interviewers at a growth-stage company like Alaan are screening for a specific combination of traits that go beyond raw coding ability.

Reliability-first thinking. In financial software, a bug is not just a poor user experience, it can mean a wrong payment or a missing audit record. Interviewers want to hear you ask 'what happens on failure?' without being prompted. This is the single biggest differentiator between a generic software engineer and a strong fintech candidate.

Ownership and follow-through. Alaan is a growth-stage company. They want engineers who follow a problem from design to deployment to monitoring, not those who hand things off at the PR merge stage. In behavioral answers, show the full arc: you designed it, shipped it, and verified it was working correctly in production.

Clear, structured communication. System design interviews are partly a communication exercise. Interviewers want to see you structure your thinking out loud, invite feedback, and update your design when they push back. An engineer who goes quiet without narrating is harder to evaluate and harder to work with day to day.

Domain curiosity. You do not need prior fintech experience, but showing that you understand why fintech is technically different from a standard web app, such as why concurrency matters, why idempotency keys exist, why audit logs are non-negotiable, will differentiate you from candidates who treat every product the same.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Organize your prep into four phases. Adjust the depth of each based on your current experience level.

Phase 1: Solidify your coding foundation.
Practice array, hashmap, tree, and graph problems at the medium difficulty level. Focus on clean, readable code rather than micro-optimized solutions. For each problem, practice explaining your approach out loud as you would in a live session. This habit alone measurably improves performance in live coding rounds.

Phase 2: Build your system design vocabulary.
Review core distributed systems concepts: load balancing, database sharding, caching, message queues, and API gateway patterns. Then practice designing two or three full systems end to end, including at least one payments or financial workflow. For each design, force yourself to answer 'what happens if this component fails?' before moving on.

Phase 3: Go deep on fintech-specific design.
Study idempotency keys and why they matter in payment APIs. Understand double-entry bookkeeping at a conceptual level, as it underpins most expense management products. Review how multi-currency conversion is typically handled in financial databases. Reading publicly available engineering blogs from fintech companies can show you real-world design patterns directly relevant to Alaan's domain.

Phase 4: Prepare your behavioral stories.
Write out several stories from your career using the STAR format. Cover at least one incident or debugging story, one technical disagreement, and one feature you owned end to end. Practice saying each story out loud so it feels natural and stays concise.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to code before clarifying requirements. In a fintech interview, requirements matter a great deal. Skipping clarification signals you might do the same at work, where a misunderstood transaction flow can have real financial consequences.

Giving team credit in behavioral answers. 'We built...' and 'the team decided...' tell the interviewer nothing about what you personally did. Use 'I' and be specific about your own contribution at each step.

Ignoring failure modes in system design. If your design assumes the payment gateway always responds, the interviewer will probe it. Anticipate failure and address it proactively rather than waiting to be asked.

Under-preparing behavioral rounds. Many candidates over-index on coding and arrive at behavioral rounds with vague, unstructured stories. At a growth-stage company like Alaan, culture and ownership carry real weight. A weak behavioral round can offset a strong coding performance.

Not knowing Alaan's product. Arriving at an interview for a corporate spend management company without knowing what corporate cards, expense policies, or approval workflows are signals low motivation. Spend an hour with their public product information before your first round.

Treating salary negotiation as an afterthought. For Software Engineers in India, Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly report ranges of 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). Know which band applies to your experience and be ready to negotiate when an offer arrives.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Alaan typically have for Software Engineers?

Candidates typically report a process that includes a coding assessment (take-home or live), a system design round, and one or two behavioral interviews. Some also report a brief introductory call with a recruiter or hiring manager before the technical rounds begin. The exact number of rounds can vary by team and role level, so confirm the structure with your recruiter early in the process.

What programming languages does Alaan use, and should I prepare in a specific one?

Based on publicly available information, Alaan's stack includes JavaScript and TypeScript, with some Python usage on the backend. For coding rounds, most interviewers will accept a language you are comfortable in. That said, demonstrating fluency in TypeScript or Python tends to feel natural to interviewers reviewing fintech backend code. Confirm the preferred language with the recruiter when scheduling your coding round.

Does fintech experience matter, or can a candidate from a non-fintech background apply?

Fintech experience helps but is not a hard requirement. What matters more is that you understand why financial software has stricter requirements around correctness, consistency, and auditability than a typical consumer app. Candidates from e-commerce, logistics, or any domain that handled high-stakes transactions at scale can make a strong case. Spend time learning core concepts such as idempotency, reconciliation, and double-entry bookkeeping before your interview so you can speak the language confidently.

What salary can I expect for a Software Engineer role at Alaan?

Alaan is headquartered in the UAE, so compensation structures may differ from a purely India-based company. For Software Engineers in India broadly, Glassdoor and levels.fyi commonly report ranges of 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years). For Alaan specifically, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi for current data, and factor in any equity or remote work component when evaluating an offer.

How long does the Alaan hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report the full process taking two to three weeks, though this varies based on interviewer availability and how quickly rounds are scheduled. If you have not heard back within a week of completing a round, sending a polite follow-up to the recruiter is reasonable. Having another active offer and communicating it professionally can also help compress the timeline.

Is Alaan a good company to work at for an Indian Software Engineer?

Alaan is a growth-stage fintech with a product focused on the Middle East market, which gives engineers exposure to cross-border financial infrastructure and international scale. Whether it suits your goals depends on what you are optimizing for. If you want to own meaningful pieces of a growing financial product and gain exposure to a high-growth regional market, it is worth a close look. Check recent employee reviews on Glassdoor for candid perspectives on culture, work pace, and growth opportunities.
