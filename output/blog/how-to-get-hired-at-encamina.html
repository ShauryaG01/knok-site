01 Hiring Overview

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The exact stages can vary by role and team, but based on publicly reported candidate experiences, here is what to expect:

Stage 1: Application and CV screen. The talent team reviews your profile for relevant Microsoft stack experience and certifications. A tailored CV that closely mirrors the role description improves your chances of moving forward.

Stage 2: HR introductory call. A short video call covers your background, motivations, and availability. This is also a good moment to ask about team structure and day-to-day responsibilities.

Stage 3: Technical interview. This is the most important round. Expect scenario-based questions tied to the Microsoft tools the role focuses on. For development roles, you may be asked to walk through a solution design. For consulting roles, expect a scenario where you advise a client on adopting a Microsoft solution and must justify your recommendation.

Stage 4: Final conversation. A discussion with a senior team member or hiring manager focusing on cultural fit, collaboration style, and longer-term goals. Some candidates report this as a combined technical and cultural round rather than a separate stage.

The full process typically spans a few weeks from application to offer, though timelines vary by role and team availability.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Hands-on Microsoft stack experience. Encamina's work is built on Microsoft technology, so practical knowledge of Azure, SharePoint, Power Platform, or Microsoft Teams is central. They look for candidates who can demonstrate real project experience, not just theoretical knowledge.

Microsoft certifications. Relevant certifications in Azure development, Power Platform administration, or Microsoft cloud architecture signal genuine commitment to the ecosystem. While a certification alone is not enough, it often helps your CV get noticed at the screening stage.

Client communication skills. As a consultancy, Encamina regularly works directly with enterprise clients. The ability to explain technical decisions clearly to non-technical stakeholders is as important as the technical skill itself.

Problem-solving and adaptability. Candidates who can break down a complex client requirement and propose a structured, Microsoft-based solution stand out. They want people who think through problems methodically, not just execute tasks.

Teamwork and knowledge sharing. Encamina places visible emphasis on internal collaboration and learning. Candidates who contribute to team knowledge through documentation, internal sessions, or community engagement are viewed favourably.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Align your CV to the Microsoft stack. Go through the job description carefully and make sure your CV reflects the specific tools mentioned. Use the same terminology the role uses: for example, if the role says 'Power Apps', use that exact phrase rather than a synonym.

Earn or refresh a relevant Microsoft certification. If you do not hold a current certification in Azure development, Power Platform, or Microsoft cloud administration, consider working toward one before applying. Candidates with active certifications are commonly reported to move faster through screening.

Prepare scenario-based answers. Encamina's technical interviews lean heavily on real-world situations. For each Microsoft tool you know, prepare a short story: the client problem, your approach, and the outcome. Keep answers concise and structured.

Research Encamina's public work. Look at their blog and published case studies to understand the kinds of clients they serve and the solutions they deliver. Mentioning a specific challenge or approach you read about shows genuine interest.

Practice explaining technical concepts simply. Pick a Microsoft tool you know well and practice explaining it to someone with no technical background. This mirrors the consulting scenarios you may face in the interview.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Encamina's culture centres on continuous learning and knowledge sharing within a Microsoft-focused community. As a Microsoft partner, the company invests in keeping its people current with changes across the Microsoft ecosystem, and employees are generally encouraged to pursue certifications and attend Microsoft community events.

The working environment is collaborative by design. Internal knowledge-sharing sessions, peer learning, and contributions to team documentation are part of how the company operates day to day. This is not a place where people work in isolation; the expectation is that expertise circulates across the team.

The company has a consultancy culture, which means client delivery and professional communication are taken seriously. At the same time, publicly reported employee feedback highlights a supportive management style and a reasonable work-life balance, though individual experiences can vary by project and team.
