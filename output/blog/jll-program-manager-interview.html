01 Overview

Overview

JLL (Jones Lang LaSalle) is a global real estate services company accelerating its technology transformation, building platforms for property management, workplace analytics, and enterprise data. A Technical Program Manager at JLL typically oversees large cross-functional programs, working with engineering teams, business stakeholders, and external vendors across multiple geographies.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracks 313 Technical Program Manager openings across India. JLL alone has 404 open roles across all functions, signalling active hiring. Among TPM openings, Bangalore leads with 41 postings, followed by Delhi (14), Pune (13), Hyderabad (12), Chennai (5), and Mumbai (1).

Interviews typically run across multiple rounds covering behavioral depth, program delivery scenarios, stakeholder management, and technical awareness. This guide covers the questions candidates commonly face and how to prepare for them.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a large-scale program you led end-to-end.

*Situation:* My company was migrating a core property management platform used by multiple regional teams to a new cloud-based system.

*Task:* I was the program manager coordinating delivery across several engineering squads, two vendor teams, and business stakeholders across multiple cities.

*Action:* I set up a weekly steering committee, built a dependency map covering all workstreams, and introduced a RAG (Red-Amber-Green) status dashboard that every team updated each Friday. When one vendor slipped their integration milestone, I escalated early, brought in a backup resource, and restructured the timeline using a phased go-live approach.

*Result:* The first two phases launched on schedule. The third phase had a short delay due to the vendor issue, but the phased approach meant zero disruption to live operations. Leadership cited the RAG dashboard as a model for future programs.

---

Q: Tell us about a time a critical dependency slipped.

*Situation:* We were running a data platform rollout that required a security sign-off from the InfoSec team before moving to UAT.

*Task:* My job was to keep the program timeline intact despite this external dependency sitting outside my direct control.

*Action:* I scheduled a working session with the InfoSec lead to understand their backlog, identified items we could resolve immediately on the engineering side, and proposed a parallel-track review where InfoSec could clear lower-risk components first. I also flagged the risk to the program sponsor well in advance so there were no surprises.

*Result:* Sign-off came significantly sooner than it would have without intervention. The UAT phase started with only a minor adjustment to the overall timeline, and the early escalation built trust with the program sponsor.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you influenced a decision without direct authority.

*Situation:* Two product teams were in disagreement about which integration pattern to use for a shared data pipeline, stalling a program I owned.

*Task:* I needed to break the deadlock without having formal authority over either team.

*Action:* I organised a structured decision session, prepared a one-page comparison of both options covering delivery risk, maintainability, and timeline impact, and invited a senior architect as a neutral voice. I framed the discussion around program outcomes rather than technical preferences.

*Result:* The teams reached consensus in a single session. The program resumed without further delay, and the agreed pattern was later adopted as a standard for two other programs.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions: Keep your Situation and Task brief (2-3 sentences each), spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what the team did), and always close with a concrete Result. Vague endings like 'it went well' lose marks.

Plan-Align-Execute-Review for 'how would you approach' questions: Start by outlining your initial plan, then explain how you would align stakeholders, describe how you would execute with clear checkpoints, and close with how you would review and adapt. This shows structured thinking without sounding robotic.

Identify-Assess-Mitigate-Communicate for risk questions: Show that you spotted the risk early, assessed its potential impact, took a specific mitigation step, and kept the right stakeholders informed throughout. JLL interviewers, candidates report, pay close attention to whether you distinguish between risks you personally owned versus ones you escalated.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

JLL interviewers are looking for program managers who can operate in a matrixed, global environment. Real estate technology delivery often involves legacy systems, large vendor ecosystems, and business stakeholders who think in business outcomes rather than sprint cycles.

Structured thinking: Can you break a complex program into trackable workstreams and communicate status clearly? Interviewers probe whether your planning is reactive or proactive.

Stakeholder communication: Can you translate technical program health into business language for senior leaders? JLL operates at an executive level globally, and your ability to frame issues clearly matters.

Risk instinct: Do you spot risks before they become incidents? Candidates who only describe problems they solved after the fact score lower than those who show early identification.

Vendor management: JLL works with a large ecosystem of technology partners. Demonstrating that you have held vendors accountable while maintaining the relationship is a strong differentiator.

Cross-functional influence: Can you drive alignment and decisions without direct authority? This is central to how TPMs operate at JLL.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Understand JLL's technology context. Review JLL's public investor communications, technology press releases, and any publicly available case studies on their workplace and property technology platforms. Understand what they are building and why, so your interview answers can reference relevant program types.

Week 2: Map your experience to the role. For each responsibility in the job description, prepare one STAR story. Focus on programs with clear scope, measurable outcomes, and cross-functional complexity. Write down the key metrics and decisions from each program so you do not have to reconstruct them under pressure.

Week 3: Practice out loud. Run through your top 6-8 STAR stories. Aim for a focused, concise answer each time. Record yourself if possible and listen back for filler words and clarity. Ask a colleague to play interviewer and push back on your answers.

Day before the interview: Review the job description carefully. Re-read your resume from the interviewer's perspective. Prepare 3-4 thoughtful questions to ask at the end of each round, focused on program culture, tooling, and what success looks like in the first six months.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Saying 'we' when you mean 'I'. Interviewers want to know what you specifically did. Replace 'we delivered' with 'I drove' or 'I decided' wherever you were the one making the call.
  1. Skipping the Result. A STAR answer without a measurable or observable outcome sounds incomplete. Always close with what changed because of your actions.
  1. Being vague about stakeholders. Instead of 'I worked with senior leaders', name the function (VP of Engineering, Head of InfoSec, Regional CFO) to show you operate at the right level.
  1. Pretending everything went smoothly. JLL programs involve real trade-offs and friction. Candidates who claim every project succeeded without challenges sound inexperienced. Pick stories where there was genuine conflict or risk and show how you resolved it.
  1. Not researching JLL specifically. Generic answers about large enterprise programs without connecting to real estate technology, global operations, or JLL's known transformation agenda will land flat. Show you did your homework.
  1. Over-explaining the technical solution. The TPM role is about delivery, not the technical design. Spend your time on how you managed the program, not on the architecture decisions the engineering team made.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does JLL typically have for a TPM role?

Candidates report anywhere from 3 to 5 rounds for senior TPM positions. The process typically starts with an HR screening call, followed by a hiring manager discussion, and then panel or case-based rounds with cross-functional stakeholders. Some roles include a written case study or program scenario exercise. Ask the recruiter at the start of the process about the exact structure so you can prepare accordingly.

Does JLL ask coding questions in TPM interviews?

Technical Program Manager roles at JLL are generally not coding-heavy, candidates report. However, interviewers do probe your understanding of software architecture, APIs, data pipelines, and cloud infrastructure. You should be able to discuss these concepts clearly enough to work credibly with engineering teams, even if you are not writing code yourself. Focus on demonstrating technical fluency, not technical depth.

What salary can I expect for a TPM role at JLL India?

JLL does not publicly publish salary bands for India TPM roles. Glassdoor lists some figures for program managers at JLL India, but publicly reported sample sizes are small. Levels.fyi and industry surveys for senior program managers at large MNCs in India can give you a broader market benchmark. Come prepared with a range based on publicly reported data for your experience level and city, and raise the compensation conversation with the recruiter early.

Is Bangalore the best city for JLL TPM openings?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, Bangalore has 41 of the 313 TPM openings tracked across India, making it the top city for this role. Delhi follows with 14 openings and Pune with 13. JLL also has a presence in Hyderabad and Chennai. If you are open to relocation or hybrid arrangements, checking openings across all these cities will give you the broadest set of options.

How important is real estate domain knowledge for a TPM at JLL?

Candidates report that deep real estate domain knowledge is not typically a hard requirement, but familiarity with enterprise platforms, facility management tools, or workplace technology is a strong differentiator. JLL values program managers who can ramp up on a new domain quickly and translate business needs into delivery outcomes. In your interview, demonstrate how you have done this in past roles, even in unrelated industries.

What tools and methodologies should I know for a JLL TPM interview?

Interviewers typically ask about experience with program tracking tools such as Jira, Smartsheet, or Microsoft Project, as well as your approach to Agile and hybrid delivery. Being able to describe how you adapted your methodology to fit the team or program context is more valuable than claiming deep expertise in one specific tool. JLL programs span both Agile engineering teams and more traditional business project structures, so flexibility matters.
