01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Typeface is an enterprise AI platform that helps brands generate on-brand content at scale. The company sits at the intersection of generative AI, brand personalization, and marketing technology, making it one of the more interesting AI-native startups to join if you work in engineering, product, design, or go-to-market roles.

As of July 2026, Typeface has 19 open roles listed across its hiring channels. Positions span engineering (AI/ML, backend, frontend), product management, design, sales, and customer success. The company is in a growth phase, so hiring tends to be deliberate: they look for people who can build and iterate quickly in a fast-moving AI landscape.

For Indian candidates, most roles are remote-friendly or based across their distributed team. Roles requiring US presence will say so explicitly in the job description. Check the careers page for the latest location details before applying.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Typeface's interview process is fairly standard for an AI-focused startup: a screening call, one or more technical rounds, and a final cross-functional conversation. The exact number of rounds varies by role and seniority, but most candidates report a process that runs across two to three weeks from first contact to offer.

Here is a typical stage-by-stage breakdown:

StageWhat happens
Application screeningResume and portfolio review by the recruiting team. Strong matches get a recruiter call within a week or two.
Recruiter callA brief intro conversation covering your background, motivations, and role fit. Good time to ask about team structure and comp range.
Technical interviewsTwo to three rounds depending on role. Engineers tackle coding problems and system design. PM and design roles get case-style or portfolio reviews.
System design (senior roles)Senior engineers and architects typically get a dedicated design round covering scalable systems or AI pipeline architecture.
Final roundA cross-functional conversation with a hiring manager or leadership. Covers your experience in depth, how you collaborate, and your interest in the company's mission.

Feedback turnaround between rounds is commonly cited as a few days to a week. If you have not heard back after a round, a polite follow-up email to your recruiter is perfectly fine.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Typeface builds AI products for enterprise customers, which means they care a lot about reliability, scalability, and user trust. Here is what tends to matter across roles:

For engineers: Comfort with large language models, generative AI APIs, and production ML systems. Backend engineers need strong fundamentals in distributed systems. Frontend engineers benefit from experience building rich, interactive editors or content tools. Familiarity with AWS or GCP is a plus.

For product managers: Experience shipping B2B or SaaS products, ideally with an AI or content angle. They want PMs who can translate vague enterprise needs into clear product specs and who are comfortable working directly with engineering and design.

For go-to-market roles: Enterprise sales and customer success experience matters, especially if you have sold to marketing or brand teams. Understanding AI products well enough to demo and explain them confidently is a strong differentiator.

Across all roles: They consistently look for people who are curious about AI, move fast without breaking things, and communicate well across functions. Typeface is still shaping its culture, so early joiners are expected to bring energy and ownership, not just execute a playbook.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product first. Typeface's core offering is brand-personalized AI content generation. Before any interview, sign up for a trial or watch demo videos so you can speak to specific features and use cases. Interviewers notice when candidates have actually used the product.

Brush up on generative AI basics. Even for non-ML roles, you should be able to explain how large language models work at a high level, what prompt engineering means, and how AI content tools handle brand voice. This shows you can speak the company's language.

Prepare your 'why AI, why now' story. Typeface hires people who are genuinely excited about generative AI as a category. Have a clear, honest answer for why you want to work on AI products at this stage of the market, not just why you want any job.

For engineering interviews: Practice system design with an AI or content pipeline flavour. Think through how you would design a system that takes a brand brief and generates hundreds of content variants reliably. Coding practice covering data structures and algorithms is still relevant for the coding rounds.

For PM and design interviews: Come with a case study of a product decision you owned end-to-end. Be ready to walk through your research process, how you prioritized features, and what you would do differently. Enterprise product experience is a strong signal.

Ask smart questions. In the final round, questions about how the team measures success, how AI features are evaluated for quality, and what the biggest open product or engineering problems are will land much better than generic questions about perks.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Typeface was founded by Abhay Parasnis, former CTO of Adobe, along with other senior product and engineering leaders. That lineage shapes the culture: there is a premium on craft, design quality, and enterprise-grade thinking, combined with the speed and ambition of an early-stage company.

The team is relatively small and distributed, which means communication has to be intentional. Async writing skills matter. People are expected to be self-directed and not wait for detailed instructions on every task.

Typeface publicly emphasizes responsible AI, which shows up in how they think about content quality, bias in generated outputs, and data privacy for enterprise customers. If you care about building AI thoughtfully and not just quickly, that value resonates here.

For Indian candidates specifically: Typeface has hired engineers and product people based in India, and the company is comfortable with distributed collaboration across time zones. The interview process itself is fully remote, so you will not need to travel for any round.
