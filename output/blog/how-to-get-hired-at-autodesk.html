01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Autodesk builds software that architects, engineers, filmmakers, and product designers rely on every day. Its flagship products, including AutoCAD, Revit, Maya, and a growing suite of cloud-based design tools, are used across industries worldwide. The company has a strong presence in India, with offices in Pune and Hyderabad handling core engineering, product development, and customer-facing work.

As of mid-2026, Autodesk has 99 open roles listed on its careers page. Positions span software engineering, product management, data science, technical support, and sales. Whether you are a seasoned professional or a recent graduate, Autodesk hires across experience levels. The company has been investing in cloud platforms, AI, and data infrastructure, so demand for engineers in these areas tends to stay strong.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The Autodesk interview process typically follows a multi-stage structure. Here is what most candidates report, though exact steps can vary by role and team.

StageWhat to Expect
1. Application and ScreeningYou apply online, and a recruiter reviews your resume. If shortlisted, you get a call to discuss your background, salary expectations, and interest in the role.
2. Technical AssessmentFor engineering roles, expect a coding test or take-home assignment covering data structures, algorithms, and sometimes system design. Non-technical roles may have a case study or written exercise instead.
3. Technical InterviewsOne to three rounds with engineers or hiring managers. Questions focus on problem-solving, coding (for tech roles), and domain knowledge. You may be asked to walk through past projects in detail.
4. Behavioural / Culture RoundA conversation about how you work in teams, handle disagreements, and approach challenges. Autodesk values collaboration, so expect questions on real teamwork scenarios.
5. Hiring Manager / Final RoundA wrap-up discussion with the hiring manager or a senior leader covering career goals, role fit, and any remaining questions from either side.

The entire process usually takes a few weeks from first contact to offer. Recruiters at Autodesk are generally responsive, so do not hesitate to follow up if you have not heard back after a reasonable time.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Strong technical fundamentals. For engineering roles, Autodesk looks for solid skills in data structures, algorithms, and system design. Familiarity with cloud technologies (AWS, Azure) and modern development practices is a plus.

Product curiosity. Candidates who have actually used Autodesk products, or who understand the problems they solve for designers and engineers, stand out. Showing genuine interest in the domain matters.

Collaboration and communication. Autodesk teams work across geographies. Being able to communicate clearly, give and receive feedback, and work well in cross-functional groups is important at every level.

Growth mindset. The company looks for people who are eager to learn, adapt to new technologies, and take ownership of their work. If you can point to examples of picking up something new and applying it quickly, that helps.

Customer empathy. Even in purely technical roles, understanding how end users (architects, engineers, filmmakers) interact with the product is valued. This is especially true for roles in product management and design.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the product suite. Spend time exploring AutoCAD, Revit, or Maya through free trials or student licenses. Understanding what Autodesk builds, and who uses it, gives you a real edge in interviews.

Brush up on fundamentals. For software engineering roles, practice coding problems focused on data structures, algorithms, and system design. Platforms like LeetCode and GeeksforGeeks are your best friends here.

Prepare STAR-format stories. For behavioural rounds, have three to four stories ready using the Situation, Task, Action, Result format. Focus on teamwork, handling ambiguity, and delivering results under pressure.

Know the company's direction. Read up on Autodesk's recent moves in cloud, AI, and sustainability. Mentioning specific initiatives during your interview shows you have done your homework.

Ask thoughtful questions. At the end of each round, ask about the team's current projects, tech stack, or biggest challenges. Good questions signal genuine interest and help you evaluate whether the role is right for you.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Autodesk positions itself as a company that values impact, inclusion, and innovation. Employees frequently mention a culture of flexibility, with hybrid and remote work options available for many roles in India. Teams tend to be collaborative rather than hierarchical, and there is an emphasis on giving engineers ownership of their projects.

The company is vocal about sustainability and social impact, building tools that help customers design more energy-efficient buildings and reduce waste in manufacturing. If these themes resonate with you, they make for strong talking points in interviews.

Internal mobility is another highlight. Autodesk encourages employees to explore different teams and roles over time, which is worth asking about during the hiring process. Many employees in India have moved across functions or geographies within the company.
