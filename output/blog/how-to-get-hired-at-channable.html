01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Channable is a Netherlands-based SaaS company that builds feed management and PPC automation software for e-commerce businesses. Their platform helps online retailers sync product listings to marketplaces, comparison engines, and ad networks, cutting down the manual work that would otherwise take hours. Founded in Utrecht, the company has grown into a well-regarded name in the European e-commerce tech space.

As of mid-2026, Channable has 19 open roles across engineering, product, marketing, sales, and customer success. The openings span both technical and non-technical functions, so whether you are a software developer, a data analyst, or a customer success manager, there is likely something worth exploring. Most roles are based in the Netherlands, though Channable has historically been open to remote candidates for select positions.

Competition for seats at a focused SaaS company like Channable is real, so a targeted, well-prepared application matters more than a generic one.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Channable does not publish a step-by-step breakdown of its hiring process, but candidate accounts and job descriptions point to a fairly standard pattern for a European SaaS company. Here is what most applicants report experiencing, in order.

Application and screening. You submit your resume and a cover letter where asked. A recruiter or hiring manager reviews your materials. Customising your application to the role is not optional. A generic CV with no context rarely makes it through.

Recruiter call. If your profile is a fit, someone from the talent team will reach out for an introductory conversation. This call is usually short and exploratory. They want to hear your career story, understand what you are looking for, and explain what the role involves. Come prepared with a clear, honest summary of your background.

Skills or technical assessment. For engineering roles, this typically means a coding challenge or take-home project. For roles in marketing, operations, or customer success, it might be a short case study or a practical exercise. This stage is about showing, not just telling, that you can do the work.

Team interviews. You will likely meet the hiring manager and a colleague or two. Expect questions about past experience, how you handle specific situations, and how you work with others. Channable teams are cross-functional, so interviewers often probe for collaboration and communication skills alongside hard skills.

Final conversation or offer. Some candidates report a brief final call with a senior leader before the offer is extended. Once the team aligns, the recruiter walks you through compensation, start date, and next steps.

The whole process can span a few weeks, depending on team availability and how quickly interviews are scheduled.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Channable wants people who take ownership, communicate clearly, and have genuine curiosity about e-commerce and software. A few qualities show up consistently across their job descriptions and candidate accounts.

Ownership and initiative. This is not a company where you wait for detailed instructions. If you spot a problem, they expect you to either fix it or raise it clearly. Bring concrete examples of times you acted without being asked.

Clear, direct communication. Channable teams work across engineering, product, and customer-facing functions. The ability to explain complex things simply, whether in writing or in conversation, is valued at every level.

E-commerce or SaaS fluency. You do not need years of specialist experience, but showing you understand how product feeds, marketplace integrations, or performance marketing campaigns work will set you apart. Candidates who can speak the language of their customers tend to do well.

Collaboration across functions. Expect to work closely with people outside your own team. Engineers who understand customer problems and marketers who can talk to product managers are consistently valued.

Comfort with ambiguity. Priorities shift and processes evolve at a growing company. Showing that you can update your approach when things change, without losing momentum, is a meaningful signal.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know the product before the first call. Explore Channable's platform through demos, their documentation, or a free trial if available. Being able to reference a specific feature or use case in the interview shows you have done more than skim the homepage.

Map your experience to the job description. Go through the requirements line by line and match each one to a real example from your background. Use a simple structure: what the situation was, what you did, and what the outcome was. Vague answers are easy to give and easy to forget.

Prepare for the assessment round. Engineering candidates should be comfortable with Python and working with APIs or data pipelines, as these are relevant to Channable's technical work. For non-technical roles, practice structuring a recommendation with a clear rationale and supporting logic.

Know why Channable, not just why SaaS. Generic motivation answers do not land well. Look at who their customers are, how they compare to other feed management tools in the market, and what they have shipped recently. Specificity shows genuine interest.

Bring thoughtful questions. Prepare questions for each interview stage, not just the final round. Ask about how the team makes decisions, what success looks like in the first few months, or how they handle disagreements. Good questions signal that you are already thinking like a future team member.

If you are applying from India to a remote role, highlight your experience with async collaboration and written communication. Both matter to distributed European teams.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Channable operates with a relatively flat structure and a direct, open communication style typical of Dutch tech companies. Hierarchy is minimal, and people are expected to share feedback honestly, including upward. Waiting to be told what to do is not the norm here.

The team is driven by genuine care for customer outcomes. Because their platform directly affects how e-commerce businesses run their ads and manage inventory, there is a real sense of responsibility in the work. Product and engineering teams stay close to customers, keeping the feedback loop short.

Work-life balance is taken seriously. Like most European technology companies, Channable does not celebrate overwork. Meetings have a purpose, and async communication is a regular part of how teams stay aligned, especially across time zones.

For Indian candidates, particularly those applying to remote positions, it helps to demonstrate comfort with structured async communication, a habit of writing clearly rather than defaulting to calls, and familiarity with European professional norms where directness is a sign of respect, not rudeness.
