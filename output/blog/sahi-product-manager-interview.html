03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a product you shipped that did not perform as expected.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we launched a push notification feature to nudge users to complete their KYC after starting a loan application. We expected strong completion rates, consistent with what industry surveys commonly cite for similar notification campaigns.

*Task:* I was the PM responsible for the feature from spec through post-launch review. When numbers came in flat after two weeks, I owned the investigation.

*Action:* I pulled the funnel data and found that most users who tapped the notification were dropping at the document upload step, not before it. I spoke with a small sample of users and found the camera permission prompt was confusing them. I worked with engineering to simplify the permission copy and added a one-screen explainer before the upload step. We shipped the fix in the next sprint.

*Result:* KYC completion improved in the following two weeks. More importantly, I made testing the full journey on a low-end device a standard step before every future feature launch.

Q: How have you worked with compliance or legal teams when building a fintech product?

*Situation:* We were building a feature that showed a personalised credit limit to users before they completed their application. Legal flagged it as potentially misleading if the displayed amount differed from the final approved amount.

*Task:* My job was to find a solution that kept the feature commercially valuable while satisfying the compliance requirement.

*Action:* I set up a working session with legal, design, and the head of credit risk. Instead of defending the original design, I asked them to define exactly what language would make them comfortable. We agreed on showing a range rather than a single number, with a clear note that final approval was subject to verification. I wrote the spec with their exact approved copy so there was no back-and-forth during review.

*Result:* The feature launched on schedule. The process we used became a template for future compliance reviews, cutting the delays our team had typically seen on earlier projects.

Q: Describe a situation where you had to convince a senior stakeholder to change direction.

*Situation:* The VP of Growth wanted to run an aggressive re-engagement campaign targeting lapsed borrowers with pre-approved loan offers. I had data suggesting a portion of those users had missed payments due to income shocks, not disengagement.

*Task:* I needed to make the case that a blanket push could hurt both users and the loan portfolio, without appearing to block a business priority.

*Action:* I prepared a one-page analysis splitting lapsed users into two segments based on repayment history. One segment (users with a clean history who had gone quiet) was a strong target. The second segment (users who had previously defaulted) would likely produce higher delinquency if re-activated with pre-approval messaging. I proposed a modified campaign targeting only the first segment, with a different message for the second.

*Result:* The VP approved the segmented approach. The campaign ran on schedule, and the default rate on re-activated users came in below our overall portfolio average, which I shared in the following month's review.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions, start by clarifying the goal (is this about growth, retention, or reducing drop-off?), identify the user (who is the borrower, what device are they on, what is their financial literacy level?), list the key pain points, propose two to three solutions, and recommend one with a clear reason. Avoid jumping to a solution before you have framed the problem.

For metric drop questions, use a top-down breakdown: start at the headline metric, split it into components (volume and conversion rate, for example), find which branch fell, then look at internal causes (a product change, a bug, a recent campaign) versus external causes (seasonality, competitor action, a macro event). Always ask whether anything changed in the product or environment around the time of the drop before diving deeper.

For prioritisation questions, use a simple impact-effort or RICE-style reasoning out loud. State your assumptions, rank the options, and explain the trade-offs clearly. At a startup like Sahi, also factor in regulatory risk and user trust, which standard prioritisation templates often leave out.

For stakeholder influence questions, follow STAR but make sure the 'Action' step shows that you first sought to understand the other person's concern before presenting your counter-view. Interviewers at growth-stage companies want evidence that you can bring people along, not just win an argument.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Sahi interviewers, based on what candidates typically report, look for a few specific qualities beyond standard PM skills.

Fintech context awareness is table stakes. Know how digital lending works in practice: KYC, credit bureau checks, disbursement, EMI collection, and default management. You do not need to be a credit risk expert, but these terms should not be new to you on the day of the interview.

Empathy for the Bharat user carries real weight. Sahi's borrower base commonly includes first-time credit users in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, using mid-range or entry-level Android phones. Interviewers notice when a candidate designs solutions that assume a high-literacy, urban, premium-device user without questioning that assumption.

Data fluency without data paralysis. Talk about metrics, funnels, and experimentation confidently, but show that you can also make a call when data is incomplete or ambiguous. Defaulting to 'I would run an A/B test' for every scenario reads as hesitation rather than rigour.

Speed with responsibility. Fintech moves fast but operates in a regulated space. Interviewers want to see that you take compliance seriously without treating every constraint as a blocker.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain and product context

Start by experiencing Sahi's product directly. Download the app, go through the onboarding and loan application flow as far as you can without actually applying, and note every point of friction. Read up on the digital lending space in India, including RBI guidelines on digital lending, and understand how credit-line products differ from traditional personal loans.

Days 4-5: Metrics and analytical prep

Practise walking through a funnel metric drop from first principles. Pick a fintech funnel (application, KYC, credit check, disbursal, repayment) and break it down out loud until the reasoning feels fluent. Build your own structure for metric-drop questions so you are not improvising on the day.

Days 6-7: Behavioural prep

Prepare four to five STAR stories from your own experience. Cover at least: a product that underperformed, a time you used data to change a decision, a time you navigated a compliance or legal concern, and a time you influenced a senior stakeholder. Write each story in bullet form and practise saying it out loud, aiming for two to three minutes per story.

Final days: Mock and sharpen

Do at least one mock interview with someone who can give honest feedback, or record yourself and review it. Also revisit salary benchmarks so you walk into any compensation conversation informed: PM roles in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate level to 40-60 LPA for Senior PM roles, based on the knok jobradar dataset.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a generic PM interview. Sahi is a fintech company with a specific user base and regulatory environment. Candidates who bring only e-commerce or SaaS examples without connecting them to credit or payments context tend to struggle. Practise translating your past experience into fintech analogies before you walk in.

Ignoring the low-end Android user. Many candidates design solutions that assume strong connectivity, a large screen, or high financial literacy. In a product discussion for Sahi, always ask about the target user's device and internet reliability before proposing a solution.

Over-relying on data to avoid a decision. 'I would run an A/B test' is a valid answer to some scenarios, but saying it to every question reads as decision-avoidance. Show that you can make a reasoned call even when data is incomplete or the timeline does not allow a full experiment.

Not knowing what Sahi actually does. Some candidates describe Sahi's product incorrectly or confuse it with another brand. Spend time on the app and their public-facing content before the interview so your examples feel grounded.

Vague STAR results. The most common issue is ending a story with 'the stakeholder was happy' or 'the feature launched.' Practise closing every story with a concrete, honest outcome. Partial wins with clear learnings are perfectly acceptable to share.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Sahi PM interview typically have?

Candidates typically report going through multiple rounds for PM roles at Sahi, though the exact number varies by seniority and team. The process commonly includes a product thinking or case discussion, an analytical or metrics conversation, and a round with senior leadership or a cross-functional stakeholder. Always confirm the expected structure with your recruiter before each stage so you are not caught off-guard.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Sahi?

Based on the knok jobradar dataset, PM compensation in India ranges from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, and 40-60 LPA for Senior PM roles. The specific offer from Sahi will depend on your experience, current CTC, and the level of the role you are interviewing for. Use Glassdoor and levels.fyi to benchmark fintech PM pay at growth-stage startups and walk into compensation discussions with a clear number in mind.

Do I need a fintech background to clear a PM interview at Sahi?

A formal fintech background is not always required, but you need to show you understand the lending and credit space well enough to contribute from day one. Candidates from e-commerce, SaaS, and consumer apps have cleared fintech PM interviews by doing focused domain preparation beforehand. Understand KYC, credit bureau integration, disbursement flows, and EMI collections at a working level before you go in.

How should I prepare for the product case round?

Pick a specific fintech problem, such as improving repayment rates for gig workers or reducing KYC drop-off on low-end devices, and practise solving it end-to-end out loud. Use a clear structure: define the user, state the goal, list pain points, propose solutions, and recommend one with a reason. Interviewers at startups like Sahi notice when candidates flag compliance or data privacy constraints proactively rather than only after being prompted.

What is Sahi looking for in a PM hire right now?

With 3 open PM roles currently listed, Sahi appears to be in an active growth phase and is likely looking for PMs who can own a product area with limited oversight. Interviewers will want to see evidence of ownership, cross-functional collaboration, and comfort with ambiguity. STAR stories that show initiative and end with honest outcomes, rather than perfectly polished successes, will carry more weight than a rehearsed interview style.

How long does the full Sahi PM hiring process typically take?

Candidates typically report the process taking two to four weeks from the first round to an offer, though startup timelines can move faster or slower depending on team urgency. It is reasonable to ask your recruiter for an expected timeline after your first round so you can manage other applications or competing offers without unnecessary pressure.
