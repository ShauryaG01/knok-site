01 Overview

Overview

Greytip Software builds greytHR, one of India's most widely adopted HR and payroll platforms for small and medium businesses. As of July 2026, the company has 78 open roles, reflecting active expansion across product and technology functions. PM interviews at Greytip typically assess your ability to think like an HR executive at a growing SME, someone juggling payroll accuracy, leave tracking, and statutory compliance without a large IT team behind them.

Candidates report a mix of product sense rounds, case-based discussions, and behavioural conversations, though the exact format varies by role level. Greytip's product scope covers payroll, attendance, leave, expenses, and compliance, so familiarity with how these workflows connect is a meaningful advantage going in.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you simplified a complex workflow for a non-technical user.

*Situation:* At my previous company, our expense reimbursement feature required employees to fill in several fields per claim, attach a receipt, and go through two approval levels. Usage was low and support tickets were high.

*Task:* I was asked to improve the submission rate without changing the approval policy.

*Action:* I ran user interviews with finance and operations staff and found that multiple fields could be pre-filled from the employee profile, and one field existed only because of a legacy audit requirement that had since been dropped. I worked with engineering to auto-populate those fields and removed the redundant one. I also added a photo-capture shortcut on mobile so attaching receipts took one tap instead of navigating through a file picker.

*Result:* Submission rate improved in the first month, and support tickets on that workflow dropped. The finance team reported faster processing because claims came in more complete.

---

Q: How do you handle conflicting priorities between a high-revenue client request and a roadmap item that benefits many smaller customers?

*Situation:* A key client requested a custom leave policy rule specific to their organisation. Our roadmap already included a configurable leave rule engine intended to serve a wide range of SME customers.

*Task:* I needed to respond to the client without derailing the broader roadmap.

*Action:* I mapped the client's request against our planned rule engine and found that the general feature would cover most of what they needed, with one additional configuration option. I proposed a phased approach: ship the rule engine on schedule, then deliver their specific toggle in the follow-up release, backed by a written date commitment. I aligned the sales team and the client on this plan before development started.

*Result:* The client accepted the phased plan. The rule engine shipped on schedule and served a wide customer base. The client's configuration was delivered in the next sprint as committed.

---

Q: How would you define and track success for a new attendance device integration?

*Situation:* My team was building an integration between our HR platform and a biometric device provider used by manufacturing clients.

*Task:* I needed to define what success looked like before development began.

*Action:* I spoke with HR managers who had attempted similar integrations elsewhere and struggled. Their top pain points were sync delays and time spent correcting attendance errors manually. I defined three success metrics: daily sync completion rate, time HR spent on manual corrections each month, and setup time for a new company going live. I also set a qualitative bar: an HR manager should complete setup without calling support.

*Result:* Having these metrics upfront helped the team make clear tradeoffs during development. At launch, most customers completed setup quickly, and support calls for this integration remained low in the first quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

RICE for prioritisation: When asked how you would prioritise a feature list, score each item on Reach (how many SME customers are affected), Impact (how much it changes a core workflow like payroll or leave), Confidence (how well you understand the problem), and Effort (engineering time). At Greytip, Reach carries extra weight because the customer base is broad and diverse.

Jobs-to-be-Done for design questions: Frame every design question around what the user is actually trying to accomplish. An SME HR manager is not 'managing payroll', they are trying to close the month without a compliance error and without staying late. This lens stops you from over-engineering and keeps the solution grounded.

Structured tradeoff for build-vs-improve questions: When asked whether to build a new feature or improve an existing one, structure your answer in three steps: identify the problem clearly, check what signals exist about where users get stuck, then pick the option that resolves it faster with less risk. Avoid picking a side before establishing the problem.

Metrics pyramid for success questions: Start with the business outcome (retention, expansion revenue), then the product metric (feature adoption, error rate), then the operational signal (support tickets, time-on-task). Interviewers appreciate candidates who connect product activity to business results rather than treating metrics as standalone numbers.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain fluency, not memorised facts: greytHR touches Indian payroll, PF, ESI, and leave policies. You do not need to be an HR professional, but you should speak sensibly about why compliance updates are hard to ship quickly and why SME needs differ meaningfully from enterprise ones.

Empathy for the actual user: The greytHR user is often an HR executive at a small company who juggles multiple roles and has limited time for complex software. Interviewers want to see that you think about their anxiety around payroll errors and their low tolerance for confusing interfaces.

Structured, concise communication: Candidates report that Greytip interviewers value answers that get to the point quickly. Lead with your conclusion, then support it. A long preamble before your recommendation signals unclear thinking, not thoroughness.

Comfort with limited data: Unlike PMs at large consumer platforms, you may be working with smaller data samples. Show that you can make reasonable decisions under uncertainty and validate assumptions quickly after release rather than waiting for perfect information.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the product. Sign up for the greytHR free tier or watch their onboarding content. Map the core modules (payroll, attendance, leave, compliance) and note where the experience feels smooth versus where it creates friction. Write down three specific things you would improve and the reasoning behind each.

Build domain context. Read about Indian payroll basics: PF, ESI, TDS, and professional tax. You do not need to be an expert, but you should understand why compliance is a first-class product constraint at Greytip, not just a legal footnote that engineering handles separately.

Prepare STAR stories. Cover at least five scenarios: simplifying a complex workflow for a non-technical user, handling conflicting stakeholder asks, defining and tracking success metrics, prioritising a roadmap under pressure, and shipping within a constrained timeline. Rehearse each out loud and keep answers under three minutes.

Practice product design cases. Work through one case per day in the week before your interview. Use Jobs-to-be-Done framing and always close with a clear metric. Ask a peer to play the interviewer and give you honest feedback on whether your answer is crisp.

Stay current on Greytip. Follow their blog and LinkedIn page for recent product launches or company updates. Being able to reference a specific recent development naturally signals genuine interest rather than rehearsed enthusiasm.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Greytip like an enterprise SaaS company. Many candidates default to enterprise-PM thinking: complex permission hierarchies, long deployment cycles, and heavy integrations. Greytip's core customers are SMEs who need quick setup and minimal hand-holding. Keep your solutions grounded in simplicity and speed to value.

Ignoring compliance as a product constraint. PMs who treat statutory compliance as a legal footnote miss a core reality of HR tech in India. Interviewers will notice if you approach a compliance update the same way you approach a UI refresh, because the risk profile and urgency are fundamentally different.

Using jargon without grounding it. Saying 'we need to improve activation' without explaining what activation means in the context of an HR platform leaves the interviewer guessing. Define your terms and tie them to specific user behaviour you can observe or measure.

Skipping the 'why' in prioritisation. Many candidates score features without first connecting them to a user problem or business outcome. Starting with a scoring matrix for features you have not tied to real pain points looks mechanical and signals shallow product thinking.

Jumping straight to a solution in case rounds. Candidates report that skipping the scoping step is a common failure. Take the first minute to clarify the user, the constraint, and the success metric before you propose anything. Interviewers want to see structured thinking, not the fastest answer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Greytip PM interview typically have?

Candidates report anywhere from three to five rounds, typically including a recruiter call, a product sense or case session, a behavioural discussion, and a final conversation with senior leadership. The exact structure varies by role level and the hiring team. Ask your recruiter for the specific format before you start preparing.

Do I need HR domain experience to get a PM role at Greytip?

Direct HR domain experience is a plus but not always required. Candidates report that strong product thinking combined with genuine curiosity about HR workflows can offset a lack of formal domain background. That said, invest time before interviews to understand PF, ESI, TDS, and leave management basics, because these topics come up frequently in product and case discussions.

What salary can I expect as a PM at Greytip?

Greytip does not publicly confirm its compensation bands, but broader market context is helpful. Based on industry surveys and candidate-reported data on Glassdoor and levels.fyi, Associate PMs in Indian product roles typically see 12-20 LPA, PMs with 3-6 years of experience see 24-40 LPA, and Senior PMs see 40-60 LPA. Actual offers depend on your level, specific skills, and negotiation.

Does Greytip ask technical questions in PM interviews?

Candidates report that Greytip PM interviews focus on product thinking and domain knowledge rather than deep technical content. You may be asked how an API integration works conceptually or how you would approach a data sync issue at a high level, but you are unlikely to be asked to write code. A solid understanding of how SaaS products are built and deployed is sufficient.

How should I prepare for a product design case at Greytip?

Pick a real pain point in the HR or payroll space, frame it through Jobs-to-be-Done thinking, and propose a solution with clear tradeoffs and success metrics. Open every case by clarifying the user and the key constraint before designing anything. Greytip serves SMEs, so keep your solution simple and fast to adopt rather than enterprise-grade complex.

Is Greytip a good company for PM career growth?

Greytip operates in a stable and growing HR tech segment with a large SME customer base across India. Candidates report meaningful product scope and room to own roadmap areas. As with any company, growth depends on your team, your manager, and the ownership you are given. It is worth asking directly about team size and roadmap autonomy during your interview rounds.
