01 Overview

Overview

Nutanix builds cloud infrastructure software, best known for its hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI) platform, Prism management console, and Nutanix Cloud Platform. Product Managers here work at the intersection of deep technical architecture and enterprise buyer decisions. Your customer is typically an IT admin or CTO, not an everyday consumer, and that shapes everything from how you design features to how you talk about success metrics.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural and case questions. Keep Situation and Task brief and spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Q: Tell me about a time you prioritised features when different customers wanted opposite things.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built a storage management product used by both small IT teams and large enterprise accounts. SMBs wanted a wizard-driven UI while enterprise admins wanted full API access and CLI controls.

*Task:* I had to decide which direction to take for the next quarter without losing either segment.

*Action:* I interviewed customers from both groups, mapped their jobs-to-be-done, and scored requests against revenue impact, retention risk, and engineering cost. I proposed a layered approach where a guided wizard sat on top of full API access, so neither group lost capability.

*Result:* The feature shipped the following release. Enterprise NPS improved for that cohort and we saw no churn from SMB accounts.

---

Q: Describe a time you made a product decision with incomplete data.

*Situation:* We were evaluating whether to add native Kubernetes integration to our platform. Most customers had not yet adopted Kubernetes in production, so usage data was thin.

*Task:* I needed a build-vs-wait recommendation for the VP of Product within two weeks.

*Action:* I combined internal signals with publicly available Kubernetes adoption surveys and conversations with enterprise architects at customer accounts. I built a simple expected-value model across low, mid, and high adoption scenarios and recommended an MVP approach to test demand before committing full engineering resources.

*Result:* Leadership approved the MVP. Within two quarters, three enterprise pilots signed up, validating the investment before we scaled it.

---

Q: How did you handle a disagreement between product and engineering on priorities?

*Situation:* My engineering lead wanted a full quarter for infrastructure refactoring. I had committed to a customer that a key feature would ship in that same window.

*Task:* I needed a path that addressed both the technical debt concern and the customer commitment.

*Action:* I ran a scoping session to separate blocking refactor work from 'nice to have' cleanup. I proposed prioritising the blocking items, deferring the rest, and protecting capacity for the customer feature.

*Result:* We shipped the feature on time, resolved the most critical debt, and documented the deferred backlog. The engineering lead felt heard and the customer renewed.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For prioritisation questions: Score options across business impact, customer value, engineering effort, and strategic fit. Show your reasoning aloud, not just your conclusion.

For product design questions: Start with the user. For Nutanix, the user is almost always an IT admin or DevOps engineer. Design for their workflow and mental model, not a consumer app experience.

For metrics questions: Separate input metrics (feature adoption, onboarding completion) from output metrics (retention, expansion revenue). Name a north-star metric and explain what core value it captures.

For competitive questions: Anchor on the customer segment where Nutanix wins and explain why. Avoid positioning competitors as simply worse. Interviewers appreciate candidates who understand enterprise buying dynamics and total cost of ownership.

For behavioural questions: Keep Situation and Task to two or three sentences each. Spend the majority of your answer on Action and Result.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that Nutanix PM interviewers evaluate a few specific signals.

Technical comfort without needing to code. You should understand what HCI means, why enterprises sometimes choose on-prem over public cloud, and how storage, compute, and networking relate at a conceptual level. Vague answers on infrastructure topics tend to be penalised.

Enterprise empathy. Customers are IT teams managing large deployments. Defaulting to consumer PM thinking (virality, growth loops, app ratings) without adapting signals a mismatch with the role.

Decisiveness under ambiguity. Interviewers want to see you move forward when data is limited. Paralysis or endless hedging are red flags.

Clear communication. Nutanix PMs work with distributed engineering teams and senior enterprise buyers. Your ability to explain complex tradeoffs simply is tested throughout every round.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build product and market context. Read Nutanix's public product pages for AOS, Prism, and Nutanix Cloud Platform. Find publicly available HCI market overviews to understand how Nutanix positions against competitors. Get comfortable with the basics of hypervisors and virtualisation if these are new to you.

Week 2: Practice your answers. Work through the twelve questions listed above. Record yourself and listen back. Tighten STAR answers to under three minutes each. Cut filler and generic statements.

Week 3: Run a mock case. Pick one prioritisation or design question and prepare a full structured answer with a framework, clear tradeoffs, and a recommendation. Have someone challenge your assumptions.

Before each round: Map your experience to the specific job description. Look up your interviewers and note whether they are engineers, PMs, or business leaders so you can adjust your language. Prepare two or three sharp questions about the team roadmap and how success is measured in the role.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Nutanix like a consumer product company. Talking about DAU, virality, or app-store ratings without adapting signals you have not done your homework. The buyer is an enterprise IT team with very different priorities.

Being vague about the product. Saying 'I would research the market' without showing you already understand the HCI space gets candidates filtered early. Do your homework before round one.

Citing frameworks by name without applying them. Dropping 'RICE' or 'JTBD' without actually using the framework comes across as shallow. Show the thinking, not just the label.

Weak results in STAR answers. Ending with 'the team was happy' without a business or customer outcome leaves interviewers wanting more. Quantify where you can. If you cannot, explain the qualitative impact clearly.

Not preparing questions for your interviewers. Nutanix interviewers typically leave time for your questions. Asking about roadmap challenges or how success is measured in your first few months signals genuine PM-level curiosity.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Nutanix PM interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to five rounds. This commonly includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, a product case exercise, a technical round with engineers, and a final leadership or cross-functional round. The exact structure varies by team and level. Ask your recruiter to outline the full process at the start so you can prepare accordingly.

Do I need a technical background to become a PM at Nutanix?

A deep engineering background is not required, but technical comfort is important. Nutanix products sit in the infrastructure layer, so you should understand HCI, virtualisation, and cloud architecture at a conceptual level. Candidates with prior experience in enterprise software, storage, or networking typically find the technical rounds more comfortable. If your background is purely consumer, invest extra prep time on infrastructure concepts before applying.

What PM salary can I expect at Nutanix in India?

Based on Glassdoor and publicly reported data, PM compensation at Nutanix broadly tracks market ranges: Associate PMs typically see 12-20 LPA, mid-level PMs with three to six years of experience see 24-40 LPA, and Senior PMs see 40-60 LPA. These are market-wide figures. Your actual offer will depend on your experience level, the specific team, and how you negotiate.

Does Nutanix give a take-home assignment or live product case?

Candidates report that a product case is common, though the format varies by team. Some use a live case during the interview where you work through a problem with the interviewer present. Others send a take-home prompt in advance. Prepare for both formats by practising how to structure an answer with a clear user definition, a prioritised problem space, a proposed solution, and measurable success metrics.

Which cities in India is Nutanix actively hiring PMs in?

Based on current knok jobradar data, Bangalore has the highest concentration of open PM roles, followed by Delhi. Nutanix also has some presence in Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad, and Chennai. If you are open to relocation, Bangalore offers the most options right now. Listings shift across quarters, so check active openings regularly rather than relying on a snapshot.

How should I talk about VMware or AWS when the interviewer asks about competitors?

Approach competitor questions with nuance rather than dismissal. Nutanix interviewers value candidates who can articulate why a customer might choose a competitor and where Nutanix specifically wins. Avoid blanket statements that competitors are inferior. Instead, anchor your answer on specific customer segments, use cases, and total cost of ownership considerations. This signals market maturity rather than simple company loyalty.
