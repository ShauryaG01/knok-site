01 Overview

Overview

Titan Company is one of India's most recognised consumer brands, part of the Tata Group. Its businesses span watches (Titan, Fastrack, Sonata), jewellery (Tanishq), eyewear (Titan Eye+), and fragrances. As a Product Manager here, you work at the intersection of consumer retail, digital product, and deep Indian market understanding.

As of July 2026, Titan has 8 open Product Manager roles according to knok jobradar data. Competition is real, and the interview process typically tests your instinct for the Indian consumer, your ability to think across physical and digital channels, and your alignment with Tata Group values around trust and long-term thinking.

Candidates report a process that typically includes an initial screening, a product case or design exercise, and one or more leadership conversations. Rounds and formats vary by team and level, so confirm the exact structure with your recruiter after your first call.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: A key Tanishq metric has dropped this quarter. How do you diagnose it?

*Situation:* At a previous role at a retail-tech platform, the jewellery category saw a sudden drop in repeat purchase rate over two consecutive months.

*Task:* I was asked to lead the investigation and recommend fixes within two weeks.

*Action:* I broke the metric into components: new vs returning customers, online vs in-store, city tier, and price band. The drop was concentrated among customers who had bought once online but visited a store for a second purchase and left without buying. I ran brief interviews with store associates and reviewed app session recordings. The root cause: customers expected store staff to see their online cart or wish-list, but no such integration existed. I drafted a brief for a 'linked cart' feature and aligned the retail ops and engineering teams on a phased build.

*Result:* The feature shipped in about six weeks. Internal tracking showed an improvement in cross-channel repeat rates for that segment in the following quarter.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked with a retail or operations team to ship a feature.

*Situation:* At a consumer electronics company, we wanted a way for customers to order products not available on the shelf directly from the store.

*Task:* My role was to align the product, retail, and supply-chain teams and ship within one quarter.

*Action:* I spent time in stores watching how staff handled out-of-stock requests manually. I mapped the existing process and spotted three friction points: order entry, delivery promise, and customer follow-up. I ran a focused design sprint with the retail ops lead, two store managers, and engineering. We chose to build a lightweight web tool that plugged into the existing billing software rather than a standalone kiosk. This reduced deployment complexity significantly and got buy-in from store managers, who had resisted earlier proposals.

*Result:* We launched in a pilot set of stores. Store managers reported a clear reduction in manual order errors, and leadership approved a wider rollout the following quarter.

---

Q: How would you prioritise: loyalty programme improvements vs an AR try-on feature for the Titan app?

*Situation:* This came up as a live prioritisation exercise during a PM interview round.

*Task:* I had to recommend which feature to build first with a fixed engineering team for one quarter.

*Action:* I framed the choice around which metric mattered most to Titan right now. For a company with Titan's retail depth, repeat purchase and customer lifetime value are core. The loyalty programme directly drives repeat visits from customers Titan already has. The AR try-on feature primarily helps first-time converters, a smaller addressable pool at that stage. I also noted that Titan has years of loyalty data it can build on immediately, whereas AR try-on requires new tech integration and must handle device variation across a wide range of handsets used by Indian consumers. I recommended the loyalty programme first, with AR try-on as the next cycle.

*Result:* The interviewer said the reasoning was strong because it was grounded in Titan's actual business model rather than a generic feature ranking.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product design questions (such as 'improve the Tanishq experience'):
Start by clarifying the goal: is this about acquisition, retention, or revenue? Then define the user segment you are solving for, surface their top pain points, brainstorm solutions, and prioritise by impact vs effort. Close with a success metric that connects back to the stated goal.

For metric drop questions:
Use a metric tree. Break the dropped number into its components (for example, repeat rate equals users who purchased again divided by users who purchased once). Segment each component by channel, geography, customer type, and time period. Form a hypothesis for the most likely cause, then describe how you would validate it with both data and direct user or store-staff input.

For strategy or 'new category' questions:
Cover three things: the size and nature of the opportunity, how it fits Titan's existing assets (brand trust, retail network, customer data), and what risks or trade-offs the company must accept. Titan interviewers value business judgment over pure framework recitation.

For behavioural questions:
Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on the Action, especially how you navigated disagreement or constraint. Close with a concrete, specific Result rather than a vague positive outcome.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Consumer empathy for Indian buyers. Titan sells across a wide price range, from a Sonata watch bought as a gift in a small-town store to a Tanishq bridal set. Interviewers want to see that you understand what trust, aspiration, and value mean to Indian consumers at different income levels.

Omnichannel thinking. Titan's strength is its physical retail network. A purely app-first answer will raise flags. Show that you can connect the in-store and digital experience rather than treating them as separate silos.

Business judgment. Titan is a profitable, disciplined company within the Tata Group. They want PMs who think about margins, operational complexity, and long-term brand impact, not just feature velocity or user growth numbers.

Cross-functional collaboration. You will work with retail operations, design, supply chain, and category teams. Interviewers often probe for how you handle disagreement with non-tech stakeholders who have deep domain knowledge you may lack.

Tata Group values. Ethics, customer trust, and a long-term orientation matter here. If a feature could compromise customer data or brand reputation, the right answer at Titan is to flag it, not ship around it.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Know the company and its products.
Browse the Tanishq, Titan Eye+, and Fastrack apps or websites as a real customer. Note what works, what feels broken, and what is missing. Read Titan's publicly available investor presentations to understand which businesses are growing and which face competitive pressure.

Step 2: Build your product case library.
Prepare a crisp answer for each of these scenarios: improve one Titan product, diagnose a metric drop at Titan, and evaluate a new category entry for the company. Practice out loud, not just in your head.

Step 3: Prep your STAR stories.
Shortlist 4-5 strong stories from your own experience: a product you shipped, a cross-functional conflict you resolved, a data-driven decision, and a time you changed direction based on user feedback. Map each story to a Titan context before your rounds.

Step 4: Research level and salary fit.
According to knok jobradar data, PM roles in India range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate level to 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level. Know where you fit and be ready to discuss your expectations clearly.

Step 5: Confirm the format with your recruiter.
Candidates report that Titan sometimes shares a case brief or problem statement before a live round. If you receive one, treat it seriously: structure your answer, define the problem, propose solutions, and state your success metrics.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating Titan as a pure tech company. Titan's moat is its retail network, brand trust, and manufacturing relationships. Answers that ignore offline realities miss what makes Titan different from a fintech or SaaS PM role.

Not knowing the portfolio. Fastrack, Sonata, and Titan are distinct brands with different customer profiles and price points. Confusing them in an interview signals you skipped basic research.

Generic PM answers without Titan context. Saying 'I would run an A/B test' without explaining what you would test, why, and how Titan's retail context shapes the hypothesis is a wasted opportunity to show genuine understanding.

Ignoring the Tata Group lens. Titan is not a startup optimising for growth at any cost. Answers that treat ethical trade-offs or brand risk as secondary to short-term gains land poorly here.

Underselling cross-functional experience. Many Titan PM roles touch retail operations, design, and supply chain. If you have worked across functions, make it explicit and specific in your stories rather than leaving it implicit.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Titan typically have for PM roles?

Candidates report a process that typically involves 3-5 rounds: an initial screening, a product case or design exercise, and one or more conversations with senior PMs or business leaders. The exact number varies by team and seniority level. Confirm the format with your recruiter after the first call so you can prepare accordingly.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Titan Company?

According to knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India broadly range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs, and 55-90+ LPA at the Group or Principal PM level. For Titan-specific figures, Glassdoor and levels.fyi have community-reported compensation data worth checking before your offer discussion.

Do I need consumer goods or retail experience to get a PM role at Titan?

Not strictly, but it helps significantly. Titan interviewers look for consumer empathy and an understanding of physical retail, so candidates from e-commerce, consumer apps, or B2C product backgrounds tend to connect well with the interview style. If you come from a B2B or infrastructure background, spend extra prep time grounding your answers in Indian consumer context and omnichannel thinking.

Is there a take-home assignment in the Titan PM interview?

Some candidates report receiving a case brief or product problem statement before a live round, though this is not universal across all teams. Ask your recruiter whether to expect one after your screening call. If you do receive a take-home, structure it clearly: define the problem, identify the user segment, propose solutions, and state how you would measure success.

How important is knowing Titan's specific products before the interview?

Very important. Interviewers regularly frame questions around Tanishq, Fastrack, Titan Eye+, or the core Titan watch portfolio. If you arrive without having used or researched these products, your answers will sound generic and disconnected from the company. Spend time on the apps, the websites, and Titan's recent launches before your first round.

How long does the Titan PM hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates report timelines that typically range from 3-6 weeks for mid-level roles, though this varies based on team availability and how quickly rounds are scheduled. The process can move faster when there is an urgent opening on a specific team. Follow up politely with your recruiter if you have not heard back within a week of completing a round.
