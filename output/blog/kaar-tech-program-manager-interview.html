01 Overview

Overview

Kaar Tech is a Chennai-based SAP and digital transformation specialist with delivery centres across India and a global client base spanning manufacturing, retail, energy, and utilities. The Technical Program Manager role sits at the centre of their delivery model: you own the full programme lifecycle from initial planning through go-live and hypercare, coordinating across multiple SAP workstreams and mixed onshore-offshore teams.

With 3 active TPM openings at Kaar Tech as of mid-2026, the company is actively growing this function. Candidates report a process that typically involves a recruiter or HR screening call, a technical and managerial discussion with a delivery or practice lead, and a final panel or leadership round. The exact structure can vary by seniority and business unit, so confirm the format with your recruiter early.

Knok jobradar tracked 313 TPM roles across India at that time, with Bangalore accounting for the largest share. Preparation should focus on three areas: end-to-end SAP programme delivery experience, cross-functional and cross-cultural stakeholder management, and familiarity with Kaar Tech's positioning as a client-centric SAP partner.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

The following questions come up frequently in Kaar Tech TPM interviews, based on what candidates report from SAP services firms with a similar delivery model.

  1. Walk us through a large SAP programme you managed end-to-end. What was the scope and how did you keep delivery on track?
  2. How do you manage scope creep in a multi-workstream SAP rollout without damaging the client relationship?
  3. Kaar Tech serves clients in manufacturing, retail, and energy. How do you ramp up quickly on a new client's domain when you are not a functional expert?
  4. Describe a time a critical go-live was at risk. What did you do and what was the outcome?
  5. How do you handle conflicting priorities between an onshore client team and an offshore delivery team?
  6. What tools and formats do you use for programme tracking? How do you adapt your reporting for executive sponsors versus workstream leads?

  9. How do you make sure quality gates are not quietly skipped when a programme is under timeline pressure?
  10. What is your experience with agile-waterfall hybrid delivery in large enterprise SAP programmes?
  11. How do you measure programme health beyond a red-amber-green status report?
  12. Kaar Tech values long-term client partnerships. How have you personally built trust with a client who was initially sceptical of your delivery team?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioural questions. Here are three worked examples tailored to the Kaar Tech context.

Q: Walk us through a large SAP programme you managed end-to-end.

*Situation:* A mid-sized manufacturing client needed to migrate from SAP ECC to S/4HANA across multiple production plants, with a fixed go-live date tied to a regulatory deadline.

*Task:* I was responsible for coordinating workstreams across FI, MM, PP, SD, and Basis, managing dependencies between the client team and a remote delivery team, and keeping the programme board informed at every stage.

*Action:* I introduced a shared dependency log that all workstream leads updated weekly, set up a single escalation channel so blockers reached me the same day they were raised, and ran a brief daily sync with the Basis and data migration leads during the final sprint.

*Result:* We achieved go-live on the agreed date with no critical production issues in the opening week. The client extended the engagement into a phase-two rollout.

---

Q: Describe a time a critical go-live was at risk.

*Situation:* A few weeks before go-live on a retail SAP implementation, the data migration team flagged that legacy data cleansing would take longer than originally scoped.

*Task:* I had to find a path forward without moving the go-live date, which the client had already communicated to their board.

*Action:* I ran a focused replanning session with the data, business, and technical leads. We identified a subset of non-critical master data that could be loaded post-go-live, drafted a risk-acceptance document, and walked the client's project sponsor through the trade-offs before requesting sign-off.

*Result:* Go-live happened on the original date. The residual data was fully loaded the following week, and the client rated the delivery positively in their post-project review.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you pushed back on a stakeholder's request.

*Situation:* Midway through an SAP Finance rollout, a senior client stakeholder requested a significant scope addition, framing it as 'just a small change.'

*Task:* I had to address this without damaging the relationship, while protecting the delivery timeline and the team's capacity.

*Action:* I requested a focused discussion with the stakeholder rather than handling the request over email. I walked through the impact on the timeline, the team's current workload, and the budget implications using data from the programme tracker. I then presented a clear choice: include the change with a revised timeline and cost, or park it as a phase-two item.

*Result:* The stakeholder chose the phase-two option. The original scope was delivered on time, and the additional requirement was picked up in the next engagement.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard for behavioural questions at Kaar Tech and most SAP services firms. Lead with context, be specific about your personal role rather than the team's, describe the actions you took, and close with a measurable or clearly observable outcome.

For programme-planning questions, structure your answer in three layers: scope and stakeholders, your delivery approach, and how you managed risk and changes. Interviewers at consulting and services firms want to see that you think in terms of governance, not just execution.

For stakeholder conflict questions, try a 'listen, align, decide' structure. Show that you gathered perspectives from both sides, found a shared objective, and drove a decision rather than letting the situation drift.

For 'how do you measure' questions, go beyond tools and dashboards. Name the leading indicators you track (dependency blockers, open risks, burn rate against plan) and explain how you translate them into decisions, not just status reports.

A useful self-check before answering: Is this answer about what I did, or what the team did? Interviewers want to understand your judgment and contribution specifically, not the group effort.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Kaar Tech TPM interviewers are typically senior programme managers or practice leads who have run large SAP implementations themselves. They look for the following qualities consistently.

Programme ownership, not just coordination. They want to see that you treat the programme as yours, proactively identify risks before they are escalated to you, and drive decisions rather than waiting for direction.

Stakeholder confidence. Because Kaar Tech operates in a client-facing model, interviewers probe whether you can hold your ground professionally with senior client stakeholders while keeping the relationship intact.

Onshore-offshore fluency. Most Kaar Tech programmes involve mixed teams across time zones. Candidates who can describe specific examples of managing communication, accountability, and alignment across geographies consistently score higher.

SAP delivery specifics. Generic programme management experience is not enough. Expect questions that probe your familiarity with SAP implementation phases, data migration risks, cutover planning, and hypercare. You do not need to be a functional consultant, but you must speak the language.

Honesty about challenges. Kaar Tech's delivery culture values directness. Interviewers respond well to candidates who acknowledge what went wrong and what they learned, rather than presenting a polished success story every time.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A structured approach across the days before your interview will make a real difference.

Research Kaar Tech first. Read their website, recent press releases, and any publicly available case studies. Understand their SAP practice areas, key industry verticals, and recent partnerships or certifications. Glassdoor reviews can give you a sense of the delivery culture from current and former employees.

Map your experience to their context. For each question theme listed above, write a short STAR outline using a real project from your background. Prioritise SAP programmes if you have them. If your background is in a different technology, prepare to draw clear parallels to SAP delivery phases.

Prepare specific questions to ask. Interviewers typically respond well to candidates who ask about current programme challenges, the client mix for the role, and how success is measured in the first few months. Avoid asking about information that is clearly on their public website.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Keeping answers too generic. Saying 'I managed stakeholders effectively' without a specific story loses credibility quickly with interviewers who have run SAP programmes themselves. Every behavioural answer needs a concrete example.

Confusing the team's work with your own. Saying 'we delivered the programme on time' is not an interview answer. Name what you specifically decided, escalated, or changed.

Underestimating SAP literacy expectations. Candidates from non-SAP backgrounds sometimes assume the TPM role is purely about process. Kaar Tech interviewers typically probe your familiarity with SAP delivery phases. Brush up on terms like cutover strategy, data migration cycles, UAT sign-off, and go-live readiness criteria.

Not asking anything at the end. Candidates who ask no questions leave a weaker impression. Prepare at least a couple of specific questions.

Overselling without acknowledging challenges. A flawless project history sounds rehearsed. When an interviewer asks what went wrong or what you would do differently, answer honestly. Self-awareness and learning are valued in a delivery culture.

Ignoring the client-facing dimension. Kaar Tech is a services firm. If your examples focus entirely on internal programme management and never mention client communication or expectation-setting, you may come across as a better fit for an in-house role than a client-facing one.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Kaar Tech TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves a recruiter screening call, a technical and managerial discussion with a practice or delivery lead, and a final leadership or panel round. The exact structure can vary based on the seniority of the role and the business unit hiring. Confirm the format with your HR contact after you receive the interview invite, so you can prepare accordingly.

Do I need deep SAP functional knowledge to clear the interview?

You do not need to be an SAP functional consultant, but you are expected to speak the language of SAP programme delivery. Interviewers will typically probe your understanding of areas like cutover planning, data migration cycles, and go-live readiness. Candidates with strong general programme management backgrounds can do well if they can map their experience clearly to SAP delivery phases.

What should I ask Kaar Tech at the end of the interview?

Ask about the specific programme or client you would be joining, how success is defined in the first few months, and what the current team structure looks like. Questions about the delivery culture and how escalations are typically handled also tend to land well with interviewers. Avoid asking about things clearly covered on their public website, as this signals you have not done basic research.

Is Kaar Tech open to candidates from non-SAP backgrounds?

Kaar Tech TPM roles are generally oriented toward SAP programme delivery, so prior SAP exposure is a real advantage. Candidates from adjacent areas such as large-scale ERP transformations, systems integration, or enterprise IT programmes can sometimes bridge the gap if they demonstrate strong programme governance skills. Being honest about your background and showing a clear plan to build SAP knowledge tends to go down better than overstating your experience.

How important is onshore-offshore team experience for this role?

Very important. Most Kaar Tech programmes involve delivery teams spread across multiple locations, often with client stakeholders onshore and technical teams offshore. Candidates who can describe specific examples of managing communication, accountability, and alignment across time zones consistently receive stronger feedback from interviewers. If you have this experience, make it a central part of your preparation.
