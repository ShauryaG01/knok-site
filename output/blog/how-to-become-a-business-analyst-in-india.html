01 Role Overview

Role Overview

A Business Analyst (BA) is the person who figures out what a business actually needs before the tech team builds anything. You gather requirements from stakeholders, map out current processes, identify gaps, and write clear specifications so developers and product teams know exactly what to build. In India, BAs are hired across IT services (TCS, Infosys, Wipro), BFSI (banks, insurance, fintech), e-commerce, consulting firms, and funded startups.

The role often goes by different names: Systems Analyst, Functional Consultant, Product Analyst, or Requirements Engineer. The work is similar across these titles. What changes is the domain (finance, logistics, healthcare, software) and the tools you are expected to use.

As of July 2026, knok's job radar is tracking 398 active Business Analyst openings across India. The top cities are Bangalore (53 openings), Delhi (48), Mumbai (24), Hyderabad (21), Pune (21), and Chennai (16). This distribution tells you where demand is strongest and where you may want to focus your search.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Technical skills are the foundation recruiters screen for first.

  • SQL: Writing queries to pull and analyse data is expected even in non-tech BA roles. Learn SELECT, JOIN, GROUP BY, and subqueries at minimum.
  • Excel and Google Sheets: Pivot tables, VLOOKUP/XLOOKUP, and basic data modelling. Most Indian companies still run a lot on spreadsheets.
  • Business Intelligence tools: Power BI or Tableau for dashboards and reporting. Even a basic certification here signals seriousness.
  • JIRA and Confluence: Almost every tech team in India uses Atlassian tools. Know how to write user stories, manage backlogs, and document requirements.
  • Process mapping: Flowcharts in tools like Lucidchart, draw.io, or Visio. You need to show a process visually to get stakeholder sign-off.

Soft skills separate average BAs from the ones who get promoted.

  • Clear written and verbal communication, especially in English, because most Indian BA roles require working with global clients or cross-functional teams.
  • Stakeholder management: the ability to get agreement from people who disagree.
  • Structured thinking: breaking a messy problem into components before proposing solutions.
  • Documentation discipline: writing requirements that are complete, unambiguous, and traceable.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

1. Settle the education baseline first.
A bachelor's degree in any discipline is the minimum for most BA roles in India. Engineering (any branch), commerce, and MBA graduates all enter this field. If you are still in college, focus on analytical courses, internships, and projects with real data.

2. Learn SQL and Excel to a working level.
Start here before anything else. Free resources on YouTube and practice platforms like HackerRank or Mode Analytics will get you started. Set a concrete target: write and debug queries confidently before moving to the next step.

3. Pick up one BI tool.
Power BI is the most commonly asked-for tool in Indian BA job descriptions right now. Microsoft's free learning path covers it in a few weeks. Publish one or two dashboards on GitHub or a portfolio site to show employers proof of work.

4. Get your first domain exposure.
BA roles are domain-heavy. Pick one vertical (BFSI, e-commerce, logistics, healthcare IT) and learn its basic vocabulary, regulations, and workflows. Read industry reports, follow domain newsletters, and if possible, do a short internship or freelance project in that space.

5. Build a portfolio of requirement documents.
This is what most freshers skip. Create sample BRDs (Business Requirement Documents), user stories, and process maps for a fictional or open-source project. Post them on GitHub or a simple portfolio site. Recruiters in India increasingly ask for proof of work.

6. Earn one certification.
The ECBA (Entry Certificate in Business Analysis) from IIBA is the most recognised globally and carries weight on Indian resumes. The CBAP is for experienced professionals. Alternatively, a BA course from NASSCOM FutureSkills or a Coursera specialisation from a reputed university works for freshers who cannot yet afford IIBA fees.

7. Apply smartly, not broadly.
Tailor your resume to match keywords in the job description. Naukri.com and LinkedIn are the dominant platforms in India. Update your Naukri profile weekly because the algorithm surfaces recently updated profiles. Apply to mid-size IT services companies and product startups first; large IT firms have structured fresher hiring through their own portals.

8. Prepare for Indian BA interviews.
Expect a combination of: SQL test, Excel case study, a 'tell me about a process you improved' behavioural question, and domain questions if the role is BFSI or healthcare. Practice case studies from consulting interview prep resources, adapted to a BA context.

04 Timeline And Milestones

Timeline And Milestones

Here is a realistic picture of how long each phase takes for most people entering BA roles in India.

Months 1-2: Skill foundation
Learn SQL basics, Excel to an intermediate level, and start one BI tool. Do this in parallel. Set concrete goals: work through a set of SQL practice problems and publish one Power BI dashboard before moving to the next phase.

Months 3-4: Portfolio and certification
Write two sample BRDs and a set of user stories for a fictional product (a food delivery app, a hospital management system, anything with clear business logic). Start your ECBA prep or equivalent course. Begin reading about one target domain.

Months 5-6: Active job search
Update Naukri and LinkedIn with your new skills and portfolio links. Apply to roles in your target city. If you are a fresher, also look at internships, since a 3-month BA internship at a mid-size company often converts to a full-time offer.

Months 7-12: First role or contract project
Most people who follow this path consistently land their first BA role or a contract project within this window. If you are switching from an unrelated field, it may take a few additional months, because you are building domain credibility alongside technical skills at the same time. Stay patient and keep applying.

Year 2-3: Senior BA and specialisation
After your first role, the path accelerates. Focus on one domain, mentor juniors, and take on end-to-end project ownership. This is when certifications like CBAP or domain-specific credentials (FRM for finance, for example) start adding real value to your resume.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

Where the jobs actually are
Bangalore leads with 53 of the 398 openings tracked in July 2026, followed by Delhi (48). If you are open to relocating, Bangalore and Delhi-NCR give you the widest options. Hyderabad and Pune (21 each) are strong for IT services and product companies. Chennai (16) skews toward manufacturing and automotive domain BA roles.

The Naukri reality
Naukri is still the dominant platform for BA hiring in India, especially for IT services companies. Keep your profile 'active' by logging in and updating it every week. Recruiters filter by 'last active' date, so a stale profile gets buried. Write a crisp profile summary using keywords from job descriptions: 'Business Analyst', 'BRD', 'SQL', 'Agile', 'JIRA' are commonly searched terms.

LinkedIn works differently here
In India, LinkedIn is more useful for consulting firms, BFSI companies, and startups than for IT services giants. Connect with BAs who work at companies you want to join, comment meaningfully on their posts, and send personalised connection requests. Avoid generic 'I want to work at your company' messages.

Communities and learning groups
Join the IIBA India chapter if you are serious about the CBAP/ECBA path. The BA Times India newsletter and LinkedIn groups focused on Business Analysis in India are active communities. Offline, cities like Bangalore and Delhi have BA meetups listed on Meetup.com.

MBA vs no MBA
A tier-1 or tier-2 MBA (IIM, ISB, XLRI, NMIMS, Symbiosis) gives you a shortcut into senior BA or product management roles at top companies. But many strong BAs in India start without an MBA and grow through skills and domain expertise. An MBA matters more in BFSI and consulting. In product and IT services, your portfolio and certifications often carry equal weight.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need an engineering degree to become a Business Analyst in India?

No. BA roles in India hire from engineering, commerce, science, and arts backgrounds. What matters more is your analytical skills, domain knowledge, and ability to write clear requirements. An engineering background helps if you are targeting tech-heavy BA roles, but it is not a hard requirement. Many successful BAs come from commerce and humanities backgrounds.

What salary can I expect as a fresher BA in India?

Salary varies significantly by city, company size, and domain. For current figures, check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for publicly reported BA salaries in your target city. IT services companies generally pay lower than product startups or BFSI firms at the same experience level. Negotiation room is usually larger in startups and mid-size product companies.

Is an MBA necessary to grow as a BA in India?

Not necessarily. Many BAs in India reach senior and lead roles without an MBA by building strong domain expertise and relevant certifications. An MBA from a top school (IIM, ISB, XLRI) can accelerate entry into consulting or BFSI firms. In IT services and product companies, your track record and skills often matter more than the degree.

Which certification is best for BA freshers in India?

The ECBA (Entry Certificate in Business Analysis) from IIBA is the most widely recognised globally and is valued by Indian recruiters who know the field well. For freshers on a budget, a Coursera specialisation or NASSCOM FutureSkills course is a practical starting point. Certifications matter less than a portfolio of actual work, so pair any certificate with sample BRDs and user stories you can show an interviewer.

How is the BA role different from a Product Manager role?

A BA focuses on documenting requirements, mapping processes, and bridging the gap between business stakeholders and the tech team. A Product Manager owns the product vision, prioritises the roadmap, and is accountable for business outcomes. In Indian IT services companies these roles are usually separate. In startups, a BA and PM sometimes overlap, with one person doing both.

What domains hire the most BAs in India?

BFSI (banking, financial services, and insurance) is the largest employer of BAs in India, followed by IT services, e-commerce, and healthcare IT. Consulting firms like Deloitte, EY, and Accenture also hire BAs in significant numbers. Choosing a domain early and learning its terminology helps you stand out, since most job descriptions ask for domain experience alongside core BA skills.
