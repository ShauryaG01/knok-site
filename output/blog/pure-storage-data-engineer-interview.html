02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions reflect the kinds of problems Pure Storage Data Engineering teams work on, compiled from candidate reports and public job descriptions. Use this as your core prep checklist.

  1. Walk us through how you designed a data pipeline end to end, from ingestion to serving.
  2. Pure Storage collects telemetry from a large fleet of storage arrays deployed at enterprise customers. How would you design a pipeline to ingest, process, and store this data reliably at scale?
  3. How do you handle schema evolution when upstream data sources change without warning?
  4. Explain the difference between batch and streaming processing. For a near-real-time storage capacity alert use case, which would you choose and why?
  5. How do you ensure data quality in a production pipeline? What checks do you add and at which stage?
  6. Describe a time a pipeline you owned failed in production. How did you diagnose and fix it?
  7. How would you design a data model for tracking customer storage usage over time, including historical snapshots?
  8. What cloud platforms have you worked with? How do you approach cost control for large data workloads?
  9. How do you make pipelines idempotent so they can safely re-run without duplicating records?
  10. What orchestration tools have you used (Airflow, Prefect, Dagster)? How did you handle dependency management and failure retries?
  11. How do you work with downstream consumers such as analysts and data scientists to understand their data needs and SLA requirements?
  12. How do you balance sprint commitments with urgent production data incidents that come up mid-sprint?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time a pipeline you owned failed in production. How did you diagnose and fix it?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a nightly aggregation pipeline feeding a finance dashboard started producing incorrect totals. The error was caught two days before a major stakeholder review.

*Task:* I needed to find the root cause quickly, fix it without breaking downstream tables, and prevent a recurrence.

*Action:* I compared output row counts for the current run against the previous week using an audit query. I found that one source table had introduced a null value in a join key after an upstream schema change. I patched the transformation to handle the null, wrote a quality check that alerts on null join keys before aggregation runs, and backfilled the affected partitions.

*Result:* The dashboard was corrected before the review. The null-key check was adopted as a standard template across five other pipelines in the team.

---

Q: How would you design a pipeline to ingest telemetry from storage arrays at scale?

*Situation:* In a system design round at a previous role, I was asked to design a high-frequency device metrics ingestion pipeline.

*Task:* The requirement was reliable low-latency ingestion, durable storage, and the ability to query historical trends.

*Action:* I proposed a Kafka-based ingestion layer to absorb spikes from a large fleet of devices, a Flink job for real-time aggregation and anomaly flagging, and Parquet files partitioned by device ID and date written to object storage. For serving, I suggested a columnar warehouse layer for analytical queries with a hot cache for the most recent data.

*Result:* The design was well received. The interviewer probed on late-arriving data, which led me to add a watermark strategy to the Flink job on the spot, turning a gap into a demonstration of adaptability.

---

Q: How do you handle schema evolution in a data warehouse?

*Situation:* At a previous company, a source CRM system added three new columns and renamed one existing column without notifying the data team.

*Task:* Downstream reporting tables broke and I was responsible for the affected pipeline.

*Action:* I implemented a schema registry check at the ingestion layer that compares incoming schema to a stored baseline and raises an alert on any structural change before data lands in the warehouse. For the immediate fix, I used ALTER TABLE to add the new columns and mapped the renamed column via a compatibility alias in the transformation layer.

*Result:* Reporting was restored the same day. The schema registry check was adopted team-wide and caught two more upstream changes in the following quarter before they caused any outages.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for every behavioural question. Situation and Task together should take no more than thirty seconds. Spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did, not what 'the team' did) and Result (a concrete outcome, even if the impact seems modest).

For system design questions, follow a four-step structure. First, clarify requirements and scale by asking about data volume, latency needs, and read/write patterns before drawing anything. Second, sketch the high-level architecture covering ingestion, processing, storage, and serving. Third, go deep on the component the interviewer probes on most. Fourth, walk through trade-offs and failure modes. Candidates report that Pure Storage interviewers tend to focus on reliability and cost efficiency, which fits the company's product context closely.

For 'why Pure Storage' questions, connect your background to their product. Pure Storage works at the intersection of hardware and software data infrastructure. If you have experience with telemetry pipelines, time-series data, or high-throughput ingestion, say so directly. 'I want to work on data that is close to the product' lands better than generic answers about company culture.

For SQL and Python rounds, think out loud. Interviewers are evaluating your reasoning process as much as your syntax. If you get stuck on a query, describe what result you are trying to produce and work backwards from there.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Ownership over tasks, not just execution. Pure Storage Data Engineering roles involve pipelines that real products depend on. Candidates who say 'I owned' or 'I was accountable for' rather than 'I helped' or 'the team did' tend to make a stronger impression in behavioural rounds.

Depth in at least one core area. You do not need to know every tool in the ecosystem, but you should have genuine depth in either pipeline orchestration, distributed processing, or cloud data warehousing. Surface-level answers on all three tend to score lower than strong answers on two with honest gaps acknowledged on the third.

A reliability mindset. Given that Pure Storage's customers depend on storage uptime, their data teams are expected to think about pipeline reliability with the same seriousness. Candidates who naturally discuss monitoring, alerting, idempotency, and failure recovery without being prompted stand out.

Communication with non-technical stakeholders. Data Engineers at Pure Storage typically work with product managers and finance teams who consume the data. Candidates report that questions about explaining data issues to non-technical people come up in most hiring manager rounds.

Comfort with ambiguity. You will be asked about situations where requirements were unclear or changed mid-project. Interviewers want to see that you ask the right clarifying questions and do not wait for perfect specs before making progress.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Technical foundations. Revisit SQL (window functions, CTEs, query optimization), Python for data (pandas, PySpark basics), and one orchestration tool you know well. Practice writing queries from scratch without autocomplete to simulate the interview environment.

Week 2: System design for data. Study common data architecture patterns: Lambda vs. Kappa, event-driven ingestion with Kafka, data lakehouse design, and slowly changing dimensions in a warehouse. For Pure Storage specifically, read about telemetry pipeline patterns and time-series data storage, since their product context makes these highly relevant.

Week 3: Behavioural prep. Write out four to five STAR stories from your experience covering: a production incident you resolved, a pipeline you built from scratch, a time you pushed back on a requirement, and a cross-functional collaboration. Practice saying each story out loud in under three minutes.

Week 4: Mock interviews and company research. Do at least two mock interviews with a peer or on a practice platform. Read Pure Storage's engineering blog and recent product announcements so you can speak to their technical direction. Glassdoor and levels.fyi have publicly reported compensation and interview experience data worth checking for the most current numbers from verified employees.

On the day: For each design question, write your approach before speaking. For each behavioural question, take five seconds to pick your best story before answering.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping into system design without clarifying requirements. Candidates who skip straight to an architecture often design for the wrong scale or the wrong constraints. Always ask about data volume, latency requirements, and existing infrastructure before drawing boxes.

Generic STAR answers that could apply anywhere. Saying 'I optimised a query and it got faster' is weak. 'I replaced a correlated subquery with a window function, which unblocked the finance team's daily report' is specific and credible. Use real details even when the numbers seem small.

Underselling reliability work. Candidates often focus on features built and skip over the monitoring, alerting, and incident response work that kept those features running. At Pure Storage, where reliability is a core product value, this operational work is worth mentioning explicitly.

Overclaiming tool expertise. If you have used Spark only for small datasets in a notebook, say that. Interviewers who probe deeper will find the gap quickly, and it damages trust for the rest of the interview.

Asking no questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing, or ask only about compensation, leave a weak final impression. Ask about team structure, the biggest data challenge the team is currently solving, or how success is measured in the first six months. These questions signal genuine interest in the role.
