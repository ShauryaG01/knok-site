01 Overview

Overview

HCL Technologies is one of India's largest IT services companies, with a global delivery model spanning banking, telecom, manufacturing, and retail verticals. For Data Architect roles, HCL typically runs a structured multi-round process that candidates report covers technical depth, system design, and delivery-focused behavioural questions.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 349 open roles at HCL Technologies across India, reflecting strong hiring momentum. Across the broader Data Architect market, Delhi led with 8 openings, followed by Bangalore (7), Chennai (5), Hyderabad (2), and Pune (1), out of 57 total Data Architect positions tracked.

HCL's interviews for this role typically test hands-on experience with cloud data platforms, enterprise data modelling, and the ability to translate business requirements into scalable, governed data solutions. Because HCL is a services company, client-facing communication skills are weighted alongside pure technical ability.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Technical and Design Questions

  1. Walk us through how you would design a data warehouse from scratch for an enterprise client. What layers would you include and why?
  2. HCL serves clients in banking, telecom, and manufacturing. How does your data architecture approach differ between a transactional OLTP system and an analytical OLAP system?
  3. Explain the differences between a data lake, a data lakehouse, and a data warehouse. When would you recommend each?
  4. You are inheriting a legacy on-premise data system and the client wants to migrate to Azure or AWS. How do you plan this migration with minimal disruption to live operations?
  5. How do you ensure data quality and data lineage in a large-scale pipeline? What tools or frameworks have you applied?
  6. Describe your experience with dimensional modelling. How do you choose between a star schema and a snowflake schema?
  7. A client's data pipelines are failing intermittently and business reporting is impacted. How do you debug and resolve this?
  8. What is your approach to data governance, data cataloguing, and metadata management in an enterprise setting?

Behavioural and Situational Questions

  1. Tell me about a time you had to convince a non-technical stakeholder to adopt a new data architecture. How did you handle pushback?
  2. Describe a complex data integration project you led. What was your biggest challenge and how did you resolve it?
  3. How do you stay current with evolving data technologies? Give an example of a new tool or pattern you introduced at a previous employer.
  4. HCL values client-centricity. Tell me about a time you went beyond the original brief to deliver a data solution that exceeded client expectations.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through how you would design a data warehouse from scratch for an enterprise client.

*Situation:* At my previous employer, a large retail chain needed a centralised reporting platform. Their data was spread across point-of-sale systems, an ERP, and a CRM, with no single source of truth.

*Task:* I was responsible for end-to-end architecture: gathering requirements, selecting the tech stack, and producing a design the client's internal team could maintain independently.

*Action:* I ran requirement workshops with business and IT stakeholders to identify the top reporting use cases. I proposed a three-layer architecture: a raw ingestion layer in cloud object storage, a cleansed and conformed layer in a columnar data warehouse, and a presentation layer with pre-built dimensional models. I chose a star schema for the sales domain to keep queries simple for business analysts, and documented data lineage from source to report so the client could trust the numbers.

*Result:* The platform went live and the client's finance team moved from manual spreadsheet reports to automated dashboards. The client publicly reported the project as a significant operational improvement.

---

Q: Describe a complex data integration project you led. What was your biggest challenge?

*Situation:* A telecom client had acquired two smaller companies and needed their billing, customer, and network data unified before a regulatory deadline.

*Task:* I led the data architecture workstream, coordinating with separate vendor teams and the client's internal architects simultaneously.

*Action:* I created a canonical data model that all source systems would map to, rather than building brittle point-to-point integrations. I introduced a staging area where conflicts in customer master data were surfaced and resolved through defined matching and merging logic. I held regular syncs with each vendor to track mapping progress and surface blockers before they became critical path issues.

*Result:* The unified data layer was delivered ahead of the regulatory deadline. The client extended the engagement to build analytics on top of the same foundation.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you convinced a non-technical stakeholder to adopt a new data architecture.

*Situation:* A client's finance director was resistant to moving away from a legacy on-premise data warehouse, citing risk and cost as primary concerns.

*Task:* I needed to build a case that addressed their specific concerns without overwhelming them with technical detail.

*Action:* I prepared a side-by-side comparison of the current state (maintenance burden, scalability limits, licensing costs) versus the proposed cloud architecture, using publicly reported industry benchmarks alongside the client's own operational data. I focused on business outcomes: faster reporting cycles, self-service analytics, and reduced dependency on IT for ad-hoc queries. I also proposed a phased migration to remove the big-bang cutover risk that was their main concern.

*Result:* The director approved the project after a second presentation. The phased approach I proposed was later adopted as the standard template for similar migrations within the same HCL vertical team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions use a three-part structure: first, clarify requirements and constraints before touching the whiteboard; second, walk through the architecture layer by layer explaining your reasoning; third, address trade-offs explicitly (cost vs. performance, simplicity vs. flexibility). HCL interviewers typically want to see that you ask good questions before jumping to a solution.

For behavioural questions use STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep the Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your answer on the Action, specifically what you personally did rather than what the team did. Where you cannot share exact figures due to confidentiality, describe the outcome in terms of business impact ('reporting cycle dropped from weekly to daily') rather than inventing numbers.

For technology-choice questions frame your answer around trade-offs. Avoid sounding like a vendor brochure. Explain why you would choose one tool over another for a specific context, and acknowledge the limitations of your choice. Interviewers at this level expect balanced judgement, not advocacy.

For migration and modernisation questions show you understand risk. Cover data validation, rollback planning, stakeholder communication, and how you would run parallel operations before cutting over. HCL's enterprise clients often have very low tolerance for reporting downtime.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Candidates report that HCL Data Architect interviews place heavy weight on practical, client-facing experience rather than purely academic knowledge. Interviewers want evidence that you have delivered data architectures in messy, real-world conditions, not just designed them in isolation.

Client communication skills matter as much as technical depth. HCL is a services company, so the ability to translate complex data concepts into business language for a client's CTO or finance director is directly valued and tested in behavioural rounds.

Cloud platform fluency is consistently flagged in candidate feedback, particularly around Azure, AWS, and GCP data services. Knowing the managed services available on each cloud and when to use them versus a custom-built solution is expected at the architect level.

Governance and compliance awareness has become increasingly important. Candidates who speak confidently to data security, PII handling, regulatory requirements relevant to banking or telecom clients, and data lineage practices tend to score well in later rounds.

Collaborative mindset is probed in behavioural rounds. HCL interviewers typically look for how you have worked across vendor teams, onshore-offshore delivery models, and with client stakeholders who may have conflicting priorities.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Audit your experience against the job description. Pull the specific HCL Data Architect JD you are applying for and list every technical skill mentioned. Note which ones you can speak to with a concrete project example and which ones need revision.

Step 2: Prepare your signature project stories. Choose a set of projects that demonstrate data architecture at scale, stakeholder influence, migration delivery, data quality work, and handling a major incident or failure. Practise telling each in STAR format, keeping each story focused and crisp.

Step 3: Revise core concepts. Focus on dimensional modelling, cloud data warehouse patterns (Medallion architecture, Lambda vs. Kappa), ETL vs. ELT trade-offs, and data governance frameworks. If you are less confident on a specific cloud platform, work through its data services documentation and practise explaining service choices out loud.

Step 4: Practise system design out loud. Draw a data architecture for a common enterprise scenario (a retail analytics platform or a bank's data lake, for example). Narrate your decisions as you draw. This mirrors what interviewers typically ask in live technical rounds.

Step 5: Prepare smart questions for your interviewer. Asking about the client context for the role, the current state of the data platform, and the team structure signals genuine seniority and engagement.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to solutions without clarifying requirements. In system design rounds, candidates who start drawing architecture diagrams immediately without asking about scale, budget, team capability, or regulatory constraints often lose marks. Slow down and ask questions first.

Using generic answers for behavioural questions. Saying 'I am a team player' or 'I always follow best practices' without a specific story is the fastest way to be screened out. Every behavioural question needs a real, named example.

Overlooking the services context. HCL is not a product company. Candidates who talk only about building internal platforms sometimes miss that the interviewer wants to hear about client deliverables, stakeholder management, and working within client constraints and budgets.

Weak on trade-offs. Architects who can only describe what a technology does, but not when NOT to use it, come across as tool advocates rather than problem solvers. Always frame technology choices around the specific context and the trade-offs involved.

Neglecting data governance. Many candidates focus entirely on pipeline and modelling questions and are caught off guard when asked about data cataloguing, lineage, or PII handling. These topics are standard at the architect level.

Not preparing questions to ask. Candidates who have nothing to ask at the end of the interview signal low engagement. Prepare at least a couple of thoughtful questions about the role, the client domain, or the current state of the data platform.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the HCL Technologies Data Architect interview typically have?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four rounds, covering an initial screening with HR or a recruiter, a technical round focused on data architecture concepts and system design, and one or two rounds with senior architects or delivery managers. Some candidates report an additional round for roles tied to specific client accounts. Round structure can vary by business unit, so confirm the process with your recruiter at the start.

Is there a coding or SQL test in the HCL Data Architect interview?

Data Architect roles at HCL are typically design-heavy rather than coding-heavy, but candidates commonly report being asked to write SQL queries to demonstrate data modelling skills or to debug a given query. You are unlikely to face algorithmic coding rounds of the kind used for software engineering positions, but solid SQL and some exposure to scripting in Python or shell is advisable. Prepare for whiteboard-style data modelling problems as well.

What cloud platforms should I focus on for an HCL Data Architect interview?

HCL works with clients across Azure, AWS, and GCP, so working knowledge of at least one major cloud data stack is expected. Azure (Synapse Analytics, Data Factory, ADLS) and AWS (Redshift, Glue, S3) come up most frequently in candidate feedback. Be ready to compare the data services across clouds at a conceptual level, and to explain why you would choose one over another for a given client context.

How should I talk about salary expectations in the HCL interview process?

HR rounds typically include a compensation discussion. Research Data Architect salary ranges on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before your interview so you have a realistic anchor for this role and level. Be prepared to state a range rather than a single number, and factor in your current CTC, the seniority level of the role, and the location. Delhi and Bangalore currently have the most active Data Architect openings, and compensation can vary by city.

Does HCL conduct panel interviews for Data Architect roles?

Some candidates report panel interviews for senior architecture roles, where a technical lead, a delivery manager, and sometimes a domain specialist are present simultaneously. If you are told your round will be a panel, prepare to address different priorities at once: one interviewer may probe technical depth while another focuses on delivery and client management experience. Maintain eye contact with all panel members when answering, not just the person who asked the question.

How long does the HCL Technologies hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report the end-to-end process taking a few weeks to over a month, depending on the urgency of the role and the number of shortlisted candidates. Delays are common when roles are tied to client projects still in the staffing phase. Following up politely with your recruiter after each round is standard practice and generally well-received.
