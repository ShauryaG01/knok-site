03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process typically unfolds over four to five stages, though the exact number of rounds can vary by role and team. Here is what most candidates report going through.

Stage 1: Application and Resume Shortlisting. Your resume is the first filter. Recruiters look for relevant experience, the right keywords for the role, and signs of impact in past work. Tailoring your resume to the job description matters more than sending a generic one.

Stage 2: HR Screening Call. A recruiter or HR executive reaches out for a short call. Expect questions about your current role, notice period, salary expectations, and why you are interested in this company. This round is mostly to confirm basic fit before passing you to the hiring manager.

Stage 3: Technical or Domain Round. For tech roles, this is usually a coding round or system design discussion. For non-tech roles, expect a case study, a written assignment, or a domain knowledge conversation. Interviewers at this stage are typically senior team members who want to see how you think, not just what you know.

Stage 4: Hiring Manager Interview. A deeper conversation with the person you would directly report to. Expect a mix of situational questions ('tell me about a time you...'), role-specific scenarios, and a discussion of how you work. This is also your chance to ask smart questions about the team and the growth path.

Stage 5: Culture or Leadership Round (for senior roles). Senior hires often face an additional round with a cross-functional stakeholder or a senior leader. The focus shifts to values alignment, collaboration style, and how you handle ambiguity.

Offer and Onboarding. If all rounds go well, HR shares a verbal offer followed by a written letter. Background verification typically runs in parallel with your notice period. Timelines between stages can stretch from a few days to a couple of weeks, so follow up politely if you hear nothing after a week.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Beyond the job description, hiring managers consistently look for a few traits across roles.

Ownership mindset. Candidates who take responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks, stand out. In interviews, this shows up when you describe problems you spotted and fixed without being asked.

Communication clarity. Whether you are writing code, managing a client, or running operations, the ability to explain your thinking clearly is valued across all functions.

Problem-solving under ambiguity. Many interview questions are intentionally open-ended. Interviewers want to see how you structure a problem, not just whether you land on the 'right' answer.

Team collaboration. Cross-functional work is common in growing companies. Be ready to share examples of working with people outside your immediate team to get things done.

Relevant technical or domain depth. For specialist roles, depth still matters. Fresher candidates can offset limited experience with strong fundamentals and a portfolio of projects or internships.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

A little targeted preparation makes a big difference across all stages.

Research the company thoroughly. Read recent news, product updates, and any public interviews with leadership. Knowing what the company is focused on in 2026 helps you frame your experience relevantly and ask sharper questions.

Tailor your resume per role. With 143 open roles, there is likely more than one that fits your profile. Pick the best match and rewrite your resume bullet points to mirror the language in that specific job description.

Practise STAR answers. Situational and behavioural questions ('tell me about a time...') are common in HR and hiring manager rounds. Use the Situation, Task, Action, Result format to keep your answers tight and concrete.

Prepare your own questions. Interviewers notice when a candidate asks nothing or asks only about salary. Prepare two or three specific questions about the team, the product, or what success looks like in the first few months.

For tech roles, revisit core concepts. Depending on the level, expect data structures and algorithms, system design principles, or domain-specific coding problems. Practise on relevant platforms and review your past projects so you can discuss them clearly.

Follow up after each round. A short thank-you note to the recruiter after an interview is not common in India, which is exactly why it stands out. Keep it brief and genuine.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Culture at fast-growing companies tends to reward people who move quickly, own their work, and communicate proactively. While every company describes itself as 'collaborative' and 'innovative,' the real culture shows up in how decisions get made and how failure is handled.

Candidates who do well in interviews here often describe an environment where initiative is encouraged and where people are expected to ask for help rather than stay stuck. Cross-team collaboration is common, which means good interpersonal skills matter as much as technical ability.

If you are evaluating this company as a place to grow, ask your interviewer how the team handles a project that did not go as planned. The answer will tell you a lot about how honest the culture actually is about learning from mistakes.

For those earlier in their careers, joining a company with 143 active openings often signals a growth phase. That typically means faster promotions and broader responsibilities than a more mature, stable organisation would offer.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How long does the hiring process typically take?

The full process from application to offer can take a few weeks to over a month, depending on the role and how quickly the team moves. Senior roles tend to take longer because of additional rounds and internal approvals. Following up with the recruiter after a week of silence is perfectly acceptable and often speeds things up.

Can I apply for multiple roles at the same time?

Yes, and with 143 open roles you may find more than one that fits your background. It is best to let the recruiter know you have applied to multiple positions so they can route you correctly. Applying to roles where your skills are a strong match is more effective than applying broadly to every open position.

What salary can I expect?

Compensation varies significantly by role, level, and your current package. For specific numbers, Glassdoor and levels.fyi have publicly reported salary data for similar companies that can give you a realistic benchmark. Go into salary discussions knowing your market value and be ready to justify it with your experience and the scope of the role.

Do they hire freshers or only experienced professionals?

Companies with this many active openings typically hire across levels, including freshers for entry-level and trainee roles. Look for job descriptions that explicitly mention limited or no prior experience as acceptable. Having a strong academic project or a brief internship on your resume significantly improves your shortlisting chances as a fresher.

How important is it to know someone inside the company?

A referral can move your resume to the top of the pile, but it is not a requirement to get hired. Many candidates get in through direct applications, especially when their resume is well-tailored to the role. If you do know someone at the company, a referral is worth asking for since most companies run internal referral programmes that reward the employee too.

What should I do if I get rejected?

Ask the recruiter politely for feedback, since not all companies share it but some do. Use the experience to identify gaps in your preparation and consider reapplying after several months once you have addressed those gaps. Many people get hired on their second or third attempt at the same company after building more experience or refining how they present themselves.
