01 Overview

Overview

Freshworks is one of India's best-known SaaS companies, with products like Freshdesk, Freshsales, Freshservice, and Freshchat used by businesses across the world. An Engineering Manager at Freshworks typically owns a product engineering team, drives sprint delivery, and partners with Product and Design to ship features that directly impact customers.

Freshworks currently has 145 open roles, making it one of the more active SaaS hirers for engineering leadership in 2026. The company is known for a strong 'customer obsession' culture and a 'frugal innovation' mindset, and both show up clearly in how they interview Engineering Managers.

The interview process typically runs across several rounds covering people leadership, engineering design thinking, product sense, and a values conversation with senior leaders. Candidates report that the emphasis on real customer impact sets Freshworks interviews apart from generic EM processes at other product companies.

Salary bands for Engineering Manager roles sit at 35-60 LPA at the Manager level, 55-90 LPA at Senior Manager, and 90-150+ LPA at Director, based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026.

Most Asked Questions

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a time you managed an underperforming engineer.

*Situation:* One of my senior engineers had been missing sprint commitments repeatedly over two quarters. The team had started working around him, which was hurting morale and creating knowledge silos.

*Task:* I needed to address the performance issue fairly, protect team morale, and ideally retain someone who had deep domain knowledge.

*Action:* I started with a candid one-on-one to understand what was happening. It turned out he was dealing with unclear expectations and felt disconnected from the product direction. I set up a structured improvement plan with weekly check-ins, paired him with a buddy on a well-scoped project, and made sure he attended roadmap review meetings going forward so he had full context.

*Result:* His output improved within the agreed timeframe and he became a key contributor to a customer-facing launch. The team noticed the change, and it raised trust in how the team handled these conversations.

---

Q: Describe a disagreement with a Product Manager over scope or priority.

*Situation:* Before a major release, the Product Manager wanted to add a new integration feature to the sprint that the team had not planned for. This would have pushed us past our committed delivery date.

*Task:* I had to protect the team's capacity and the release date while keeping a good working relationship with the PM and ensuring the customer value of the new feature was not lost.

*Action:* I asked the PM to walk me through the customer urgency behind the new feature. We found it was driven by one large enterprise account, not a broad need. I proposed a phased approach: ship the planned release on time, then tackle the integration in the next sprint with proper scoping. I also looped in the account manager so the customer got a clear timeline.

*Result:* We shipped on time, the integration went out in the following sprint without last-minute pressure, and the PM appreciated the structured process. We now use a similar 'customer urgency check' before any scope additions.

---

Q: How have you improved delivery velocity without burning out your team?

*Situation:* My team was consistently overrunning sprint commitments, leading to weekend work and early signs of burnout. Deadlines were slipping and team satisfaction was dropping.

*Task:* I needed to improve delivery predictability while actually reducing the pressure people were under.

*Action:* I ran a retrospective focused purely on where time was going. We found that unplanned support requests were consuming a large chunk of each engineer's week. I introduced a rotating 'support champion' role so only one person handled incoming requests at any time, and the rest could focus. I also reduced work-in-progress limits per engineer and moved to smaller, more shippable stories.

*Result:* Sprint completion improved noticeably over the following quarters and weekend work dropped to near zero. The team reported feeling more in control, and we shipped more features per quarter than before.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The STAR framework (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the standard structure Freshworks interviewers expect for behavioral questions. Keep Situation and Task brief, go deeper on the specific actions you took, and close with a clear result that connects to customer or business impact.

People, Process, Product is a useful mental model when you get an open-ended question about your leadership approach. Structure your thinking around how you grow people, how you improve delivery processes, and how you connect engineering work to product outcomes. Freshworks interviewers value all three, but they typically weight people and product thinking most.

The Customer Impact lens is particularly important at Freshworks. Before you close any STAR answer, add a sentence connecting the outcome to the customer or business. Even a purely internal process improvement should link back to how it helped the team serve customers better.

For technical design or trade-off questions, think in terms of options and decisions rather than 'correct answers'. Freshworks values pragmatic engineering leadership. Frame your answer as: what you optimized for, what you deprioritized, and why that was the right call given the context and constraints.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Customer obsession in every answer. Freshworks builds for customers, and EMs are expected to keep that lens at all times. Interviewers want to see that your engineering decisions, process choices, and people decisions all ultimately connect back to customer outcomes.

Real people leadership depth. Generic answers like 'I trust my team' will not pass at Freshworks. They want specifics: how you handled a difficult engineer, how you built psychological safety, how you ran a tough performance conversation. The more concrete and honest, the better.

Technical credibility without micromanagement. You do not need to be writing code, but you need to show that you can have a real technical conversation with your team, challenge design decisions, and spot when a technical choice will create future problems.

Product thinking. Freshworks EMs work closely with Product. Interviewers will check whether you understand product trade-offs, can push back on poor requirements, and care about what ships, not just that something shipped.

Frugal innovation mindset. This is a Freshworks-specific value. They want leaders who find smart ways to deliver more with less: smaller teams, faster cycles, pragmatic tech choices. If you have examples of building something meaningful without large budgets or headcount, bring them.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the company and the role first.

Read Freshworks' own product pages and recent blog posts to understand Freshdesk, Freshsales, Freshservice, and Freshchat. Note which customer segments each product targets, primarily SMBs. Read the specific job description closely and note which teams and products are mentioned.

Build your story bank.

Prepare several STAR stories covering: hiring and onboarding, performance management, cross-functional conflict, technical trade-offs, production incidents, and delivery process improvements. For each story, add a closing line that connects the result to customer impact. This theme will come up in almost every round.

Practice and calibrate.

Do mock interviews with a peer or record yourself answering the most-asked questions above. Check whether your answers are specific enough. Vague answers like 'I improved team morale' will not land at Freshworks. You need the concrete actions you took and the observable change that followed.

Before each round.

Prepare two to three genuine questions for each interviewer. Questions about how engineering and product collaborate at Freshworks, how the team measures customer success, or what the biggest engineering challenges are for the specific team all signal the right kind of curiosity.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving process answers instead of personal answers. Saying 'I would use a structured feedback process' is much weaker than 'here is exactly what I did, step by step, in a real situation.' Freshworks interviewers have heard every textbook answer. They want your specific experience.

Over-indexing on technical depth. Some EM candidates spend too long on architecture and system design details, trying to prove they are still engineers. Freshworks wants to see that you have moved into a leadership mindset. Lead with the people and process dimension, then show technical credibility, not the other way around.

Not connecting outcomes to customers. A delivery success story that ends with 'we shipped on time' is incomplete at Freshworks. Add what the customer experienced as a result. Candidates report this is the most common gap in post-interview feedback.

Underestimating the product sense questions. Engineering Managers at Freshworks are expected to have real product opinions. If you have never pushed back on a product requirement or contributed to a roadmap discussion, think hard about examples where you engaged at that level.

Not researching Freshworks products before the interview. Not knowing what Freshdesk does, or confusing Freshsales with Freshservice, signals low interest in the company. Spend time with the free trials of the products closest to the role you are applying for.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Freshworks Engineering Manager interview process typically have?

Candidates report the Freshworks EM process typically runs across four to six rounds. These usually include a recruiter screening, one or two people and leadership conversations, an engineering design or systems thinking round, and a final conversation with a senior leader. The exact structure varies by team and location, so ask your recruiter for the specific format after your first call.

What salary can I expect as an Engineering Manager at Freshworks?

Based on knok jobradar data as of July 2026, Engineering Manager roles at Freshworks sit in the 35-60 LPA range, Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range, and Director-level roles at 90-150+ LPA. Actual offers vary by experience, team size managed, and negotiation. Platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi have community-reported data that can help you benchmark a specific offer before you accept.

Is there a coding round in the Freshworks Engineering Manager interview?

Most candidates report that Freshworks does not require live coding for EM roles, though some teams include a technical design or systems thinking conversation to assess credibility. The focus is much more on leadership, people management, and product thinking than on writing code. Confirm with your recruiter whether any technical exercise is part of your specific loop, since it can vary by team.

How long does the Freshworks EM hiring process take from first call to offer?

Candidates typically report the full process taking two to four weeks from the first recruiter call to an offer, though this can vary based on interviewer availability and internal feedback timelines. Freshworks generally moves faster than large MNC product companies. Following up with your recruiter after each round is a reasonable way to stay informed on where you stand.

Do I need prior SaaS experience to get an Engineering Manager role at Freshworks?

SaaS experience is helpful but candidates from non-SaaS product backgrounds do get hired. What matters more is demonstrated experience leading customer-facing engineering teams and a clear understanding of how your decisions impacted end customers. If your background is in enterprise software or services, frame your stories around the customers your products served and highlight any exposure to subscription or product-led growth models.

Which Freshworks products should I know before the interview?

At a minimum, understand what Freshdesk (customer support), Freshsales (CRM), Freshservice (IT service management), and Freshchat (messaging) do and who the target customers are. Signing up for a free trial of the product closest to the team you are interviewing for gives you a genuine feel for the UX and the kinds of engineering challenges involved. Interviewers at Freshworks notice and appreciate when candidates have actually used the product.
