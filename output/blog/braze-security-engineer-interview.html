01 Overview

Overview

Braze is a customer engagement platform that powers marketing for global brands, handling large volumes of customer data across email, push, SMS, and in-app channels. A Security Engineer at Braze is expected to protect that data pipeline, support compliance, and partner with product and engineering teams to ship secure features.

As of July 2026, Braze had 252 open roles across the company, signaling active hiring. Of the 628 Security Engineer jobs tracked across India by knok jobradar, Bangalore accounts for 69 openings, making it the strongest city for this role.

Candidates report that the Braze interview process typically runs across three to four rounds: a recruiter call, a technical screening focused on security fundamentals, a scenario or take-home exercise, and a panel with the security and engineering teams. The process can vary by level and team, so treat this as a guide rather than a guarantee.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have gone through Braze security interviews, or similar SaaS security-engineer rounds, commonly report these questions:

  1. Walk me through a security incident you handled from detection to resolution.
  2. How do you threat-model a new product feature before it ships?
  3. What is your experience with SOC 2 or ISO compliance frameworks, and how have you contributed to an audit?
  4. How would you secure a multi-tenant SaaS environment where one customer's data must never reach another?
  5. Describe your approach to secrets management in a cloud-native or containerized environment.
  6. How do you prioritize vulnerabilities when you have more findings than engineering bandwidth to fix them?
  7. What security controls would you apply to a new third-party vendor integration?
  8. Describe a time you influenced a product or engineering team to change how they handled sensitive data.
  9. How would you detect and investigate a potential data exfiltration event?
  10. Walk me through a penetration test or vulnerability assessment you contributed to.
  11. How do you keep a fast-moving engineering team from introducing security regressions in a continuous deployment setup?
  12. What experience do you have with SIEM platforms, security automation, or detection-as-code approaches?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk me through a security incident you handled from detection to resolution.

*Situation:* While on call at my previous company, our SIEM flagged a spike in failed authentication attempts against an internal API that handled customer PII.

*Task:* My job was to assess whether this was an active attack, contain any damage, and coordinate a full remediation.

*Action:* I started by reviewing the alert context and confirmed the pattern looked like credential stuffing. I immediately rate-limited the endpoint at the WAF layer, rotated the service credentials involved, and looped in the backend team and the data privacy lead. I also pulled the relevant logs into our incident timeline so we had a clear chain of events for the post-mortem.

*Result:* We contained the attempt before any accounts were compromised, documented the incident fully, and used it to push through a long-pending MFA enforcement policy for internal APIs.

---

Q: How would you secure a multi-tenant SaaS environment?

*Situation:* At my previous employer, we onboarded a large enterprise customer who required strict data isolation as a contract condition.

*Task:* I led the security review of our tenancy model to verify that one customer's data could not be accessed by another, even in edge cases.

*Action:* I mapped every place where tenant identifiers were passed through the stack, reviewed our database query layer for missing tenant-scope filters, and ran a targeted test to simulate a misconfigured request. I also recommended adding automated checks in the CI pipeline to catch missing tenant scoping in new code.

*Result:* We passed the customer's third-party audit, discovered and fixed two minor edge cases before they were exploited, and built a repeatable checklist the team now uses for every new feature.

---

Q: Describe a time you influenced an engineering team to adopt a security practice.

*Situation:* Our engineering team was committing secrets directly into the repository because the existing secrets management setup was seen as 'too slow' for local development.

*Task:* I needed to fix the root cause without asking developers to slow down their work.

*Action:* I sat with several senior engineers to understand the exact friction points, then set up a developer-friendly secrets injection workflow using a vault tool. I ran a short lunch-and-learn, wrote a one-page quick-start guide, and got the team lead to make it part of standard onboarding.

*Result:* Secret commits dropped to near zero within a month, and the practice was later adopted by two other teams in the organization.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Use STAR for behavioral questions. Braze interviewers, candidates report, tend to probe depth with follow-up questions, so do not stop at the surface story. After stating the Result, be ready to explain what you would do differently now.

For technical scenario questions, think out loud in layers. Start with the threat you are defending against, move to the control you would apply, then cover how you would monitor and respond if that control fails. This shows structured security thinking rather than a list of tool names.

Quantify where you can, but be honest about estimates. Saying 'we went from manually reviewing logs once a week to automated alerting that fires in minutes' is stronger than a vague claim about improving response time. If you do not have a precise number, say 'roughly' or 'our estimate at the time was'.

For compliance questions, show you understand the 'why'. Explaining what a control actually prevents is more impressive than reciting a list of framework requirements. Braze handles large volumes of marketing data, so data minimization, access controls, and audit logging are themes that resonate with their context.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Product-security instinct. Braze is a product company, not a consultancy. Interviewers want engineers who can look at a feature spec and spot the security implications early, not ones who only review after the code is written.

Collaboration over gatekeeping. Security Engineers here are expected to work alongside product and engineering, not block them. Show examples where you found a way to be secure and fast at the same time.

Cloud and SaaS fluency. Braze runs on cloud infrastructure and processes high-throughput data. Familiarity with cloud security controls, identity and access management, and API security will come up in most rounds.

Incident response readiness. The ability to stay calm, triage quickly, and communicate clearly under pressure matters. Practice telling your incident stories in a crisp, structured way.

Compliance as context, not as a crutch. Knowing SOC 2 and ISO compliance frameworks helps, but interviewers care more about whether you understand what the controls actually protect than whether you can name them.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: Review Braze's public security posture. Read their trust and privacy pages, their engineering blog, and any public incident reports. Note the kinds of data they handle and the regulations they likely comply with. This gives you context to make your answers feel relevant rather than generic.

Week two: Strengthen your scenario practice. Take one security design prompt per day, such as 'design the access control model for a multi-tenant SaaS dashboard', and write out your approach. Review it against common threat categories before looking at model answers.

Week three: Polish your STAR stories. Write out four to five stories covering an incident you handled, a time you influenced a team, a compliance project you contributed to, and a vulnerability you found and fixed. Practice each out loud until it flows naturally in conversation.

Before each round: Re-read the job description and map your stories to the specific responsibilities listed. Braze had 252 open roles as of July 2026, which suggests active scaling. Framing your experience around enabling growth securely will resonate more than a purely defensive posture.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Talking tools instead of thinking. Listing certifications or tool names without explaining how you used them to solve a real problem does not impress security interviewers. Lead with the problem and the outcome, then mention the tool.

Being vague about your own contribution. In team incidents or projects, clearly state what you personally did. 'We fixed it' is weak. 'I identified the root cause, coordinated the patch with two engineers, and wrote the post-mortem' is strong.

Treating compliance as the goal. Saying your job is 'to achieve SOC 2 compliance' signals a checkbox mindset. Talk about compliance frameworks as a tool for improving actual security outcomes, not as the outcome itself.

Ignoring the developer experience angle. If your security fix would slow down shipping significantly, mention how you thought about reducing that friction. Braze is a product company and developer velocity matters to the teams you will work with.

Not asking good questions at the end. Asking nothing, or asking only generic questions about culture, wastes the end-of-interview opportunity. Ask about how the security team is structured, what their biggest current challenges are, or how they measure security program maturity.

Is a security certification required to get hired at Braze?

Braze's job postings for Security Engineer roles typically list certifications as 'nice to have' rather than required. Candidates report that demonstrated hands-on experience with cloud security, incident response, and secure development practices carries more weight than certifications alone. That said, relevant certifications can help your resume clear the initial screen, especially for mid-to-senior roles.

What cloud platform experience does Braze look for in security candidates?

Braze runs on cloud infrastructure, and candidates report that familiarity with AWS or GCP security controls, IAM, and network security is commonly tested. How should I talk about compliance frameworks in the Braze interview?

Show that you understand what the controls actually protect, not just that you know the framework exists. Explaining why encryption at rest matters for a specific data type is more impressive than reciting requirement names. Braze handles large volumes of customer engagement data, so data privacy and access control connect directly to their business context and will land better than abstract compliance talk.
