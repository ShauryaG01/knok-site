01 Overview

Overview

GrayQuest is an edtech-fintech company that helps Indian families pay school and college fees through structured monthly installments, partnering directly with institutions to make education costs manageable. As a Product Manager here, you sit at the crossroads of consumer lending, school partnerships, and digital onboarding, which makes interviews focused on both product thinking and fintech domain awareness.

The company had 42 open roles as of mid-2026, a sign of active team growth across product, tech, and business functions. PM interviews at GrayQuest typically cover product sense, analytical reasoning, and past behavioural experience. Candidates report anywhere from two to four rounds, including a product case, a metrics or data discussion, and a leadership or culture round.

Among the 2,009 Product Manager openings tracked by knok jobradar as of July 2026, Bangalore leads with 271 roles and Delhi follows with 177, both cities where GrayQuest has a notable presence. Salary ranges for the role sit at 12-20 LPA for Associate PM, 24-40 LPA for PM with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA for Senior PM, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PM levels.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you investigate a drop in on-time repayments at GrayQuest?

*Situation:* In a previous fintech role, our monthly repayment completion rate dropped noticeably over two consecutive weeks and leadership needed a root cause quickly.

*Task:* As the PM on the repayments squad, I had to diagnose the issue, coordinate with engineering and collections, and propose a fix before month-end.

*Action:* I started by segmenting the drop: new versus returning borrowers, city, loan tenure, and payment channel. I found the drop was concentrated among first-time borrowers who had chosen UPI auto-debit. A recent backend change had shifted the debit timing by one day, causing mandates to trigger when accounts ran low before salary credit. I worked with engineering to roll back the timing change and with the CX team to send proactive reminders to the affected cohort.

*Result:* Repayment completion recovered to its previous level within the next billing cycle, and we added a 'low balance' early warning notification to the roadmap to prevent recurrence.

---

Q: How would you design onboarding for a parent who has never used a digital lending product?

*Situation:* At a previous company, we were expanding into Tier 2 markets where many users were digital-first only because of smartphones, not because of any prior fintech experience.

*Task:* I was tasked with redesigning the onboarding flow to reduce drop-off at the KYC and mandate setup steps.

*Action:* I ran user interviews with parents in smaller cities, many of whom were confused by terms like 'NACH mandate' and 'credit assessment.' I proposed replacing jargon with plain language, adding a short video explainer in the regional language, and breaking the flow into a checklist of small steps rather than one long form. I also added a 'save and resume' feature so parents could complete onboarding across multiple sittings.

*Result:* Candidates report that user-tested redesigns of this kind typically improve onboarding completion in low-familiarity segments. In our case, drop-off at the KYC step came down significantly in the pilot cohort, though I cannot share the exact figure due to confidentiality.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you balanced B2B and B2C needs that were in conflict.

*Situation:* A school partner wanted a feature that would let them see each parent's repayment status in real time, which raised privacy concerns for parents who had not consented to that visibility.

*Task:* I needed to satisfy the school's need for oversight without exposing sensitive financial data to third parties.

*Action:* I facilitated a workshop with the school's admin team and our legal and product teams. We agreed on a middle ground: schools could see a traffic-light status (on track, overdue, needs attention) without seeing the rupee amount or account details. We updated our consent flow so parents knew this summary was shared, and gave them an opt-out path.

*Result:* The school accepted the solution, parent complaints on the issue dropped to near zero in the following quarter, and we used the same consent framework across other school partners.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Product sense questions respond well to the CIRCLES method: clarify the goal, identify the user, report on needs, cut the scope, list solutions, evaluate trade-offs, summarise. Apply this when asked to design or improve a feature.

Metrics and investigation questions benefit from a structured diagnosis approach. Start by confirming the data is accurate, then segment by user type, geography, device, and channel before proposing a cause. This shows you treat data rigorously and do not guess.

Prioritisation questions are well suited to RICE scoring: Reach (how many users), Impact (how much does it move the needle), Confidence (how sure are you), Effort (how much engineering time). For GrayQuest, always tie Reach and Impact back to the business model: school partnerships and repayment health are the two levers that matter most.

Behavioural questions follow STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep the Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences each), spend most of your time on Action, and make the Result concrete. If you cannot share exact numbers due to confidentiality, say so honestly and describe the direction of change.

Fintech-specific framing: GrayQuest operates in a regulated space under RBI guidelines for NBFCs and lending products. When discussing product decisions, show awareness of compliance constraints alongside user experience trade-offs. Interviewers value candidates who treat regulation as a design input, not an afterthought.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain curiosity over domain expertise. GrayQuest interviewers typically want to see that you understand the problem they are solving: making education affordable for Indian families. You do not need a lending background, but you should be able to discuss why trust, transparency, and simplicity matter in a first-time borrower experience.

Structured thinking under pressure. Expect product cases with incomplete information. Interviewers are watching whether you ask clarifying questions, state your assumptions, and organise your answer before diving in. Rushing to a solution without framing the problem is a common reason candidates do not clear the case round.

Stakeholder fluency. Because GrayQuest serves both schools and parents, interviewers pay attention to how you talk about multiple user types. Show that you can hold both perspectives and make principled trade-offs when their needs conflict.

Data literacy without over-reliance on data. You should be comfortable discussing metrics, funnels, and cohort behaviour, but interviewers also want to see qualitative judgment. Not every decision has clean data behind it, especially in a growth-stage company.

Ownership and hustle. Candidates report that interviewers respond well to examples where you took initiative, worked across teams without formal authority, and moved a metric or shipped a feature despite ambiguity.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Know the product and the market. Use GrayQuest's app or explore it the way a school admin would. Understand the end-to-end flow: how a school signs up, how a parent applies, how repayments work. Read up on NBFC lending regulations in India and how RBI guidelines shape product decisions for consumer lending apps.

Week 2: Practice product cases with a fintech lens. Take standard PM case questions and adapt them to GrayQuest's context: fee financing, mandate setup, repayment nudges, school onboarding. Practice the CIRCLES and RICE frameworks out loud until you can structure an answer clearly before diving into details.

Week 3: Prepare your STAR stories. Pull out four to six experiences that cover: a product you launched, a metric you diagnosed, a stakeholder conflict you resolved, and a time you operated under regulatory or legal constraints. Write out the STAR structure for each and rehearse the Action portion, since that is where most candidates go thin.

Week 4: Mock rounds and research. Do at least two mock interviews with someone who can give honest feedback. Research GrayQuest's recent news, any announced partnerships or city expansions, and the broader edtech-fintech landscape in India. Being able to reference something specific about the company's direction shows genuine interest and prepares you for 'why GrayQuest?' questions.

Ongoing: Follow fintech and edtech communities on LinkedIn. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating GrayQuest like a pure B2C app. The school partnership (B2B) side is as important as the parent (B2C) side. Candidates who design only for parents and ignore the school admin experience miss half the product surface.

Jumping to solutions before diagnosing. On metrics questions, interviewers want to see you investigate before you prescribe. State that you would first check data quality, then segment by cohort, then hypothesise. Skipping straight to 'we should send a push notification' signals shallow thinking and is one of the most commonly cited reasons candidates do not clear analytical rounds.

Using generic fintech examples. Saying 'I would look at daily active users and retention' without tying it to GrayQuest's actual business model (school cohorts, fee cycles, repayment calendars) makes your answer sound recycled. Adapt every example to the context.

Ignoring regulatory constraints. Lending products in India operate under RBI guidelines, and features like auto-debit mandates and credit assessment have strict rules. Candidates who design features without acknowledging these constraints come across as inexperienced in the fintech domain.

Weak 'why GrayQuest?' answers. Growth-stage companies care about motivation. 'I want to work in fintech' is not enough. Be specific about what draws you to the education financing space and what you want to build here.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a GrayQuest PM interview typically have?

Candidates report typically two to four rounds covering a product case, a metrics or analytical discussion, and a behavioural or culture round. The exact structure can vary by seniority and the hiring manager. It is worth asking the recruiter about the process after you receive the first call so you can prepare for each stage accordingly.

Do I need a fintech background to apply for a PM role at GrayQuest?

Not necessarily. Candidates from consumer app, edtech, and B2B SaaS backgrounds have joined fintech PM teams at similar growth-stage companies. What matters more is your ability to learn the domain quickly and demonstrate structured thinking. Brush up on basic lending concepts like KYC, NACH mandates, and NBFC regulations before your first round so you can discuss product decisions in the right context.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at GrayQuest?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India run from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience, and 40-60 LPA for Senior PMs. Actual offers depend on your experience, negotiation, and the specific role. For company-specific data points, check platforms like Glassdoor and levels.fyi alongside your recruiter conversation.

How should I prepare for a product case on GrayQuest's product?

Start by using or thoroughly exploring GrayQuest's app as both a parent and a hypothetical school admin. Practice applying the CIRCLES framework to fee financing scenarios: designing a repayment reminder feature, improving the school onboarding flow, or reducing drop-off at the KYC step. Tie every design decision back to GrayQuest's core goal of making education fees manageable for Indian families, since that framing signals genuine alignment with the product mission.

As of July 2026, GrayQuest had 42 open roles across functions according to knok jobradar data. What is the best way to answer 'why GrayQuest?' in an interview?

Be specific rather than generic. Connect your interest to something real: the problem of school fee stress for Indian families, the B2B2C model that requires both school and parent empathy, or GrayQuest's focus on a segment that traditional banks underserve. Avoid phrases like 'I want to work in a fast-growing startup' without backing them up with what specifically about GrayQuest's mission draws you in, since growth-stage interviewers consistently probe for genuine motivation.
