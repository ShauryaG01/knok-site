03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Based on what is commonly reported for mid-size recruiting firms, SG2 Recruiting's hiring process is likely to follow a structure similar to this. The exact stages and timelines can vary by role, so treat this as a general guide.

Stage 1: Application and initial screen
You apply online or through a job portal. A recruiter reviews your profile and may send a short screening questionnaire or call you for a brief initial conversation to confirm basic fit, location, and availability.

Stage 2: HR or talent acquisition interview
This is usually a video or phone call with someone from the internal HR or TA team. Expect questions about why you want to work in recruiting, your experience with targets or KPIs, and how you handle rejection. Since this is a recruiting firm, the person interviewing you will be assessing your communication style closely.

Stage 3: Functional or hiring manager round
A deeper conversation with the manager of the team you are joining. For a recruiter role this might include a practical exercise, such as sourcing a candidate profile or writing a job description on the spot. For non-recruiting roles (operations, marketing, finance) this round focuses on your domain skills.

Stage 4: Case or role-play (for recruiting roles)
Many recruiting firms include a short simulation. You might be asked to do a mock cold call to a candidate, pitch a job to a 'candidate', or walk through how you would fill a difficult role. This is the stage where preparation makes the biggest difference.

Stage 5: Final discussion and offer
Some candidates have a final conversation with a senior leader before the offer is extended. Compensation, incentive structure, and start date are discussed at this point.

Overall, the process typically wraps up in a few weeks, though timelines can be faster when a role is urgent.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Recruiting firms look for a specific combination of traits that do not always show up clearly on a CV. Here is what tends to matter at companies like SG2 Recruiting.

Communication that is clear and warm. You will be speaking to candidates and clients every day. The ability to explain a role simply, handle objections gracefully, and follow up without being pushy is something interviewers actively test for.

Comfort with targets and accountability. Recruiting is a metrics-driven business. Commonly cited KPIs in the industry include calls made, profiles sourced, interviews scheduled, and offers closed. If you have worked in sales, business development, or any target-based role, that experience translates well here.

Curiosity about industries and people. The best recruiters are genuinely interested in what different companies do and what motivates different candidates. Showing in interviews that you follow hiring trends or understand the sectors SG2 serves can set you apart from other candidates.

Resilience. Rejection is a daily reality in recruiting. Candidates drop out, clients change requirements, and offers fall through. Interviewers at recruiting firms often probe for how you handle this, so prepare a real example from your past.

Organised follow-through. Managing multiple open positions at once requires a system. Whether you use a spreadsheet, a CRM, or a structured notebook, being able to describe how you track your pipeline shows operational maturity.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Research the firm before your first call.
Look at SG2 Recruiting's LinkedIn page, check which sectors they recruit for, and note any recent hires or announcements. Interviewers notice when a candidate has done their homework, and at a recruiting company it signals exactly the kind of diligence they want on their team.

Prepare your 'why recruiting' story.
This question comes up in almost every recruiting interview. Avoid generic answers like 'I like people'. Instead, connect it to a specific moment: a time you helped someone land a role, coached a friend through a job search, or realised you were the go-to person for career advice in your network.

Practice a mock sourcing exercise.
If you are applying for a recruiter or talent acquisition role, run through a real exercise on your own. Pick a sample job description, find a few matching profiles, and write a couple of lines on why each fits. This kind of preparation is hard to fake and easy to demonstrate in the interview.

Quantify your past experience.
Even if your previous job was not in recruiting, put numbers to your work wherever you can. Industry surveys suggest candidates who cite specific results in interviews are more likely to move forward. Think about volume (how many clients, calls, or projects), speed (how quickly you delivered), and outcomes (what improved as a result).

Have questions ready about growth and incentives.
SG2 Recruiting is actively hiring, with 25 open roles as of mid-2026. Asking about team structure, learning and development, and how the incentive scheme works shows you are thinking about a real career here, not just a job.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Recruiting firms tend to build cultures that are fast-paced, target-oriented, and collaborative within teams, even when individual performance is measured closely. SG2 Recruiting, as a growing firm with 25 active openings, is likely in a phase where the culture is still being shaped. That can be both an opportunity and a consideration depending on what you are looking for.

The opportunity side: Joining during a growth phase often means faster career movement, more visibility with leadership, and a real chance to help shape how processes get built. People who join early in a firm's expansion often look back on it as the most formative period of their career.

What to clarify before you join: Growth-phase companies can also mean shifting targets, evolving processes, and less formal structure. In your interviews, ask specifically about onboarding, how performance is reviewed in the first few months, and what support is available while you ramp up. These are fair questions and a good employer will have clear answers.

In general, people who do well at recruiting firms enjoy a sense of daily momentum, are comfortable with some ambiguity, and find satisfaction in closing placements. If you prefer slow-moving, highly structured environments, it is worth being honest with yourself about that before accepting an offer.

Frequently asked

Does SG2 Recruiting hire people with no prior recruiting experience?

It depends on the role. Many recruiting firms have entry-level or associate positions designed for career changers who bring transferable skills from sales, customer service, or HR. If you are applying with no recruiting background, focus your application on communication skills, any experience meeting targets, and genuine enthusiasm for the industry. Highlight moments where you connected people to opportunities, even informally.

How long does the SG2 Recruiting interview process typically take from application to offer?

For most mid-size recruiting firms, the process wraps up in a few weeks when everything moves smoothly. If a role is urgent, some candidates report receiving an offer within a week of their first call. The safest approach is to follow up politely after each stage if you have not heard back within a few business days, since recruiters are often managing multiple open positions and a gentle nudge keeps you top of mind.

What is the work environment like at a recruiting firm?

Recruiting offices tend to be high-energy environments where the team is always working multiple open positions at once. Remote and hybrid setups are common at many firms now, but some prefer in-office arrangements for junior hires to make onboarding faster. Ask specifically about the setup for your role during the interview so there are no surprises on day one.

Is the pay mostly fixed salary or commission-based at SG2 Recruiting?

Most recruiting firms offer a fixed base salary plus a variable incentive tied to placements or billings. The exact structure varies by role and seniority. Industry surveys suggest the base-to-variable split is usually discussed during the final offer stage, so it is reasonable to ask about this clearly before you sign. Make sure you understand what targets you need to hit to earn the variable component.

What skills should I highlight on my resume when applying to SG2 Recruiting?

Prioritise anything that shows you can manage relationships, work to targets, and stay organised under pressure. Keywords that commonly appear in recruiting job descriptions include candidate sourcing, stakeholder management, applicant tracking systems, pipeline management, and offer negotiation. If you have used tools like Naukri, LinkedIn Recruiter, or any ATS, mention them by name and quantify your past work wherever possible.
