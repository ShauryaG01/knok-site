01 Overview

Overview

HighRadius is a fintech SaaS company whose AI-powered products help large enterprises automate accounts receivable, cash application, and treasury operations. Data Scientists at HighRadius work on problems like payment matching, credit risk scoring, collections prioritisation, and NLP extraction from financial documents, so the interview tests both ML fundamentals and the ability to connect models to real finance workflows.

As of July 2026, HighRadius had 71 open Data Scientist roles on knok's radar, making it one of the more active hirers in this niche. Candidates typically report 3 to 5 rounds: an online screen covering aptitude or coding, one or two technical interviews on ML and statistics, a domain or product round focused on finance use cases, and a final HR conversation.

Salary bands from knok job radar data show entry-level roles (0-2 years) in the 8-16 LPA range, mid-level (3-5 years) in the 18-30 LPA range, senior profiles (6-9 years) in the 30-48 LPA range, and lead or principal-level positions at 45-70+ LPA.

The interview leans more product-aware than a typical DS interview. Interviewers want to see that you can connect your ML work to business outcomes, not just optimise a metric in isolation.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in candidate-reported HighRadius Data Scientist interviews. Prepare concrete answers for each.

  1. Walk me through how you would build a cash application model that auto-matches incoming payments to open invoices.
  2. How do you handle class imbalance when training a fraud or exception detection model on AR transaction data?
  3. Describe your approach to credit risk scoring for a new B2B customer with little payment history.
  4. HighRadius uses NLP to read remittance advice emails. What NLP pipeline would you design, and what pre-trained models would you consider?
  5. How would you build a time series model to forecast daily cash collections for a large enterprise client?
  6. You deploy a model and its performance drops noticeably three months after go-live. How do you diagnose and recover?
  7. How do you explain a complex model prediction to a CFO or finance head who has no ML background?
  8. What is the difference between precision and recall? When would you prioritise one over the other in a collections context?
  9. You have a large dataset of invoice records with missing values and outliers. What is your data cleaning strategy before modelling?
  10. Tell me about a time your analysis directly changed a business decision.
  11. How would you design an A/B test to check whether a new collections prioritisation model actually improves recovery rates?
  12. What ML frameworks and tools have you used end to end in production, and what was the hardest engineering challenge you faced?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioural and case questions. Three examples below.

---

Q: How do you explain a complex model to a non-technical finance stakeholder?

*Situation:* At my previous company, I built a customer churn propensity model that finance leadership needed to trust before acting on its output.

*Task:* My job was to present the model to a CFO who cared about revenue impact, not F1 scores.

*Action:* I dropped all technical jargon and built a one-page visual showing which accounts were most at risk and what it would cost to lose them. I used SHAP values to show the top risk drivers per account in plain words: 'this customer is flagged mainly because payment delays have been increasing over the past quarter.' I also ran a small retrospective on past accounts to show the model would have caught real churners earlier.

*Result:* The CFO approved a pilot immediately. The team adopted the model into their weekly workflow within a month, which was faster adoption than any prior analytics project.

---

Q: A deployed model's performance drops after go-live. How do you handle it?

*Situation:* During an internship, a payment classification model I helped deploy started misclassifying a higher share of transactions after a client changed their ERP system.

*Task:* I needed to identify the root cause and propose a fix without taking the model fully offline.

*Action:* I compared feature distributions between training data and recent live data using population stability index checks. I found that several key fields had shifted significantly because of the ERP change. I retrained on a rolling window that included the new data format and added a drift-monitoring alert to catch similar shifts earlier.

*Result:* Model performance recovered within the next billing cycle, and the drift alert caught a second data shift before it could affect business reporting.

---

Q: Tell me about a time your analysis changed a business decision.

*Situation:* My team was about to launch a blanket early-payment discount programme to improve cash flow.

*Task:* I was asked to estimate expected uptake before the finance team committed the budget.

*Action:* I segmented customers by historical payment behaviour and built a logistic regression model to predict which accounts were likely to use the discount versus those who would have paid early anyway. The model showed that a large share of the projected discount cost would go to customers who did not need the incentive.

*Result:* The team narrowed the programme to the high-propensity segment only, which cut the cost of the initiative significantly while still achieving the cash collection target.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical design questions (model building, pipeline design): use a Problem-Approach-Tradeoff structure. State the business problem clearly, describe your chosen approach and why, then name at least one alternative you considered and the tradeoff that led to your choice. Interviewers at product companies like HighRadius want to see that you think in tradeoffs, not just in 'best practices.'

For ML theory questions (precision vs recall, bias vs variance, class imbalance): anchor your answer in a concrete AR or finance example. Saying 'I would optimise recall in fraud detection because a missed fraud costs more than a false positive' lands better than a textbook definition.

For behavioural questions (tell me about a time...): use STAR cleanly. Keep Situation and Task short, spend most of your time on Action (what you specifically did), and always close with a measurable or observable Result.

For product or case questions (how would you design X): start by clarifying the objective and constraints before jumping to a solution. Candidates report that HighRadius interviewers pay close attention to whether you ask the right questions upfront rather than rushing to an answer.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain awareness matters as much as ML skill. HighRadius builds products for finance teams, so interviewers want to see that you understand concepts like days sales outstanding, cash application, collections waterfall, and credit exposure. You do not need to be a chartered accountant, but you should be able to speak the language comfortably.

Production mindset, not just notebook mindset. Questions about model monitoring, data drift, retraining pipelines, and feature stores come up regularly. Candidates who have only worked in Jupyter environments without thinking about deployment tend to struggle in later rounds.

Communication to non-technical audiences. Many HighRadius clients are CFOs and treasury heads. Interviewers probe whether you can translate model outputs into business language. Practice explaining your past work as if you are briefing a finance director, not a data science peer.

Python and SQL hands-on ability. Candidates report SQL questions appearing even in rounds that felt ML-focused. Brush up on window functions, aggregations, and pandas. If your resume mentions deep learning or NLP, be ready to go deeper on those areas.

Curiosity and initiative. Interviewers look for candidates who proactively found a problem worth solving, not just those who solved problems handed to them.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: ML and statistics fundamentals
Revise classification, regression, clustering, and ensemble methods. Focus on intuition, not just maths. Practise explaining bias-variance tradeoff, regularisation, and cross-validation out loud. Solve several SQL problems involving window functions and aggregations to keep those skills sharp.

Week 2: Finance domain and HighRadius products
Read about order-to-cash processes: the invoice-to-cash cycle, cash application, credit management, and collections. Explore the HighRadius website to understand their product lines. You do not need deep accounting knowledge, just enough to connect ML techniques to those workflows naturally.

Week 3: Applied ML and coding practice
Build or revisit one end-to-end project involving imbalanced classification or time series forecasting. Practise SHAP or LIME for model explainability. Do at least one NLP mini-project covering entity extraction or text classification, since remittance NLP is a core HighRadius use case.

Week 4: Mock interviews and STAR stories
Prepare several STAR stories from your past work covering different problem types. Practise one full mock interview with a peer. Record yourself explaining a technical concept to a pretend finance stakeholder. Review your resume and make sure every project listed has a business outcome attached, not just a model accuracy number.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure academic ML interview. Candidates who lead with loss functions and skip the business context often do not make it past the domain round. Always tie your answers back to the financial or operational outcome.

Not researching HighRadius products. Interviewers notice when a candidate has clearly not read what the company actually builds. Spend an hour on their product pages before your first round.

Overclaiming model performance. If you cite a specific accuracy from a past project, interviewers will dig into the baseline, the evaluation setup, and the business impact. Be honest and know your numbers inside out.

Weak SQL. Several candidates report being surprised by SQL questions in what felt like an ML-focused round. Do not neglect SQL prep, especially window functions and complex aggregations.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'we improved the process' without a concrete result makes your answer forgettable. Always close with an observable or measurable outcome, even a qualitative one.

No plan for model monitoring. If you describe a project but cannot answer 'what happened after you deployed it,' that signals a gap in your production experience. Prepare a thoughtful answer for how you would monitor and maintain a model post-deployment.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the HighRadius Data Scientist interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves 3 to 5 rounds. This usually includes an online coding or aptitude screen, one or two technical interviews covering ML and statistics, a domain or product round, and a final HR discussion. Round structure can vary by team and seniority level, so confirm the format with your recruiter after you apply.

Does HighRadius ask coding questions or is it mostly theory?

Both come up, based on candidate reports. The online screen is typically coding-heavy, covering Python or SQL. Technical interview rounds tend to mix ML theory with applied problem-solving, and some rounds include a live coding task on the call. Practise writing clean Python for data manipulation and SQL for aggregations and window functions.

Do I need finance or accounting knowledge to clear the interview?

You do not need to be a finance expert, but basic familiarity with order-to-cash concepts (invoices, payment matching, collections, credit risk) is expected. HighRadius products serve finance teams, so interviewers want to see that you can connect ML work to those workflows. Spending a few hours reading about accounts receivable processes before your first round will help noticeably.

What salary can I expect as a Data Scientist at HighRadius?

Based on knok job radar data, entry-level roles (0-2 years) typically fall in the 8-16 LPA band, mid-level roles (3-5 years) in the 18-30 LPA band, and senior roles (6-9 years) in the 30-48 LPA band. For more specific benchmarks at HighRadius, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi, which carry self-reported numbers from past offer holders.

How important is NLP experience for HighRadius Data Scientist roles?

NLP is a core part of several HighRadius products, particularly around extracting data from remittance emails and financial documents. Candidates report NLP questions coming up frequently, especially for roles in the cash application space. Even if your background is primarily in tabular ML, prepare at least one NLP example covering text classification or entity extraction before your interview.

Is there a take-home assignment in the HighRadius Data Scientist interview?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home case study, while others go through fully live rounds. The format is not universal and appears to depend on the specific team and role. When you hear back after applying, ask your recruiter directly whether a take-home is part of the process so you can plan your preparation time accordingly.
