01 Overview

Overview

DriveNets is a network software company building Distributed Cloud Networking (DCN) solutions used by telecom carriers and large cloud operators worldwide. Their QA Engineers test carrier-grade network operating systems, routing protocols (BGP, OSPF, MPLS), and distributed software at scale. The work sits at the intersection of deep networking knowledge and modern test automation, making it a strong fit for engineers who enjoy both protocol-level debugging and Python scripting.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 459 QA Engineer openings across India, with DriveNets alone showing 41 open roles. That signals active, ongoing hiring across experience levels. The interview process typically spans three to five rounds. Candidates report an initial recruiter screen, followed by one or two technical rounds covering networking concepts and test automation, a hands-on coding or scripting assessment, and a final round with a senior engineer or hiring manager.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report these topics coming up frequently in DriveNets QA technical rounds:

  1. Walk me through your experience testing networking software or protocols such as BGP, OSPF, or MPLS.
  2. How do you design a test plan for a new feature in a distributed network operating system?
  3. Describe your Python automation experience. What frameworks have you used (pytest, Robot Framework, or similar)?
  4. How do you handle flaky tests in a CI/CD pipeline? Give a concrete example.
  5. What is the difference between functional, regression, and performance testing? How do you balance all three under time pressure?
  6. How would you approach end-to-end testing of a BGP routing feature on white-box switch hardware?
  7. Describe your experience in Linux-based or CLI-heavy testing environments.
  8. Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle and how you escalated it.
  9. How do you prioritise test cases when release time is short?
  10. DriveNets software runs in carrier networks. How do you think about test coverage for high-availability and failover scenarios?
  11. How do you work with software developers during the bug-fixing phase to reduce back-and-forth?
  12. Have you built or maintained test infrastructure such as CI pipelines, lab testbeds, or automated reporting dashboards?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioural questions. Here are three examples tailored to the DriveNets QA context.

---

Q: Tell me about a critical bug you found late in a release cycle.

*Situation:* My team was close to releasing a new route redistribution feature when I noticed intermittent packet drops during a routine regression run on the staging testbed.

*Task:* I had to quickly confirm whether this was a new defect or pre-existing flakiness, then communicate the risk clearly to the release team.

*Action:* I isolated the failing test case, ran it multiple times under controlled conditions, and confirmed a reproducible failure pattern. By correlating system log timestamps I traced the root cause to a race condition in the redistribution state machine. I wrote a detailed bug report with exact reproduction steps and shared it directly with the lead developer and release manager.

*Result:* The fix landed within the same day. The release shipped a day late but cleanly. The team added a dedicated regression test to catch that class of timing issue going forward.

---

Q: Describe a time you improved test coverage for a complex distributed feature.

*Situation:* Our QA team had good unit-level coverage for a new ECMP (Equal-Cost Multi-Path) routing feature but almost no end-to-end topology tests.

*Task:* I was asked to close that gap before the feature reached production.

*Action:* I mapped out key failure scenarios with the developer who wrote the feature, then built a topology automation script in Python that could spin up multiple virtual nodes, inject route changes, and verify forwarding tables automatically. I also added chaos-style tests that killed a node mid-convergence to validate failover behaviour.

*Result:* The automation caught two edge-case bugs that manual testing had missed. It was added to the nightly CI run and continued catching regressions in later sprints.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you had to push back on a developer who said a bug was 'not reproducible'.

*Situation:* A developer marked a routing flap bug as 'cannot reproduce' after trying it once on their local setup.

*Task:* I believed the bug was real and tied to a specific lab topology the developer did not have access to.

*Action:* I wrote a concise reproduction guide with exact testbed config, a topology diagram, and relevant log snippets. I then sat with the developer on a short call, walked through the steps on the actual lab, and reproduced the issue within minutes.

*Result:* The developer confirmed it was a genuine defect and fixed it before the next release. The experience led our team to start sharing testbed access more broadly so developers could reproduce QA environment issues themselves.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical 'how would you test X' questions: Structure your answer in three parts. First, clarify scope (what counts as a pass, what are the edge cases, what hardware or topology is involved). Second, describe your test levels (unit, integration, end-to-end) and which automation tools you would use. Third, mention how you would integrate it into CI and what a test failure would signal to the team. This shows you think beyond 'write a test' toward 'maintain a living test suite'.

For 'debugging a flaky test' questions: Interviewers at networking companies want to see methodical isolation. Say you would first determine whether the flakiness is environment-related (testbed instability, timing) or code-related (race condition, non-deterministic output). Describe how you would add logging or temporary retry logic to gather data, then fix the root cause rather than suppressing the failure.

For 'tell me about yourself' and career questions: Lead with your domain (networking, automation, embedded, telecom), then your tool stack (Python, pytest, Linux, Wireshark, or similar), then one concrete impact from a past role. Keep it brief. DriveNets interviewers are technical and will steer you quickly into a deep-dive once they have context.

For 'why DriveNets' questions: Mention the disaggregated networking space by name. Saying you are genuinely interested in the shift from proprietary hardware to white-box software shows you understand what makes their product distinct. Back it up with something from their public engineering content or a talk by their team.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Deep networking fundamentals. DriveNets builds software for carrier networks. Interviewers want genuine understanding of routing protocols, not surface-level familiarity. Be ready to explain how BGP path selection works, what ECMP does, or how OSPF converges after a link failure, in your own words without reading off a definition.

Python automation fluency. Most QA automation at DriveNets is Python-based. Candidates report being asked to write or review test scripts during the technical round. Know your pytest fixtures, parametrize, and conftest patterns, and be ready to explain how you would mock a network device response in a unit test.

Linux comfort. Testing happens in CLI-heavy environments. You should be comfortable with tcpdump, netstat, ip route, journalctl log reading, and basic shell scripting. Hesitating on Linux basics is a red flag even if your Python is strong.

Ownership mindset. DriveNets is a product company, not a services firm. Interviewers look for engineers who treat test coverage as a product responsibility, not a checklist item. Show that you have proactively improved test suites, built automation from scratch, or caught production issues before customers did.

Clear communication about trade-offs. When asked to design a test approach, do not just say 'I would test everything'. Talk about what you would prioritise first and why, what you would cut if time was short, and how you would communicate coverage gaps to the team.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Networking fundamentals review. Go through BGP attributes and path selection, OSPF LSA types and flooding, MPLS label switching, and basic ECMP behaviour. You do not need to memorise RFCs but you should be able to explain each concept conversationally, as if describing a topology to a colleague on a whiteboard call.

Week 2: Python and automation practice. Revisit pytest (fixtures, markers, parametrize), then write a small automation script that connects to a mock device via SSH using Paramiko or Netmiko and checks routing table output. Practice reading unfamiliar test code and explaining what it does and where it might break.

Week 3: Testbed and CI practice. If you have access to GNS3 or EVE-NG, build a simple multi-node topology and write a Python script to verify BGP peering state automatically. Read about how teams integrate network tests into Jenkins or GitLab CI pipelines. Candidates report that hands-on lab experience is a strong differentiator in the final rounds.

Week 4: Behavioural prep and company research. Prepare several STAR stories covering: finding a critical bug, improving automation coverage, handling disagreement with a developer, working under a tight release deadline, and picking up a new technology quickly. Read DriveNets public engineering content to understand their architecture before your interview.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating networking as optional. Some QA candidates lean entirely on automation skills and assume networking knowledge is a 'nice to have'. At DriveNets it is a core requirement. Going in without being able to explain BGP or discuss packet forwarding will typically end the technical round early.

Vague automation answers. Saying 'I used pytest' without being able to describe fixture scope, test data setup and teardown, or how tests ran in CI is a red flag. Be ready to go one level deeper on every tool you mention on your resume.

Over-claiming tool experience. If you list Wireshark, YANG, or OpenConfig on your resume, expect follow-up questions. Only include tools you can discuss with enough depth to handle a short technical follow-up without hesitation.

Not asking about the testbed environment. DriveNets QA involves real or emulated network hardware. Candidates who never ask about lab setup, topology scale, or how the team handles test environment provisioning can come across as lacking practical experience.

Giving a weak 'why DriveNets' answer. Saying 'I want a good opportunity' or 'the compensation looks good' falls flat at a product company. Connect your background to their specific problem space: disaggregated networking, carrier-grade reliability, or software-defined infrastructure.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the DriveNets QA interview typically have?

Candidates report three to five rounds in total. This typically includes an initial HR screen, one or two technical rounds covering networking fundamentals and test automation, a practical coding or scripting assessment, and a final conversation with a senior engineer or hiring manager. Round count can vary by team and seniority level, so ask the recruiter upfront what to expect.

Do I need deep networking knowledge or is strong automation enough?

Both matter, but networking knowledge is not optional at DriveNets. Their product sits in carrier networks and the QA role involves testing routing protocols, failover behaviour, and high-availability scenarios directly. Candidates with strong Python but weak networking fundamentals typically do not clear the technical rounds. You do not need CCIE-level depth, but you should be able to discuss BGP, OSPF, and ECMP conversationally and correctly.

What Python skills should I brush up on before the interview?

Focus on pytest (fixtures, parametrize, markers, conftest), SSH-based device interaction using Paramiko or Netmiko, and parsing CLI output with regex or JSON. Candidates also report being asked to review or debug an existing test script, so practice reading unfamiliar code quickly and identifying failure points. Knowing how to wire tests into a Jenkins or GitLab CI pipeline is a plus.

Is there a take-home assignment as part of the process?

Some candidates report a take-home coding or automation assignment, while others go straight to a live coding round. This varies by team and role level, so confirm with your recruiter. Either way, be comfortable writing a Python script that automates a network validation task from scratch, as this type of exercise is commonly reported across DriveNets QA interviews.

How do DriveNets QA salaries compare to the broader market?

Salary bands tracked for QA Engineer roles in India show entry-level at 4-9 LPA, mid-level at 9-17 LPA, senior at 17-30 LPA, and lead at 28-45+ LPA. DriveNets roles are commonly cited by candidates as sitting competitively within these bands, particularly at senior and lead levels given the domain specialisation. Verify current numbers on Glassdoor or levels.fyi before negotiating your offer.

Is Bangalore the best city to look for DriveNets QA roles?

Bangalore has the highest concentration of QA Engineer openings in the broader market, with 87 openings tracked across all companies as of July 2026, compared to 67 in Delhi and 13 in Chennai.
