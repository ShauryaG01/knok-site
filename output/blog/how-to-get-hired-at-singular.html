01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Singular is a mobile marketing analytics platform that helps brands track, measure, and optimize paid campaigns across hundreds of channels. Their core product connects fragmented marketing data into a single view, which makes the work highly relevant for professionals in data engineering, backend development, product management, and sales or customer success.

As of July 2026, Singular has 18 open roles listed on knok's radar. The mix covers both technical and non-technical tracks, so engineers and business professionals alike can find relevant openings. Because this is a focused company rather than a large enterprise, competition for each role tends to be specific and the hiring team generally reads applications carefully.

Singular operates as a global company with a distributed team. Indian candidates may apply for roles that are fully remote, India-based, or globally open. Check each job description for location requirements before applying.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Singular's hiring process typically runs across several stages. The exact number of rounds and format can vary by role and team, so treat this as a general guide based on publicly reported candidate experiences.

Stage 1: Application and Resume Screen
The recruiter or hiring manager reviews your resume against the job description. Matching your resume keywords to the job post improves your chances of clearing this stage. Highlight tools, domains, and outcomes that directly relate to marketing analytics or the specific function you are applying for.

Stage 2: Recruiter Introductory Call
If your profile clears the screen, a recruiter sets up a short introductory call. Expect questions about your background, why you are interested in Singular, and your notice period. This is also a good moment to clarify the role scope and ask about the team structure.

Stage 3: Technical or Skills Assessment
For engineering and data roles, this usually means a live coding screen or a take-home assignment focused on real problems. For product or business roles, a case study or written exercise is more common. The problems tend to reflect Singular's actual domain: marketing attribution, data pipelines, and campaign measurement.

Stage 4: Team Interview Round
This stage typically involves conversations with the hiring manager and a few team members. Expect a mix of technical depth questions, behavioral questions, and scenario-based discussions. Interviewers want to see how you think through ambiguous problems, not just whether you know the right answers.

Stage 5: Final Discussion and Offer
For senior roles, there is sometimes a final conversation with a senior leader or cross-functional stakeholder before the offer stage. The recruiter then follows up with a verbal offer and, once accepted, a written offer letter.

End-to-end, the process is publicly reported to take a few weeks to around a month depending on team availability and the seniority of the role.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Singular looks for people who can work comfortably with data and think analytically, regardless of the function they are joining. Even on the go-to-market side, the expectation is that you can read a dashboard and make data-informed decisions rather than relying on gut feel alone.

Technical roles (engineering, data, product): Strong fundamentals in your area are important. For engineers, that means solid knowledge of the languages and frameworks in the job description, comfort with distributed systems or data pipelines, and a track record of shipping reliable software. For data roles, SQL proficiency and experience working with large datasets are commonly expected.

Business and customer-facing roles (sales, customer success, marketing): Singular's customers are performance marketing teams at brands and agencies. Experience in mobile marketing, AdTech, or marketing analytics gives you a clear edge. Being able to hold a technical conversation with a marketing ops team is valued.

Across all roles, Singular looks for:

  • Clear communication, especially the ability to explain complex data concepts simply
  • Ownership mindset: candidates who have driven outcomes, not just completed tasks
  • Curiosity about the marketing technology space
  • Comfort working across time zones in a distributed team
05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand what Singular actually does
Before any interview, spend time understanding mobile attribution, what marketing mix modeling means, and how brands use Singular's platform to optimize their ad spend. You do not need to be an expert, but speaking fluently about the problem space shows genuine interest and saves time in the interview itself.

Match your resume to the job description
Singular's roles are specific. Pull the exact tools and skills from the job description and make sure your resume mentions them (honestly) near the top of each bullet. Applicant tracking systems filter on keywords before a human sees your profile.

Prepare for behavioral questions with real stories
Singular interviewers are publicly reported to ask questions like 'tell me about a time you had to work with incomplete data' or 'describe a situation where you had to convince a stakeholder to change direction.' Prepare a handful of specific examples from your past work that cover impact, ownership, and collaboration.

For engineering interviews: practice on real problems
Focus on the areas mentioned in the job description. If the role involves data pipelines, practice designing systems for high-volume ingestion and transformation. If it is backend-heavy, brush up on API design and system reliability concepts.

For business interviews: know your numbers
Be ready to talk about deals closed, churn rates you managed, campaigns you ran, or revenue you influenced. Singular's customers are data-driven, and your interviewers expect the same from candidates.

Research the company before every round
Read recent press coverage, check their product changelog, and look at what their customers say publicly. Referencing a specific product feature or a recent partnership during your interview shows you did the work.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Singular positions itself as a data-first company, and that philosophy extends to how the team operates internally. Decisions are expected to be backed by evidence, and team members are generally expected to bring data to discussions rather than opinions alone.

The company has a distributed team spread across multiple time zones. This means written communication matters a lot. Clarity in async messages, documentation, and handoffs is treated as a core skill, not a nice-to-have. Candidates who have worked in remote or hybrid settings before tend to adapt faster.

Because Singular sits at the intersection of marketing and technology, there is an expectation that everyone, including engineers, maintains a basic understanding of how performance marketing works. Teams collaborate closely across product, engineering, and customer success, so siloed thinking does not fit well here.

Growth is a consistent theme in how current and former employees describe the company culture. Roles tend to be broad enough that ambitious professionals can take on more scope over time, rather than waiting for a formal promotion cycle. If you are looking for a place where you can build deep expertise in the marketing analytics space, Singular offers that kind of focus.
