01 Overview

Overview

SolarSquare is one of India's leading rooftop solar companies, offering home solar installations with a monitoring app, financing options, and end-to-end service. As of July 2026, knok jobradar counts 177 open roles at the company across functions, a signal of aggressive growth. PM roles sit at the intersection of customer experience, field operations, and digital product: you coordinate with sales, installation crews, and engineering on the same roadmap.

The interview process typically runs 3-5 rounds. Candidates report a combination of product sense questions, analytical cases, and a strong check on mission alignment with clean energy. Come prepared to discuss India-specific solar adoption challenges, hardware-plus-software product thinking, and how to measure impact in a long, offline-heavy customer journey.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Company-specific questions SolarSquare interviewers commonly focus on:

  1. Why clean energy, and why SolarSquare specifically? What do you know about the rooftop solar market in India?
  2. How would you improve the SolarSquare homeowner app for tracking solar generation and rupee savings?
  3. A large share of leads drop off between the site survey and contract signing. How do you diagnose this, and what would you fix first?
  4. How would you prioritize features for a solar EMI or financing product aimed at middle-income Indian households?
  5. Design a field tool for SolarSquare's installation teams to manage daily schedules, job status, and customer communication.
  6. How would you measure the success of a customer referral program at SolarSquare?
  7. SolarSquare wants to enter a Tier-2 city where solar awareness is low. Walk us through how you would plan this launch.
  8. The operations team tracks installations one way and the tech team tracks them another way. How do you resolve this as a PM?
  9. What is your north-star metric for post-installation customer satisfaction, and how would you track it?
  10. How would you use geo data and publicly available census information to decide which localities to prioritize for solar sales outreach?
  11. SolarSquare is exploring solar for housing societies and apartment complexes. How is this a different product problem from individual homes?
  12. Tell us about a time you worked on a product involving physical-world constraints or last-mile operations.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you improve the SolarSquare homeowner app?

*Situation:* In a past role I was responsible for a utility-facing app where users checked usage data but rarely returned after the first week.

*Task:* I needed to improve weekly active usage without adding features that confused low-engagement users.

*Action:* I ran a survey with a group of users and found that most only cared about one thing: 'Am I saving money this month compared to last month?' I redesigned the home screen to lead with a monthly savings card in rupees, added an alert when generation dipped below the weekly average, and removed three screens that analytics showed were almost never opened.

*Result:* Weekly active sessions improved over the following months and support tickets asking 'is my system working' dropped noticeably. For SolarSquare, I would apply the same thinking: lead with rupee savings rather than units generated, and build smart alerts that tell customers when something needs attention instead of making them search for it.

---

Q: A large share of leads drop off after the site survey. How do you approach this?

*Situation:* At a previous company, we had a high-intent funnel step that converted poorly because customers left to 'think about it' and never returned.

*Task:* I was asked to improve conversion between a key offline step and the final purchase decision.

*Action:* I mapped every touchpoint after the offline step. Customers were leaving because follow-up was a generic email, the quote took several days to arrive, and no one called back the same day. I worked with sales ops to introduce a same-day quote SLA, added a WhatsApp follow-up sequence, and gave each sales rep a simple dashboard showing which leads had opened the quote PDF.

*Result:* Conversion at that funnel stage improved within the quarter. At SolarSquare, I would start by splitting the drop-off reason: is it price, trust, or timeline friction? Then I would test targeted fixes for the largest segment first.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you worked on a product with physical-world constraints.

*Situation:* I was PM for a logistics feature where field staff had to confirm job completion in an app, but connectivity in their work zones was unreliable.

*Task:* The team needed the app to work in low-signal areas without losing data or creating friction for the field staff.

*Action:* I worked with engineering to build an offline-first flow where confirmations were queued locally and synced when connectivity returned. I also simplified the UI to three taps instead of eight, because staff were using the app while standing at a customer's door under time pressure.

*Result:* Adoption of the confirmation feature went from very low to near-complete within weeks. At SolarSquare, installation teams face the same reality: apps must work at rooftops with weak signal and be simple enough to use without stopping the job.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

CIRCLES (for product design questions): Comprehend the situation, Identify customers, Report customer needs, Cut through prioritization, List solutions, Evaluate trade-offs, Summarize. SolarSquare interviewers ask many 'design a product for X user' questions, so this structure keeps you organized and signals strong product thinking.

MECE root-cause analysis (for funnel and metrics questions): Break the problem into mutually exclusive buckets. Is the issue in awareness, consideration, purchase, or post-sale? Within each bucket, separate supply-side problems (SolarSquare's own ops) from demand-side problems (customer mindset, trust, affordability). This works especially well for SolarSquare's long and offline-heavy sales cycle.

Jobs-to-be-done (for customer understanding questions): Instead of asking 'what features do users want,' ask 'what job is the customer hiring this product to do?' For the SolarSquare homeowner app, the job is usually 'confirm my investment is working and show me the savings.' That reframe helps you cut features that look appealing but do not serve the core need.

OKR-style metric framing (for success measurement questions): Structure your answer as: one north-star metric, two or three supporting metrics that explain the north star, and one counter-metric that catches unintended side effects. For a referral program, the north star might be qualified leads from referrals, with a counter-metric tracking whether those leads have a longer or shorter sales cycle than average.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

SolarSquare PMs work in a sector where the customer is a homeowner making a one-time, high-stakes purchase, not a daily app user. Interviewers look for a few specific qualities.

Genuine mission alignment: This comes up in almost every round, candidates report. SolarSquare is a mission-driven company. They want to know you understand why solar adoption in India is hard (upfront cost, trust barriers, awareness gaps, grid policy complexity) and that you care about the outcome, not just the product craft.

Comfort with offline and field operations: A large part of the product surface involves field teams, site surveys, and physical installations. Interviewers want PMs who think about software and operations together, not PMs who treat the app as the entire product.

Customer empathy for Indian households: Talking fluently about affordability concerns, EMI-driven purchase behavior, WhatsApp as a primary communication channel, and word-of-mouth as a trust signal shows you understand the actual customer rather than a generic urban tech user.

Structured thinking under ambiguity: Case questions are open-ended by design. Interviewers want to see you ask a clarifying question, state your assumptions clearly, and move through a framework before jumping to a solution.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Know the company deeply (start here). Use SolarSquare's website, their blog, and publicly available founder interviews. Understand their product lineup: panels, monitoring app, financing. Know their typical customer and the end-to-end sales process. Write down two or three things you would fix or build if you joined next week.

Practice product sense. Pick two or three SolarSquare product surfaces (the homeowner app, the installer field tool, the lead conversion funnel) and practice designing or improving them using the CIRCLES framework. Set a fixed time limit per question, record yourself, and listen back to check whether your answers are concrete or vague.

Prepare your STAR stories. Map your past experience to SolarSquare themes: handling physical-world constraints, working alongside operations teams, serving non-tech customers, and measuring impact across a long sales cycle. Prepare four or five stories and keep each one under three minutes.

Run mock interviews and build market fluency. Find a peer or join a PM community to run full mock sessions. Practice metric questions by reading SolarSquare's publicly available announcements and asking 'what would I measure here?' Read about rooftop solar policy and net-metering regulations in India so you can speak credibly about the market context in any round.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating it like a pure tech PM interview. SolarSquare is hardware plus software plus field operations. Candidates who only discuss app features miss a large part of the problem space.

Generic mission statements. Saying 'I am passionate about sustainability' without specific knowledge of rooftop solar in India or SolarSquare's business model reads as hollow. Prepare a concrete and personal angle.

Skipping the customer on design questions. Many candidates jump straight to features. SolarSquare interviewers expect you to spend real time identifying who the customer is and what they actually need before proposing anything.

Not asking clarifying questions on open-ended cases. These questions are designed to have multiple valid answers. Jumping straight to a solution without scoping the problem signals weak PM instincts.

Citing numbers without sources. If you state a conversion rate, market size, or adoption figure, be ready to say where you got it. If you do not have a real source, frame it as a hypothesis and explain your reasoning clearly.

Ignoring the length of the sales cycle. SolarSquare's customer journey from first awareness to installation can run for weeks. Metrics and product decisions designed for a daily-use consumer app often need to be completely rethought for this context.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the SolarSquare PM interview typically have?

Candidates report 3-5 rounds in total. This typically includes a recruiter or hiring manager screen, one or two product sense and case rounds, and a final round with senior leadership or the founding team. The exact structure varies by seniority level, so ask your recruiter for a roadmap after you receive the interview invite.

Does SolarSquare ask deep technical questions in PM interviews?

Candidates report that technical depth is not the primary focus. The interview typically centers on product sense, customer understanding, and operational thinking. A basic understanding of how solar monitoring systems and data integrations work is useful context, but you are not expected to go deep into system design or write code.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at SolarSquare?

SolarSquare does not publicly publish salary ranges. Based on knok jobradar data for PM roles in India, mid-level PMs with 3-6 years of experience are commonly in the 24-40 LPA band at growth-stage startups, while senior PMs are commonly in the 40-60 LPA band. Actual offers depend on your experience level and negotiation. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for publicly reported SolarSquare-specific numbers.

Is domain knowledge in solar or clean energy required to get the role?

It is not a hard requirement, but it is a meaningful advantage. SolarSquare is a mission-driven company and interviewers consistently check whether candidates have done their homework on the rooftop solar market in India. Understanding customer concerns around upfront cost, payback periods, and net-metering policy will help you in both product sense rounds and culture-fit conversations.

How do I show genuine mission fit without sounding rehearsed?

The most effective approach is to tie your personal perspective to a specific problem in solar adoption. If someone in your family considered going solar but did not go ahead, talk about what stopped them and what a better product experience would have looked like. Specific, personal angles land better than broad statements about climate change. If you do not have a personal story, referencing something concrete from India's solar policy or grid net-metering rules works just as well.
