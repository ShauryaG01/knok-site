01 Role Overview

Role Overview

Product Designer is one of the fastest-growing roles in India's tech ecosystem right now. Unlike a pure UI designer who only hands off visuals, a Product Designer owns the full user experience, from early research and rough wireframes all the way to polished interfaces that ship in real apps. Companies hiring for this role span early-stage startups, growth-stage product firms, global MNCs with India design centres, and large consumer internet companies.

The job market reflects this demand. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 393 open Product Designer roles across India. Bangalore leads with 62 openings, Delhi has 33, and Mumbai has 13. Salaries scale with experience: entry level (0-2 years) typically falls in the 6-12 LPA range, mid-level (3-5 years) in 14-24 LPA, senior (6-9 years) in 26-40 LPA, and lead or principal designers at 36-55+ LPA.

02 Skills You Need

Skills You Need

Figma. This is non-negotiable in 2026. Every product team in India uses Figma for wireframes, prototypes, and design systems. Learn it deeply, not just the basics.

UX research and thinking. You need to know how to talk to users, run usability tests, and turn messy feedback into clear design decisions. This is what separates a product designer from a visual artist.

Visual design fundamentals. Typography, colour theory, spacing, and hierarchy. Your eye for detail matters here, even when working inside an existing design system.

Prototyping and interaction design. You should be able to show how a flow works, not just what screens look like. Figma prototypes handle most of this, but tools like Principle or ProtoPie help for complex micro-interactions.

Design systems. Understanding how to create and maintain component libraries is a big differentiator, especially at product companies and MNCs.

Developer handoff. You do not need to code, but you should understand how your designs translate to implementation. Knowing what is easy vs. hard to build makes you a better partner to engineers.

Communication and storytelling. Presenting your work, defending decisions with data, and aligning stakeholders are daily realities. Strong communication often matters as much as craft.

03 Step By Step Path

Step By Step Path

1. Build your design eye before picking up tools.
Spend a few weeks studying apps you use every day. Ask yourself why buttons are placed where they are, why certain flows feel smooth, and where you get confused. Read 'The Design of Everyday Things' by Don Norman and browse free resources from Nielsen Norman Group.

2. Learn Figma properly.
Complete at least one structured Figma course (many good free and paid options exist on YouTube and course platforms). Practice by recreating screens from apps like Swiggy, Zepto, or CRED. Speed and precision in Figma matter in real jobs.

3. Study UX fundamentals.
Learn about user personas, journey maps, usability heuristics, and basic research methods. Free courses from Google (available on Coursera) or interaction-design.org are solid starting points.

4. Do three to five real portfolio projects.
Do not just redesign random apps for practice. Pick specific problems: redesign the bill-splitting flow in a payments app, improve the search experience on an e-commerce site, or design a feature for a local service. Show your process, not just final screens.

5. Put your portfolio online.
Behance and a personal portfolio site are both important. Recruiters at Indian product companies and MNCs actively search Behance. Your portfolio should explain your thinking, not just look pretty.

6. Get real-world experience as early as possible.
Apply for internships, even stipend-based ones, at startups. Many Indian startups take design interns who show strong portfolios. Freelance work for small businesses also counts.

7. Apply strategically for your first full-time role.
Target companies at the right stage for your experience. Early-stage startups give you breadth. Product-led companies give you rigour. Use LinkedIn, Naukri, and direct company career pages. Warm introductions through communities accelerate the process.

8. Level up continuously once you are in the door.
Take on cross-functional projects, learn to lead design reviews, and start contributing to or building design systems. Your growth from junior to mid-level depends mostly on the quality of problems you choose to work on.

05 India Specific Tips

India Specific Tips

Formal education paths.
India has a handful of design institutions that product companies genuinely respect: NID (with campuses in Ahmedabad, Bangalore, and other cities), IDC at IIT Bombay, Srishti Manipal in Bangalore, MIT Institute of Design in Pune, and Pearl Academy. That said, many strong product designers in India are self-taught or come from engineering or arts backgrounds. A degree from NID opens doors, but a strong portfolio and the right internships can get you the same interviews.

The Naukri and LinkedIn reality.
Naukri still drives a large share of recruiter outreach, especially at service companies and mid-size firms. Keep your Naukri profile updated and keyword-rich. LinkedIn is more important for product companies, startups, and MNC roles. Recruiters at top product firms source candidates almost entirely through LinkedIn, so a complete profile with portfolio links is essential.

Communities worth joining.
India has a growing design community. Look for UX India, local chapters of IxDA (Interaction Design Association), and city-based designer meetups in Bangalore, Delhi, and Mumbai. Slack and Discord communities like Design Buddies and Product Folks have India-specific channels where job referrals and portfolio feedback happen regularly.

Freelance as a learning accelerator.
Freelancing for small businesses, NGOs, or early-stage founders is one of the fastest ways to get real-world experience before your first job. You handle client communication, scope constraints, and real feedback, which is often more educational than academic projects.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need a design degree to become a Product Designer in India?

No, a degree is not required at most companies, especially startups and product-led firms. What matters is your portfolio, your ability to explain your design decisions, and your performance in design tasks or take-home assignments. A degree from NID or IDC IIT Bombay does carry weight and can open specific doors, but many working product designers in India come from engineering, graphic design, or entirely unrelated fields.

Which city should I target for product design jobs?

Bangalore has the most openings by a significant margin (62 out of 393 tracked in July 2026), driven by its concentration of product startups and tech companies. Delhi (33 openings) is strong for consumer internet and e-commerce roles. Mumbai (13 openings) has opportunities in fintech and media. If you are open to remote work, location matters less, and many product companies now hire designers remotely across India.

How important is Figma versus other design tools?

Figma is the industry standard in India in 2026. Most job descriptions list it as a requirement, and product teams collaborate directly inside Figma files. Adobe XD usage has dropped sharply, and Sketch is rare outside of some older teams. You should focus your time on Figma. Other tools like Principle, ProtoPie, or FigJam are useful additions, but none are as universally expected as Figma.

What should a product design portfolio include?

Your portfolio should show 3-5 case studies that walk through your process: the problem you identified, the research you did, the options you explored, the decisions you made, and the final outcome. Final visuals matter, but recruiters and hiring managers also want to see how you think. Each case study should be a story, not a gallery. Behance is widely used by Indian recruiters, so having your work there as well as on a personal site helps.

Can I transition into product design from a software engineering or graphic design background?

Yes, and both backgrounds have genuine advantages. Engineers transitioning to product design often ramp up quickly on developer handoff, feasibility thinking, and working with agile teams. Graphic designers bring strong visual craft and tool familiarity. The gap for both groups is usually UX research and systems thinking. Focus your learning and portfolio projects on filling that gap, and highlight your background as a strength in interviews rather than trying to hide it.

How long does it realistically take to get a first product design job in India?

For someone starting from scratch, a realistic window is 10-14 months from starting to learn to landing a first role. This assumes you build a focused portfolio, do at least one internship or freelance project, and apply actively. People with related backgrounds (engineering, graphic design, psychology) often get there faster. The bottleneck is almost always portfolio quality, not the number of applications sent.
