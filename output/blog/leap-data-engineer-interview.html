01 Overview

Overview

Leap is a fast-growing Indian startup at the intersection of fintech and edtech, helping students access study-abroad financing and related services. As of mid-2026, Leap has 22 open Data Engineer roles, making it one of the more active hirers in this category among Indian product companies right now.

Candidates report a process of typically three to four rounds: an initial recruiter call, a take-home or live coding round covering SQL and Python, a system design or pipeline design discussion, and a final hiring-manager conversation. Some candidates also mention a data modelling case study tied to Leap's core business data (loan applications, partner integrations, student journeys).

The broader Data Engineer market currently has 542 open roles across India (knok jobradar, July 2026). Bangalore leads with 92 openings, followed by Delhi (66), Hyderabad (23), Pune (23), Chennai (14), and Mumbai (8).

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These 12 questions come up repeatedly in Leap Data Engineer interviews, based on what candidates report publicly and the typical priorities of a fintech-edtech product company.

  1. Walk me through a data pipeline you built end-to-end. What were the biggest technical challenges?
  2. How would you design a pipeline to ingest loan-application data from multiple partner APIs that have different and evolving schemas?
  3. Write a SQL query to find users who performed an action in two consecutive months. (Candidates report variations of this window-function problem.)
  4. What is the difference between a star schema and a snowflake schema, and when would you choose one over the other?
  5. How do you handle late-arriving or out-of-order events in a streaming or micro-batch pipeline?
  6. Explain how you would debug a dbt model that is silently producing incorrect aggregation results.
  7. How would you set up monitoring and alerting for a pipeline that feeds a business-critical dashboard?
  8. Which orchestration tool have you worked with (Airflow, Prefect, Dagster) and what drove that choice?
  9. How do you enforce data quality checks at each stage of a pipeline without adding too much latency?
  10. Describe a specific slow SQL query you optimised. Walk through your reasoning step by step.
  11. How would you build a daily cohort analysis for loan-repayment behaviour across different student segments?
  12. What is your experience with cloud data warehouses such as BigQuery, Redshift, or Snowflake, and how have you handled cost optimisation?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioural question. Here are three worked examples relevant to Leap-style interviews.

---

Q: Describe a data pipeline you built end-to-end.

*Situation:* My team was manually pulling partner-API data into spreadsheets each morning, causing a several-hour delay before analysts could see the previous day's numbers.

*Task:* I was asked to automate the ingestion and make data available by 7 AM daily.

*Action:* I built an Airflow DAG that called each partner REST API, normalised the varying JSON schemas into a canonical format using Python, loaded the data into BigQuery via the streaming insert API, and set up a dbt model to apply business logic. I added row-count and null-check assertions at each stage.

*Result:* Data was consistently ready well before the 7 AM target. The manual effort dropped to near zero, and the team caught a partner-side data issue within the first week because the row-count alert fired.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you optimised a slow SQL query.

*Situation:* A dashboard used by the growth team was timing out. The underlying query joined three large tables and ran a correlated subquery on every row.

*Task:* I needed to reduce query time significantly without changing the business logic.

*Action:* I ran EXPLAIN ANALYZE to identify a full table scan on the largest table. I replaced the correlated subquery with a window function, added a partition-filter on the date column so BigQuery only scanned recent partitions, and materialised an intermediate aggregation as a separate dbt model refreshed nightly.

*Result:* Query time dropped from over a minute to a few seconds. The dashboard became usable in real time and no engineering effort was needed each time someone opened it.

---

Q: Describe a time you handled a data quality issue that affected a downstream team.

*Situation:* Finance noticed that their weekly loan-disbursement report was off. The discrepancy was small but consistent across several weeks.

*Task:* I needed to trace the root cause and prevent recurrence without disrupting the live pipeline.

*Action:* I compared checksums at each pipeline stage and found that a currency-conversion step was applying an exchange rate from the wrong day because of a timezone mismatch between the source API and our warehouse. I fixed the timezone handling, backfilled the affected records, and added a reconciliation test that compares our totals against a source-of-truth API endpoint each morning.

*Result:* The Finance report balanced correctly from that week onward. The reconciliation test caught two more minor discrepancies in the following quarter before they reached any downstream team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design and pipeline design questions, structure your answer around four areas: ingestion (where does data come from, how often, what format), transformation (business logic, schema design, tooling like dbt or Spark), storage (warehouse choice, partitioning, cost), and reliability (monitoring, alerting, backfill strategy). Leap interviewers particularly care about reliability because financial and student data cannot afford silent failures.

For SQL questions, think out loud. State your approach before writing any code: which tables you need, what the join key is, whether you need a window function or a subquery. Candidates report that Leap interviewers value clear reasoning over a perfectly typed query.

For behavioural questions, use STAR every time. Keep the Situation brief (one or two sentences). Spend most of your time on Action, and make sure the Result is specific. Vague results like 'the team was happy' are weak. Tie your result to a concrete outcome you can describe clearly.

For data modelling questions, anchor your answer to a real use case. For Leap, that might be modelling a student's journey from application to disbursement to repayment. Show that you think about who queries the model and how often, not just how it is structured.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report and Leap's public engineering content, interviewers are looking for a few specific things.

Ownership mindset. Leap is a startup. Interviewers want engineers who track data all the way to the business outcome, not just to the warehouse load. Expect follow-up questions like 'How did you know it was correct?' or 'What would you do if the pipeline broke at 2 AM?'

SQL fluency. Window functions, CTEs, and query optimisation are table stakes. Candidates who fumble basic GROUP BY or cannot explain an execution plan typically do not progress past the technical round.

Pipeline thinking at scale. Even at a startup, interviewers ask how you would handle volume growth. You do not need to have built a massive pipeline, but you should be able to reason about partitioning, incremental loads, and backfill strategies.

Clear communication. Data Engineers at Leap work closely with product managers and analysts. Interviewers notice whether you can explain a technical decision to someone non-technical. Practice translating your answers into plain terms.

Pragmatism over perfection. Candidates report that Leap values getting something working and improving it iteratively over designing an ideal system that takes months to ship. Show that you can make trade-offs consciously and explain your reasoning.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week plan covers the main areas candidates report being tested on.

Week 1: technical foundations

Days 1-2: Revise window functions (RANK, DENSE_RANK, LAG, LEAD, running totals), CTEs, and query optimisation. Practice on a public dataset in BigQuery or a free Postgres instance.

Days 3-4: Review pipeline design patterns: batch vs. streaming, idempotent loads, incremental models in dbt, and late-data handling. Draw out at least one pipeline architecture you have built or studied.

Days 5-7: Go through cloud warehouse concepts: partitioning, clustering, and cost controls in BigQuery or Redshift. Read one public engineering blog post from a company similar to Leap.

Week 2: communication and practice

Days 8-9: Write out three STAR answers to behavioural questions using the sample answers above as a template. Record yourself speaking them aloud and aim for concise, focused delivery.

Days 10-11: Do two mock system-design sessions. Design a pipeline for a loan-application ingestion system and a student-cohort analytics system. Ask a peer or mentor to push back with follow-up questions.

Days 12-14: Review Leap's public product and engineering content. Prepare two or three questions for the interviewer that show you understand their data challenges. Tidy your resume to surface pipeline work and concrete impact.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping to code before understanding the problem. In SQL and design rounds, candidates who start typing immediately often miss a key constraint. Spend a moment restating the problem and confirming your assumptions before writing anything.

Vague STAR results. Saying 'the pipeline ran faster' is weak. Tie your result to a concrete outcome: query time dropped from over a minute to a few seconds, or the manual morning effort that used to take several hours went to zero.

Over-engineering the design. Leap is a startup. Proposing a complex distributed streaming stack for a modest daily ingestion volume signals poor judgement. Match the complexity of your solution to the actual scale of the problem.

Ignoring monitoring and data quality. Candidates report that Leap interviewers specifically probe what happens when a pipeline fails. If you describe a pipeline without mentioning alerts, retries, or data checks, expect a pointed follow-up.

Not asking questions. Leaving the hiring-manager round without asking anything signals low interest. Prepare genuine questions about the team's data maturity, current pain points, or how success is measured in the first few months.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Leap Data Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report typically three to four rounds. This usually includes a recruiter or HR screen, a take-home or live coding assessment covering SQL and Python, a technical round on system or pipeline design, and a final discussion with the hiring manager. Some candidates mention an additional case-study round, but this is not universal. Expect the process to vary depending on the seniority of the role.

What salary can I expect as a Data Engineer at Leap?

Based on knok jobradar data, Data Engineer salaries in India currently range from 6-12 LPA at entry level (0-2 years), 14-26 LPA at mid level (3-5 years), 28-45 LPA at senior level (6-9 years), and 42-65+ LPA at lead or staff level. Company-specific figures for Leap are not publicly confirmed at scale. For a more precise sense of what Leap pays, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi, which aggregate self-reported compensation data.

Is SQL or Python more important for the Leap Data Engineer role?

Candidates report that SQL is tested more rigorously, especially window functions and query optimisation. Python fluency matters too, particularly for writing pipeline logic, data transformations, and automation scripts. You are unlikely to get through the technical round on SQL alone, so practise both. If you are stronger in one, make sure the other is solid enough to handle a live coding question.

Does Leap ask data modelling or dbt questions?

Yes, candidates report questions about data modelling (star vs. snowflake schemas, normalisation trade-offs) and at least one question about dbt or a similar transformation tool. You do not necessarily need production dbt experience, but you should be able to explain what a dbt model does, how you would test it, and how you would handle incremental loads. Frame your answers around Leap's domain (student data, loan applications, partner integrations) to make them land better.

How competitive is hiring for Data Engineer roles at Leap right now?

Leap currently has 22 open Data Engineer roles listed, which is a significant number for a single company. The broader Data Engineer market shows 542 open roles across India (knok jobradar, July 2026), with Bangalore (92 openings) and Delhi (66 openings) being the largest hubs. More open roles generally means more chances to get to a screen, but each individual round is still competitive. A well-prepared SQL and pipeline design round is your clearest differentiator.

What should I research about Leap before the interview?

Look at Leap's core products: their study-abroad financing platform, partner integrations, and the student journey from application to repayment. Think about what data problems a company like this faces, such as reconciling disbursement data across lenders, tracking student conversion funnels, and monitoring partner-API reliability. Reading one or two of their public engineering or product blog posts will give you vocabulary that resonates with interviewers. Arriving with a genuine question about their data infrastructure shows initiative.
