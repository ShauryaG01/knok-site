05 How To Stand Out

How To Stand Out

Engineering Manager is a role where your resume tells two parallel stories: what your teams shipped, and how you built or grew the team itself. Most candidates undersell the second story.

Lead with outcomes, not activities. 'Managed a team of engineers' is an activity. 'Grew the team, reduced delivery delays, and shipped multiple product launches on schedule' is an outcome. Pune hiring managers at Fiserv and Allianz come from large-org backgrounds and respond to specifics over vague scope statements.

Show cross-functional depth. Pune's EM roles span BFSI, automotive software, and deep-tech robotics. If you have worked with product managers, business stakeholders, or compliance teams, call that out clearly. EMs who can hold a business conversation get shortlisted faster than those who only talk code.

Prepare a 'manager story' for interviews. Be ready to discuss a time you handled underperformance, ran a difficult hiring process, or navigated a team restructure. These are near-universal EM interview questions and separate candidates who have genuinely managed from those who have mostly led technically.

Stay technically visible. Even as an EM, having recent technical contributions or architecture decisions documented helps, especially at startups like prismforce or zentrades where the EM is expected to stay close to the code and contribute to hiring bar decisions.

06 Application Strategy

Application Strategy

With only 20 roles in Pune, you cannot afford to be slow or generic. A focused, personalised approach gets more interviews per application than scattering the same resume everywhere.

Apply within the first week a role goes live. Engineering Manager roles fill faster than IC roles because the pipeline is smaller and companies move quickly once they find a strong match. Being in the first wave puts you in front of hiring managers before heavy filtering begins.

Customise your resume summary for each company type. A one-line tweak, replacing generic language with the company's domain, such as 'BFSI engineering teams' for Allianz or Fiserv, or 'automotive software delivery' for BMW TechWorks India, signals that you have read the JD and understand the context.

Message the hiring manager or recruiter on LinkedIn directly. A short, specific note that references one line from the JD and connects it to something concrete you have done gets a reply far more often than a cold application. Keep it concise, under five lines.

Track your applications in a simple spreadsheet. With multiple companies and rounds, things slip without a system. Note the date applied, your current stage, and your next follow-up date.
