01 Overview

Overview

KrazyBee is a Bengaluru-based fintech company offering personal loans, buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) products, and consumer credit, primarily to young professionals and college students. The Technical Program Manager (TPM) role sits at the intersection of engineering, product, compliance, and operations.

TPMs here typically own end-to-end delivery of lending features: coordinating mobile, backend, risk, and collections teams while keeping releases aligned with RBI and NBFC guidelines. The role demands both technical fluency (understanding APIs, data pipelines, and system dependencies) and strong stakeholder management skills.

As of mid-2026, KrazyBee has 85 open roles, which signals an active growth phase and broader ownership opportunities for incoming TPMs. Candidates report a structured interview process, typically covering behavioural rounds, a program management case study, and a technical depth discussion. Interviewers pay particular attention to how you handle cross-team complexity, regulatory constraints, and communication with non-technical stakeholders.

Most Asked Questions

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell us about a complex cross-team program you owned end-to-end.

*Situation:* At my previous company, we were rolling out a new loan repayment feature that required changes across the mobile app, backend APIs, payment gateway integrations, and a third-party credit bureau connection.

*Task:* As the TPM, I owned alignment across four separate teams, managed external vendor timelines, and was responsible for delivering the feature before a regulatory audit window.

*Action:* I started by mapping all dependencies into a shared project tracker and setting up a risk register that each team lead updated weekly. When the mobile team fell behind due to an unexpected OS-level API change, I escalated early, pulled in a platform engineer from another squad for two weeks, and negotiated a phased rollout: the web flow launched first while the app version followed a few weeks later.

*Result:* We shipped the core repayment flow on schedule. The phased approach reduced rollback risk, and the compliance team completed the audit without flagging any gaps.

---

Q: A key vendor integration is delayed and your launch is at risk. What do you do?

*Situation:* A payment gateway we relied on for loan disbursements notified us mid-sprint that their API upgrade would be delayed by several weeks, threatening our launch date.

*Task:* I needed to protect the launch date or find an alternative that did not require re-scoping the entire program.

*Action:* I immediately mapped two paths: building a thin abstraction layer to support both the old and new gateway versions in parallel, or switching to a backup gateway we had partially integrated previously. I presented both options to product and engineering leads within a day, with trade-offs clearly laid out. We chose the abstraction layer path since it required less regression testing.

*Result:* After we shared our timeline constraints formally, the gateway vendor pushed their fix forward. We launched with a brief delay of a few days rather than weeks, and the abstraction layer became a reusable component for future integrations.

---

Q: How have you handled a regulatory constraint that disrupted your delivery plan?

*Situation:* A new RBI guideline on loan disclosure formats was published partway through our product cycle, requiring significant changes to our in-app loan summary screens.

*Task:* Engineering estimated several additional weeks of work at current sprint velocity, but the business wanted the original launch date preserved.

*Action:* I broke the compliance requirement into a must-have set (what the RBI required at launch) and a nice-to-have set (UX improvements we had also planned). I worked with legal and compliance to confirm the minimum viable disclosure format, then presented a phased plan: phase one would be fully compliant at launch, phase two would add the UX layer in the following sprint.

*Result:* We launched on a date that satisfied both the business and the compliance team. Legal signed off on the phased plan, and phase two shipped in the next sprint without incident.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions. Structure every story as Situation (brief context), Task (your specific ownership), Action (what you personally did, not what 'we' did), and Result (a concrete outcome). For a TPM, the Action section should highlight how you coordinated people and information, not just what was built.

Problem-framing for case questions. When given a scenario like 'plan a new lending feature launch,' start by clarifying scope and constraints before jumping to milestones. Interviewers at KrazyBee want to see you ask about regulatory requirements, team capacity, and success metrics first.

Trade-off framing for technical decisions. When asked how you would choose between two approaches, name the trade-offs explicitly: speed versus quality, build versus buy, phased release versus big-bang. Show that you gather input and then decide, rather than guessing.

Stakeholder mapping for cross-team questions. When describing how you manage stakeholders, name the different groups (engineering leads, product managers, business owners, compliance, legal) and explain what each group needs from you and at what cadence. This shows you understand that communication is not one-size-fits-all.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Ownership without authority. TPMs at KrazyBee do not manage engineers directly. Interviewers want to see that you can drive alignment through influence: clear communication, early escalation, and building trust with team leads.

Regulatory awareness. KrazyBee is a licensed lending company. Interviewers flag candidates who treat compliance as an afterthought. Show that you understand how RBI guidelines on loan disclosures, interest rate communication, and collections practices affect product decisions.

Structured thinking under ambiguity. Fintech programs rarely go exactly to plan. Interviewers look for candidates who stay calm, break down the problem, and communicate clearly when things shift, rather than candidates who freeze or escalate everything upward.

Metrics fluency. Be ready to discuss how you have measured program health: not just delivery dates, but quality indicators like defect escape rates, rollback frequency, or feature adoption in the early weeks after launch.

Mobile-first context. KrazyBee's product is predominantly mobile. Candidates who speak to the specific challenges of mobile releases (app store review cycles, force-upgrade strategies, crash rate monitoring) stand out.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: understand the company and domain.
Read KrazyBee's publicly available product pages and any publicly reported news on funding rounds, product launches, or regulatory updates. Understand their core products: personal loans, BNPL, and credit for students. Read up on RBI's key lending regulations that affect consumer fintech apps in India.

Week two: prepare your stories.
Pick four to five programs from your own experience that map to the question themes above: a complex multi-team delivery, a regulatory or compliance challenge, a vendor or dependency failure, a stakeholder conflict, and a metric you introduced. Write out the STAR structure for each story before the interview, not during it.

Week three: practise case questions.
Ask a peer or mentor to give you a 'plan this launch' case and time yourself. Practise starting with clarifying questions before diving into a plan. Common case themes for TPM interviews include feature launch planning, incident post-mortems, and cross-team prioritisation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Using 'we' instead of 'I'. Interviewers want to understand your specific contribution. When you say 'we shipped it,' they cannot evaluate your impact. Be precise: 'I mapped the dependencies,' 'I escalated to the VP,' 'I negotiated the phased rollout.'

Underestimating the compliance angle. Candidates with experience in e-commerce or SaaS sometimes treat KrazyBee like a product-only tech company. It is a regulated lender. Not mentioning RBI requirements, NBFC norms, or data localisation in relevant answers signals a mismatch.

Skipping the clarifying questions. In case-style rounds, jumping straight to a plan without asking about team size, regulatory constraints, or success metrics reads as shallow. Take a moment to ask before you answer.

Being vague about outcomes. Saying 'the project was successful' is weak. Even if you cannot share internal numbers, describe what improved: faster release cycles, fewer rollbacks, better stakeholder satisfaction, or a compliance sign-off that unblocked the business.

Over-indexing on tools. Mentioning Jira, Confluence, or Slack is fine, but interviewers want to see judgment, not a list of tools. Focus on why you made certain decisions, not which software you used to track them.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does a KrazyBee TPM interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically involves a recruiter screening call, one or two behavioural rounds with senior TPMs or engineering managers, a program management case study round, and a final round with a business or product leader. The exact number of rounds can vary based on the seniority of the role. Always confirm the structure with your recruiter after the first call so you can prepare accordingly.

Does KrazyBee ask technical questions in TPM interviews?

Candidates report that the technical depth is moderate, not deep. You are typically expected to understand APIs, mobile release pipelines, and data dependencies well enough to have informed conversations with engineers. You are unlikely to be asked to write code, but you may be asked to explain how a specific system component (like a payment gateway or a credit bureau integration) works at a high level.

Is KrazyBee a good company for a TPM career move?

KrazyBee offers exposure to regulated lending products, mobile-first architecture, and the challenges of scaling a consumer credit business in India. These are valuable experiences if you want to move deeper into fintech program management or into product roles at lending companies. As of mid-2026, KrazyBee has 85 open roles, suggesting active growth, which often means broader ownership opportunities for incoming TPMs.

How should I prepare for the program management case study round?

Practise structuring your answer before you start talking. KrazyBee cases often involve fintech-specific constraints like regulatory timelines or vendor integrations, so weave in that awareness even if the prompt does not explicitly mention compliance.
