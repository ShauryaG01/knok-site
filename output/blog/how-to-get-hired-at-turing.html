01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Turing is a Silicon Valley-based AI-powered talent platform that connects software engineers from across the world with US tech companies for fully remote work. For Indian developers, it is one of the most direct paths to work on US-level products, earn in dollars, and remain based in India.

As of mid-2026, Turing has 30 open roles listed across engineering, data science, and platform functions. Positions include senior engineers, full-stack developers, ML engineers, and DevOps specialists. Most are fully remote, making them attractive for professionals in smaller cities who want global exposure without relocating.

Turing's model works differently from a traditional employer. You first go through their own vetting process to join their talent network, and then get matched to client projects. The hiring journey has two parts: Turing's internal assessment and, after you clear it, a separate client interview for the specific role you are matched to.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Turing's process is multi-stage. Here is what candidates commonly report, stage by stage.

Stage 1: Profile and application
You sign up on the Turing platform and complete your developer profile, listing your skills, years of experience, and preferred role types. This is the starting point for every candidate.

Stage 2: Seniority assessment
Turing typically runs a short automated test to gauge your level. This may include domain knowledge questions and a brief coding exercise. Your result determines which client roles you qualify for.

Stage 3: Technical coding challenge
This is the primary filter. Candidates commonly report a timed coding test covering data structures, algorithms, and problem-solving. Difficulty is comparable to LeetCode medium-to-hard problems. Some roles include domain-specific tests, such as SQL challenges for data roles or system design prompts for senior engineers.

Stage 4: Technical interview
Candidates who clear the coding test move to a live or asynchronous technical interview. This typically includes a real-time coding problem, questions on your past projects, and a system design discussion for senior positions.

Stage 5: Communication and English assessment
Since Turing's clients are US-based teams, English fluency is an active filter. You may be asked to record a short video or answer verbal questions. Clarity, structure, and confidence all matter here.

Stage 6: Client matching and client interview
Once Turing clears you for their network, they match you to active client openings. You then go through a separate client interview, which varies by company. This could be a further technical round, a culture fit conversation, or both.

The full process from first application to client match can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, depending on how quickly you move through each stage and how many matching openings are live at the time. With 30 open roles currently listed, there is real opportunity to move quickly if you are well prepared.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Turing's clients are US product companies with high technical expectations, so Turing vets for a specific profile before making any match.

Strong core engineering skills are non-negotiable. Whether you are a frontend developer, a backend engineer, or a data scientist, you need to be genuinely solid in your primary stack. Gaps in fundamentals show up quickly in their automated assessments.

Problem-solving ability is tested directly. Turing's coding challenges are designed to find people who can think through unfamiliar problems under time pressure, not just recall familiar patterns.

Remote-work readiness is a real criterion. Clients need developers who work independently, communicate proactively, and manage their own schedules across time zones. How you describe your past experience signals this.

Clear English communication is a hard requirement. You will collaborate with US teams every day, so written and spoken English needs to be professional and easy to follow. The emphasis is on clarity, not accent.

A consistent and credible work record also matters. Turing's reputation with clients depends on the quality of talent it places, so a sparse profile or unexplained gaps can work against you.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Practice data structures and algorithms before you apply. Turing's coding tests are algorithm-focused, so work through problems covering arrays, trees, graphs, dynamic programming, and string manipulation on a practice platform. Aim for fluency, not just familiarity.

Brush up on system design if you are targeting senior roles. Be ready to walk through how you would architect a scalable backend, handle large data volumes, or design a database schema. The book 'Designing Data-Intensive Applications' and system design video content are commonly recommended by candidates who cleared this stage.

Work on your English communication actively. Practice explaining your projects in clear, structured English. Record yourself answering a question like 'Tell me about a technically challenging problem you solved' and review the recording honestly. Aim for clarity and natural confidence, not a rehearsed script.

Fill out your Turing profile completely. Treat it the way you would a strong resume. List your key technologies, describe your most relevant projects with specific impact where possible, and make your experience easy to scan. A sparse or incomplete profile is filtered out early.

Prepare for the video or async component. Some candidates are caught off-guard by the video assessment. Practice on camera beforehand, check your audio quality, and set up a clean, well-lit background.

Apply when you have focused time. The process spans multiple stages over several days. Rushing through the coding test or submitting a half-complete profile leads to early rejection. Block time and treat each stage like it counts.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Turing operates as a remote-first, globally distributed company. The culture is performance-driven and results-oriented, which reflects the expectations of the US product companies it serves.

Remote discipline is central to everything. Turing values developers who are self-directed, document their work clearly, and communicate without needing to be followed up. If you depend on a structured office environment or close supervision, the adjustment takes real effort.

Learning between projects is encouraged. Turing has internal training resources and a developer community. Many engineers report using the time between client assignments to upskill, explore adjacent domains, or work toward certifications.

Diversity is core to the business model. Turing's pitch to US clients is that world-class engineering talent exists everywhere. Indian developers are a significant part of the network, and there is a genuine peer community among them.

Day-to-day culture depends heavily on your client. Two Turing engineers can have quite different experiences based on which client they are placed with. Team size, product stage, and management style all vary. It is worth asking about the client environment during your matching conversation before you commit to a project.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Is Turing suitable for freshers or only for experienced developers?

Turing is primarily aimed at experienced developers. Most client projects require solid, hands-on experience in a specific technology stack, and freshers will find the technical bar quite high. A better path is to build real product or service experience first, then apply to Turing once your skills can comfortably clear their coding assessment.

How long does the Turing vetting process take?

The vetting process typically spans a few days to a couple of weeks, depending on how quickly you move through each stage. The coding challenge and technical interview are the main time factors. Once you are cleared into the Turing network, client matching can take additional time depending on how many relevant openings are active at that moment.

Can I reapply to Turing if I fail the assessment?

Yes, Turing allows candidates to reapply after a waiting period, commonly reported as a few months. They do track previous attempts, so reattempting without genuinely improving is unlikely to change the outcome. Use the gap to strengthen your weak areas, particularly algorithms and English communication, before trying again.

Do I need US work authorization to work with Turing from India?

No. Most Indian developers working with Turing do so remotely from India as contractors or through Turing's employment structure, which does not require a US work permit. You should, however, understand the basics of contractor income, GST applicability, and how to receive dollar payments. Consulting a CA familiar with freelance tech income is a practical first step.

What salary can I expect from a Turing role?

Turing does not publicly disclose salary ranges, and compensation varies by role, seniority, and the specific client. Glassdoor and levels.fyi carry some developer salary data for remote platform roles, though sample sizes are small. Experienced engineers commonly report above-average Indian market compensation due to the dollar component, but the actual figure depends on your seniority and negotiation.

What is the difference between Turing's assessment and the client interview?

Turing's own assessment, covering coding, communication, and English fluency, is what gets you into their talent network. The client interview is a separate step that happens after Turing matches you to a specific opening, and it varies widely in format depending on the company. Clearing Turing's assessment does not guarantee a client offer. It means you are eligible to be matched and interviewed by clients.
