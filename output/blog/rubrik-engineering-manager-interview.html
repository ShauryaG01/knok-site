Most Asked Questions

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for all behavioral questions. Here are three examples tailored to Rubrik's context.

Q: How do you handle an underperforming engineer?

*Situation:* An engineer on my team was consistently missing sprint commitments and receiving repeated quality feedback in code reviews.

*Task:* I needed to address the gap honestly while remaining fair and supportive.

*Action:* I had a direct but private conversation to understand root causes first, because performance gaps often have a hidden reason. I then co-created a written improvement plan with clear milestones and weekly check-ins. I also paired the engineer with a senior teammate for structured code review mentorship rather than leaving them to figure it out alone.

*Result:* The engineer's output improved noticeably within the following quarter. They later became a reliable contributor. The process also gave me a repeatable, defensible framework for early intervention that I have applied since.

---

Q: Tell me about a major incident your team experienced. How did you lead through it?

*Situation:* A critical integration between our service and a downstream dependency failed in production during peak business hours, affecting several enterprise customers.

*Task:* My job was to coordinate a fast, calm response, keep stakeholders informed, and make sure the team could learn from the incident afterward.

*Action:* I immediately set up a live incident channel, assigned a dedicated engineer to triage the root cause, and took on stakeholder communication myself so the rest of the team could focus on the fix. We rolled back the offending change quickly. After stabilisation, I facilitated a structured post-mortem focused on process gaps rather than blame, and we published the findings internally by the next business day.

*Result:* Customers were restored to normal service quickly. The post-mortem led to a new pre-deployment checklist for integration changes, and leadership later cited it as a model incident response.

---

Q: How do you push back on a product roadmap decision?

*Situation:* Product wanted to ship a customer-facing feature on an aggressive timeline, but our internal security review flagged potential data exposure risks.

*Task:* I needed to advocate for a safer approach without damaging trust with my PM partner.

*Action:* I documented the specific risks in plain language, proposed a phased rollout that addressed the critical security concern first, and brought in the security team to validate my assessment independently. I framed it as a lower-risk path to the same goal, not a blocker.

*Result:* Product agreed to the phased approach. The feature shipped with the security issues resolved. My PM told me the collaborative framing made it easier to take to leadership, and this became the standard process for security-sensitive launches on our team.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioral questions. Use it for anything starting with 'Tell me about a time...'

Situation-Complication-Resolution works well for conflict and escalation questions. Lead with the normal state, describe what went wrong or what tension emerged, then explain how you resolved it. This keeps answers concise and shows you can diagnose before acting.

Data-driven framing matters at Rubrik because it is an enterprise company where decisions need to be defensible. When possible, anchor your answers to impact: velocity change, incident reduction, hiring outcomes, or customer feedback. You do not need exact numbers if you do not have them. 'Measurably improved' with a qualitative outcome is better than an invented figure.

The 'why I did that' layer separates good EM answers from great ones. After describing an action, briefly explain your reasoning. Interviewers at Rubrik typically probe with 'Why did you choose that approach?' or 'What would you do differently?' Be ready to defend your choices and acknowledge trade-offs honestly.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on what candidates report about the process, Rubrik interviewers are typically looking for these qualities in an Engineering Manager:

People-first leadership. Can you grow engineers, handle difficult conversations, and retain talent? Rubrik is scaling, so people development is not optional.

Technical credibility. You do not need to write production code, but you need to understand architectural trade-offs, call out risks in design reviews, and earn trust from senior individual contributors. Expect to be tested on whether you can hold a substantive technical conversation.

Customer obsession tied to the domain. Rubrik's customers trust it with their most critical backups. Interviewers want to see that you treat reliability and security as product values, not just engineering niceties.

Cross-functional maturity. Engineering Managers at Rubrik work closely with product, sales engineering, and customer success. Candidates who can only talk about their own team in isolation typically struggle in this area.

Calm under pressure. The incident management question is almost always present. Interviewers want to see structured thinking, clear communication, and a blame-free approach to post-mortems.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Phase 1: Understand Rubrik's business and domain
Read Rubrik's public engineering blog and recent press coverage. Understand the product: cloud data management, ransomware recovery, and enterprise backup. Know why reliability and security are central to the business, not just good engineering practice. This context will make your leadership stories land differently in interviews.

Phase 2: Build your story bank
Prepare a set of leadership stories using STAR. Aim for at least one strong story covering each of these themes: underperformance, conflict resolution, incident management, technical direction-setting, cross-functional pushback, and team hiring. Write them out, then cut them to the essentials.

Phase 3: Refresh technical credibility (in parallel)
Revisit distributed systems concepts relevant to data protection: replication, consistency trade-offs, backup at scale. You will not be asked to code, but you may be asked to evaluate a design or discuss an architectural risk. Practice explaining trade-offs out loud.

Phase 4: Mock interviews and calibration
Do a couple of mock behavioral interviews with a peer or mentor. Focus on conciseness: answers should be crisp and targeted, not sprawling. Record yourself once to catch filler words and long wind-ups.

On the day: Rubrik interviewers typically appreciate directness. Answer the question asked, layer in context only if it adds value, and be honest about what you would do differently in hindsight.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Staying too surface-level on leadership philosophy. Saying 'I care about my team' is not an answer. Interviewers want to hear specific behaviours: how you run 1:1s, how you handle a missed commitment, how you give critical feedback. Go concrete.

Avoiding the 'what went wrong' part. Many candidates tell stories where everything worked out smoothly. Rubrik interviewers reportedly probe for failure and recovery. Lean into what actually went wrong and what you learned from it.

Underselling technical credibility. Some candidates with strong people skills downplay their technical background. At Rubrik, where the product is deeply technical, this is a mistake. Show that you can engage meaningfully with architecture, even if you are not writing the code.

Treating the post-mortem question as a blame exercise. If you describe an incident and your instinct is to name who made the mistake, recalibrate. Rubrik values blameless post-mortems. Focus on systemic fixes, not individuals.

Not having questions ready for the interviewer. Candidates who ask shallow questions at the end leave a weak impression. Ask about how engineering decisions get made, how the team handles technical debt, or what the biggest engineering challenge is right now.

Ignoring the enterprise customer context. Generic answers about 'moving fast' can work at a consumer startup but feel misaligned at a company whose customers are enterprises trusting it with disaster recovery. Frame your answers around reliability, trust, and accountability.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Rubrik Engineering Manager interview typically have?

Candidates report that the process typically includes a recruiter screen, a hiring manager conversation, and a panel of engineering leaders or cross-functional partners. Some candidates also report a bar-raiser or senior leadership round at the end. Rubrik does not publish official round names or a fixed structure, so the exact format can vary by team and level.

Is there a coding round for the Engineering Manager role at Rubrik?

Candidates generally report that Engineering Manager interviews at Rubrik do not include a live coding exercise. However, you should expect technical system design or architectural judgment discussions. Rubrik's product is technically complex, so being able to evaluate designs and discuss trade-offs is important even without writing code.

What salary can I expect for an Engineering Manager role at Rubrik in India?

Based on publicly reported data and Glassdoor, Engineering Manager roles in India typically fall in the 35-60 LPA range, Senior Manager roles in the 55-90 LPA range, and Director-level roles at 90-150+ LPA. Actual offers depend on your total experience, specific team, location, and negotiation. Rubrik is known to offer competitive packages that include equity.

How should I prepare for the incident management question at Rubrik?

Pick a real incident where things went wrong and you had to lead the response. Use the STAR format: describe the situation briefly, your specific role in the response, the actions you took (especially around communication and coordination), and the outcome including what changed afterward. Rubrik values blameless post-mortems, so frame the result around process improvements rather than naming individuals who made mistakes.

Does Rubrik ask domain-specific questions about data protection or cybersecurity?

Candidates report that most questions are behavioral, but domain context shapes how interviewers evaluate your answers. You will be expected to understand why reliability and security matter to Rubrik's customers. You do not need to be a security specialist, but you should understand the basics of backup, recovery, and why enterprise customers treat data protection as a trust product.

How active is Rubrik's hiring for Engineering Manager roles right now?

As of mid-2026, knok jobradar tracks 109 open roles at Rubrik across all levels, which suggests active hiring. Engineering Manager and senior leadership roles are fewer in number than individual contributor roles at any company, so competition is real. A strong story bank, clear leadership philosophy, and demonstrated technical credibility will help you stand out in the process.
