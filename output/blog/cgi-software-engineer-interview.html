02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates who have interviewed at CGI report questions across three broad areas: core technical skills, client and delivery experience, and behavioural fit. Here are the questions that come up most often.

  1. Walk me through your experience with the tech stack listed in this role.
  2. How do you handle a situation where requirements change mid-sprint?
  3. Describe a time you debugged a production issue under pressure. What was your process?
  4. How do you ensure your code is maintainable and readable for teammates who join later?
  5. Have you worked directly with a client or business stakeholder? How did you manage their expectations?
  6. Explain a design pattern you have used and why it was the right choice for that project.
  7. How do you prioritise tasks when you have multiple deadlines competing for your time?
  8. Describe your experience with Agile or Scrum. What role did you typically play?
  9. How do you approach writing unit tests and ensuring adequate coverage on a feature you built?
  10. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision made by a senior colleague. What did you do?
  11. What do you know about CGI's business and the kind of clients it serves?
  12. How do you stay current with new technologies while still delivering on day-to-day work?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use these three STAR examples as templates. Adapt the details to your own real experience before your interview.

Q: Describe a time you debugged a production issue under pressure.

*Situation:* Our payment service began throwing errors after a late-night deployment, and several clients were unable to complete transactions.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer that night. My job was to identify the root cause, restore service, and document what went wrong.

*Action:* I rolled back the latest deployment first to restore service, then compared the deployment diff line by line, traced the issue to a missing null-check in a new code path, and wrote a targeted fix along with a test that would have caught it earlier.

*Result:* Service was restored within the hour. The post-mortem I wrote was adopted as a template for future on-call handoffs.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision made by a senior colleague.

*Situation:* My team lead wanted to add a third-party library to handle date formatting, but I felt the built-in language utilities were sufficient and the new dependency added unnecessary risk.

*Task:* I needed to raise my concern constructively without creating conflict or slowing the sprint.

*Action:* I prepared a short written comparison showing the added bundle size and maintenance overhead of the external library versus a few lines of native code, then asked my lead for a brief sync to talk it through.

*Result:* My lead agreed the native approach was cleaner for our use case. The decision was reversed, and the lead later mentioned that the structured write-up made the conversation easy.

---

Q: How do you handle a situation where requirements change mid-sprint?

*Situation:* Midway through a sprint, the product owner received new input from a banking client that changed a core workflow we had already partly built.

*Task:* I needed to assess the impact, communicate it clearly, and decide what to carry forward versus rebuild.

*Action:* I mapped the existing work against the new requirement, identified which parts were still usable, flagged the change to the scrum master, proposed moving the incomplete story to the next sprint, and documented the pivot decision so the team had a clear record.

*Result:* We delivered the revised feature in the following sprint without rework on the parts I had flagged as reusable. The client appreciated that we surfaced the impact early rather than over-promising.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

The STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the right structure for every behavioural question CGI asks. Keep each section concise: one to two sentences for Situation and Task, more depth in Action, and a concrete outcome in Result.

For technical questions, use a think-aloud approach. State your assumptions first, walk through your reasoning step by step, and flag trade-offs. CGI interviewers typically care as much about how you think as whether you land on the exact right answer.

For client or stakeholder questions, anchor your answer in empathy and communication. Show that you can translate technical problems into plain language, and that you proactively surface risks rather than waiting to be asked.

Before answering any question, pause and ask a clarifying question if the prompt is vague. This signals professional maturity and helps you give a focused answer rather than a rambling one.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

CGI delivers long-running IT projects for large enterprises, so interviewers look for engineers who are reliable, communicative, and client-aware, not just technically strong.

Reliability: Can you finish what you start, raise blockers early, and own your work end to end? Give examples where you followed through without being chased.

Client mindset: CGI's work is largely client-facing. Interviewers will probe whether you understand that the end goal is business value, not just clean code. Show that you can work alongside non-technical stakeholders.

Team fit: Candidates report that interviewers pay close attention to how you talk about past teammates and managers. Avoid blame. Frame conflicts as problems you solved together.

Adaptability: Project requirements shift, clients change scope, and technologies evolve. Show examples of times you adjusted course without losing momentum.

Communication: Clear, structured answers matter at CGI more than at a pure product company. Practice speaking in paragraphs, not bullet points.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused two-week plan based on what candidates typically report about the CGI process.

Days 1-3: Company and domain research. Read about CGI's major service lines (IT managed services, consulting, systems integration). Understand which industries it serves in India. Think about how your own project experience maps to client delivery work.

Days 4-6: Technical review. Refresh the core concepts most relevant to the role: data structures, system design basics, and the frameworks listed in the job description. Write small code samples to warm up.

Days 7-9: Behavioural story bank. Write out six to eight stories from your career using STAR format. Cover a technical challenge, a delivery under pressure, a conflict or disagreement, a time you learned something new quickly, and a client or stakeholder interaction.

Days 10-12: Mock interviews and polish. Practice your answers out loud, ideally with a friend or recorded on your phone. Review your stories for any claims you cannot back up. Prepare a few thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer about the team and the project.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Skipping company research. CGI interviewers commonly ask what you know about the company. Candidates who cannot describe CGI's business model or client focus leave a poor impression. Spend real time on this before your interview.

Vague STAR answers. Saying 'I improved performance' without explaining what you did and what changed is not enough. Make the action concrete and the result measurable where possible, using your own real data.

Talking negatively about past employers or teammates. CGI values long-term client relationships and stable teams. Interviewers will flag candidates who seem difficult to work with.

Over-engineering the technical answer. CGI projects often prioritise maintainability and delivery predictability. A clean, working solution beats a clever but complex one.

Not asking any questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing signal low interest. Prepare a question about the current project, the team structure, or what success looks like in the first few months on the job.

Treating every round identically. Candidates report that later rounds at CGI tend to be more behavioural and client-focused, while earlier rounds test technical depth. Adjust your preparation accordingly.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does CGI typically have for Software Engineers?

Candidates typically report two to three rounds: an initial screening call, a technical interview, and a final HR or manager round. Some roles add a written or coding assessment in the early stage. The exact number varies by level and location, so confirm the process with your recruiter after you apply.

Does CGI ask data structures and algorithms questions?

Candidates report that CGI's technical rounds focus more on practical coding, debugging, and system design than on competitive-style algorithm puzzles. You should still be comfortable with common data structures and basic complexity analysis. Deep competitive-coding prep is typically not the priority for CGI roles.

What salary can I expect as a Software Engineer at CGI in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, Software Engineer salaries in India broadly range from 6-12 LPA at entry level, 15-25 LPA at mid level, and 28-45 LPA at senior level. CGI-specific figures are not independently verified in our data. Glassdoor and levels.fyi may have more detailed reports for this company specifically.

Is CGI a good company for freshers or early-career engineers?

CGI does hire at the entry level and offers exposure to large enterprise clients across banking, insurance, and government sectors. The work is typically project-based and structured, which suits engineers who want to build delivery and client-handling skills early in their career. Growth can be steady but tends to be more process-driven than at a product startup.

How long does the CGI hiring process take?

Candidates typically report a process that spans a few weeks from application to offer, though timelines vary by location and team urgency. Following up with your recruiter after each round is standard practice and is generally well received at CGI.

What should I wear or prepare for a CGI video interview?

CGI is a professional services company, so business casual is a safe choice for video interviews. Test your audio and camera beforehand, choose a quiet background, and keep your STAR stories and the job description open in a separate window for easy reference during the call.
