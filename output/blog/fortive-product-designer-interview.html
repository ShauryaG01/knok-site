01 Overview

Overview

Fortive is an industrial technology company whose portfolio includes software products for field service, asset management, and workflow automation in sectors like utilities, manufacturing, and healthcare. Their Product Designer roles sit inside business units such as Gordian, ServiceMax, Accruent, and Tektronix, so the design work is almost entirely B2B and enterprise-focused.

Candidates report a process that typically includes a recruiter screen, a portfolio walkthrough with the hiring manager, a design exercise or take-home challenge, and a panel round with cross-functional stakeholders. The exact number of rounds varies by business unit, so ask your recruiter for the specific format early.

As of mid-2026, Fortive had 38 open Product Designer roles on knok's tracker, making it one of the more active enterprise tech employers for designers in India. Bangalore leads product design demand among Indian cities, with 62 openings tracked across all employers.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Walk us through a complex B2B product you designed. How did you handle competing stakeholder needs?

*Situation:* I was the sole designer on a field service scheduling tool used by operations managers and field technicians at a mid-sized utilities company.
*Task:* The product team wanted a new calendar-based dispatch view. Operations managers wanted bulk-assign features, field technicians wanted a simple mobile check-in flow, and engineering had limited capacity.
*Action:* I ran separate contextual inquiry sessions with both user groups, mapped their workflows side by side, and identified overlapping pain points. I proposed a unified 'job card' pattern that worked across both the desktop dispatch view and the mobile technician app, reducing the number of distinct components engineering needed to build.
*Result:* The new dispatch view reduced average job assignment time (as reported by users in post-launch surveys), and engineering shipped it within the agreed sprint window. Both user groups rated the redesign as an improvement in internal NPS surveys.

---

Q: Tell us about a time you simplified a workflow that had years of accumulated complexity in the UI.

*Situation:* An asset management platform I worked on had a configuration screen that had grown over several years, with new fields added each quarter and outdated ones never removed.
*Task:* I needed to restructure the screen to reduce setup errors, without disrupting existing users who had memorised the old layout.
*Action:* I audited usage logs with the data team, found that a large share of fields were rarely touched, and proposed hiding advanced options behind a 'show more' pattern. I ran usability tests with power users before shipping to confirm no critical paths were broken.
*Result:* Support tickets related to misconfiguration dropped noticeably in the first quarter post-launch, according to the support team's internal report.

---

Q: How do you work with PMs who want features shipped quickly when your research says users need something different?

*Situation:* A PM at my previous company wanted to ship a notification centre feature in the next sprint. My discovery research showed users were overwhelmed by notifications and needed better filtering, not more of them.
*Task:* I had to present a counter-proposal without blocking the sprint or damaging trust with the PM.
*Action:* I put together a short research readout with a few user quotes and a rough journey map showing where notification fatigue was causing users to disengage. I then proposed a phased approach: ship a lightweight notification filter in sprint one, gather data, and revisit the full notification centre in sprint two.
*Result:* The PM agreed to the phased plan. The filter shipped on time, and engagement data from the following weeks supported the case for redesigning the notification centre entirely. The PM later cited this as a model for how to work with design.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For portfolio walkthroughs: Use a 'problem, process, decision, outcome' spine rather than a chronological story. State the business context first, then the user problem, then your key design decisions (and why you made them over alternatives), then the measurable outcome. Fortive interviewers work in B2B contexts, so tie outcomes to operational metrics like time saved, error reduction, or adoption rates wherever you can.

For design exercise rounds: Candidates report being given a prompt related to an industrial or enterprise workflow scenario. Use a 'define, diverge, decide' structure: restate the problem in your own words, sketch multiple directions before committing, and explain your selection criteria out loud. Ask clarifying questions upfront about user type, platform, and constraints.

For behavioural questions: Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but keep Situation and Task brief. Interviewers care most about your Action and the reasoning behind it. If a project did not go well, add a fifth beat: what you would do differently.

For 'how do you measure success' questions: Name a specific metric tied to user behaviour (task completion rate, time on task, support ticket volume) rather than a vanity metric like satisfaction scores alone.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fortive's product design roles sit inside enterprise software businesses where users are professionals doing operational work, not consumers. Interviewers are typically looking for a few things.

B2B design sensibility. Can you talk fluently about designing for expert users, complex workflows, and data-heavy interfaces? Candidates who only have consumer app portfolios may struggle if they cannot connect their work to enterprise contexts.

Cross-functional maturity. Fortive products involve close collaboration with engineering, product management, and domain experts such as field technicians, asset managers, and finance teams. Interviewers will probe for examples where you navigated stakeholder disagreement or translated domain expertise into design decisions.

Research rigour. Candidates report that interviewers push back on vague claims like 'users wanted X.' Be prepared to explain exactly how you recruited participants, what methods you used, and how you handled conflicting data.

Ownership. Because many Fortive business units are relatively lean on design headcount, interviewers want to see that you can drive a project from an ambiguous brief to a shipped product without needing constant direction.

Systems thinking. Questions about design systems, component reuse, and consistency across products come up frequently, particularly for mid-level and senior roles.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: Understand the business unit. Fortive is a portfolio company, so research which specific business unit is hiring (Gordian, ServiceMax, Accruent, Tektronix, and others) and understand their product domain. Read their public product pages, look for case studies, and note the types of users their products serve.

Week one: Curate your portfolio. Select two or three projects that show B2B or complex-workflow design. If your portfolio skews consumer, reframe at least one project to highlight operational or workflow complexity. Prepare a crisp five-minute walkthrough for each project.

Week two: Prepare your STAR stories. Draft answers for the twelve questions listed above. Focus especially on stakeholder conflict, design iteration under constraints, and measuring post-launch outcomes. Practise speaking them aloud rather than reading from notes.

Week two: Practise a design exercise. Candidates report take-home prompts involving enterprise workflow redesigns. Practise scoping a problem, sketching three to five directions, and presenting a rationale for your chosen direction. Timing yourself builds comfort with the format.

Before each round: Review Fortive's recent news, product launches, and any publicly available design or UX writing from their teams. Showing domain awareness in an interview signals genuine interest and preparation.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Presenting work without explaining decisions. Many candidates walk through what they built but not why. Interviewers at enterprise tech companies want to hear the reasoning behind design choices, especially when you chose one direction over a reasonable alternative.

Vague research claims. Saying 'I talked to users' without specifying how you recruited them or what method you used reads as thin. If your sample was small, acknowledge it and explain how you mitigated the risk.

Ignoring the business context. Framing every design decision purely around user needs, without acknowledging business constraints or commercial goals, signals a lack of cross-functional awareness. Fortive's products serve business customers, so ROI and operational efficiency are part of the design brief.

Treating the design exercise as a solo deliverable. Candidates report that Fortive interviewers value seeing how you think and communicate during an exercise, not just the polish of the final artefact. Ask questions, narrate your reasoning, and show your sketches alongside your finished screens.

Not researching the specific business unit. Fortive is not a single product company. Showing up with no knowledge of which business unit you are interviewing for, or what their product domain is, leaves a poor impression in a company where domain knowledge is genuinely valued.

Underselling scope and ownership. Designers who worked on large teams sometimes describe their contribution in collective terms ('we did X'). Be specific about what you personally owned, decided, or shipped.

How many rounds does the Fortive Product Designer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs recruiter screen, portfolio review, design exercise or take-home, and a panel round, though the exact number varies by business unit within the Fortive portfolio. Ask your recruiter for the specific format when you schedule your first call, since Gordian, ServiceMax, and Accruent each run their own hiring processes.

Does Fortive give a take-home design exercise or a live whiteboard challenge?

Both formats are reported by candidates depending on the business unit. Some teams prefer a take-home prompt (typically a day or two to complete) while others use a live scenario discussion in the interview itself. Confirm with your recruiter which format to expect so you can prepare the right way.

What salary can I expect for a Product Designer role at Fortive in India?

Based on knok's job radar data, mid-level Product Designer roles (3-5 years experience) across the Indian market typically see ranges of 14-24 LPA, while senior roles (6-9 years) are often in the 26-40 LPA range. Specific Fortive compensation is not publicly reported in enough detail to give a precise number, so use market benchmarks from Glassdoor or levels.fyi when you negotiate.

Is a consumer product portfolio acceptable, or does Fortive only want enterprise design experience?

A consumer portfolio is not disqualifying, but candidates report that Fortive interviewers look for evidence of complexity, workflow thinking, and cross-functional collaboration rather than visual polish alone. If your portfolio is consumer-focused, prepare to clearly articulate how your skills transfer to enterprise and operational contexts.

How important is design system experience for Fortive roles?

Candidates at mid-level and above report questions about design system contribution and component thinking. Fortive has multiple business units with their own products, so experience maintaining or contributing to a shared component library is a strong signal of readiness, particularly for senior and lead roles.
