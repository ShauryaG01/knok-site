01 Hiring Overview

Founded in Switzerland and now operating globally, Nexthink works with large organisations to monitor device health, application performance, and employee sentiment in real time. Their platform sits at the intersection of IT operations and employee experience, which makes their hiring both technical and business-focused.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The process at Nexthink typically runs across four to five stages, though the exact structure varies by role and team. Candidates report a process that feels thorough but moves at a reasonable pace.

Stage 1: Application and screening. After you apply, a recruiter or talent partner reviews your profile. If your background matches the role, you will get a short screening call covering your background, interest in Nexthink, and basic logistics like availability and location.

Stage 2: Hiring manager conversation. This is a deeper discussion with the person who leads the team you are joining. Expect questions about your past work, how you have handled specific situations, and why you want to work at Nexthink. For technical roles, there may be a light technical discussion here as well.

Stage 3: Technical or skills assessment. Engineering candidates typically face a coding exercise or system design discussion. Sales and customer success candidates may be asked to do a product demo, a mock customer call, or a case study. Implementation and professional services candidates are often tested on their ability to understand a customer problem and propose a solution.

Stage 4: Panel or cross-functional round. You may speak with two or three people from different teams. This round checks how you communicate with stakeholders beyond your immediate manager, and whether your working style fits the team.

Stage 5: Final discussion and offer. A senior leader or the hiring manager wraps up any remaining questions. Offer timelines vary, but candidates commonly report hearing back within one to two weeks after the final round.

The total process from application to offer commonly runs three to five weeks, depending on role complexity and team availability.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Nexthink looks for people who can connect technical knowledge to real business outcomes. Their platform serves enterprise IT teams, so understanding how large organisations run their technology matters across almost every function.

For engineering roles, strong fundamentals in backend systems, cloud infrastructure, and data pipelines are important. Experience with observability tools, monitoring platforms, or IT service management products is a clear advantage. Nexthink's stack is modern and cloud-native, so comfort with distributed systems and APIs is valued.

For sales and go-to-market roles, the key is the ability to sell to enterprise IT buyers: CIOs, IT directors, and heads of digital workplace. Candidates with experience in B2B SaaS sales, especially to large corporates, tend to do well. Comfort with long sales cycles and multiple stakeholders is essential.

For customer success and professional services, they want people who can manage complex implementations and keep enterprise customers engaged over the long term. Strong project management, stakeholder communication, and the ability to explain technical concepts to non-technical people are all highly valued.

Across all roles, they look for clear communicators who are comfortable working across time zones and cultures. Nexthink operates globally, so being able to collaborate with colleagues in Europe or North America as well as customers in diverse markets is a practical requirement, not just a nice-to-have.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Understand the product before any conversation. Nexthink's core offering is digital employee experience management. Before your first call, spend time on their website and read a few customer stories to understand what problems they solve and who buys from them. Being able to speak to why their product matters will immediately set you apart.

Research their target buyers. Nexthink sells to enterprise IT teams. Knowing the typical pain points of a head of IT, a digital workplace manager, or a CIO will help you in both technical and business conversations during interviews.

Prepare your STAR stories. Behavioral questions are common across all roles. Structure your answers using the Situation, Task, Action, Result format. Pick examples that show impact: projects you shipped, customers you retained, revenue you influenced, or problems you solved at scale.

For technical roles, practise system design and debugging. Review concepts around cloud infrastructure, distributed systems, and monitoring. Be ready to talk through how you would design a system that collects and processes telemetry data from thousands of devices.

For sales and customer success roles, prepare a mock pitch. Practise walking through what Nexthink does in a few minutes, as if you were speaking to a sceptical IT director. Recruiters appreciate candidates who already think like someone on the team.

Prepare thoughtful questions for the final round. Ask about team structure, what success looks like in your first few months, and how the team collaborates across time zones. Good questions show genuine interest and help you evaluate whether the role is the right fit for you.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Nexthink describes itself as a company that genuinely cares about the employee experience, which makes sense given that their product is built around improving it. Employees across review platforms commonly describe the culture as collaborative, inclusive, and focused on outcomes rather than hours.

Global and remote-friendly. Nexthink teams are spread across multiple continents. Meetings often happen across time zones, and the expectation is that you communicate clearly in writing as well as verbally. Asynchronous work is part of the culture, especially for roles that interface with teams in Europe.

Customer obsession is central. Because Nexthink sells to enterprise customers, the company has a strong orientation toward solving real customer problems. In interviews, candidates who talk about impact on customers, not just internal deliverables, tend to resonate well.

Transparency and ownership. Former employees commonly note that Nexthink gives people real ownership of their work. There is less micromanagement than at larger legacy tech companies, but that also means you are expected to drive your own outcomes and communicate proactively when something is off track.

Growth-stage energy in a maturing company. Nexthink is past the startup phase but still evolving fast. Processes are more defined than at an early-stage company, but there is still room to shape how things are done. People who enjoy building, not just executing, tend to find the environment energising.
