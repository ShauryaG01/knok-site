01 Overview

Overview

Armory builds enterprise-grade continuous delivery software centred around Spinnaker, an open-source CD platform originally developed at Netflix. The company helps engineering teams at large organisations automate complex multi-cloud deployments safely and at scale. As of mid-2026, Armory has 3 open Software Engineer roles.

Candidates typically report a process of 4-5 rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen, one or two rounds covering coding or system design, and a closing culture conversation. The process is DevOps and distributed-systems heavy. Expect in-depth questions on deployment pipelines, Kubernetes, and cloud infrastructure, and be ready to walk through real incidents and trade-offs from your own experience.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Coding and system design

  1. Walk me through how you would design a CI/CD pipeline for a microservices application.
  2. What is your experience with Kubernetes? Describe a production incident you resolved involving a K8s cluster.
  3. Have you worked with Spinnaker or a similar CD platform? What deployment strategies did you configure?
  4. How do you approach rollback strategies when a deployment causes problems in production?
  5. How would you implement canary deployments, and what metrics would you monitor to decide whether to proceed?
  6. How do you handle secrets management in a cloud-native application?
  7. How would you build observability (logs, metrics, traces) into a new service from day one?
  8. What is your experience with infrastructure-as-code tools like Terraform or Helm?
  9. How do you design services for high availability in a distributed environment?

Behavioural

  1. Describe a time you improved deployment frequency or reliability on your team.
  2. Tell me about a time you worked with a cross-functional team to ship a complex feature.
  3. Describe a situation where you had to make a trade-off between delivery speed and system reliability.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you improved deployment frequency or reliability on your team.

*Situation:* My team was releasing a monolithic backend every two weeks, and each release took nearly half a day with frequent rollbacks.

*Task:* I was asked to reduce both the release cycle time and the rollback rate ahead of a key product launch.

*Action:* I broke the deployment process into smaller, independently deployable services, introduced feature flags so incomplete work could ship safely, and set up automated smoke tests that ran on every merge to main.

*Result:* Within three months we moved from fortnightly releases to daily deployments, and the rollback rate dropped noticeably. The product launch went out on schedule.

---

Q: Tell me about a time you worked with a cross-functional team to ship a complex feature.

*Situation:* We were integrating a real-time audit-logging feature that required changes from the backend, security, and data-platform teams, all on different timelines.

*Task:* I was the backend engineer responsible for coordinating the API contract and keeping all three teams unblocked.

*Action:* I proposed and documented an API contract upfront so the data-platform team could build their consumer before the backend was ready. I ran weekly syncs with all three teams to surface blockers early and set up a shared test environment with mocked endpoints.

*Result:* All three workstreams landed within the same sprint and the feature went to production with zero integration-related incidents.

---

Q: Describe a situation where you had to trade delivery speed against reliability.

*Situation:* A high-priority client needed a deployment-configuration feature in two weeks, but the proper implementation required a full data-model change that would take at least a month.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to ship a quick version now or push back on the deadline.

*Action:* I proposed a two-phase approach: ship a lightweight config override in week two that solved the client's immediate need, then follow with the proper data-model change in the next sprint. I documented the short-term approach as technical debt with a clear remediation plan.

*Result:* The client got their feature on time, the override was low-risk so the reliability bar held, and the proper implementation shipped three weeks later as planned.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioural questions: Open with a brief *Situation* (1-2 sentences of context), then your *Task* (your specific responsibility), the *Action* you personally took (not what the team did), and the *Result* (a concrete outcome). Armory interviewers reportedly value specificity, so replace vague phrases like 'we improved things' with actual observations about what changed.

For system design questions: Start with a clarifying question on scale and constraints, then walk through components in a logical order: data flow first, then storage, then failure modes. For CD-specific design questions, always address rollback strategy and observability. These are core to Armory's product area, so naming them proactively signals the right instincts.

For technical deep-dives: Think out loud. Armory engineers care about how you reason through a problem, not just the final answer. If you have worked with Spinnaker, Argo CD, or similar platforms, name them explicitly and describe the specific pipeline stages or deployment strategies you configured.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Hands-on CD and DevOps experience: Armory's product lives in deployment automation. Interviewers want to hear that you have actually built, maintained, or troubleshot pipelines, not just read about them.

Kubernetes fluency: Candidates report that K8s questions appear in almost every technical round. Know your deployments, services, resource limits, and how you would debug a pod that fails to start or a rollout that stalls.

A reliability mindset: Because Armory's customers deploy critical software at scale, interviewers pay close attention to how you think about rollbacks, failure modes, and safe releases. Proactively raising these in design questions signals the right engineering instincts.

Clear communication: Armory is remote-first, so written and verbal clarity matters more than usual. Practise explaining complex technical decisions simply, as if writing a design doc for a colleague in a different time zone.

Ownership and follow-through: Behavioural questions typically probe whether you drive work to completion and escalate blockers early. Stories where you navigated obstacles and still shipped land well here.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build your foundation

Revise Kubernetes fundamentals: deployments, services, ingress, config maps, and resource management. If you have not worked with Spinnaker or Argo CD, spend a few hours reading their official documentation to understand pipeline stages and deployment strategies (blue/green, canary, rolling).

Week 2: System design practice

Practise designing a complete CI/CD system from scratch, then extend it to support canary deployments. Focus on where failures happen and how you detect and recover from them. Review how secrets are managed in cloud-native environments, for example with Vault, cloud KMS, or sealed secrets.

Week 3: Behavioural preparation

Write out 6-8 STAR stories covering: improving reliability, resolving a production incident, navigating a technical disagreement, and shipping under pressure. Map each story to Armory's product domain wherever possible.

Week 4: Final polish

Do at least two mock system-design interviews out loud, either with a peer or recorded for self-review. Check Armory's engineering blog or any public conference talks for context on how their team approaches CD problems. Prepare 3-4 thoughtful questions to ask each interviewer about team structure and deployment practices. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating CD as an afterthought. Candidates who discuss CI/CD only at the surface level ('we used Jenkins') struggle at Armory. Go deeper: what strategies did you use, what broke, and what did you fix?
  1. Skipping failure modes in design questions. Describing only the happy path signals you have not operated systems at scale. Always address what happens when a deployment fails partway through.
  1. Using 'we' throughout behavioural answers. Interviewers want to know your specific contribution. Replace 'we built X' with 'I designed the pipeline stage that handled X while my teammate covered Y.'
  1. Not asking clarifying questions in system design. Jumping straight into architecture without establishing scale, SLAs, or constraints is a common slip. A brief set of questions at the start shows engineering maturity.
  1. Ignoring observability. In the CD space, knowing how to detect that a deployment is failing (error rate, latency spikes, custom health metrics) is as important as knowing how to deploy. Mention your observability approach in every relevant design answer.
  1. Researching only the product, not the culture. Armory is remote-first and values written communication. Show that you can explain complex ideas clearly and that you document your decisions.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Armory Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report 4-5 rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical phone screen, one or two technical rounds covering coding and system design, and a final culture or values conversation. The exact structure may vary by role level and team. It is always worth asking your recruiter for the specific plan at the start of the process.

Do I need Spinnaker experience to get a Software Engineer role at Armory?

Direct Spinnaker experience is a plus but candidates report it is not always required for every role. Strong hands-on experience with a comparable platform (Argo CD, Harness, Tekton) is well received. What matters more is that you understand deployment strategies like canary and blue/green and that you have operated pipelines in production rather than just configured them once.

What salary can I expect for a Software Engineer role at Armory in India?

Armory is a US-headquartered company and compensation for India-based roles is not widely published. Based on Glassdoor and industry surveys for similar DevOps-focused product companies, mid-level Software Engineer roles in India commonly cite ranges in the 15-25 LPA band, while senior roles are commonly cited in the 28-45 LPA range. Verify current numbers directly with the recruiter, as these figures shift with the specific role and team.

Is the Armory interview process conducted remotely?

Armory is a remote-first company and candidates typically report that the entire interview process is conducted online via video calls. This makes written and verbal communication skills especially important, as interviewers are also assessing whether you can collaborate clearly across time zones and through async channels.

How long does the Armory hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on what candidates publicly report, the process from first recruiter contact to offer typically takes 3-5 weeks. The timeline can stretch if there are scheduling delays or a larger interview panel. Following up with your recruiter after each round is a straightforward way to stay informed on where you stand.

What programming languages does Armory use and test in interviews?

Armory's core platform is built with Go and Java, and candidates report that coding rounds can involve either language. Interviewers are generally flexible about language choice for standard coding problems. More important is writing clean, readable code and explaining your approach clearly. Knowing Go is a notable advantage if you are applying for a role focused on core platform work.
