01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Sardine is a fraud detection and compliance platform built for fintech companies, neobanks, crypto exchanges, and banks. The company's core product scores transactions in real time, runs KYC checks, and helps financial institutions stay compliant with regulations. It sits at a genuinely interesting crossroads: fraud prevention that also supports financial inclusion.

As of July 2026, Sardine has 35 open roles spanning engineering, data science, product, and go-to-market functions. The team is globally distributed and remote-first, which means Indian professionals are genuinely in the running for many positions, particularly in backend engineering, machine learning, fraud analytics, and compliance.

If your background touches payments, risk, financial crime, or real-time data systems, Sardine is worth a close look. The company is growing and the roles reflect that: they are building, not just maintaining.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Sardine's process typically covers four to five stages. Here is what candidates commonly report, though the exact flow can vary by role.

Application and initial screening: Your resume is reviewed against the role's requirements. Tailor it to highlight any experience with fraud, payments, fintech, or financial compliance. A direct message to the hiring manager on LinkedIn can help you get noticed faster.

Recruiter conversation: If shortlisted, a recruiter reaches out for a brief call. They want to understand your background, your interest in Sardine specifically, and whether your expectations around role, location, and compensation are a fit. Prepare a clear 'why Sardine' answer and know your target compensation range.

Technical or domain round: This stage depends on the role. Engineers typically face coding problems and system design questions, often focused on real-time data processing. Fraud analysts and compliance candidates are asked to walk through how they would investigate a suspicious transaction or structure a fraud prevention framework.

Deep-dive or panel interview: A more detailed look at your technical skills and past work. Expect pointed questions about specific projects you have shipped, how you handled ambiguity, and the trade-offs you made. For senior roles, architecture or system design discussions are common at this stage.

Final round with the hiring manager or leadership: The last stage brings in the hiring manager and sometimes a cross-functional leader. Expect a mix of technical, strategic, and culture questions. This is also your best opportunity to ask sharp questions about the team's roadmap and priorities.

Timelines vary by role and team bandwidth. Budget a few weeks between your application and a final decision. Sardine's recruiters are generally responsive, so one polite follow-up after a week of silence is reasonable.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Sardine looks for people who understand financial fraud at a meaningful level, not just candidates who can code or manage projects in the abstract.

For engineering roles: Strong skills in Python and SQL are expected. Experience with machine learning, anomaly detection, or risk-scoring systems stands out. Familiarity with streaming data, real-time pipelines, or distributed systems is a clear advantage because Sardine's core product scores transactions as they happen.

For fraud, risk, and compliance roles: Knowledge of fraud typologies such as account takeover, synthetic identity fraud, and money laundering patterns is highly valued. Familiarity with KYC, AML regulations, and compliance tooling puts you ahead of generalist candidates.

Across all roles: Sardine values intellectual curiosity, the ability to work independently in a remote setup, and clear communication. Being a strong writer matters more than at many companies because the team is distributed and decisions are made in writing. Candidates who can explain complex ideas simply and precisely tend to progress further.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Know the product before your first call. Understand what Sardine actually does: real-time fraud scoring, device intelligence, KYC orchestration, and compliance automation for fintech. Read their public blog and any recent news coverage. Candidates who speak about the product specifically, not just the category, stand out immediately.

Build your fraud fundamentals. Even for engineering roles, knowing the basics of fraud patterns, chargebacks, and financial crime signals shows the recruiter you care about the problem, not just the tech stack.

Practice system design for real-time risk. Engineers should be ready to design systems that process large transaction volumes quickly and flag anomalies with low latency. Think through trade-offs on data pipelines, feature stores, and model serving before your interview.

Prepare stories using the STAR format. Sardine's interviews lean on past experience. For each major project, have a clear story ready: the Situation, the Task you owned, the Actions you took, and the Results you delivered. Concrete outcomes land better than general descriptions.

Prepare smart questions. Have two or three questions ready about Sardine's fraud detection roadmap, how the team measures success, or how cross-functional collaboration works. Thoughtful questions signal genuine interest and leave a strong impression.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Sardine runs as a remote-first company with a distributed team across time zones. The culture leans toward ownership and autonomy: people are expected to identify problems and drive solutions without waiting to be directed. If you work best when given a clear goal and the freedom to figure out the path, that matches how Sardine operates.

The mission is central to the culture. Sardine's work sits at the intersection of fraud prevention and financial inclusion. Catching fraud protects real consumers and enables safer access to financial services. Candidates who connect their own values to this mission tend to do well in the final rounds, where culture conversations go deeper.

Communication is deliberate and written-first, as is common at distributed companies. Decisions and context are shared in writing so teammates across time zones stay aligned. Clear, concise writing is genuinely valued on the job, not just assessed during interviews.

The company has grown quickly and the pace reflects that. Roles evolve, priorities shift, and people often wear more than one hat. If you are comfortable with some ambiguity and enjoy building from relatively early stages, that energy fits well with where Sardine is headed.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does Sardine hire candidates based in India?

Sardine is remote-first and hires globally. Indian candidates are considered for many roles, particularly in engineering, data science, and fraud analytics. Check each job description carefully for location restrictions, as some go-to-market roles may require candidates in specific regions. With 35 open roles as of July 2026, there is a reasonable variety of positions to explore.

How long does the full hiring process take?

Most candidates report a process that spans a few weeks from first contact to offer. The pace depends on how quickly both sides can schedule interviews and how many stages a role involves. Following up with the recruiter after each stage is perfectly acceptable and can help keep things on track.

Do I need a fintech or banking background to apply?

It helps but is not always required. Sardine values deep understanding of fraud and risk, so experience in adjacent domains like e-commerce fraud, cybersecurity, or payments can translate well. For engineering roles, strong technical fundamentals matter as much as domain background, especially if you can connect your skills to the fraud prevention problem.

What does compensation look like at Sardine?

Sardine does not publish salary bands publicly. For realistic ranges, check platforms such as Glassdoor or levels.fyi where employees share compensation data. Compensation discussions typically come up early in the recruiter call, so research your target range before that conversation and go in with a number in mind.

Is there a take-home assignment?

Some roles include a take-home exercise, particularly for fraud analytics and data science positions. These are usually practical: you might be given a dataset and asked to identify fraud patterns or build a simple scoring model. Engineers may receive a coding problem to complete independently before a live technical interview.

How should I follow up after an interview?

Send a brief thank-you note to your recruiter within a day of each interview. Reference something specific from the conversation to make it genuine rather than a template. If you do not hear back within a week, one polite follow-up is appropriate. Sardine's team is generally communicative, so silence usually means the team is busy rather than a sign you are out of the running.
