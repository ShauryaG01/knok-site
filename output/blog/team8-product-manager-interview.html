01 Overview

Overview

Team8 is a Tel Aviv-headquartered venture creation group that builds and backs enterprise security, data, and cloud companies. Unlike a typical product company, Team8 functions as a company builder: it recruits technical co-founders, pressure-tests problems with CISOs and senior enterprise executives, then spins out funded startups. As a PM here, you sit at the intersection of deep domain knowledge in cybersecurity and enterprise B2B product thinking.

The knok jobradar currently shows 81 open roles at Team8 across functions, with Product Manager positions spanning early company-building work through growth-stage execution. This level of hiring signals active portfolio expansion in 2026.

Candidates report that Team8 interviews are rigorous and domain-intensive. Panels typically include senior PMs, founders, and security advisors or practitioners. Expect conversations designed to probe your grasp of enterprise buying dynamics, security product categories, and your ability to partner with highly technical founders. If you are applying from a consumer or mid-market background, preparation around enterprise nuances is non-negotiable.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Here are the questions candidates most commonly report in Team8 PM interviews:

  1. Tell us about a B2B product you owned end-to-end. What was the core problem, who was the buyer, and who was the daily user?
  2. How do you prioritize features when your champion inside the account loves one direction but the economic buyer cares about something else?
  3. Team8 builds in security, data, and cloud. Pick one area and walk us through how you would validate whether a new problem is worth founding a company around.
  4. Describe a time you disagreed with a very technical co-founder or engineering lead on product direction. How did you resolve it?
  5. Enterprise feedback loops can be slow. How do you stay learning without waiting for deal closures to tell you whether you are right?
  6. Walk us through how you would size the addressable market for a new enterprise identity security product.
  7. A large design partner is asking for a feature that only they need. How do you decide whether to build it?
  8. How do you write a product spec when the end user is a SOC analyst but the sign-off authority is a CISO?
  9. Tell us about a product metric you owned. How did you choose it, and what did you do when it moved in the wrong direction?
  10. How do you approach competitive positioning when a market already has well-funded incumbents?
  11. Walk us through how you would run discovery interviews with CISOs who have limited time and high skepticism toward vendors.
  12. You are joining a Team8 portfolio company at seed stage. What do you focus on in your first few weeks?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Describe a time you disagreed with a technical co-founder on product direction. How did you resolve it?

*Situation:* I was PM at an early-stage B2B security startup. Our CTO wanted to build a highly customizable policy engine, believing enterprise customers would want deep control over configuration.

*Task:* My job was to validate whether that was actually what customers valued most, and to align the roadmap without damaging the founder relationship.

*Action:* I ran discovery calls with security leads at several mid-market companies. I documented verbatim feedback and brought the CTO into two of the calls as a listener. Customers kept describing alert fatigue, not a desire for more configuration knobs. I framed the evidence as a shared learning rather than a disagreement, and proposed a narrower first version that could still extend toward the CTO's vision later.

*Result:* We shipped the simpler version first. Customers onboarded faster and our champion at the first design partner moved the deal forward without the lengthy security review that complex products typically trigger. The CTO said the calls changed his perspective more than any argument I could have made.

---

Q: How do you prioritize features when the end user and the economic buyer want different things?

*Situation:* I was managing a data observability product. Our ops team users wanted a richer alert customization UI. The VP of Engineering who controlled the budget cared primarily about reducing mean time to detect incidents.

*Task:* I had to decide which to build next while keeping both sides engaged.

*Action:* I mapped every feature request to either 'user productivity' or 'buyer outcome.' I proposed a sprint focused on detection speed, which directly addressed the buyer's goal, paired with one UX improvement that ops users would feel immediately. I shared a simple one-pager with the economic buyer showing how the detection improvement connected to the outcome they owned.

*Result:* The buyer renewed and expanded the contract at the next review. The ops team saw the UX change and felt heard. Neither group got everything at once, but both stayed aligned because the tradeoff was explained clearly.

---

Q: How would you size the market for a new enterprise identity security product?

*Situation:* In a previous role, I was asked to validate whether an identity threat detection product had a large enough market to justify building.

*Task:* I needed to produce a credible market sizing that the founding team and investors could pressure-test.

*Action:* I used a bottom-up approach. I started with the number of enterprises above a certain employee threshold in target geographies, used publicly available analyst reports for average security spend benchmarks, and triangulated against comparable products whose pricing was publicly disclosed. I also identified the key assumptions that could break the model and listed them explicitly.

*Result:* The sizing gave the team a working range with clear sensitivity. It held up through investor diligence because the methodology was transparent and the assumptions were named rather than hidden.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For product strategy questions, use a problem-first structure: start with who the customer is and what job they are hiring the product to do, then move to the current solution gap, then your approach, then how you would know you are right. Team8 interviewers care deeply about whether you can identify real enterprise pain rather than assumed pain.

For prioritization questions, be explicit about what signals you use. Candidates report that interviewers push back when answers are too framework-mechanical. Mention customer evidence, sales input, and business outcome alongside any scoring method. In enterprise contexts, explain how you weigh design partner requests against broader market signals.

For market sizing questions, use bottom-up sizing with named assumptions. Start from a defined customer segment rather than from a large market headline number. Interviewers at a company builder like Team8 have seen many sizing exercises and quickly notice when assumptions are glossed over.

For behavioral questions, follow STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) but keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your time on Action and Result. For enterprise product roles, try to include at least one of: a sales interaction, a design partner dynamic, or a technical trade-off. These are the contexts Team8 interviewers consider most relevant.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Team8 interviewers are typically founders and senior operators who have built enterprise security products. They are looking for a few qualities that separate strong candidates from average ones.

Enterprise domain fluency. You do not need to be a security engineer, but you must speak the language of CISOs, SOC analysts, and enterprise procurement. Candidates who confuse buyers and users, or who describe enterprise selling as similar to consumer conversion funnels, typically struggle in later rounds.

Founder-ready collaboration. Because Team8 companies are early-stage, PMs work closely with technical co-founders. Interviewers look for evidence that you can earn trust from engineers and researchers without needing a large team or established process behind you.

Evidence-led thinking. Interviewers consistently test whether your decisions are backed by customer evidence or driven by assumption. Bring specific quotes, specific observations, or named customer interactions wherever possible.

Comfort with ambiguity and slow feedback loops. Enterprise products often have outcomes that take a long time to show up in metrics. Candidates who can articulate how they design learning loops without relying only on lagging indicators tend to stand out.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain immersion. Read publicly available threat reports from major cybersecurity vendors. Understand the broad categories: endpoint, identity, cloud security, and data security. You do not need to become technical, but you should be able to describe a CISO's top priorities without prompting.

Week 2: Company and portfolio research. Go through Team8's public website and press coverage to understand which portfolio companies exist and what problems they solve. For each one, practice describing the buyer, the user, and the core value proposition in two or three sentences.

Week 3: Behavioral story prep. Map your work history to the themes most common in Team8 interviews: co-founder collaboration, enterprise customer discovery, prioritization under constraint, and market validation. Write out at least one STAR story per theme.

Week 4: Mock interviews and market sizing practice. Practice market sizing out loud, not just on paper. Have a friend or colleague interrupt you with follow-up questions. Candidates report that Team8 interviewers probe the assumptions behind your numbers more than the numbers themselves.

On the day, ask clarifying questions before diving into any framework. Interviewers appreciate candidates who slow down to define the problem correctly before jumping to a solution.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating enterprise like consumer. Describing your roadmap decisions purely through user engagement metrics, without mentioning buyer goals, sales cycles, or renewal dynamics, signals a consumer-product mindset. Team8 builds for enterprise, and interviewers notice this gap quickly.

Naming frameworks without substance. Saying 'I use RICE scoring' without explaining how you weighted each factor in a real situation tends to land poorly. Interviewers at Team8 push for the specifics behind the method.

Skipping the buyer-user distinction. A very common slip is describing a product decision without distinguishing who uses the product daily from who approves the purchase. In enterprise security this distinction is critical, and missing it suggests a gap in B2B PM experience.

Over-claiming on outcomes. Candidates sometimes cite improvements without being clear about sample sizes or attribution. If your data was thin, say so and explain what you would have done differently with more time or data. Intellectual honesty reads well at a company that values rigorous validation.

Ignoring the company-building context. Team8 PMs are not just shipping features into an existing product. They are often helping to define what the product is. Candidates who arrive with a pure execution mindset, without curiosity about problem selection or market validation, miss what the role actually demands.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the Team8 PM interview typically have?

Candidates report varying processes depending on which portfolio company is hiring, but typically expect a recruiter screening call, one or two domain and behavioral rounds, and a final conversation with a founder or senior leader. Some portfolio companies include a case study or product exercise. The exact structure differs by company, so ask your recruiter early about what to expect for your specific role.

Do I need a cybersecurity background to become a PM at Team8?

A dedicated security background is not always required, but strong enterprise B2B PM experience is typically expected. Candidates who demonstrate experience with technical buyers, enterprise sales cycles, and rigorous customer discovery tend to advance even without a security-specific title. Investing time in understanding the cybersecurity landscape before your interview significantly improves your chances.

What salary can I expect for a PM role at Team8 in India?

Salary varies by seniority and the specific portfolio company. Based on knok jobradar data for Indian PM roles, broad ranges are 24-40 LPA for PMs with 3-6 years of experience and 40-60 LPA at the Senior PM level. Actual offers at venture-backed startups depend on company stage, your background, and equity structure, which can vary significantly from the base compensation figure.

Which cities have the most PM openings relevant to Team8?

The knok jobradar shows 81 open roles at Team8 currently. The broader PM market in India is concentrated in Bangalore (271 PM roles across all companies), Delhi (177), and Mumbai (56) as of mid-2026. Team8 portfolio companies hire across multiple locations and some roles may be remote-friendly, so check the specific job posting for location requirements.

How should I prepare for a market sizing question in a Team8 interview?

Practice bottom-up sizing where you start from a defined customer segment and build upward, rather than starting from a large headline market number and dividing down. Name your assumptions explicitly and be ready to defend each one. Candidates report that Team8 interviewers are more interested in the quality of your reasoning than in arriving at any specific final number.

Is there a take-home case study or assignment in the process?

Some Team8 portfolio companies include a product case study or short written exercise, but this varies by company and role. Candidates report that when a case is given, it tends to focus on a real enterprise security scenario rather than a generic consumer product prompt. Ask your recruiter whether a case study is part of the process for your specific role so you can prepare accordingly.
