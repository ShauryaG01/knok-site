01 Overview

Overview

Hevodata is a Bangalore-based product company that builds a no-code data integration platform. Businesses use it to move data automatically from sources like Salesforce, Postgres, and Shopify into data warehouses, without writing custom ETL code. The engineering team works on connectors, real-time pipelines, distributed systems, and a multi-tenant SaaS backend.

As of mid-2026, Hevodata has 40 open Software Engineer positions. Candidates typically go through 3-4 rounds: an online coding screen, one or two technical rounds covering algorithms and system design, and a final hiring-manager or culture fit round. The process typically takes 2-3 weeks from application to offer.

Software Engineer compensation benchmarks from knok's broader market data run 6-12 LPA for entry-level (0-2 years), 15-25 LPA for mid-level (3-5 years), and 28-45 LPA for senior roles (6-9 years) across the Bangalore product-company market. For Hevodata-specific ranges, check Glassdoor or levels.fyi closer to your interview date.

The technical rounds lean heavily toward data pipeline knowledge, distributed systems, and real-world problem-solving. Strong candidates come in knowing the basics of ETL, CDC (Change Data Capture), and fault-tolerant system design.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How do you handle schema evolution in a data pipeline without breaking downstream consumers?

*Situation:* At a previous company, our pipeline ingested events from a third-party CRM. The vendor updated their API schema without notice, renaming a key field and adding several new ones.

*Task:* I had to prevent this change from breaking our downstream analytics tables, which over a dozen internal teams relied on daily.

*Action:* I introduced a schema registry layer that versioned incoming schemas and mapped old field names to new ones via a transformation config file. I also added automated validation checks that ran on every pipeline deploy, comparing the live source schema against the registered version and alerting before any bad data reached the warehouse.

*Result:* The next three vendor schema changes were handled with zero downtime. The registry was later adopted by two other pipeline teams in the organisation.

---

Q: Describe a time you had to make a call on a production issue with incomplete information.

*Situation:* Our main ingestion pipeline stopped processing events for a key customer at 2 AM. Monitoring showed a spike in lag but logs were ambiguous about the root cause.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and had to decide quickly whether to roll back the last deployment or dig deeper, knowing a full rollback might not fix the actual problem.

*Action:* I scanned the recent deployment diff for anything touching the affected connector and found a config change that had altered the batch size. I tested a targeted revert of just that config value in staging, confirmed it resolved the lag, then applied only that fix to production rather than triggering a full rollback.

*Result:* The pipeline recovered faster than a full rollback would have allowed. I documented the root cause and we added a batch-size validation step to the deploy pipeline to prevent recurrence.

---

Q: How would you design a multi-tenant system so one customer's load does not affect others?

*Situation:* We were onboarding a new enterprise customer whose data volume was far higher than our typical customers. Our shared ingestion workers were near capacity before the new customer even went live.

*Task:* I needed a strategy to protect existing customers from performance degradation during and after the new onboarding.

*Action:* I proposed tenant-aware work queues with separate worker pools for high-volume tenants, using a threshold-based classifier to route jobs at ingestion time. I also added per-tenant rate limiting at the API layer and a per-tenant lag dashboard so the team could spot isolation failures in real time.

*Result:* The enterprise customer was onboarded without any SLA breach for existing customers. The architecture was later adopted as the default isolation model for all new enterprise onboarding.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For system design questions, use a 4-part structure: clarify scope and scale, sketch the high-level architecture, dive into the trickiest component (usually fault tolerance or consistency), then discuss the tradeoffs you made. For Hevodata roles, anchor your design in real pipeline concepts: connectors, message queues, workers, schema validation, and delivery guarantees.

For data pipeline questions specifically, lead with 'what happens when something fails.' Hevodata's product is about reliability, so showing you think about retries, dead-letter queues, and idempotency will stand out far more than a clever algorithm alone.

For coding questions, talk through your approach before writing a single line of code. Interviewers at product companies typically value readable, working code over micro-optimised solutions. State time and space complexity once you have a working answer.

For behavioral questions, use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief (2-3 sentences combined) and spend the bulk of your time on the Action. End with a concrete, specific Result. Candidates report Hevodata interviewers respond well to answers showing ownership and follow-through, not just technical knowledge.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Domain familiarity with data engineering concepts. You do not need to have built a Hevodata-style product before, but you should understand ETL, CDC, data warehouses, and the challenges of moving data reliably at scale. Brush up on Kafka, Spark, or Flink basics even if your hands-on experience is limited.

Ownership mindset. Hevodata is a product company where engineers typically own features end to end. Interviewers look for candidates who have debugged production issues, made architectural decisions, and genuinely cared about the customer impact of their work.

Clear thinking under pressure. The system design round tests whether you can think out loud, handle new constraints mid-discussion, and adjust your design when the interviewer introduces a new requirement. Practise talking through your reasoning rather than jumping straight to a final answer.

Practical coding skill. Expect standard data structure and algorithm questions, but also applied problems: parsing a log file, writing a retry mechanism, or building a simple rate limiter. Clean, working code matters more than algorithmic perfection.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Build Your Foundation
Revise core data structures (trees, graphs, hashmaps, heaps) and work through 10-15 medium-level coding problems. Spend a few hours reading how data pipelines work end to end: source connectors, transformation layers, and sink destinations. Understanding the vocabulary helps in every single round.

Week 2: System Design and Domain Depth
Study distributed systems basics: CAP theorem, eventual consistency, message queues, and exactly-once delivery semantics. Read up on CDC using Debezium or a similar tool. Practice designing one complete data pipeline system from scratch, including failure scenarios and recovery paths.

Week 3: Company-Specific Preparation
Sign up for Hevodata's free trial and explore how their pipeline product actually works. Prepare 4-5 STAR stories from your own experience covering: a production incident you owned, a system design decision you made, a cross-team collaboration, and a time you improved a system's reliability or performance.

Do at least 2 mock system design interviews with a peer. Record yourself answering behavioral questions and listen back for clarity and filler words.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating the interview like a generic coding screen. Hevodata engineers work on data infrastructure every day. Candidates who never mention pipeline-specific concerns (retries, ordering guarantees, schema changes) come across as underprepared for the domain, even if their algorithm answers are strong.

Skipping requirements in system design. Jumping straight to a solution without asking about data volume, freshness requirements, or consistency needs makes you look like someone who builds before thinking. Always clarify constraints first.

Vague behavioral answers. Saying you 'improved system performance significantly' without any supporting detail loses impact. Use specific context from your own work, even qualitative details, and label them clearly as your own experience.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates report that Hevodata interviewers notice when a candidate shows genuine curiosity about the product and the engineering challenges the team faces. Prepare 2-3 specific questions in advance.

Ignoring multi-tenancy. Because Hevodata is a SaaS product, questions about tenant isolation, noisy-neighbour problems, and per-customer reliability come up often. Do not design every system as if it serves a single user.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does Hevodata typically have for Software Engineers?

Candidates report typically 3-4 rounds: an online coding assessment, one or two technical rounds covering algorithms and system design, and a final round with a hiring manager or senior engineer. The exact number can vary by team and the level of the role. The full process from first contact to offer typically takes 2-3 weeks.

What programming language should I use in Hevodata interviews?

Candidates report being allowed to choose their preferred language for coding rounds, with Java and Python being the most common choices. Hevodata's backend is primarily Java-based, so comfort with Java can be a plus. Focus on writing clean, readable code rather than trying to impress with language-specific tricks.

Do I need prior data engineering experience to get a Software Engineer role at Hevodata?

Not necessarily, but you should understand the problem space well enough to discuss it confidently. Knowing what a data pipeline does, why schema changes are painful, and how distributed queues work will set you apart from candidates who treat it as a purely algorithms interview. Spend a few hours exploring their product and reading about ETL and CDC before your first round.

What salary should I expect at Hevodata for a Software Engineer?

Hevodata-specific salary data is limited in public sources. Based on knok's broader Software Engineer market data for Bangalore, mid-level roles (3-5 years) commonly fall in the 15-25 LPA range and senior roles (6-9 years) in the 28-45 LPA range. For current Hevodata-specific figures, check recent posts on Glassdoor or levels.fyi, and always negotiate with any competing offers in hand.

Is there a take-home assignment in Hevodata's interview process?

Some candidates report receiving a take-home coding task focused on a data processing problem, though this varies by team and role level. If you are given one, treat it as a production-quality submission: include basic error handling, tests where appropriate, and a short write-up explaining your design choices. Candidates who submit minimal code with no explanation typically do not advance to the next round.

How should I prepare for the system design round specifically at Hevodata?

Design at least one data pipeline system from scratch before your interview. Pick a scenario like 'ingest a large volume of events per minute from multiple sources into a data warehouse' and work through the full architecture including failure handling and schema evolution. Focus on fault tolerance, tenant isolation, and delivery guarantees, since these are central to what Hevodata's product does. Practise explaining your tradeoffs out loud rather than just sketching diagrams in silence.
