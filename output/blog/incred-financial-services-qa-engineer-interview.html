01 Overview

Overview

InCred Financial Services is an NBFC (non-banking financial company) focused on consumer and business lending: personal loans, education loans, and SME finance. As of mid-2026, knok's jobradar shows 6 open QA Engineer roles at InCred, a meaningful hiring push for a company of this size.

The interview process typically spans 2-3 rounds. Candidates report an initial HR or recruiter screening call, followed by one or two technical rounds covering manual testing, automation, and sometimes a domain-specific case study. A final round with a hiring manager or team lead assesses communication and cultural fit.

QA Engineers at InCred are expected to understand how financial workflows operate, not just how to write test cases. Think loan disbursement flows, EMI calculations, credit scoring APIs, and regulatory compliance checks. If you have fintech or BFSI testing experience, highlight it from your very first answer.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you design test cases for a loan EMI calculation module?

*Situation:* At my previous company, we built an EMI calculator for personal loans with variable interest rates, processing fees, and prepayment options.

*Task:* I was responsible for writing end-to-end test coverage before the feature went live on the mobile app.

*Action:* I started by mapping every input combination: loan amount range, tenure options, interest rate tiers, and fee structures. I wrote positive tests for standard scenarios, negative tests for boundary values (zero tenure, maximum loan amount), and edge cases like mid-cycle prepayment. I also added API-level tests to validate the calculation logic independently of the UI, using a data-driven test suite in Postman.

*Result:* We caught a rounding error in the interest calculation that would have charged users a slightly higher EMI than quoted. The fix went in before launch, and the product team credited the thorough test design for preventing a compliance issue.

---

Q: Tell me about a critical defect you found close to a release.

*Situation:* The day before a scheduled go-live for a loan disbursement feature, I was running final regression tests.

*Task:* My job was to sign off on the release, but I noticed an anomaly in the disbursement API response.

*Action:* I isolated the issue to a missing null check in the bank account validation step, which would silently skip IFSC code verification under certain input conditions. I immediately raised a P1 defect ticket, shared a detailed reproduction script with the dev lead, and flagged the risk to the product manager with a clear impact statement: 'If this goes live, disbursements to invalid accounts will fail silently and require manual reversal.'

*Result:* The team pushed back the release by several hours, patched the bug, and ran a targeted re-test. The delayed launch prevented what would have been a significant customer and compliance incident.

---

Q: Which automation framework would you recommend for a fintech product and why?

*Situation:* At a previous role, we had almost no automation coverage and were manually regression-testing a payments application every sprint.

*Task:* I was asked to evaluate and set up an automation framework from scratch.

*Action:* I compared Selenium with TestNG, Cypress, and Playwright for the web layer, and RestAssured for APIs. I recommended Playwright for the UI layer because of its built-in waiting mechanisms and parallel test support, combined with RestAssured for API testing. I wrote a proof-of-concept covering the most critical user journeys, integrated it with our Jenkins pipeline, and documented the setup so other team members could contribute tests.

*Result:* Within a few months, we had automated coverage on our core payment flows, and regression time dropped from multiple days to a fraction of that per sprint.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For technical questions: Start with your approach (how you think about the problem), give a concrete example (what you actually did), then state the outcome or lesson learned. Avoid listing tool names without context. Interviewers want to see that you understand 'why' you chose a tool, not just 'what' you used.

For behavioral questions: Use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Keep Situation and Task brief. Spend most of your answer on Action (what exactly you did, step by step) and close with a concrete Result. Vague outcomes like 'the team was happy' are weak. Specific outcomes like 'the bug was caught before launch' are strong.

For domain questions about financial testing: Acknowledge the domain context first ('In a fintech context, data accuracy is critical because...'), then answer. This signals that you understand the stakes of working in a regulated industry, which is exactly what InCred interviewers are probing for.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Fintech testing is not the same as testing an e-commerce app. A bug in a loan calculation or a KYC flow has real financial and regulatory consequences. InCred interviewers typically look for candidates who feel the weight of that responsibility.

Domain awareness: You do not need to be a finance expert, but you should understand loan lifecycle basics, what EMI calculation involves, and why KYC and AML checks matter. Candidates who treat a financial API the same as a grocery cart API stand out for the wrong reasons.

Test depth: Can you go beyond happy-path scenarios? Interviewers watch for whether you naturally think about boundary values, negative cases, and data integrity, especially in financial calculations.

Communication: Can you write a defect report that gets acted on? Can you escalate a risk clearly without creating panic? These skills matter more at InCred than at a generic product startup, because the cost of a silent failure in financial services is high.

Automation maturity: Not just 'I know Selenium' but 'here is how I built and maintained a suite that actually helped the team ship faster.' They want to hire someone who makes the team more reliable, not someone who just runs scripts.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Core QA fundamentals. Review test case design techniques (equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, decision tables), the defect lifecycle, and test planning basics. Revisit your strongest automation framework and make sure you can explain your choice clearly and write a clean test from scratch.

Week 2: Fintech domain knowledge. Study how loan origination works, what EMI calculation involves (principal, rate, tenure, processing fees), how KYC and AML checks are conducted, and how APIs function in a financial system. Practise writing test cases for a sample EMI calculator and a bank account validation API. You do not need to become a finance professional, just fluent enough to talk about it credibly.

Week 3: Interview practice. Prepare STAR answers for several common behavioral questions: a defect you escalated, automation you built, a conflict with a developer, a tight deadline. Do at least two mock technical rounds where you talk through your test approach out loud. If you have any fintech or BFSI project work, prepare to walk through it in detail.

Day before: Review InCred's current product offerings. Know whether you are interviewing for a team working on personal loans, education loans, or SME lending. Tailoring one or two examples to their specific domain will make you memorable.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

  1. Treating it like a generic IT company interview. InCred operates in financial services. Ignoring domain context in your answers signals that you have not thought about what QA means in a regulated industry.
  1. Only naming tools without explaining your thinking. Saying 'I use Selenium' tells an interviewer very little. Explain why you chose it for a specific problem and what trade-offs you considered.
  1. Writing only happy-path test cases in live exercises. Fintech interviewers specifically look for negative tests, edge cases, and data validation scenarios. A test plan with only sunny-day scenarios is a red flag.
  1. Being vague about bugs you found. Always have a specific, concrete example ready: what the bug was, how you found it, what the impact would have been, and what happened after you raised it.
  1. Not asking about the team's current stack and process. Asking thoughtful questions shows curiosity and helps you tailor any remaining answers to what they actually care about.
  1. Underestimating the behavioral or final round. Senior and lead-level QA roles at InCred especially weigh communication, stakeholder management, and the ability to advocate for quality under pressure.

Do I need fintech or BFSI experience to get a QA role at InCred?

Fintech experience helps, but candidates report getting through with strong core QA skills and a genuine understanding of financial workflows. If you do not have direct BFSI experience, study how loan origination, EMI calculation, and KYC processes work, and practise applying your testing skills to those scenarios before the interview.
