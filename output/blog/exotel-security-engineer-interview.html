01 Overview

Overview

Exotel is one of India's largest cloud communications platforms, handling calls, SMS, and messaging integrations for thousands of businesses in banking, healthcare, and logistics. A Security Engineer here works across a stack that includes cloud infrastructure (primarily AWS), microservices, telecom APIs, and real-time data pipelines. The role blends application security, infrastructure hardening, and compliance work (covering India's DPDP Act and telecom regulatory requirements). With 65 open roles at Exotel as of mid-2026, the company is clearly in a growth phase and security is a priority function. Candidates report the hiring process typically involves a resume screening, a technical assessment or take-home problem, two to three technical interviews, and a final round with a hiring manager. The entire process can run two to four weeks. Bangalore is the primary hub for these roles.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you handle a security incident where customer call recordings were potentially exposed?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a misconfigured S3 bucket policy briefly exposed an internal logging service to public read access. The bucket held metadata (not recordings), but the risk pattern was identical to a call recording exposure scenario.

*Task:* I was the on-call security engineer that night and needed to contain the exposure, assess impact, and communicate clearly to stakeholders without causing panic.

*Action:* I immediately revoked the public bucket policy and enabled S3 Block Public Access at the account level. I pulled CloudTrail logs to check for any GetObject calls from external IPs during the window the policy was open. I cross-referenced those IPs against our known internal ranges and flagged two suspicious access events for further review. I drafted an internal incident report within four hours and worked with legal to determine customer notification obligations under applicable data protection rules.

*Result:* No customer data was confirmed accessed by an unauthorised party. We added automated Config rules to alert on any future public bucket creation, and the incident drove a full S3 access review across all accounts.

---

Q: Walk us through your approach to threat modeling for a cloud-hosted platform.

*Situation:* My team was launching a new real-time transcription feature that would process audio streams from live calls.

*Task:* As the security DRI for the feature, I needed to identify risks before the first line of production code was written.

*Action:* I ran a STRIDE session with the product and engineering leads, mapping every data flow: audio in from the telephony layer, transcription by an ML service, storage in encrypted buckets, retrieval via an internal API. For each flow I asked who can spoof the source, where data can be tampered, what happens if a service is unavailable, and which flows bypass our usual auth checks. We identified two high-risk paths where the ML service had overly broad IAM permissions.

*Result:* We scoped down the IAM policies before launch, added mutual TLS between the telephony and ML services, and documented the threat model so future engineers could update it as the feature evolved. No security incidents were reported on this feature in the months after launch.

---

Q: How do you embed security in a CI/CD pipeline without slowing engineers down?

*Situation:* The engineering team at my last company deployed multiple times a day, but security reviews were manual and often skipped under release pressure.

*Task:* I was asked to design an automated security gate that would catch high-severity issues without blocking every deployment.

*Action:* I integrated SAST (Semgrep) and dependency scanning (OWASP Dependency-Check) into the GitHub Actions workflow. Critical findings blocked the merge; medium findings created JIRA tickets automatically but did not block. I also added a secrets-detection step using truffleHog to catch hardcoded credentials before they reached any environment. I ran a short knowledge-sharing session to explain to engineers what each check caught and why, which reduced dismissed alerts significantly.

*Result:* Within the first quarter, the pipeline caught three instances of hardcoded API keys before they reached production and flagged a critical CVE in a third-party library two days before a public exploit was published.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for every behavioural question. Keep the Situation and Task brief (two to three sentences combined) and spend most of your answer on the Action: what you specifically did, the tools you chose, and the trade-offs you made. The Result should be concrete, but if you cannot share exact numbers (Exotel interviewers understand NDAs), describe the outcome qualitatively, for example 'no customer data was confirmed accessed' or 'reduced mean time to detect by roughly half'.

STRIDE for threat modeling questions. If asked about threat modeling, name the framework and walk through at least two or three of the six categories (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege) as they apply to the specific system in the question. This signals structured thinking, not just a list of generic risks.

Defense-in-depth for architecture questions. When asked how you would secure a system, layer your answer: network controls first, then identity and access, then data protection, then monitoring and detection. This mirrors how most mature security teams think and shows you understand that no single control is sufficient.

DACI or RACI for cross-team questions. If asked how you would drive a security programme or get engineers to adopt a new control, briefly map out who Drives, Approves, is Consulted, and is Informed. It shows you know security is a team sport, not a solo gatekeeping role.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Exotel interviewers, based on candidate reports, are looking for a few things beyond pure technical knowledge.

Practical, hands-on depth. The company runs real production systems at scale. Interviewers probe for the difference between someone who has read about a technique and someone who has actually done it. Expect follow-up questions like 'what went wrong?' or 'what would you do differently?'

Telecom and cloud security awareness. Even if you have not worked at a CPaaS company before, showing awareness of telecom-specific threats (toll fraud, SSRF in carrier integrations, regex denial-of-service in call routing logic) and cloud-native controls (IAM least privilege, VPC design, CloudTrail) signals you understand the domain.

Comfort with ambiguity. Exotel is a fast-moving company. Candidates who ask clarifying questions before diving in, and who can articulate trade-offs when the 'right answer' depends on context, tend to perform better than those who present a single definitive solution.

Communication clarity. Security engineers at Exotel work closely with product and engineering teams who are not security specialists. Interviewers look for candidates who can explain a risk or a control in plain language, not just in security jargon.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week 1: Domain grounding.

Review the core concepts that come up repeatedly for CPaaS security roles: API authentication patterns (OAuth 2.0, API keys, mTLS), cloud IAM (especially AWS), the OWASP API Security Top 10, and common telecom fraud patterns. Read Exotel's engineering blog and any public case studies to understand their architecture and the problems they have already solved.

Week 2: Practice and mock interviews.

Pick three to five past incidents or projects from your own experience and structure them as STAR stories. Practice saying them out loud, timing yourself at two to three minutes per answer. Do at least two mock technical interviews with a peer or using an AI tool. Focus on threat modeling exercises: pick a system (a payment gateway, a messaging API) and walk through STRIDE on your own before you are asked to do it live.

Week 3: Company and role specifics.

Check Exotel's LinkedIn page, Glassdoor reviews, and any public engineering talks or blog posts. Understand their product lines (cloud telephony, CPaaS APIs, contact centre solutions) and think about what security challenges are unique to each. Prepare two or three specific questions to ask the interviewer about their current security roadmap and what a typical week looks like for the team.

On the day. For take-home assessments (which candidates report are common), read the brief twice before starting and state your assumptions clearly in writing. Reviewers often care as much about how you think as about whether your final answer is 'correct'. 07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Giving textbook answers without context. Saying 'I would use encryption at rest and in transit' without explaining which keys, which service, and what the threat is tells an interviewer very little. Always anchor your answer to a real or realistic scenario.

Skipping the 'why'. Exotel engineers ask follow-up questions. If you recommend a tool or a control, be ready to explain why you chose it over alternatives. 'I used Semgrep because it lets you write custom rules for our specific codebase' is far stronger than 'I used Semgrep because it is popular.'

Underselling non-traditional experience. If your background is in network security, sysadmin work, or software development, do not dismiss it. Frame it: 'My development background means I can read the code in a security review, not just run a scanner against it.'

Ignoring the compliance angle. Exotel processes communications data for Indian businesses. Candidates who never mention India's DPDP Act, the IT Act, or TRAI guidelines miss an important dimension of the role. You do not need to be a legal expert, but showing awareness matters.

Not asking questions at the end. Candidates who ask nothing at the close of a round leave a weak final impression. Prepare at least two genuine questions about the team, the current threat landscape they are dealing with, or how they measure security maturity.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does Exotel's Security Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates report a process that typically runs three to four rounds: an initial screening (sometimes with HR, sometimes directly with a technical lead), one or two technical interviews covering security concepts and past experience, and a final round with a senior leader or hiring manager. A take-home assessment or live scenario exercise is also commonly included. The exact structure can vary by team and the seniority of the role.

Does Exotel ask coding questions in security interviews?

Candidates report that heavy algorithmic coding (LeetCode-style problems) is not the main focus for security roles at Exotel. However, you may be asked to write a small script, commonly in Python, to parse logs, detect anomalies, or demonstrate a security concept. Knowing how to read and write basic code is more important than mastering competitive programming.

What salary can I expect for a Security Engineer at Exotel?

Exotel does not publish salary bands publicly. Glassdoor and industry surveys suggest Security Engineer compensation at comparable Indian CPaaS and SaaS companies varies widely based on years of experience and specialisation. Check Glassdoor reviews for Exotel specifically and benchmark against publicly reported data for your target level before entering the negotiation conversation.

Is prior CPaaS or telecom experience required?

Not strictly. Candidates report that Exotel values strong security fundamentals and cloud experience highly, and that telecom-specific knowledge can be learned on the job. Showing even basic awareness of telecom threats (toll fraud, SSRF in carrier APIs, TRAI regulatory requirements) in your interview answers will set you apart from candidates who have only worked in SaaS or enterprise IT security.

How long does the Exotel hiring process take from application to offer?

Based on candidate reports, the full process from application to offer typically takes two to four weeks, though it can run longer depending on team availability and the number of rounds required. Following up politely after each round within two to three business days is considered acceptable and can help keep your candidacy moving forward.

Are there Security Engineer roles at Exotel outside Bangalore?

The majority of Security Engineer openings at Exotel are based in Bangalore, with a smaller number appearing in cities like Delhi and Pune. If you are based elsewhere, it is worth confirming remote or hybrid options directly with the recruiter, as policies can vary by team and seniority level.
