01 Overview

Overview

IMC is a global market maker and high-frequency trading firm with a strong technology culture and offices across Amsterdam, Chicago, Sydney, and India. Product Manager roles here sit at the intersection of engineering, quantitative research, and trading operations, which makes the interview process more technical than you might expect at a typical tech company. Candidates report that IMC values PMs who can hold detailed conversations with engineers and quants, not just translate requirements between teams.

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows IMC has 162 open roles, a sign of active growth across product and technology teams. PM positions here typically focus on internal-facing products: trading platforms, risk management systems, market data infrastructure, and tooling built for traders and quantitative researchers. Across India, there are 2,009 PM openings in the same period, with Bangalore leading at 271 and Delhi at 177. If you are coming from a SaaS or consumer tech background, expect IMC interviews to probe your comfort with high-stakes internal users, low-latency systems, and genuinely ambiguous problem spaces.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

Candidates report the following themes appearing frequently across IMC PM interviews. Questions typically span behavioral, technical product, and case formats.

  1. Walk me through a product you built that required close collaboration with engineers on performance or reliability constraints.
  2. Describe a time you made a major product decision with incomplete data. What was your process and how did it turn out?
  3. How would you prioritize features on a trading platform where latency, reliability, and usability all compete for attention?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to align traders, quants, and engineers around a single product direction.
  5. How do you gather requirements from internal users who have strong domain expertise and equally strong opinions?
  6. Describe a live incident or production failure that affected a product you owned. Walk me through your response.
  7. How would you define and measure success for a new order management feature deployed to a trading desk?
  8. IMC values first-principles thinking. How would you approach redesigning a legacy internal tool from the ground up?
  9. Tell me about a time you decided to kill or descope a feature. How did you justify that call to stakeholders?
  10. How do you balance short-term user requests from traders against longer-term platform scalability goals?
  11. How do you handle a situation where regulatory requirements shift mid-roadmap and affect your current sprint?
  12. Walk me through how you would structure a discovery process for a new analytics product targeting quantitative researchers.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you made a major product decision with incomplete data.

*Situation:* Our trading analytics dashboard showed a sharp drop in usage, but we had only two weeks of post-launch data and no direct feedback from the trading desk.

*Task:* I had to decide whether to roll back a recent UI overhaul or push forward with targeted fixes, without a clear signal on root cause.

*Action:* I ran a focused session with three senior traders to understand their daily workflows, mapped the friction points they described to the specific UI changes we had shipped, and used session recordings to confirm the pattern. I proposed rolling back two specific components rather than the full release, and got buy-in from engineering and the trading desk lead within two days.

*Result:* Usage recovered within the following week, and the findings from the investigation fed directly into our design review process going forward.

---

Q: Describe a live incident that affected a product you owned.

*Situation:* A data pipeline feeding our real-time risk dashboard went down during market hours, leaving risk managers without position visibility for a significant stretch of time.

*Task:* As PM, I had no direct ability to fix the pipeline, but the incident needed coordination across three engineering teams while traders and risk managers were escalating in parallel.

*Action:* I set up a bridge call immediately, assigned a single engineering lead to own the technical resolution, and kept traders updated on a regular cadence with plain-language status. I also documented a workaround using a backup report so risk managers were not operating blind while the fix was in progress.

*Result:* The pipeline was restored before end of session, no trading positions were affected, and the post-mortem I led produced three concrete reliability improvements that shipped in the next sprint.

---

Q: How do you balance short-term trader requests against longer-term platform goals?

*Situation:* Our trading platform roadmap was anchored to a multi-quarter infrastructure migration, but the equities desk had an urgent request for a custom filter feature they said was blocking their workflow.

*Task:* I needed to either fit the request into the current sprint, defer it with a clear timeline, or find a middle path, without derailing the migration.

*Action:* I broke down the request with the engineer closest to the relevant module. We found we could ship most of the value as a configuration change in a few days, with no migration risk. I communicated this to the desk with an honest note that the remaining scope would arrive post-migration, along with the reason why.

*Result:* The desk unblocked their workflow, the migration stayed on schedule, and the traders appreciated being told the 'why' rather than just 'not now'.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions. Use Situation, Task, Action, Result for any question that starts with 'Tell me about a time.' Keep your Situation and Task brief (one or two sentences each) so you have room to detail your Action. IMC interviewers typically probe on what you personally did, so avoid 'we' without following up with 'specifically, I.'

Prioritization frameworks for product questions. When asked how you would prioritize features, reach for a structured approach: clarify the goal first, then rank by impact on that goal versus effort or risk. For a trading firm, reliability and latency are often non-negotiable constraints, so your framework should treat them as hard filters before any other scoring.

First-principles for redesign questions. IMC specifically values starting from the problem, not the existing solution. If asked to redesign a product, state the user's core job to be done, list the constraints (latency, compliance, user type), then build up a solution from there. Avoid anchoring on how the current system already works.

Data-first for metrics questions. When defining success for a new feature, name a primary metric, a guardrail metric, and a leading indicator. For internal tools at a trading firm, adoption rate and time-on-task are commonly cited proxies for value, but be ready to explain why you chose them over other candidates.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Technical depth without an engineering title. IMC PMs are expected to understand system architecture, data flows, and performance trade-offs at a meaningful level. You do not need to write code, but you should be able to explain how a feature you shipped worked under the hood, and ask informed questions when engineers push back on feasibility.

Comfort with internal-user dynamics. Unlike consumer products, IMC's products serve traders, quants, and risk managers. These users are highly expert, time-pressured, and direct. Interviewers want to see that you can earn credibility with these users rather than simply gather requirements from them.

Structured thinking under pressure. Candidates report that case-style questions at IMC often have no clean answer. Interviewers are watching your process: do you clarify the problem before jumping in, do you state your assumptions, and do you course-correct when given new information?

Ownership and accountability. Stories where you deflected blame or waited for direction tend to land poorly. IMC values people who run toward ambiguity, raise problems early, and close the loop on outcomes.

Collaboration with quants and engineers. If you can show a specific example of working closely with a quantitative or engineering team to shape a product outcome, that carries significant weight in the overall evaluation.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: Know the company and domain.
Read publicly available material on how market makers operate, what a trading platform does, and what risk management means in practice. You do not need deep quant knowledge, but you should be able to speak about why latency matters, what a trade lifecycle looks like, and what kinds of internal tools a trading desk relies on daily.

Week two: Prepare your STAR stories.
Pick five or six real examples from your past that map to the themes in the most-asked questions above. For each one, write out all four parts of the STAR structure. Practice saying them out loud, aiming for two to three minutes per story. Cut ruthlessly: if you are still on Situation after a full minute, restart.

Week three: Product and case practice.
Practice prioritization exercises using a trading-context framing: 'You have a platform with three pending features, a latency complaint from the equities desk, and a compliance deadline. What do you ship first?' Time yourself. Practice stating your framework before your answer, not after.

Before the interview.
Review IMC's publicly available job descriptions carefully. Note the specific tools, teams, or product areas mentioned, and prepare one or two sharp questions per area. Candidates report that asking specific questions about the team's current challenges signals genuine interest far more than generic ones.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating IMC like a consumer tech company. Framing your experience purely around user growth, engagement metrics, or app launches can signal a mismatch. Pivot to reliability, internal adoption, and risk reduction as your value levers when describing past work.

Being vague about your personal contribution. IMC interviewers probe with 'what did you specifically do?' Answers that stay at the team or project level without drilling into your individual role tend to score lower.

Skipping the constraint layer. In prioritization or design questions, many candidates jump straight to features. At a trading firm, constraints such as latency requirements, compliance rules, and system load often shape what is even buildable. Name them first before proposing solutions.

Overcomplicating the answer. IMC values clarity. A concise, well-structured answer beats an exhaustive one. If you are still talking three minutes into a STAR answer, you have likely over-indexed on context at the expense of your action and result.

Not asking questions. Candidates who ask no questions, or ask generic ones like 'what does success look like here?', tend to be remembered less. Prepare specific questions about the team's current product challenges or technical environment.

Hiding domain gaps instead of addressing them. If you lack fintech experience, acknowledge it briefly, then pivot to how quickly you have ramped on new domains in the past. Pretending to know more than you do typically surfaces in technical follow-up questions.

How many PM roles does IMC currently have open?

As of July 2026, knok jobradar shows IMC has 162 open roles, reflecting active hiring across product and technology teams. What salary can I expect as a PM at IMC in India?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India broadly range from 12-20 LPA at the Associate PM level, 24-40 LPA for a PM with 3-6 years of experience, 40-60 LPA at the Senior PM level, and 55-90+ LPA for Group or Principal PMs. IMC is a top-tier trading firm so their compensation is generally competitive within these bands, but specific IMC figures are not publicly reported at a meaningful scale.

Do I need a finance or trading background to apply for a PM role at IMC?

Not necessarily. Candidates report that IMC values technical depth and structured thinking over a pure finance background. You should, however, be able to speak intelligently about how trading platforms work, what risk management means in practice, and why system reliability and latency matter to a trading desk. Spending focused time on these fundamentals before your interview makes a meaningful difference.

How many rounds does the IMC PM interview process typically involve?

Candidates report the process typically involves multiple stages, often including a recruiter screen, one or more technical or product case discussions, behavioral interviews, and a final conversation with senior stakeholders. The exact number varies by team and seniority level. Prepare for structured case questions and open-ended behavioral discussions appearing in the same process.

Is IMC a good destination for PMs coming from SaaS or consumer tech?

It can be, if you are drawn to technically complex internal products and a high-performance culture. The context shift is real: internal users at a trading firm are expert, direct, and intolerant of downtime in a way consumer app users are not. Prepare examples from your SaaS background that demonstrate reliability work, internal tooling, or stakeholder management with expert users. That framing travels well into IMC interviews.
