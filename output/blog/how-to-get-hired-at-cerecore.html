01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

CereCore is a healthcare IT services company that partners with hospitals and health systems across the United States. They manage IT operations, support EHR (Electronic Health Record) platforms like Epic and Cerner, handle application support, and provide IT staffing to healthcare clients. Their entire business runs on people who understand both technology and the hospital environment.

As of mid-2026, CereCore has 35 open roles across functions including IT support, EHR analysis, project management, and infrastructure. If you have a background in healthcare IT, or want to build one, this is a focused employer with steady demand.

The company grew from Baptist Health System's internal IT division, so it carries the culture and discipline of a large hospital network. That heritage shows up in how they hire: they value people who are as comfortable explaining a system issue to a nurse as they are solving it at the backend.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

The CereCore hiring process generally runs through four to five stages, though the exact steps vary by role and team. Based on publicly available candidate accounts, here is what to expect.

Application and resume screen. Apply through CereCore's careers page or a job board. Recruiters look for relevant healthcare IT experience, familiarity with EHR platforms, and any credentials in IT or healthcare informatics. Tailoring your resume to the specific role description is important at this stage.

Recruiter phone screen. If shortlisted, a recruiter will reach out to confirm your background and interest. Expect questions about your work history, why you want to work in healthcare IT, and basic logistics like your notice period and location flexibility. This is also your chance to ask about the role and team.

Technical or functional interview. For analyst and IT roles, this stage goes deeper on your specific skills. A team lead or subject matter expert may walk you through real-world scenarios: how you would handle an EHR outage, how you have managed a complex ticket queue, or how you approach a system go-live. Be ready with concrete examples.

Hiring manager or panel interview. Shortlisted candidates typically meet the hiring manager, and sometimes a small panel. Expect a mix of competency-based questions ('tell me about a time you handled a critical incident under pressure') and questions about working in a clinical environment alongside non-technical staff.

Offer and pre-employment checks. CereCore extends written offers followed by background verification and reference checks. This is standard in healthcare IT, where staff may work with systems that touch patient data.

With 35 open roles currently listed, the company is actively hiring across multiple functions, so application timelines can move at different speeds depending on urgency.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

CereCore hires for a specific combination: solid IT skills plus genuine comfort working in or around hospital environments. Here is what tends to matter most.

Healthcare IT familiarity. Experience with EHR platforms, especially Epic, Cerner, or Meditech, comes up repeatedly in their job descriptions. Even support-level or training exposure counts. If you hold an Epic certification, mention it prominently.

Technical depth in the relevant domain. Roles range from service desk and application support to network administration and project coordination. Match your technical highlights to the specific job description rather than listing every skill you have.

Clear communication with non-technical audiences. CereCore staff often work directly with doctors, nurses, and hospital administrators. Being able to explain a system problem in plain language, without jargon, is a genuine differentiator.

Composure under pressure. Hospital IT has strict uptime expectations. Interviewers will probe for how you behave when a critical system goes down mid-shift. Calm, methodical troubleshooting is what they are looking for.

Reliability and accountability. Because CereCore embeds teams within client hospitals, your performance reflects directly on the company's relationship with that client. Consistent follow-through matters as much as technical skill.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Learn the basics of healthcare IT before your interview. Spend time understanding how EHR systems work, how hospitals manage their IT infrastructure, and what a typical day looks like for a hospital IT team. Publicly available training resources from Epic, Cerner, and HIMSS are a good starting point.

Mirror the job description in your resume and answers. CereCore postings are specific about the tools and experience they need. Build your interview stories around those exact requirements.

Practise STAR-format answers. Behavioural questions are common throughout the process. Prepare answers using the Situation, Task, Action, Result structure for scenarios like handling a system outage, supporting a go-live, or resolving a conflict with a clinical user.

Draw connections from adjacent experience. If you have not worked in healthcare IT before, link your background to skills that transfer: working under SLAs, supporting regulated environments, managing sensitive data, or collaborating with non-technical stakeholders.

Clarify your location flexibility early. Many CereCore roles require being on-site at a hospital or health system client. If you have preferences or constraints around travel or relocation, raise them early so both sides can plan.

Brush up on relevant certifications. ITIL, CompTIA, and EHR-specific credentials come up often in CereCore job postings. Even a plan to get certified is worth mentioning if you do not hold the cert yet.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

CereCore's culture reflects where it came from: inside a large hospital system, not a typical tech startup. The work is mission-driven in a practical sense. The systems they support affect whether a doctor can access a patient record or a pharmacy can process a prescription. That creates daily accountability that goes beyond SLAs.

The company values collaboration, especially across disciplines. Because CereCore embeds IT teams within hospital clients, staff regularly work alongside clinical and administrative teams who are not IT professionals. A low-ego, solution-focused approach, fixing the problem without creating drama for the people trying to care for patients, is genuinely rewarded here.

Career development comes through certification support, formal training programs, and the chance to work across different client environments. Employees often note that exposure to multiple healthcare systems accelerates their growth in ways that a single-employer role would not.

The pace is demanding in certain roles, especially during EHR go-lives or critical incidents. People who stay and grow at CereCore tend to be those who find the healthcare mission meaningful, not just those looking for a comfortable desk job.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Does CereCore hire candidates based outside the United States?

CereCore is a US-focused healthcare IT company and most roles require working on-site at hospital clients in the US. Some remote or hybrid roles do exist, but you will need to check individual postings carefully. Candidates with valid US work authorisation are in the strongest position to apply.

Which EHR platform should I focus on learning before applying?

Epic is the most commonly cited platform in CereCore's job postings, and Epic certifications carry clear weight in the application process. Familiarity with Cerner or Meditech is also useful depending on the role. Even completing free introductory modules on these platforms before your interview can strengthen your candidacy.

How long does the full hiring process take at CereCore?

Based on publicly shared candidate experiences, the process from application to offer typically spans two to six weeks. Roles tied to active client projects or go-lives may move faster when there is urgent need on the client side. Following up politely with the recruiter after each stage can help keep your application moving.

Do I need a clinical or medical background to work at CereCore?

No clinical background is required for IT roles. However, CereCore expects you to learn the healthcare environment quickly and work comfortably alongside clinical staff. Showing genuine curiosity about how hospitals operate, and a willingness to communicate in non-technical language, matters a great deal in interviews.

What does a typical CereCore workday look like?

It depends heavily on the role and client site. Support and analyst roles often involve managing tickets, troubleshooting system issues, and coordinating with clinical users throughout the day. During EHR go-lives, the pace is intense and hours can extend. Outside those periods, the work tends to be structured and predictable.

Are there growth opportunities within CereCore?

Yes. CereCore supports certification pathways and internal mobility across their client portfolio. Working across multiple hospital environments gives employees broader exposure than they might get in a single organisation. Many staff find this variety accelerates their career growth in healthcare IT faster than a traditional employer would.
