01 Overview

Overview

TravClan is a B2B travel-tech company that builds software for travel agents, agencies, and tour operators across India. Their platform covers hotel and holiday bookings, supplier connections, agent CRM, and financial tools. As of July 2026, knok jobradar tracked 53 open roles at TravClan, with Product Manager positions among the most active hiring areas.

PM roles here span the full stack of travel operations: booking flows, agent onboarding, markup and pricing tools, supplier API integrations, and analytics dashboards. If you are coming from consumer internet, the B2B angle is the biggest adjustment. Your customer is a travel agent or agency owner, not the end traveler, and your success metrics center on agent retention, booking volume, and supplier fill rates.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Three sample STAR answers tailored for TravClan PM interviews.

---

Q: How would you improve agent retention on a B2B travel platform?

*Situation:* At my previous company, a B2B SaaS for logistics, agent churn increased after we shipped a major UI overhaul. The support team was flagging repeated complaints about changed workflows.

*Task:* My goal was to identify the root cause and bring retention back to pre-launch levels within two quarters.

*Action:* I pulled cohort data and found that agents who had not completed onboarding in their first two weeks were churning at a significantly higher rate than those who had. I ran user interviews with recently churned agents and found the new UI had broken their muscle memory on bulk booking. I prioritized three fixes: a guided onboarding checklist, a 'classic mode' toggle for high-volume agents, and a proactive check-in message at day seven.

*Result:* Early activation rates improved materially within the first quarter, and churn dropped back below the pre-launch baseline. The 'classic mode' toggle had the biggest impact among agents with the heaviest booking volumes.

---

Q: A competitor launches a feature your agents are demanding. How do you decide whether to build it?

*Situation:* At a travel-tech startup I worked with, a competing platform launched a real-time hotel availability widget that our agent community was requesting loudly on support calls and in our community forum.

*Task:* I needed to give the team a clear build, buy, or delay decision within one sprint cycle so we did not lose momentum on our existing roadmap.

*Action:* I ran a quick impact-effort mapping session. First, I surveyed a sample of agents to separate the loudly vocal from those who would actually switch platforms. Most said it was a nice-to-have, not a switching factor. Second, I checked whether any supplier API we already had could power a basic version quickly. Third, I reviewed the competitor's implementation and found it had reliability problems on peak travel days. I recommended a lightweight version built on our existing inventory feed, scoped for a single sprint, with a clear adoption target to review at the 90-day mark.

*Result:* The lightweight widget shipped on schedule. Adoption met the target we had set, and that data justified a more robust version in the next quarter's planning cycle.

---

Q: Describe a time you used data to make a difficult product decision.

*Situation:* Our team was debating whether to sunset a legacy itinerary builder that a small but vocal group of senior agents loved, even though it created significant maintenance overhead for engineering.

*Task:* I needed to make a recommendation backed by data, not gut feel, because the sales team was strongly opposed to removing any feature that customers actively used.

*Action:* I analyzed several months of usage logs. Most sessions in the legacy builder were very short and produced no bookings. I mapped those users to revenue contribution and found they accounted for a small share of platform GMV. I presented this alongside the engineering cost: the maintenance burden was consuming meaningful dev capacity every month. I proposed a 90-day migration window with a concierge handoff to the new builder, giving agents time to adjust.

*Result:* Leadership approved the sunset. The structured migration window kept complaint volume low because agents felt supported in the transition. Engineering reclaimed capacity that went into a high-impact markup automation feature the following quarter.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

Four frameworks that work well for TravClan PM interviews.

STAR (for behavioral questions). Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep each section crisp. Interviewers at growth-stage startups like TravClan want real outcomes where you have them and an honest 'I cannot share exact figures' where you do not.

The B2B-first lens (for case questions). When a question asks you to improve a feature or enter a new market, always name the user segment first (small agency, enterprise travel management company, independent agent) before jumping to solutions. B2B products fail when PMs treat all users as a single persona.

North star plus guardrail metrics. For metrics questions, name one north-star metric (for example: 'weekly active booking agents'), then name two guardrail metrics you would not let fall (for example: booking confirmation rate, supplier fill rate). This shows systems thinking rather than optimizing one number in isolation.

RICE for prioritization (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort). TravClan interviewers often probe how you choose what to build next. Walk through RICE out loud, assign rough scores, and explain your confidence levels. Saying 'I would validate confidence by talking to a handful of agents before committing engineering time' signals the right instinct.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Based on the nature of TravClan's product and what candidates typically report, interviewers look for four things.

Travel or B2B domain awareness. You do not need prior travel experience, but you do need to understand that the platform's customer is a travel agent, not the end traveler. Pitching consumer-style features without grounding them in agent workflows and booking economics is a common miss.

Data comfort without data worship. TravClan is a startup, so data is often incomplete. Interviewers want to see that you can make a call with imperfect information, state your assumptions clearly, and describe how you would validate them quickly.

Stakeholder balance. PMs at TravClan sit between sales (who promise features to close deals), engineering (who build them), and operations (who support agents after launch). Candidates who can hold a principled roadmap under pressure from each direction tend to do well.

Bias toward shipping. Growth-stage companies value PMs who scope down to an MVP, ship it, and learn from real usage rather than perfecting the spec in isolation. Show examples where you made a deliberate trade-off to move faster.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

A focused plan you can run in one to two weeks before your TravClan PM interview.

Days 1-3: Understand the product. Sign up for TravClan's platform if access is available, or watch demo content on their official channels. Map out the core agent journey: how does an agent search, price, book, and confirm a holiday? Note friction points you would prioritize fixing.

Days 4-5: Research the domain. Read publicly available material on India's travel agent ecosystem: how agents earn commissions, how suppliers connect to B2B platforms, and where digital tools are replacing manual processes. This gives you vocabulary that signals genuine interest in the case rounds.

Days 6-8: Prepare your STAR stories. Pull out five to six stories from your past work covering: a product you owned end to end, a data-driven decision, a stakeholder conflict you resolved, a launch that did not go as planned, and a feature you chose NOT to build. Practice delivering each in under three minutes.

Days 9-10: Practice case questions. Run at least three product cases using the B2B-first lens from the frameworks section. Time yourself and ask a peer to challenge your metrics choices and prioritization logic.

Day before. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for your interviewers, focused on the team's current roadmap priorities, how product and engineering collaborate, and what a strong PM's first 90 days looks like at TravClan.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating TravClan like a consumer app. Pitching features optimized for the end traveler experience (beautiful UI, personalization feeds) without grounding them in agent productivity and booking economics is one of the most common misses candidates report.

Vague metrics. Saying 'I would measure engagement' without specifying which action, which user segment, and over what time window signals a lack of PM sharpness. Always attach a metric to a decision and explain why that metric and not another.

Over-engineering the case. TravClan moves fast. If your product case answer requires months of discovery before any code is written, interviewers may flag you as too slow for the pace. Show you can scope an MVP and validate assumptions quickly.

Not asking questions at the end. At a startup with 53 open roles, team structures evolve quickly. Understanding what you will own in the first quarter, who you will partner with, and what success looks like for your manager is genuinely important, not just polite.

Assuming one agent persona. TravClan serves agents ranging from solo independent operators to large agency chains. A solution that works for a solo agent (simple, low-volume bookings) may frustrate a chain manager who needs bulk operations and multi-user access. Candidates who segment the user base tend to stand out.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does TravClan typically have for a PM interview?

Candidates report between three and five rounds in total, though the exact number varies by level and team. Typically there is a screening call, at least one product case or assignment round, a behavioral interview, and a final discussion with a senior leader. Some candidates report an additional data or analytical round for senior roles.

Does TravClan give a take-home assignment?

Several candidates have reported receiving a take-home case study, usually asking you to analyze a product problem or propose a feature for TravClan's platform. You typically get two to three days to submit. Treat it like a real work product: structure your answer clearly, state your assumptions, and be explicit about what you would validate before committing engineering time.

Do I need travel industry experience to join TravClan as a PM?

Prior travel experience is not a stated requirement, but understanding the B2B travel ecosystem helps significantly. If you are coming from a different domain, spend time learning how travel agents earn (commissions, markups, service fees) and how B2B booking platforms connect to suppliers. Showing this research in your interview signals genuine preparation and interest in the space.

What salary can I expect as a PM at TravClan?

Based on knok jobradar data, PM salaries in India range from 24-40 LPA for roles requiring three to six years of experience, and 40-60 LPA at the senior level. TravClan's specific offers depend on your experience, the seniority of the role, and negotiation. Check Glassdoor and levels.fyi for peer-reported TravClan figures to calibrate your expectations.

How should I prepare for TravClan's product case round?

Use the B2B-first lens: always name the user segment (independent agent, agency chain, travel management company) before jumping to solutions. Practice structuring your answer clearly: define the problem, name your target user, propose metrics, prioritize one solution, and describe your MVP. Interviewers typically value clear thinking and fast scoping over exhaustive analysis.

Is there anything specific about TravClan's culture I should know before the interview?

TravClan is a growth-stage startup, so candidates report that interviewers value bias toward action, comfort with ambiguity, and the ability to own outcomes end to end. An 'I need perfect data before I decide' mindset can work against you. Show examples where you made a reasonable call with incomplete information and then course-corrected based on what you learned.
