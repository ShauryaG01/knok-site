01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Cynlr is a deep-tech robotics startup building autonomous systems for industrial and manufacturing environments. The company focuses on robots that can handle unstructured, real-world tasks that traditional factory automation cannot manage. That is a genuinely hard problem, and it has attracted serious engineering talent and investor backing.

As of mid-2026, Cynlr has 46 open roles, spanning engineering, product, operations, and business development. That number signals active expansion across multiple functions. If you are a robotics engineer, computer vision specialist, embedded systems developer, or a strong generalist who wants to work on hard problems, Cynlr is worth pursuing seriously.

The hiring bar reflects the difficulty of what they are building. Cynlr is not looking for people who need hand-holding or detailed specs to get started. They want builders who think independently, move with urgency, and genuinely care about making robots work reliably in the real world.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Cynlr's interview process, based on publicly available candidate accounts, typically runs through five or six stages. The exact sequence can vary by role and team, so treat this as a general map rather than a guaranteed blueprint.

Resume and application screening. The team reviews applications for relevant technical depth. For engineering roles, projects involving robotics, computer vision, machine learning, or embedded systems attract immediate attention. A GitHub profile or a portfolio of real work strengthens your application considerably.

Recruiter or HR call. This is a brief conversation to confirm your background, explain the role in more detail, and check alignment on basics like location and notice period. Be ready to summarise your experience clearly in a couple of minutes.

Technical assignment or take-home task. Many candidates at Cynlr report receiving a practical problem to solve independently. For software and robotics roles, this may involve a coding challenge, a small system design exercise, or a problem related to perception or motion planning. Quality of your solution matters more than speed here.

Technical interview rounds (one or two rounds). These probe your core skills. Expect questions on data structures, algorithms, system design, and domain knowledge specific to your role. For robotics and computer vision roles, topics like sensor fusion, ROS, SLAM, or deep learning pipelines may come up. For software roles, competitive programming style questions alongside system design are common.

Cross-functional or team-fit interview. You will likely speak with someone from a closely related team, a senior engineer, or a product lead. This round tests how you think through ambiguous problems and collaborate across disciplines.

Founder or leadership round. For senior and specialised roles, a conversation with a co-founder or senior leader is common. They want to understand your motivation and whether you genuinely care about the problem Cynlr is solving.

Offer and negotiation. If the rounds go well, expect a discussion covering fixed pay, equity, joining date, and other terms. Come prepared with a clear sense of your expectations on all three.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Cynlr sits at the intersection of robotics, computer vision, machine learning, and mechanical engineering. The traits they hire for reflect that complexity.

Technical depth in a relevant domain. Whether it is ROS, deep learning, embedded C++, path planning, or mechatronics, the team wants people who have gone deep in at least one area. Surface-level familiarity is not enough. They want evidence that you have actually built things that work.

First-principles thinking. Robots operating in unstructured environments fail in unexpected ways. The team values engineers who can reason from the ground up when no prior solution exists, rather than looking for a tutorial to follow.

A builder's mindset. Academic knowledge alone will not carry you far here. Cynlr wants to see evidence that you have shipped things, whether through open-source projects, internship work, research with hardware in the loop, or personal builds.

Comfort with ambiguity. Startup work means requirements shift, problems evolve, and decisions often need to be made with incomplete information. Candidates who need rigid structures and detailed briefs tend to struggle in this environment.

Clear communication across disciplines. Robotics is inherently cross-disciplinary. You will work with people who think in hardware, software, and systems simultaneously. The ability to explain your thinking clearly and learn from colleagues with very different expertise is something Cynlr actively looks for.

Genuine curiosity about the mission. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually thought about why autonomous robots matter for manufacturing. That curiosity signals long-term motivation and it comes through clearly in conversation.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Start with the product. Spend time understanding what Cynlr actually builds before you apply. Read their website, look for any papers or patents they have published, and watch any demos or founder talks you can find. The more concretely you can discuss their technical approach, the more credible you will come across.

Brush up on domain skills for your specific role. For robotics and computer vision engineers, revisit topics like SLAM, object detection, sensor fusion, kinematics, and ROS-based architectures. For software engineers, practise data structures, algorithms, and distributed systems design. For embedded roles, review real-time operating systems, motor control, and hardware-software interfaces.

Build or document something relevant. If you have time before applying, a focused project in robotics or computer vision (even a simulation) gives you something concrete to walk through in interviews. If you already have relevant work, document it clearly so you can explain your decisions and trade-offs confidently.

Prepare for the take-home assignment. Treat any assignment as a mini-project rather than a quick exercise. Write clean code, add comments where logic is non-obvious, and include a short note explaining your approach and the trade-offs you considered. That extra context shows engineering maturity.

Practice explaining your past work in depth. A common pattern in deep-tech interviews is 'tell me about the hardest technical problem you have solved.' Prepare a few strong examples using the situation-action-result format, and be specific about your personal contribution versus what the team did collectively.

Have sharp questions ready. At every stage, ask something that shows you have done your homework. Questions about current technical challenges, how they validate robot performance in the field, or how teams are structured signal genuine interest rather than just going through the motions.

Optimise your resume for the application. Mirror the language from the job description, list specific tools and frameworks you have used, and keep formatting clean and simple. Many companies at this scale use applicant tracking systems before a human ever sees your resume, so keyword alignment matters.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Cynlr operates with the pace and intensity typical of a well-funded deep-tech startup. If you have spent your career in large corporations with quarterly planning cycles and multiple approval layers, the shift will be noticeable.

Mission over routine. The team is motivated by solving a genuinely difficult problem. People who thrive here tend to care about the outcome, not just the process. Expect conversations about why something matters as much as how to build it.

High ownership, low hierarchy. At this stage, individual contributors often own entire problem areas. That means more freedom to make decisions and more accountability when things do not go as planned. What you actually deliver matters far more than your title.

Collaborative problem-solving across disciplines. The cross-disciplinary nature of robotics means you will regularly work with people who have very different technical backgrounds. A culture of teaching and learning across domains is central to how the team operates on a daily basis.

Direct feedback. Feedback at a company like Cynlr tends to be candid and quick. If your approach is off or your code has a problem, you will hear it promptly. That directness can feel uncomfortable initially but it is how the team improves fast and avoids compounding mistakes.

Equity as part of the conversation. As a startup, Cynlr typically offers equity alongside fixed compensation. Joining early at a company with strong traction carries upside potential, though equity outcomes depend on many factors and are never guaranteed. Make sure you understand the vesting schedule and terms before signing.
