01 Hiring Overview

Hiring Overview

Vapi builds the infrastructure layer for voice AI, letting product teams create, test, and deploy voice agents without having to wire up telephony pipelines from scratch. The company is backed by Y Combinator and has grown quickly as demand for AI-powered voice interfaces picks up across customer support, healthcare, and sales.

As of mid-2026, Vapi has 34 open roles spanning backend engineering, AI/ML, developer relations, sales, and support. Most positions are listed as remote, which means Indian candidates with strong communication skills and the ability to work across time zones have a real shot at these roles.

If you are an engineer who has worked with APIs, real-time audio systems, or large language model integrations, Vapi is worth a serious look. The hiring bar is high and the process moves fast, which is typical for early-stage YC companies.

03 Interview Process

Interview Process

Vapi's process has roughly four stages, though the exact order and number of rounds can vary by role and team. The information below is based on publicly available candidate accounts and may not reflect every hiring track.

Stage 1: Application and initial screen
You apply through Vapi's careers page. If your resume matches, a recruiter or hiring manager typically reaches out within a week or two for a short introductory call. They want to confirm your background, your interest in voice AI, and your availability.

Stage 2: Technical or role-specific assessment
For engineering roles, this is usually a take-home task or a live coding session focused on real-world problems, not pure algorithm puzzles. Candidates commonly report tasks involving API design, audio stream handling, or building a small voice-agent prototype. Non-engineering roles may involve a case study or a short written exercise instead.

Stage 3: Deep-dive interviews
This round typically involves two or three back-to-back calls with senior engineers or team leads. For engineers, expect system design questions around scalable, low-latency systems. For product and growth roles, expect scenario-based questions on go-to-market strategy and customer understanding.

Stage 4: Founder or leadership call
For many roles, especially senior ones, a final conversation with a founder or a VP is common. This is less a test and more a mutual check: they want to see if you are excited about the mission, and you get to ask hard questions about roadmap and culture.

End-to-end, candidates commonly report the full process taking two to four weeks.

04 What They Look For

What They Look For

Vapi hires people who are comfortable with ambiguity and genuinely curious about voice AI as a technology. A few things stand out across roles.

Hands-on builders. Even for non-engineering roles, Vapi values people who have shipped things. If you have a GitHub repo, a side project, or a working demo of something voice-related, mention it prominently.

API-first thinking. Because Vapi's product is developer infrastructure, almost every team thinks in terms of how developers will integrate with their platform. Understanding webhooks, SDKs, and developer experience is a plus across functions, not just engineering.

Low-latency systems knowledge (for engineering roles). Real-time voice requires millisecond-level thinking. Familiarity with WebSockets, streaming protocols, or telephony (SIP, WebRTC) is valued for backend and infrastructure roles.

LLM integration experience. Vapi sits at the intersection of telephony and large language models. Experience connecting LLM APIs to production workflows is a strong differentiator, regardless of which provider you have used.

Written communication clarity. For a remote-first company, clear writing matters a lot. Slack messages, design docs, and concise emails are taken seriously during the hiring process itself, not just on the job.

05 How To Prepare

How To Prepare

Build something with Vapi before your interview. Vapi has a free tier and solid documentation. Spending a weekend building a small voice agent demo, even something simple, gives you real talking points and shows genuine interest. Interviewers notice when a candidate has actually used the product.

Brush up on real-time systems. If you are applying for an engineering role, review how WebSockets work, what latency trade-offs look like in audio pipelines, and how you would design a system that needs to respond in under a second. These topics come up often, based on commonly reported candidate experiences.

Know the LLM ecosystem. Understand how LLM APIs work, what streaming completions look like, and how you might switch between model providers. You do not need to be an ML researcher, but you should be comfortable integrating these APIs in a production setting.

Prepare a concise story about a past project. Pick one project where you owned something end-to-end. Explain the problem, the solution, and what you would do differently. Keep it tight so it lands clearly in a spoken conversation.

Research the voice AI space. Know who Vapi competes with, what use cases voice agents are being deployed for (customer support, healthcare triage, outbound sales), and what the open technical challenges are. This preparation shows in founder or leadership rounds.

For non-technical roles, prepare specific examples of how you have worked with engineering teams, explained complex products to non-technical buyers, or built a function from scratch at a startup.

06 Culture And Values

Culture And Values

Vapi operates as a remote-first company with a small, focused team. The culture is what you would expect from a fast-growing YC-backed startup: high ownership, direct feedback, and a bias toward shipping over planning.

Speed is a core value. Decisions are made quickly and roles often expand beyond their original scope. If you prefer detailed job descriptions and clearly bounded responsibilities, this environment may feel unstructured. If you enjoy building in open space, it can be energising.

Developer empathy is central. Because the product serves developers, the team thinks deeply about what it feels like to integrate a new tool at 2 AM when something breaks. This shows up in how they write documentation, design APIs, and prioritise bug fixes.

Async communication is the default. Given a globally distributed team, most collaboration happens through written channels. Being able to move work forward without waiting for a live meeting is genuinely important here, not just a line on a job description.

Equity and upside are part of the picture. Like most early-stage startups, Vapi relies partly on equity to attract talent. When evaluating an offer, factor in the stage, the vesting schedule, and your own risk appetite alongside the cash component.

Frequently asked

Does Vapi hire candidates based in India, or are roles limited to the US?

Vapi lists most roles as remote, and Indian candidates regularly join globally distributed startups at this stage. The main factors are your skill match, clear English communication, and willingness to overlap with US time zones for key meetings. It is worth applying directly and asking the recruiter about location flexibility on the first call.

What is the salary range at Vapi for Indian or remote candidates?

Vapi does not publicly list salary bands for all roles. For remote engineering roles at VC-backed voice AI startups at this stage, Glassdoor and levels.fyi list a wide range depending on seniority, location, and role type. Factor equity into any offer evaluation, since early-stage startup compensation is often structured with a lower cash base offset by meaningful equity upside.

How long does the full Vapi interview process take?

Candidates commonly report the full process taking two to four weeks from first recruiter contact to offer. The process can move faster if a team has an urgent need or if you are responsive between rounds. Delays are usually on the company's side during scheduling, so following up politely after a week is perfectly appropriate.

Do I need a computer science degree to get hired at Vapi?

Vapi, like most developer-tool startups, cares far more about what you have built than where you studied. A strong GitHub portfolio, open-source contributions, or a working demo will carry more weight than a degree from a top college. Non-engineering roles have even less emphasis on formal credentials and more on demonstrated results.

What is the single best thing I can do to stand out in Vapi's application?

Build a small working project using Vapi's platform before you apply or before your first technical round. A working demo, even a simple one, signals genuine interest and gives you concrete experience to discuss. Pair that with a clear, specific resume that shows real-world impact rather than just a list of technologies you have touched.
