01 Overview

Overview

Claroty is a specialist in OT (operational technology), IoT, and industrial control system security. Their platform helps manufacturers, utilities, energy companies, and healthcare facilities discover, monitor, and protect the devices running their physical operations.

As a Solutions Engineer (SE) at Claroty, you sit at the intersection of deep technical knowledge and customer-facing support. Your day involves running product demos, leading proof-of-concept (PoC) deployments at customer sites, handling technical objections during sales cycles, and feeding customer insights back to the product team. It is a role that rewards people who can talk to a plant engineer about Modbus traffic one hour and explain business risk to a CISO the next.

The broader Solutions Engineer market across India has 1,270 active openings as of July 2026. Bangalore leads with 55 listings, followed by Mumbai (23), Delhi (20), Pune (12), Hyderabad (6), and Chennai (5). Claroty itself currently has 17 open roles, reflecting active expansion in the region.

The interview process typically spans three to four rounds. Candidates report an initial recruiter screen, a technical deep-dive with a senior SE or solutions architect, a mock demo or whiteboard exercise, and a final conversation with regional sales leadership. Expect questions across OT security concepts, the Claroty product portfolio, competitive handling, and behavioral scenarios drawn from your past experience.

02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These are the questions candidates most commonly report from Claroty SE interviews. Study each one before your panel.

  1. How would you explain Claroty's platform to an OT engineer who has never used a dedicated security product?
  1. Walk us through how you would structure a proof-of-concept for a large manufacturing customer who is worried about network visibility.
  1. A customer's IT security team wants active scanning, but the OT operations team refuses to allow anything near the PLCs. How do you resolve this?
  1. What do you know about common OT protocols such as Modbus, DNP3, PROFINET, or EtherNet/IP, and how does passive monitoring work with them?
  1. Describe a time you lost a technical evaluation to a competitor. What happened, and what would you do differently?
  1. How would you respond if a customer tells you a competing product is cheaper?
  1. How do you stay current on the OT and ICS threat landscape? Name a recent threat actor or campaign targeting industrial environments.
  1. Walk us through the most complex technical implementation you have led. What was the hardest part, and how did you handle it?
  1. A key internal champion at a customer account suddenly leaves the company mid-deal. What do you do?
  1. How would you prioritize which vulnerabilities or risks to highlight during a limited-time demo for a water utility customer?
  1. Describe your experience with network segmentation or the Purdue model in industrial environments.
  1. If a customer's OT network is completely air-gapped, how does Claroty's deployment model adapt to that constraint?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every behavioral and scenario question. Here are three worked examples.

Q: Walk us through how you would structure a PoC for a manufacturing customer worried about network visibility.

*Situation:* At my previous employer, a mid-sized automotive parts manufacturer came to us after a ransomware scare. Their OT team had no idea how many devices were on the factory floor network.

*Task:* I needed to design a PoC that proved we could passively discover all OT assets without touching or disrupting a single production line.

*Action:* I coordinated with their OT lead to identify a span port on the primary OT switch. We deployed a sensor in passive, read-only mode. I set up a shared dashboard and ran brief daily syncs with both the IT and OT leads for the first two weeks to address questions as they came up.

*Result:* Within two weeks they had visibility into assets they did not know existed. The PoC converted into a full paid deployment within six weeks of kickoff.

Q: A customer's IT team and OT team disagree on deployment approach. How do you handle it?

*Situation:* A utilities customer had a strong IT security team pushing for active scanning while the OT operations team refused to allow anything near their PLCs.

*Task:* My job was to find a deployment path both teams could accept, or the deal would stall.

*Action:* I ran a joint workshop with both teams. I walked them through how passive, tap-based monitoring works versus active scanning, and shared case studies from similar utilities running passive-only deployments. I proposed starting on a non-critical segment so the OT team could confirm there was zero production impact before we expanded.

*Result:* Both teams agreed to a phased passive deployment. The OT team became our strongest internal advocates and helped accelerate budget sign-off.

Q: How would you respond if a customer tells you a competing product is cheaper?

*Situation:* During the final evaluation at a chemical plant, the procurement lead told me a competitor had quoted a lower price.

*Task:* I needed to reframe the conversation around value without badmouthing the competitor or making promises beyond my authority.

*Action:* I prepared a side-by-side comparison covering integration complexity, coverage for their specific Siemens environment, and support response times. I also arranged a reference call with a similar customer in the chemical sector who could speak to their real-world implementation experience.

*Result:* The customer chose Claroty. They told me the reference call and the integration story were the deciding factors, not the unit price.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR for behavioral questions. Every 'tell me about a time' question deserves four clearly labelled beats: Situation (brief context), Task (what was your specific responsibility), Action (what you personally did, step by step), and Result (measurable or observable outcome). Keep Situation and Task short so you spend most of your time on Action and Result.

Demo-day structure for scenario questions. When asked how you would run a demo or PoC, follow this pattern: (1) Discovery first, understand the customer's top pain before you show anything. (2) Tailor the flow, lead with the use case that maps to their specific pain. (3) Handle objections in real time, pause and invite questions rather than barreling through slides. (4) Confirm next steps before the call ends.

Value framing for pricing objections. Do not defend the price directly. Reframe around three axes: total cost of ownership (integration, training, support), risk coverage (does the cheaper product actually cover their asset types and protocols), and reference proof (let a similar customer's voice carry the argument). Practise delivering this in under two minutes.

The 'both sides' bridge for IT vs OT conflicts. Claroty SEs frequently encounter IT teams that want control and OT teams that want zero disruption. The winning frame is: acknowledge each team's concern as valid, then show how passive monitoring satisfies the OT constraint while still giving IT the visibility data they need. Never take sides in front of the customer.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

OT security credibility. Interviewers want to know you can hold a technical conversation with a plant engineer. You do not need to have worked on a factory floor, but you must understand the difference between IT and OT environments, know the major industrial protocols by name, and be able to explain why passive monitoring matters in environments where active scanning can cause real-world damage.

Customer empathy and commercial awareness. An SE at Claroty is not a pure engineer. Interviewers watch for whether you naturally think about the customer's business problem, not just the technical configuration. Candidates who anchor every answer to customer outcomes rather than product features stand out.

Objection handling without arrogance. Industrial security buying decisions involve many stakeholders, often with conflicting priorities. Interviewers probe whether you can stay calm, acknowledge concerns genuinely, and steer toward a solution without dismissing anyone in the room.

Self-directed learning. OT security threats evolve quickly. Interviewers consistently ask how you stay current. Name specific threat intelligence sources, industry groups, or recent OT-focused security reports you follow. A vague answer like 'I read the news' does not impress.

Collaboration between sales and engineering. SEs bridge two worlds. Interviewers want evidence that you have worked effectively with both account executives and internal product or engineering teams, and that you can translate customer feedback into clear, actionable product requests.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one: build your OT foundation. Study the core industrial protocols: Modbus, DNP3, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, BACnet. Understand the Purdue model for industrial network segmentation and why IT security tools do not simply transfer to OT environments. Read at least one recent OT threat intelligence report from a credible source such as Dragos or Claroty's own published research.

Week two: know the Claroty platform. Go through every publicly available Claroty data sheet, solution brief, and recorded webinar you can find. Understand their core product lines, including their main platform and their healthcare IoT capabilities. Know what 'asset discovery' and 'network monitoring' mean in the Claroty context specifically.

Week two also: prep your STAR stories. Write out at least five stories from your past covering: a successful PoC or technical win, a lost deal and what you learned, a cross-functional conflict you resolved, a complex technical implementation, and a time you handled a difficult customer stakeholder. Practise saying each one out loud in under three minutes.

The week before your interview. Research Claroty's recent press releases, customer case studies, and any publicly reported news about their expansion or product updates. Know their main competitors in the OT security space and be ready to discuss differentiation without being dismissive. Prepare two or three sharp questions for the panel that show you have done your homework.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Treating the role as a pure engineering position. SEs at Claroty are revenue-adjacent. Candidates who only talk about technical configuration and never mention customer outcomes, deal cycles, or business impact miss the point of the role.

Not knowing OT basics. Candidates from pure IT security backgrounds sometimes show up without understanding why you cannot run an active scan on a PLC. This is a quick disqualifier. Study the IT vs OT difference seriously before your panel.

Name-dropping security standards without depth. Dropping a framework name or standard identifier to sound credible, then being unable to explain what it actually requires, signals surface-level preparation. Know the concepts behind the standards, not just the labels.

Vague competitive answers. Saying 'our product is better' without explaining why in terms the customer cares about will not convince an interviewer you can handle a real objection. Practise value-based differentiation with specific examples.

Not asking discovery questions in scenario exercises. When given a customer scenario, candidates who jump straight to the solution without first asking about the customer's specific pain look like product-pushers, not trusted advisors. Always lead with a discovery question.

Forgetting to close. In a mock demo or scenario exercise, many candidates forget to propose a clear next step. Claroty SEs are expected to move deals forward. End every scenario answer with a concrete proposed next action.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need direct OT or industrial experience to apply for a Solutions Engineer role at Claroty?

Direct OT experience is a strong advantage, but candidates report getting through with solid IT network security backgrounds combined with demonstrated self-study in OT concepts. Claroty's customer base spans manufacturing, energy, utilities, and healthcare, so the more sectors you can speak to, the better. If you come from an IT background, invest time before your interview in understanding OT protocols and why passive monitoring is preferred in industrial environments.

How many interview rounds does the Claroty SE process typically involve?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: a recruiter screen, a technical panel with an existing SE or solutions architect, a practical exercise such as a mock demo or whiteboard session, and a final conversation with regional sales or solutions leadership. The exact structure can vary by region and hiring manager, so confirm the format with your recruiter at the start. Preparing for a hands-on demo exercise from the beginning is a safe approach regardless.

What certifications are useful for a Claroty SE interview?

Certifications in network security fundamentals, cloud platforms, and industrial security concepts are commonly cited as relevant by candidates in this space. Vendor-neutral OT security certifications and familiarity with recognized industrial security frameworks carry weight with Claroty interviewers. However, practical experience and the ability to explain OT security concepts clearly consistently matter more than a certification list alone.

What does the day-to-day of a Claroty Solutions Engineer actually look like?

In practice, the role combines customer-facing technical work with internal collaboration. You will run product demos and technical workshops for prospective customers, manage PoC deployments alongside account executives, respond to technical questions in RFPs, and relay customer feedback to the product team. Travel to customer sites is common depending on the territory, and the split between field work and desk work varies by region and pipeline activity.

How competitive is the Claroty SE hiring process compared to other OT security companies?

Claroty is one of the more recognized names in OT security globally, so competition for SE roles tends to be meaningful. With 17 open roles currently, they are in an active hiring phase, which works in favour of a well-prepared candidate. Differentiating yourself through specific OT knowledge, strong customer scenario answers, and a clear commercial mindset gives you a real edge over candidates with generic security backgrounds.
