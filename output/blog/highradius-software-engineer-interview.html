02 Most Asked Questions

Most Asked Questions

These questions come up repeatedly in HighRadius Software Engineer interviews, based on candidate reports:

  1. Design a high-volume invoice processing system. How do you handle traffic spikes and ensure no invoice is lost?
  2. Optimize a slow SQL query running on a large accounts-receivable table with millions of rows.
  3. Explain eventual consistency versus strong consistency. When would you choose each in a payment system?
  4. Walk me through how you would build a REST API for a cash application module.
  5. How does the order-to-cash process work end-to-end, and where does software automation add the most value?
  6. Write a function to detect duplicate invoices given a dataset that may have noisy or partial matches.

  8. How would you design a microservices architecture for a reconciliation platform?
  9. How do you ensure idempotency when processing payment requests that might be retried by the client?

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: Tell me about a time you improved the performance of a slow system.

*Situation:* At my previous company, our accounts-payable reporting module was taking a very long time to load for clients with large transaction histories. Users were complaining and some were abandoning the page entirely.

*Task:* I was asked to investigate the bottleneck and propose a fix within a single sprint.

*Action:* I profiled the backend and found that a core SQL query was doing a full table scan because an index was missing on a composite key. I added the index, rewrote part of the query to filter earlier in the execution plan, and added a caching layer for reports requested repeatedly within the same day.

*Result:* Page load times dropped noticeably, the complaints stopped, and the affected clients renewed their contracts that quarter. The fix was deployed with zero downtime.

---

Q: Describe a production issue you debugged under pressure.

*Situation:* One evening our payment processing service started throwing errors for a subset of clients. Payments were failing silently and the on-call team was alerted.

*Task:* I was the on-call engineer and needed to identify the root cause quickly to avoid breaching our SLA.

*Action:* I checked the error logs, narrowed the issue to a third-party banking API that had changed its response format without notice. I wrote a quick adapter layer, tested it against staging, and deployed a hotfix.

*Result:* Payments resumed within the hour. I wrote a post-mortem and proposed a contract-testing strategy so API drift would be caught earlier in future.

---

Q: How do you handle disagreements with a teammate over technical decisions?

*Situation:* A senior colleague and I disagreed on whether to use a message queue or direct HTTP calls for a new integration between two internal services.

*Task:* We needed to align quickly because the feature was scheduled for the next release cycle.

*Action:* I put together a short written comparison covering latency, failure handling, and operational complexity. I shared it with the team and invited everyone to weigh in. We also looked at how similar integrations in the existing codebase were built.

*Result:* The team chose the message queue based on the failure-handling requirements. My colleague appreciated the structured approach, and we delivered the feature on schedule.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

For coding questions: think out loud before writing code. State your understanding of the problem, call out edge cases (empty input, duplicates, very large datasets), and walk the interviewer through your reasoning. HighRadius candidates report that interviewers care more about how you think than whether your first attempt compiles.

For system design questions: clarify requirements first, estimate scale, choose components (API gateway, database, cache, message queue), explain trade-offs, then discuss failure handling. For fintech systems, always mention data consistency, idempotency, and audit logging as non-negotiables.

For behavioral questions: use the STAR format. Situation (brief context), Task (what you were responsible for), Action (specific steps you took), Result (observable or measurable outcome). Keep Situation and Task short and spend the bulk of your answer on Action and Result.

For domain questions about order-to-cash or treasury: spend time on HighRadius's own product pages before your interview. You do not need a finance background, but knowing terms like 'cash application', 'deductions management', and 'payment reconciliation' signals genuine interest in the work.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

Strong CS fundamentals. Data structures, algorithms, and database design come up in almost every round. Candidates report that SQL, including query optimization and indexing, is tested more at HighRadius than at a typical product startup.

Comfort with financial domain concepts. You do not need a finance degree, but you should be able to discuss how invoices, payments, and reconciliation work at a high level. Interviewers notice when a candidate has clearly done their homework.

Systems thinking. Even for junior roles, HighRadius asks how you would design something at scale. They want engineers who consider failure modes, data consistency, and performance from the start rather than as afterthoughts.

Communication under challenge. Multiple candidate reports mention that interviewers ask follow-up questions mid-answer. This is intentional. They want to see whether you can defend your choices calmly and adjust your thinking when given new information.

Ownership mindset. Behavioral questions often probe for examples where you went beyond your assigned task, caught a problem proactively, or took responsibility for an outcome. Answers that end with 'I handed it off to my manager' typically score lower.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Week one, coding and SQL. Focus on arrays, strings, hashmaps, trees, and graphs at medium difficulty. In parallel, practice SQL: joins, subqueries, window functions, and query optimization. HighRadius's product is data-heavy, so SQL appears in both the coding round and technical interviews.

Week two, system design. Study how to design event-driven systems, REST APIs, and database schemas for transactional workloads. Review idempotency keys, distributed locks, and eventual consistency. Being able to sketch a reconciliation or payment processing architecture is a strong differentiator.

Week two, domain prep. Spend focused time on HighRadius's website and product documentation. Learn what 'order-to-cash' means end-to-end, what 'cash application' does, and how 'deductions management' works. A few hours here can set you apart clearly from candidates who skipped this step.

Throughout, behavioral prep. Write out several stories from your work history using STAR format. Cover at minimum: a time you improved performance, a time you handled a production incident, a time you disagreed with a teammate, and a time you took initiative beyond your core work.

Day before. Review your STAR stories once more. Check that each Result section has a concrete, specific detail rather than a vague claim. Get a good night's rest.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Jumping into code without clarifying. Many candidates start writing code the moment a question is stated. HighRadius interviewers typically expect you to ask at least one clarifying question first. What are the constraints? What does 'duplicate' mean exactly in this context?

Ignoring SQL depth. Candidates who prepare only for algorithmic coding are often surprised by SQL questions. If you have not written a window function or explained a query execution plan recently, revisit these before your interview.

Vague behavioral answers. Answers like 'I am a good communicator' or 'I always work well in a team' do not land. Interviewers want a specific story with a specific outcome. No concrete example means no credit.

Not connecting your background to fintech. If you have worked in banking, payments, or ERP domains, say so explicitly and early. If you have not, draw a parallel: 'I have not worked in order-to-cash directly, but the consistency and audit requirements I handled in my previous role are very similar.'

Skipping failure handling in system design. Designing only the happy path and never addressing what happens when a service goes down or a message is lost will cost you marks. Always mention retry logic, dead-letter queues, or fallback strategies.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many rounds does the HighRadius Software Engineer interview typically have?

Candidates typically report three to four rounds: an online coding assessment, one or two technical interviews covering coding and system design, and a final managerial or HR round. The exact structure varies by team and experience level. Senior roles sometimes include an extra design discussion round.

Is the HighRadius coding test difficult?

Candidates generally describe the online assessment as medium difficulty, similar to medium-level problems on competitive coding platforms. SQL and data structure questions are both commonly included. Consistent practice over a couple of weeks is typically enough to clear this round comfortably.

Do I need fintech experience to join HighRadius as a Software Engineer?

Not necessarily. Many engineers at HighRadius come from non-fintech backgrounds. What matters more is that you understand the company's domain well enough to have a meaningful conversation about it. Reading up on order-to-cash workflows and HighRadius's products before your interview makes a clear difference to interviewers.

What salary can a mid-level Software Engineer expect at HighRadius?

Based on knok jobradar data, mid-level Software Engineers with three to five years of experience in India generally see offers in the 15-25 LPA range across the market. Actual packages at HighRadius depend on your specific background, team, and negotiation. For company-specific numbers, Glassdoor and levels.fyi have community-sourced data worth checking.

How long does the HighRadius hiring process take from application to offer?

Candidates typically report the full process taking anywhere from a couple of weeks to over a month, depending on the team's urgency and scheduling. Timeline is not always in your control, but following up politely after each round is generally a good practice. Having other applications in progress helps reduce the pressure of waiting on a single company.

Does HighRadius ask system design questions even for entry-level Software Engineer roles?

Candidates report that even entry-level interviews at HighRadius include some system design discussion, though the depth expected is lower than for senior positions. You might be asked to design a simple API or explain how you would structure a database schema for a given problem. Preparing basic design concepts is worth the effort even if you are a fresher applying for your first role.
