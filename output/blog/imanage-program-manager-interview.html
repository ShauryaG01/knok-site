01 Overview

Overview

iManage is a legal technology company whose document and email management platform is used by law firms, corporate legal teams, and financial services firms across the world. The Technical Program Manager (TPM) role at iManage sits at the intersection of engineering, product, and enterprise client delivery. You are expected to own complex, cross-functional programs, manage dependencies across multiple teams, and communicate clearly with both technical builders and non-technical business stakeholders.

As of July 2026, iManage had 43 open roles listed on knok jobradar, reflecting active hiring across functions. The TPM role in particular requires familiarity with iManage's product ecosystem, including its document management capabilities, work product organisation features, and integrations with enterprise productivity tools and email infrastructure.

Candidates typically go through multiple rounds covering program management fundamentals, technical depth, stakeholder communication, and behavioral competencies. The process, as reported by candidates, usually includes a recruiter screen, one or more program management or technical interviews, and a leadership or culture round. Preparing concrete stories from your own experience, anchored in enterprise delivery contexts, is the most reliable way to perform well.

03 Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Sample Answers (STAR Format)

Q: How would you manage a program spanning two teams with competing priorities?

*Situation:* At my previous company, I was leading a program that required simultaneous changes from the core platform team and the API integration team, both of which had their own quarterly commitments.

*Task:* My job was to deliver a combined feature to an enterprise client on a fixed date, without the authority to reprioritise either team's backlog directly.

*Action:* I mapped all inter-team dependencies in a shared tracker and held weekly joint syncs so both teams could surface blockers early. I escalated only one dependency conflict to engineering leadership, framing it as a risk to the client commitment rather than an internal disagreement. I also created a shared definition of 'done' so both teams agreed on handoff criteria before work started.

*Result:* We delivered on the committed date with no post-launch critical issues. The client extended their contract for another year.

---

Q: Describe how you handled regulatory constraints in a technical program.

*Situation:* I was managing a program at a fintech company where the product needed to meet data residency requirements before we could onboard a European enterprise client.

*Task:* I needed to get the engineering, legal, and security teams aligned on what 'compliant' meant and build it into the program plan without slipping the launch date.

*Action:* I ran a dedicated compliance scoping session with legal and security in the first week, documented every non-negotiable requirement, and mapped each one to a specific engineering task. I created a separate compliance checklist that ran alongside the standard program tracker and held a checkpoint review three weeks before launch.

*Result:* We passed the client's security review on the first submission, which the client's procurement team said was rare. The program launched on schedule.

---

Q: A critical integration breaks two weeks before a major client go-live. How do you respond?

*Situation:* Two weeks before a high-visibility go-live for an enterprise banking client, a third-party identity provider pushed an unannounced update that broke our authentication integration.

*Task:* I needed to restore functionality, protect the go-live date, and keep the client informed without causing panic.

*Action:* Within the first hour of the incident report, I set up a war room with engineering leads and established a regular communication cadence with the client's IT lead. I evaluated three options: a workaround using a fallback authentication method, a rollback to the previous integration version, and emergency engagement with the third-party vendor. We went with the rollback while the vendor fix was tested in parallel, and I sent written status updates to the client at consistent intervals throughout.

*Result:* Full functionality was restored within two days. The go-live happened on the original date, and the client's IT director later cited our communication during the incident as a reason they renewed.

04 Answer Frameworks

Answer Frameworks

STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) is the baseline for all behavioral questions. Keep each section tight: one to two sentences for Situation and Task, three to four sentences for Action, and a concrete Result that includes client or business impact, not just 'we launched.'

Program risk framing. When asked about handling uncertainty or blockers, structure your answer around four moves: identify the risk early, quantify its impact on timeline and client, communicate it to the right stakeholders at the right level, and drive to a decision. Interviewers want to see that you treat risk as something to surface and resolve, not hide.

Stakeholder communication layers. iManage serves enterprise clients in regulated industries, so interviewers probe how you adapt communication. Describe what you tell the engineering team (technical specifics, dependencies, timelines) separately from what you tell business stakeholders or clients (impact, mitigation, revised commitments). Showing that you shift registers confidently is a strong signal.

Technical depth check. For questions about integrations or architecture, you do not need to have built the system yourself. Show that you understand the components well enough to ask the right questions, identify where risk lives, and know when to escalate versus when to trust the engineering team. Avoid spending most of your answer on implementation details at the expense of the program narrative.

05 What Interviewers Want

What Interviewers Want

iManage hires TPMs to drive complex programs in an enterprise legal tech environment. Interviewers typically look for a specific combination of qualities.

Enterprise client empathy. iManage's customers are law firms and financial institutions with low tolerance for disruption. Candidates who demonstrate they understand regulated-industry delivery requirements, not just agile velocity, tend to stand out.

Cross-functional ownership without authority. TPMs at iManage do not manage engineers directly. Interviewers look for examples where you influenced outcomes, resolved conflicts, and kept programs on track without relying on positional authority.

Clear communication under pressure. Stories where you delivered bad news well, ran a transparent war room, or rebuilt stakeholder trust after a setback carry more weight than stories where everything went smoothly.

Technical credibility. You do not need to code, but candidates report that interviewers probe whether you understand the difference between an API dependency and a UI dependency, or how a cloud migration differs from an on-premise deployment, well enough to have productive conversations with engineers.

Structured thinking. When given an ambiguous scenario, interviewers want to see you ask clarifying questions, state your assumptions, and build a logical response rather than jumping straight to an answer.

06 Preparation Plan

Preparation Plan

Step 1: Learn the product and the industry.
Read about iManage's core products, including its document management platform, matter-centric work product organisation, and cloud offerings. Understand who their customers are and what enterprise legal tech means for program delivery constraints. Pay attention to the kinds of third-party integrations and compliance requirements that come up in legal and financial services environments.

Step 2: Build your story bank.
Map eight to ten experiences from your career to the most common TPM themes: managing dependencies across teams, handling scope creep, communicating risk to senior stakeholders, navigating enterprise client escalations, and working within compliance or security constraints. Write each one in STAR format. One story can often answer multiple questions if you trim or expand it to fit the specific question.

Step 3: Practice and sharpen.
Do mock interviews with a peer or record yourself answering questions. Focus especially on: the 'walk me through a program you owned' opener, a question about a program that went wrong, and a question about a stakeholder conflict you resolved. Strong STAR answers in an interview typically run two to three minutes, not five.

Step 4: Research before the interview.
Review iManage's recent product announcements and any publicly available client case studies. Prepare two to three thoughtful questions for your interviewers that show you have done your research, for example how the team measures program success or how TPMs collaborate with product managers on roadmap decisions.

07 Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Describing process instead of impact. Many candidates explain what a TPM does in general rather than what they personally achieved. Every answer should include a concrete result: a client outcome, a business impact, or a risk that was avoided.

Vague stakeholder stories. Saying 'I aligned the stakeholders' without explaining who disagreed, what the conflict was, and how you resolved it is a missed opportunity. Be specific about the people, the tension, and the resolution.

Underestimating the enterprise context. iManage is not a startup. Candidates who give answers that only fit fast-moving consumer product environments, without acknowledging enterprise delivery realities like compliance, change management, and client SLAs, can come across as a poor fit.

Skipping the 'so what.' In regulated industries, 'we launched on time' is not always the strongest result. If you also passed a client security audit, avoided a compliance breach, or retained a key account, say so explicitly.

Over-explaining technology. TPMs should be technically credible, not technically exhaustive. Candidates who spend most of their answer on architecture details and very little on program outcomes get this ratio wrong.

Arriving without questions. Not having prepared questions for the interviewer signals low interest or poor preparation. Have at least two ready that are specific to iManage or the role, not generic questions you could ask anywhere.

Q Questions

Frequently asked

How many interview rounds does iManage typically have for a TPM role?

Candidates report the process typically involves three to four rounds: an initial recruiter screen, one or two program management or technical interviews, and a final round with a senior leader or hiring manager. The exact structure varies by team and location. Preparing for behavioral, technical depth, and stakeholder scenario questions across all rounds is the safest approach.

Does iManage ask coding questions in TPM interviews?

Typically, no. TPM interviews at iManage focus on program management, stakeholder handling, and technical understanding rather than writing code. Candidates report that interviewers do probe your ability to read a technical situation clearly, understand system dependencies, and have credible conversations with engineering teams. Being able to explain concepts like API dependencies, data migration risks, and integration failure modes in plain language matters more than coding ability.

What salary can a TPM expect at iManage in India?

iManage does not publicly publish salary bands for India-based TPM roles. Glassdoor and levels.fyi listings for TPM roles at similar enterprise software companies in India commonly cite a wide range depending on experience level and city. Checking those platforms with filters for Bangalore or Hyderabad, where iManage has a presence, will give you the most current publicly reported data to anchor your salary discussion.

Is prior legal tech experience required for a TPM role at iManage?

Candidates report that legal tech experience is helpful but not always required. iManage values enterprise software delivery experience, especially at companies serving regulated industries such as financial services or healthcare. If you lack legal tech background, frame your experience around the qualities that matter most: managing enterprise client programs, navigating compliance constraints, and coordinating large cross-functional teams.

How competitive is the iManage TPM hiring process?

As of July 2026, knok jobradar showed 43 open roles at iManage and 313 TPM jobs across India in total. Competition for any specific role depends on the applicant pool at that time, but TPM is a specialised function and candidates with enterprise B2B or regulated-industry delivery backgrounds typically stand out. Tailoring your resume and stories to the legal tech context will help differentiate your profile.

How should I follow up after an iManage TPM interview?

Send a brief thank-you note to the recruiter or interviewer within a day of the conversation, referencing one specific topic that came up to make it feel genuine rather than templated. Candidates report that iManage's process typically moves within one to two weeks after a final round, though timelines vary by team and location. If you have not heard back within the expected window, a polite follow-up email to the recruiter is appropriate and unlikely to hurt your chances.
